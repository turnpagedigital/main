## Lead

Nothing new docketed in the 48-hour window. The headline development of the cycle remains **Keller Postman's filing of mass arbitration claims against Google/Alphabet on behalf of advertisers**, with an aggregate damages figure of **~$218 billion** — the largest mass arbitration campaign ever attempted by dollar value, and the first that meaningfully targets corporate (vs. consumer or labor) plaintiffs.

## State of play

**Google advertiser mass arbitration.** Filed in waves beginning April 2026 by Keller Postman's Ashley Keller. The campaign is built on the two 2024 federal rulings that found Google illegally monopolized (i) online search and (ii) ad tech. Damages math is being supplied by a retained economist; Keller Postman represents "thousands" of advertiser claimants and is actively recruiting more.

Why this matters more than the underlying antitrust cases:

- **Google's own contracts force the venue.** Advertiser TOS require arbitration. That funnel makes a class-action escape valve unavailable and converts each individual advertiser claim into a separately filed AAA / JAMS / Keller-Postman proceeding — exactly the structure that has cost Amazon, Uber, DoorDash, and Intuit billions in earlier campaigns.
- **First corporate-plaintiff mass arb.** Most mass arb to date has been consumer/employee. Corporate plaintiffs change the political and reputational risk surface materially — these claimants are media-buyers, agencies, and brand advertisers with the discretion to walk from Google as a media partner.
- **$218B is a credible ceiling, not a floor.** The economist's number is calibrated to the *judicially found* monopoly overcharge across the two rulings. Settlement math will compress this aggressively, but the leverage of a fully-filed slate of claims will be substantial.

## Parallel tracks worth watching

- **Big Tech antitrust remedies phase.** The remedies orders flowing from the Google judgments are still being developed; mass arb is one accountability lane, structural remedies the other.
- **Apple / Meta / Amazon mass arb pipelines.** Plaintiff-firm signaling suggests Keller Postman, Labaton, and others are evaluating parallel campaigns against Apple's developer-tax conduct, Meta's ad-pricing conduct, and Amazon's seller-fee conduct. None has reached the filing stage.
