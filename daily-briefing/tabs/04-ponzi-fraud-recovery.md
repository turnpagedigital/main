# Notes on Tab 04 — Ponzi / Fraud Recovery

**Audience:** Federal receivers, recovery-fund managers, victim-side counsel, defense counsel for "net winner" investors, SIPC trustees, forensic accountants, asset-tracing investigators.

**Distinct from adjacent tabs:** Recovery-grade focus on *recovery mechanics* — how the cash gets back to victims. NOT about insolvency mechanics (Tab 03) or about regulatory enforcement as policy (Tab 06 covers mega class-action settlements; this tab covers fraud-specific recovery vehicles).

**Voice:** Recovery-grade — pragmatic, operationally specific, deal-aware. Reads like a Norton Rose Fulbright clawback note or an Akerman receivership update. Heavy on procedural mechanics: when the receiver was appointed, who the receiver is, what the asset pool is, what the distribution waterfall looks like, what the cross-border friction points are.

**Recurring sub-themes:**
- Active receiverships: Goliath Ventures (M.D. Fla., $328M crypto Ponzi), Paramount/Prestige (Heller, $400M), and emerging matters
- The "even the winners lose" doctrine — Norton Rose's canonical framing of clawback exposure
- Crypto-Ponzi tracing — USDT/USDC flow analysis, offshore exchange cooperation
- Venezuela / Caribbean asset-tracing friction
- SEC fiscal year-end enforcement push patterns
- Section 1782 cross-border discovery applications

**Signal test for high urgency:** A new SEC complaint with asset freeze, a federal receiver appointment, a major clawback ruling, an arrest of a principal, or a cross-border recognition decision. Two or more qualifying items = "high"; one = "normal"; zero = "quiet."

**Cross-references:**
- Cross-link Tab 03 (Crypto Insolvency) when a Ponzi-scheme case is being routed through Chapter 11 rather than receivership.
- Cross-link Tab 07 (bankruptcy creditor rights) on any clawback-related appellate decision.
