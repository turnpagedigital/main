# Notes on Tab 05 — Tech Mass Arbitration

**Audience:** Plaintiffs' antitrust firms, litigation funders, in-house counsel at advertiser brands and agencies, Big Tech antitrust defense counsel, AAA/JAMS administrators, claim-aggregator firms, GCs at competitive-platforms (Yelp, Yahoo, Bing/Microsoft).

**Distinct from adjacent tabs:** Litigation-grade with an antitrust-economics overlay. This tab is the *only* tab whose dominant procedural vehicle is arbitration (not court litigation). Editorial choices should foreground arbitration mechanics, claim-aggregation strategy, and damages modeling.

**Voice:** Litigation-grade with economics fluency. Reads like a Cornerstone Research analysis or an AdExchanger antitrust column. Heavy on overcharge analysis, damages models, claim-volume math, and arbitration-rule mechanics (AAA mass-arbitration rules, fee-shifting, claimant identification).

**Recurring sub-themes:**
- Keller Postman wave count and current claimant total (started at "thousands," may grow)
- Apple / Meta / Amazon parallel-track signaling
- AAA mass-arbitration rule changes (the 25-claim trigger, joint-arbitration mechanisms)
- DOJ Section 2 remedies in U.S. v. Google (search and ad tech)
- Litigation funding announcements (who's funding which campaign)
- UK CAT and EU DMA private enforcement as comparators

**Signal test for high urgency:** A new mass-arbitration filing wave, a remedies-phase order, an arbitration-clause enforceability ruling, or a major settlement. Two or more 48h items = "high"; one = "normal"; zero = "quiet."
