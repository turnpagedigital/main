## Key Developments (48-hour scan)

### 1. **Bitcoin Depot Chapter 11 Filing — May 18, 2026**
Bitcoin Depot, Inc. (BDPT on Nasdaq), the largest bitcoin ATM operator in North America by kiosk count, filed a Chapter 11 bankruptcy petition in the bankruptcy court for the Northern District of Georgia. The company cited:

- Operational unsustainability driven by state regulatory crackdowns (Kansas, Massachusetts, Iowa regulatory moves and enforcement actions)
- Pending litigation from Massachusetts and Iowa AGs alleging facilitation of crypto fraud schemes
- Macro decline in BTCkiosk utility as institutional and retail adoption shifted to mobile/web wallets
- Financial deterioration: Q1 2026 showed 49% YoY revenue decline and $9.5M operating loss (vs. $12.2M profit in Q1 2025)

**Market context:** Crypto ATM fraud losses reached $389 million in 2025, a 58% increase from 2024, driving state enforcement action against kiosk operators. Bitcoin Depot was a primary target due to its large footprint and high transaction volumes.

**Estate & claims:** Bitcoin Depot operated 9,276 kiosks as of the filing date. The Chapter 11 petition triggered immediate shutdown of all kiosk operations, stranding customer BTC transactions in progress.

## Strategic Commentary

Bitcoin Depot's Chapter 11 filing signals maturation of the crypto ATM market as a casualty of regulatory consolidation. Unlike Celsius (a lender with commingling issues) or FTX (a fraud-driven collapse), Bitcoin Depot's insolvency reflects straightforward business-model obsolescence: regulators made the kiosk business unprofitable through enforcement and compliance costs, and crypto adoption (via app-based wallets) eliminated the utility proposition.

For creditors and customers: Bitcoin Depot's kiosk shutdown creates customer property claims (in-flight BTC transactions), which will likely be subordinated to operational wind-down costs and statutory priority claims. The company's litigation exposure (MA/IA AG suits) will be stayed under 11 U.S.C. § 362 automatic stay, but indemnity claims against officers/directors may be pursued by the estate/trustee.
