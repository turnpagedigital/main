**May 19, 2026 · 7:15 AM UTC**

## Lead Development

The U.S. Court of International Trade has dealt a significant blow to the Section 122 tariff regime, holding that the President's 10% temporary global tariff proclamation violates the statutory prerequisites of the Trade Act. In a 2–1 divided decision (Slip Op. 26-47), the CIT found that balance-of-payments and current-account deficits do not satisfy Section 122(a)'s "fundamental international payments problem" standard. However, the court's relief is narrow: a permanent injunction applies only to the named plaintiffs (Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun, and the State of Washington), leaving CBP free to assess and collect duties on all other importers while the Federal Circuit appeal proceeds. On May 12, the CAFC entered an administrative stay, freezing the CIT's order pending appellate review.

## Key Developments (48-hour scan)

### 1. **CIT Section 122 Ruling — May 7, 2026**
The Court of International Trade (Slip Op. 26-47) issued a 2–1 opinion finding that the President's invocation of Section 122(a) of the Trade Act of 1974 to impose a 10% temporary tariff on all countries was unlawful. The court reasoned that the statutory predicate—a "fundamental international payments problem"—was not satisfied by the trade imbalance alone. Judge Kamieniecki's majority opinion distinguished between "balance-of-payments deficits" and "fundamental international payments problems," applying principles drawn from historical Section 122 precedent and legislative history. The dissent (Judge Marsden) argued the statutory language grants the President broader discretion.

**Importer exposure:** The ruling applies only as to the three named plaintiffs. All other importers remain liable for Section 122 duties pending CAFC review. *Expected CAFC briefing timeline: May–July 2026.*

**Refund implications:** Importers in the Burlap & Barrel class may seek entry-by-entry refund processing outside the CAPE system, depending on CBP guidance.

*Sources:* [CIT Slip Opinion 26-47](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/26-47.pdf); [Holland & Knight client alert](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/us-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-the-administrations); [Gibson Dunn analysis](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/)

### 2. **Federal Circuit Administrative Stay — May 12, 2026**
The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (CAFC) entered an administrative stay on May 12, 2026, temporarily suspending implementation of the CIT's injunction while the government's motion for a full stay pending appeal is considered. The CAFC has not yet ruled on the merits of the Section 122 validity question. The stay effectively reinstates the status quo ante: CBP continues assessing the 10% duty on all entries except those of the three named CIT plaintiffs.

*Briefing schedule:* Government due ~May 25; plaintiff reply due ~June 5; CAFC decision likely June–July 2026.

*Sources:* [Troutman Pepper analysis](https://www.troutman.com/insights/federal-circuit-hits-pause-on-cits-section-122-tariff-ruling/); [Mondaq advisory](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/international-trade-investment/1788410/appeals-court-keeps-section-122-tariffs-in-place-for-now-what-importers-need-to-know/)

### 3. **CAPE Progress Report — 47,315 Declarations Validated**
The CBP Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) mechanism continues processing IEEPA tariff refund requests. As of the most recent update (late April 2026), 75,306 CAPE Declarations have been submitted, of which 47,315 passed validation and are slated for refund. These 47,315 declarations cover approximately 11.2 million entries. CBP anticipated issuing the first refunds on or about May 11, 2026. The CAPE Phase 1 window covers unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation.

**Importer action items:** If your entry is missing from the validated declarations, verify CSV formatting and resubmit. If an entry is within the 80-day refund window (roughly April 22–June 9, 2026 for Phase 1), file via CAPE to preserve the streamlined path before falling into Phase 2 (post-liquidation requests).

## Strategic Commentary

The CIT's Section 122 ruling is the most significant tariff-law development in three months. The statutory predicate issue touches the core of the Trade Act's delegation framework, and the CAFC's resolution will likely settle Section 122 authority for years to come. Importers should monitor the CAFC briefing calendar closely: if the government loses, expect rapid Congressional response (either legislative restoration of Section 122 or a new tariff authority). If the government wins, the ruling closes a litigation avenue for importers and signals broad presidential tariff latitude under the statute.

In the interim, the CAPE refund processing is proceeding on schedule. Importers should prioritize filing declarations for entries within the Phase 1 window (unliquidated + 80-day-post-liquidation) to lock in the streamlined processing path before transition to Phase 2.
