## Key Developments (48-hour scan)

### 1. **Bartz v. Anthropic Fairness Hearing — May 14, 2026**
The proposed $1.5 billion AI copyright class action settlement reached its fairness hearing in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (NDCA), with Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín presiding. The hearing covered approval of the settlement amount, Class Counsel's attorney's fees petition, and objections filed by class members and third parties.

**Claims rate & opt-outs:**
- Claims filed: 92.77% of eligible works (class definition captures published works used in Anthropic's training data)
- Opt-outs: 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works (~0.04% of class)
- The low opt-out rate signals strong class member acceptance despite objections from some author organizations

**Fee petition issues:**
Judge Martínez-Olguín focused questioning on Class Counsel's fee request. Counsel represented a current hours-based fee of ~$22 million, with total projected fees (including future work) at $27 million. Counsel requested a multiplier of 6.92x the hours-based figure, resulting in a total fee award request of approximately $187 million. The judge pressed on the justification for the multiplier and the scope of future work.

**Timing of final order:**
Judge Martínez-Olguín indicated she would take the matter under submission and issue short orders "as soon as tomorrow"—suggesting final approval could issue as early as May 15–17, 2026.

### 2. **OpenAI MDL Discovery Dispute — 20M ChatGPT Logs Ordered Produced**
In the In re: OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation (MDL, SDNY), a federal judge has ruled that OpenAI must produce approximately 20 million anonymized ChatGPT conversation logs to copyright claimants. OpenAI had sought to block production on privacy grounds, arguing that user chat logs contain sensitive personal information and that production would violate user expectations. The court rejected this privacy-protective argument, finding that the public interest in adjudicating the fair-use defense to copyright infringement outweighs OpenAI's privacy concerns.

**Significance:** The 20M log production is material discovery that will allow plaintiffs to demonstrate whether ChatGPT outputs routinely reproduce or compete with copyrighted works. If logs show high rates of near-verbatim text generation or derivative output, OpenAI's fair-use defense weakens considerably. If logs show minimal overlap, OpenAI's position strengthens.

**MDL structure:** The consolidated action combines 16 copyright lawsuits against OpenAI, including author class actions, news-organization claims, DMCA suits, and video-creator claims. No trial date has been set as of May 19.

*Sources:* [Jones Walker analysis](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-to-copyright-p.html?id=102lzo9); [E-Week reporting](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-logs-new-york-times-copyright-mdl/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-to-copyright)

### 3. **Fair-Use Doctrine in AI Training — Continued Cross-Circuit Drift**
The three recent federal AI copyright decisions have produced conflicting fair-use rulings: Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence (No. 2025) found AI training on copyrighted content is *not* fair use; Kadrey v. Meta (No. 2025) found AI training *is* fair use; Bartz v. Anthropic (settlement, so no ruling on fair use) avoids the question but implies some liability through the settlement amount. The Copyright Office issued guidance in May 2025 that fair use must be determined case-by-case, with no blanket category for AI training.

**Litigation outlook:** The OpenAI MDL will likely produce the next appellate-level fair-use ruling (via SDNY, followed by Second Circuit appeal). The Elsevier v. Meta and Concord/UMG/ABKCO v. Anthropic cases also carry significant fair-use exposure.

*Sources:* [Norton Rose Fulbright AI litigation update](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026/)

## Strategic Commentary

The Bartz fairness hearing outcome signals acceptance of the $1.5B settlement terms by the author community, with 92.77% claims participation and negligible opt-outs. Final approval now hinges on the court's view of Class Counsel's $27M fee petition and the reasonableness of the 6.92x multiplier. If approved, Bartz becomes the largest AI copyright settlement ever and will serve as a valuation baseline for future AI publisher and musician settlements.

The OpenAI MDL discovery dispute tilts discovery toward plaintiffs. The 20M log production will directly test whether ChatGPT outputs qualify as fair use under transformative-use and market-substitution frameworks. Discovery will likely unfold over the next 12–18 months, with the first significant summary judgment motions expected by Q4 2026.

The cross-circuit split on fair use remains unresolved. The Second Circuit's treatment of OpenAI will be closely watched by litigants in the Ninth Circuit (Kadrey), Federal Circuit (Thomson Reuters), and Third Circuit (Concord music cases).
