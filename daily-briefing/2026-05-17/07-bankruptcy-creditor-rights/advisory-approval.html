I. Equitable Mootness — Cross-Circuit Drift Update

The Fourth Circuit's published opinion in Cook v. Chapter 13 Trustee (4th Cir. Apr. 13, 2026) rejecting application of the equitable mootness doctrine to a consumer Chapter 13 plan-confirmation appeal continues to drive the doctrinal cross-circuit drift. The Fourth Circuit reaffirmed equitable mootness as a narrow pragmatic doctrine reserved for cases where effective relief is no longer practical or would be inequitable, and concluded that in a straightforward Chapter 13 case involving limited creditors, limited assets, and prospective relief, the doctrine has no application. This places the Fourth Circuit alongside the Sixth Circuit's earlier Chapter 7 carve-out and the Eighth Circuit's rigorous-standard framing as part of the Circuits-narrowing-equitable-mootness camp.

The Third Circuit's separate ruling dismissing the Boy Scouts of America abuse-claimants' plan-confirmation appeal as "statutorily moot" for failure to obtain a stay pending appeal — distinct from equitable mootness — continues to be the controlling opposite-direction precedent. The cross-circuit picture as of May 17 is: Fourth Circuit (narrowing in consumer Ch 13); Sixth Circuit (Ch 7 carve-out); Eighth Circuit (rigorous standard); Third Circuit (substantial-consummation framework + statutory mootness as alternative); Fifth Circuit (bypass-friendly under certain conditions); Ninth Circuit and Second Circuit (mid-range). The SCOTUS cert docket has no equitable-mootness petition pending as of May 17.

II. Third-Party Releases — Post-Purdue "Exceptional Facts" Framework

The post-Purdue Pharma third-party release doctrine continues to operate in its "exceptional facts" working posture. The Third Circuit's allowance of a third-party release on "exceptional facts" in a recent matter — referenced by ABI's analysis — has become the working template for any plan drafter attempting to insert non-consensual non-debtor releases in a post-Purdue environment. The S.D.N.Y. has produced parallel decisions analyzing when non-consensual releases can survive scrutiny under the Supreme Court's Purdue framework; ABI's "recent SDNY decision adds to the fray" coverage remains the canonical practitioner reference.

The Fifth Circuit's plan-gatekeeping reining-in (Jones Day analysis, mid-2025) continues to limit bankruptcy-court use of plan-confirmation injunctions to broadly enjoin non-debtor litigation. Plan drafters should expect the "exceptional facts" framework to remain the operative test through 2026, with no SCOTUS retrenchment expected.

III. Ninth Circuit Exculpation — Broader-Clause Track

The Ninth Circuit's expanded exculpation-clause track continues to be the cross-jurisdictional divergence point for plan-drafting strategy. Tonkon Torp's analysis remains the controlling practitioner reference. Plan drafters operating across multiple Circuits should treat the Ninth Circuit posture as the upper bound on exculpation scope and the Third Circuit / Second Circuit posture as the lower bound.

IV. Texas Two-Step / Divisional Merger Ecosystem

The Texas Two-Step / divisional merger ecosystem (3M Aearo, J&J) remains the dominant mass-tort restructuring architecture. No new published Circuit decisions over May 15–17. The doctrinal contestation centers on (i) the bad-faith filing standard as applied to divisional-merger debtors and (ii) the constitutional limits on bankruptcy-court reach over non-debtor parents. The expected late-2026 / early-2027 appellate cycle in the 3M Aearo and J&J derivative appeals will produce the next round of doctrine.

V. Subchapter V Threshold

The Subchapter V threshold mechanics — the $7.5M debt-cap that elevates qualifying small business debtors into the modified Subchapter V framework — continue to operate at the current threshold pending congressional action. Subchapter V case-mix continues to shift toward middle-market debtors as the cap remains at $7.5M. Practitioners should expect a SUBchapter V threshold reauthorization debate in the next congressional cycle.

VI. SCOTUS Docket Watch

The Supreme Court docket has no pending cert petition on a Chapter 11 plan-confirmation issue as of May 17. The post-Purdue retrenchment cycle is over; the next SCOTUS engagement with bankruptcy doctrine is likely to come on Subchapter V threshold questions, the divisional-merger / Texas Two-Step constitutional questions, or the equitable-mootness cross-circuit split.

Recommended Actions

Plan-confirmation litigators should: (i) treat the Fourth Circuit's Cook ruling as the new controlling authority in any consumer Chapter 13 mootness argument; (ii) preserve the statutory-mootness alternative theory in any plan-confirmation appeal where a stay has not been obtained. Indenture trustees and ad-hoc creditor groups should: (iii) calibrate their post-Purdue third-party release strategy to the "exceptional facts" framework; (iv) recognize the Ninth Circuit expanded exculpation track as the cross-Circuit divergence point. Mass-tort claimants' committees should: (v) monitor the 3M Aearo / J&J derivative appellate cycle for late-2026 / early-2027 doctrine. Restructuring counsel should: (vi) maintain the SCOTUS docket watch on cert petitions in plan-confirmation, divisional-merger, and Subchapter V threshold matters.
