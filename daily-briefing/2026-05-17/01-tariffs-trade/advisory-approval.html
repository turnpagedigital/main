Tariffs / Trade

Daily Advisory · Sunday, May 17, 2026
Briefing — Lead

The IEEPA refund track and the Section 122 stay both remain in mid-stride heading into the weekend of May 16–17. CBP's second progress report (May 12) confirmed 8.3 million entries liquidated without IEEPA duties (~$35.46B in anticipated refunds + interest), and the Federal Circuit's May 12 temporary administrative stay continues to hold Section 122 tariffs in place pending expedited briefing that closes May 22. The June 7 DOJ appeal deadline on the scope of Judge Eaton's refund order — reset after the CIT's re-issued March 4 order — now functions as the central calendar peg, with no government filing or signaling through the weekend. Trade-law commentary over the last 48 hours has consolidated around the Greenberg Traurig and Buchalter framings: importers should run CAPE-plus-protest in parallel and treat Section 122 stay outcomes as binary, not gradient, exposure.

I. CAPE Refund Track — Operational Stabilization

CBP's second progress report to the Court of International Trade, filed May 12, remains the controlling operational snapshot heading into the May 17 weekend. The report confirms 8,338,081 entries liquidated or reliquidated without IEEPA duties against an anticipated refund and interest pool of approximately $35.46 billion. The phase-1 CAPE module is now capturing roughly 82% of IEEPA entries; the remaining 18% — entries with non-standard liquidation postures, suspended-entry status, or compliance flags — continues to be routed to manual processing. No major operational changes were reported over May 15–17.

The 1,880 stalled consolidated refunds previously flagged for missing ACH information remained outstanding as of the May 12 update, and Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg has reiterated that importers should not rely on CAPE alone — protest filings under 19 U.S.C. §1514 remain the durable preservation mechanism, particularly for entries that have not yet liquidated or where compliance review may trigger additional CBP scrutiny. Judge Eaton has set the next progress-report date for May 26.

II. Section 122 — Federal Circuit Stay Holds, Briefing Closes May 22

The Federal Circuit's May 12 temporary administrative stay of the CIT's May 7 ruling continues to keep the 10% Section 122 global tariff in force for all importers. The expedited briefing schedule — plaintiffs' response due May 19, DOJ reply due May 22 — closes this week, after which the Federal Circuit is expected to issue a substantive stay order on a compressed timeline. The CIT's underlying May 7 decision held by 2–1 that the statutory predicate of a "large and serious United States balance-of-payments deficit" was not satisfied and that persistent trade deficits could not stand in as a substitute; the relief, however, was narrow, extending only to Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun, and the State of Washington as named plaintiffs.

Regardless of the appeal outcome, the Section 122 surcharge expires by operation of statute on July 24, 2026 under the Trade Act's 150-day limit absent affirmative congressional extension — a prospect that remains uncertain in light of bipartisan reluctance to ratify a successor regime in the wake of the Supreme Court's IEEPA decision. The practical advice converging across the trade bar (Greenberg Traurig, Buchalter, Perkins Coie, Snell & Wilmer) is uniform: importers should continue paying the Section 122 duty during the stay and file their own CIT actions to preserve refund rights, since entries are liquidating in real time while the appeal proceeds.

III. DOJ Appeal Window — June 7 Pressure Point

With the reissuance of Judge Eaton's refund order on March 4, the 60-day DOJ appeal window has been reset to approximately June 7. As of May 17, no notice of appeal, motion for extension, or public administration signaling has surfaced. The lengthening silence is itself a market-moving fact: trade-law commentators are increasingly modeling a non-appeal scenario, in which the refund architecture stabilizes through the summer with the only residual litigation risk centered on the small subset of high-compliance-flag entries. Refund-recovery counsel should treat the June 7 date as the operative pivot — a missed appeal date converts the refund obligation from contingent to settled.

IV. Consumer Pass-Through and State AG Pressure

The coalition letter from eight state comptrollers and treasurers to President Trump (released publicly May 14) demanding disclosure of IEEPA refund applications and protections for consumers who bore the pass-through has begun to generate downstream activity. Minnesota State Auditor Julie Blaha has continued to advocate for per-household reimbursement frameworks anchored to Yale Budget Lab impact estimates; class-action plaintiffs' firms are filing putative nationwide actions under unjust-enrichment, contract, and state consumer-protection theories. Refund-recipient companies should be working through their pricing documentation now — the discovery posture in the early class-action filings is being built around whether companies retained the IEEPA-tariff surcharge in their pricing after the refund became foreseeable.

V. Section 301 Hearing Cycle and Onshoring Pipeline

USTR's 76 Section 301 investigations launched May 5 continue through their public-hearing cycle, with Ambassador Greer reiterating before House Ways and Means on May 11 that the administration is not entertaining exclusion requests. Findings will roll out as proposed new targeted tariffs through the summer. Separately, Commerce released applications for onshoring agreements that allow domestic producers to reduce Section 232 exposure in exchange for U.S.-based capacity-expansion commitments — a meaningful new lever for companies in steel, aluminum, semiconductors, and critical minerals.

Recommended Actions

Importers with IEEPA exposure should: (i) verify ACH registration on the CBP electronic refund portal to clear stalled consolidated refunds; (ii) continue filing §1514 protests in parallel with CAPE declarations; (iii) treat the June 7 DOJ deadline as the operative pivot for refund-finality modeling. Section 122-exposed importers should: (iv) continue paying the 10% duty during the Federal Circuit stay; (v) file their own CIT actions before the Federal Circuit decides the stay question, since the May 7 ruling was expressly named-plaintiff limited. Companies that passed IEEPA tariffs to customers should: (vi) document their pricing rationale and consider voluntary-disclosure strategies before consumer class-action discovery posture hardens. Companies in Section 301-affected sectors should: (vii) prepare comments to USTR hearings; and Section 232-exposed companies should: (viii) evaluate the new Commerce onshoring application as a tariff-reduction lever.
