**Headline theme:** Visa/Mastercard MDL 1720: 27 days to final-approval hearing, objections preserved

## LinkedIn Post

Twenty-seven days out from final approval of the revised Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement, In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation, MDL 1720 (E.D.N.Y.), is now on a hard calendar.

The hearing: June 17, 2026, 11:00 a.m. Judge Brian M. Cogan, who took the matter in September 2025 after Judge Margo K. Brodie's recusal.

The settlement: the November 10, 2025 revision after Judge Brodie rejected the 2024 deal on June 25, 2024. Coverage runs to approximately 12 million U.S. merchants and is positioned to close out 21 years of litigation.

The numbers in dispute:

— Headline interchange reduction: 10 basis points over five years.
— Rate cap: 1.25% over eight years on standard consumer credit transactions.
— Estimated overcharge per annum that the settlement is sized against: $13–15 billion.

The April 29 objection deadline closed with substantive objections from the National Retail Federation, National Association of Convenience Stores, National Restaurant Association, Walmart, and Hugo Boss preserved for argument. The merchant-group objection thesis is the same one that animated rejection of the 2024 deal: 10 bps over five years is insufficient relative to the overcharge baseline.

For merchant interested parties: pro-rata recovery modeling against the 10-bps cap and the 1.25% rate cap is the work to finish before June 17. Distributions, if approved, follow within 90–120 days of entry of final judgment.

Twenty-one years of litigation is about to settle. Or not.
