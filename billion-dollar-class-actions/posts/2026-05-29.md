## LinkedIn

Six days. That's all that's left for Roundup claimants to make the single most consequential decision in the entire settlement: stay in, or opt out.

The June 4 opt-out deadline in the Roundup MDL before Judge Chhabria (N.D. Cal.) is the kind of date that's easy to treat as procedural and devastating to miss. Here's why it isn't procedural.

Bayer's $7.25 billion class settlement, preliminarily approved in March, caps per-claimant recovery at roughly $165,000 — calibrated by disease severity and age. For many claimants that's a fair, fast, certain number. For a smaller group — those with the strongest causation evidence, the most severe non-Hodgkin lymphoma diagnoses, the cleanest exposure histories — it may be a fraction of what an individual trial could yield. The settlement is built for the median case, not the outlier.

The opt-out decision is binary and irreversible. Stay in, and you take the matrix payment and release your claim. Opt out by June 4, and you keep the right to sue individually — bearing the cost, delay, and risk of trial, but preserving your full upside. Do nothing, and you're in.

There's a live legislative wildcard too: Senator Booker's Pesticide Injury Accountability Act would amend FIFRA to create a federal right of action for pesticide-injury claimants, and he's filed an amicus at the Supreme Court supporting plaintiffs in the pending Roundup review. Neither changes the June 4 math, but both matter to how an opt-out's individual case might look in 2027.

If you or a client has a Roundup claim, this is the week to run the matrix number against the individual-case model. After June 4, the choice is made for you.
