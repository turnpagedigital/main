## Roundup MDL — Opt-Out Deadline June 4, Fairness Hearing July 9

*In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation* before Judge Vince Chhabria (N.D. Cal.) received preliminary approval of Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion class settlement on March 4, 2026, with the fairness hearing firmly calendared for July 9, 2026 (__[The New Lede](https://www.thenewlede.org/2026/03/bayer-wins-preliminary-court-approval-for-its-proposed-roundup-class-action-settlement/)__). The opt-out deadline of June 4 is now three days out and is the operative pivot for plaintiffs deciding between participation and individual prosecution; once the window closes, individual litigation rights extinguish for class members who do not opt out. The per-claimant ceiling is approximately $165,000 based on disease exposure and age, with distributions anticipated Q4 2026 if approved. Senator Cory Booker has filed an amicus brief at the Supreme Court supporting plaintiffs in the upcoming review of the Roundup litigation and has introduced the Pesticide Injury Accountability Act in the Senate, which would amend FIFRA to create a federal right of action for pesticide-injury claimants — a parallel legislative track that may interact with the settlement-approval timeline. With the opt-out deadline this week, plaintiffs with strong individual damage models above the $165,000 ceiling face their final election point.

## Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL 1720 — Final Approval Hearing June 17

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 1720, is now in its final pre-approval phase, sixteen days from hearing. The revised settlement announced November 10, 2025 — after Judge Margo K. Brodie rejected the original 2024 settlement on June 25, 2024 — is calendared for final-approval hearing on June 17, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. in the Eastern District of New York before Judge Brian M. Cogan, who took the case in September 2025 following Judge Brodie's recusal. The objection deadline closed April 29, 2026 with substantive objections preserved from the National Retail Federation, National Association of Convenience Stores, National Restaurant Association, Walmart, Hugo Boss, and other major merchant groups arguing that the headline 10-basis-point interchange reduction over five years and the 1.25 percent rate cap over eight years on standard consumer credit transactions are insufficient relative to estimated $13–15 billion-per-annum overcharges; Walmart and the national retail trade groups have also pressed Judge Cogan to let plaintiffs opt out of the injunctive-relief settlement (__[Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-defend-card-fee-settlement/818655/)__). The settlement covers approximately 12 million U.S. merchants and ends 21 years of litigation; the new terms also end the "honor all cards" rule and grant merchants the right to decline higher-cost cards and to surcharge. Merchant interested parties should expect Judge Cogan to take the matter under submission, with Mastercard's own securities disclosures projecting that any approval would land "most likely in late 2026 or early 2027." Distributions, if approved, will follow within 90–120 days of entry of final judgment (__[Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/visa-and-mastercard-agree-to-settlement-that-reduces-credit-card-fees-and-expands-merchant-control)__).

## Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — $218B Campaign

Keller Postman LLP's formal launch on May 11, 2026 of the largest mass-arbitration campaign ever attempted targets Google for alleged advertising overcharges and antitrust violations. The firm represents thousands of U.S. businesses — local enterprises through Fortune 500 advertisers — that purchased Google Search or Google Display Network advertising on or after August 2016, when the relevant TOS arbitration clause first applied. Potential claims are estimated at $218 billion or more, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law and potential recovery of 20–30 percent of total Google ad spend per claimant (__[MassArbitrationClaims.com](https://www.massarbitrationclaims.com/blog/google-mass-arbitration/)__) (__[PPC.Land](https://ppc.land/mass-arbitration-targets-google-over-alleged-billions-in-ad-overcharges/)__). The campaign sits on the 2024 federal antitrust rulings against Google in *FTC v. Google* and the DOJ ad-tech case, which arbitrators will likely consider as either admissions of liability or strong evidence of market power. The first AAA / JAMS filing wave is anticipated to open June 23, 2026 — twenty-two days out, the operative calendar milestone; coordinated mass arbitration against Google may accelerate settlement negotiations as Google seeks to avoid thousands of parallel arbitration rulings (__[AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/antitrust/for-google-advertisers-who-overpaid-the-monopoly-dont-hate-arbitrate/)__). The parallel Apple App Store developer track and the Meta advertiser-waiver track remain the next mass-arb dollar concentrations behind the Google campaign.

## Bartz v. Anthropic — Final-Approval Order Imminent

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), the $1.5 billion class settlement — the largest copyright class resolution in United States history — continues to await Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín's final-approval order following the May 14 fairness hearing, now eighteen days under submission. The 91.3 percent claims rate (440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed by approximately 120,000 rightsholders) forecloses any meaningful adequacy-of-notice objection, and the settlement administrator is scheduled to calculate per-work distributions by June 11. The $3,000-per-book settlement structure now serves as the operative comparator across the *OpenAI* MDL, *Concord/UMG v. Anthropic*, and the *Disney v. Midjourney* docket. Class counsel revised the fee request downward from 15 percent to 12.5 percent of the gross fund, yielding a petition of approximately $187.5 million plus $3 million in incurred expenses, an $18.22 million cost reserve, and $50,000 service awards to each of the three class representatives. Payouts will issue only after final approval and resolution of any appeals, with distributions likely commencing in late summer at the earliest.
