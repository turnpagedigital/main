## Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL 1720 — Final Approval Hearing June 17

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 1720, is now in its final pre-approval phase. The revised settlement announced November 10, 2025 — after Judge Margo K. Brodie rejected the original 2024 settlement on June 25, 2024 — is calendared for final-approval hearing on June 17, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York before Judge Brian M. Cogan (who took the case in September 2025 following Judge Brodie's recusal). The objection deadline closed April 29, 2026 with substantive objections preserved from the National Retail Federation, National Association of Convenience Stores, National Restaurant Association, Walmart, Hugo Boss, and other major merchant groups arguing that the headline 10-basis-point interchange reduction over five years and the 1.25 percent rate cap over eight years on standard consumer credit transactions are insufficient relative to estimated $13–15 billion-per-annum overcharges (__[Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-defend-card-fee-settlement/818655/)__). The settlement covers approximately 12 million U.S. merchants and is positioned to end 21 years of litigation. Merchant interested parties tracking the docket should expect Judge Cogan to rule on the record from the bench or take the matter under submission with a written order in the four-to-eight week window. Distributions, if approved, will follow within 90–120 days of entry of final judgment (__[Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/visa-and-mastercard-agree-to-settlement-that-reduces-credit-card-fees-and-expands-merchant-control)__).

## Bartz v. Anthropic — Final-Approval Order Pending

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), the $1.5 billion class settlement — the largest copyright class resolution in United States history — continues to await Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín's final-approval order following the May 14 fairness hearing. The 92.77 percent claims rate forecloses any meaningful adequacy-of-notice objection, and the 350 valid opt-outs covering 1,802 works cap the carve-out exposure. The $3,000-per-book settlement structure now serves as the operative comparator across the OpenAI MDL, *Concord/UMG v. Anthropic*, and the *Disney v. Midjourney* docket. Class counsel revised the fee request downward from 15 percent to 12.5 percent of the gross fund, yielding a petition of approximately $187.5 million plus $3 million in incurred expenses, an $18.22 million cost reserve, and $50,000 service awards to each of the three class representatives. Distribution preparations are well underway; the Authors Alliance has flagged that distribution communications and claims-intake systems should be ready for activation upon entry of the final-approval order. With the fairness hearing now twelve days behind, the order is within the customary 7–21 day post-fairness-hearing window.

## Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — $218B Campaign

Keller Postman LLP's formal launch on May 11, 2026 of the largest mass-arbitration campaign ever attempted targets Google for alleged advertising overcharges and antitrust violations. The firm represents thousands of U.S. businesses — local enterprises through Fortune 500 advertisers — that purchased Google Search or Google Display Network advertising on or after August 2016, when the relevant TOS arbitration clause first applied. Potential claims are estimated at $218 billion or more, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law and potential recovery of 20–30 percent of total Google ad spend per claimant (__[MassArbitrationClaims.com](https://www.massarbitrationclaims.com/blog/google-mass-arbitration/)__) (__[PPC.Land](https://ppc.land/mass-arbitration-targets-google-over-alleged-billions-in-ad-overcharges/)__). The campaign sits on the 2024 federal antitrust rulings against Google in *FTC v. Google* and the DOJ ad-tech case, which arbitrators will likely consider as either admissions of liability or strong evidence of market power. The first AAA / JAMS filing wave is anticipated to open the gates on June 23, 2026 — twenty-eight days out from today, the operative calendar milestone for the docket; coordinated mass arbitration against Google may accelerate settlement negotiations as Google seeks to avoid thousands of parallel arbitration rulings (__[AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/antitrust/for-google-advertisers-who-overpaid-the-monopoly-dont-hate-arbitrate/)__).
