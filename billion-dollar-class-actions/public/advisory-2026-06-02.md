## Roundup MDL — Opt-Out Deadline June 4 (Two Days), Fairness Hearing July 9

*In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation* (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria) has its opt-out deadline on June 4, 2026 — two days from today — and the July 9, 2026 fairness hearing. Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion class settlement received preliminary approval on March 4, 2026. The per-claimant ceiling is approximately $165,000 based on disease exposure and age, with distributions anticipated Q4 2026 if approved (__[Drugwatch — Opt-Out Explainer](https://www.drugwatch.com/news/2026/03/11/roundup-settlement-explained-who-is-eligible-and-how-the-opt-out-process-works/)__). The settlement creates a "futures" subclass that automatically covers any U.S. person exposed to Roundup or other glyphosate-based weed killers before February 17, 2026 — including children and individuals not yet diagnosed with non-Hodgkin lymphoma — meaning that class members who do not opt out by June 4 extinguish their future individual litigation rights even for claims not yet accrued. Senator Cory Booker's amicus brief at the Supreme Court supporting plaintiffs and his Pesticide Injury Accountability Act — which would amend FIFRA to create a federal right of action for pesticide-injury claimants — represents a parallel legislative track that may interact with the settlement-approval timeline and that creates a litigation-forum vehicle for the opt-out cohort even after FIFRA's current preemption framework. Plaintiffs with individual damage models above the approximately $165,000 ceiling face their final election point in two days (__[Lawsuit Information Center](https://www.lawsuit-information-center.com/roundup-lawsuit.html)__) (__[Drugwatch — Opt-Out](https://www.drugwatch.com/news/2026/05/29/roundup-settlement-opt-out-deadline-is-days-away-but-the-legal-fight-is-far-from-over/)__).

## Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL 1720 — Final Approval Hearing June 17

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Brian M. Cogan), is fifteen days from its June 17 final-approval hearing at 11:00 a.m. The revised settlement announced November 10, 2025 — following Judge Margo K. Brodie's June 25, 2024 rejection of the original settlement — covers approximately 12 million U.S. merchants and ends 21 years of litigation. The new terms include a 10-basis-point interchange reduction over five years, a 1.25 percent rate cap over eight years on standard consumer credit transactions, elimination of the "honor all cards" rule, and merchant rights to decline higher-cost cards and surcharge. Substantive objections preserved for argument from NRF, NACS, NRA, Walmart, and Hugo Boss center on the argument that the headline rate reductions are insufficient relative to $13–15 billion-per-annum overcharges (__[Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-defend-card-fee-settlement/818655/)__). The objection-deadline record closed April 29, 2026. Mastercard's securities disclosures continue to project that any final approval will land "most likely in late 2026 or early 2027," and distributions, if approved, would follow within 90–120 days of entry of final judgment. Merchant objectors should ensure appearance counsel is confirmed for the June 17 hearing; post-hearing, the matter goes under submission and distributions timeline depends on Judge Cogan's ruling cadence.
