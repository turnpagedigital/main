## Roundup MDL — Opt-Out Window Closed; Fairness Hearing July 9

*In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation* (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria) passed its acute deadline overnight. Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion class settlement received preliminary approval on March 4, 2026; the opt-out deadline closed at 11:59 p.m. on June 4. The per-claimant ceiling is approximately $165,000 based on disease exposure and age. The settlement creates a "futures" subclass covering U.S. persons exposed to Roundup or glyphosate-based herbicides before February 17, 2026 — including children and individuals not yet diagnosed with non-Hodgkin lymphoma — who forfeit future individual litigation rights now that the window has closed. Senator Cory Booker's Pesticide Injury Accountability Act, which would amend FIFRA to create a federal right of action for pesticide-injury claimants, is a parallel legislative track that may interact with the settlement-approval timeline and provides a post-opt-out forum vehicle for the individual-litigation cohort. Bayer has now reached settlement in approximately 100,000 Roundup lawsuits and paid approximately $11 billion in total, making the $7.25 billion class settlement the capstone of a more than decade-long global litigation campaign (__[Lawsuit Information Center](https://www.lawsuit-information-center.com/roundup-lawsuit.html)__). The matter now proceeds to the July 9 fairness hearing before Judge Chhabria; objections were due before the opt-out deadline and are preserved for argument. The opt-out cohort — plaintiffs with individual damage models above $165,000 who elected not to settle — will pursue individual cases in the federal MDL or parallel state proceedings, with Booker's Act as the potential alternative federal vehicle.

## Visa/Mastercard MDL 1720 — Final Approval Hearing June 17

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Brian M. Cogan), is twelve days from its June 17 final-approval hearing at 11:00 a.m. The revised settlement announced November 10, 2025 — following the prior court's June 25, 2024 rejection of the original $5.5 billion deal — covers approximately 12 million U.S. merchants and ends 21 years of litigation. The new terms include a 10-basis-point interchange reduction over five years, a 1.25 percent rate cap over eight years on standard consumer credit transactions, elimination of the "honor all cards" rule, and expanded merchant rights to decline higher-cost cards and surcharge. Substantive objections preserved for argument from the National Retail Federation, National Association of Convenience Stores, National Restaurant Association, Walmart, and Hugo Boss center on the argument that the headline rate reductions are insufficient relative to $13–15 billion-per-annum estimated overcharges (__[Payments Dive — Visa/Mastercard](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/visa-mastercard-defend-card-fee-settlement/818655/)__) (__[Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/visa-and-mastercard-agree-to-settlement-that-reduces-credit-card-fees-and-expands-merchant-control)__). On the separate damages-class track, the court approved a plan on October 30, 2025 to issue initial partial payments to certain claimants, with qualified merchants that filed valid claims expecting payouts beginning February 2026. Mastercard's public securities disclosures project that final approval of the injunctive-relief settlement will land "most likely in late 2026 or early 2027," with distributions following within 90–120 days of final judgment. Merchant objectors should ensure appearance counsel is confirmed for the June 17 hearing; post-hearing, the matter goes under submission.

## Keller Postman Google Mass Arbitration — $218B Campaign, June 23 First Wave

Keller Postman LLP's Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign — launched May 11, 2026 — is eighteen days from its June 23 first-AAA-wave milestone. The campaign targets Google for alleged advertising overcharges and antitrust violations under the federal antitrust rulings in *FTC v. Google* and the DOJ ad-tech case, representing U.S. businesses that purchased Google Search or Google Display Network advertising on or after August 2016. The $218 billion-or-more in potential claims rests on treble-damages availability under 15 U.S.C. § 15, with projected individual recovery of 20–30 percent of total ad spend during the relevant period (__[Bloomberg — Google Mass Arb](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-13/google-faces-mass-arbitration-by-advertisers-seeking-billions)__) (__[MassArbitrationClaims.com — Google](https://www.massarbitrationclaims.com/blog/google-mass-arbitration/)__). The June 23 first-wave date is the threshold at which AAA's mass-arbitration-rules fee schedule triggers at scale, creating per-claim administrative-fee exposure for Google across thousands of simultaneous individual arbitrations. The *AdExchanger* framing captures the campaign's settlement-calculus thesis: the administrative-fee exposure is the settlement-forcing mechanism independent of merits (__[AdExchanger — Google](https://www.adexchanger.com/antitrust/for-google-advertisers-who-overpaid-the-monopoly-dont-hate-arbitrate/)__). Parallel Apple App Store developer and Meta advertiser-waiver mass-arb tracks are the next dollar concentrations; both are in pre-filing intake phase. Google advertisers evaluating participation should model net-recovery economics — contingency fee plus AAA filing cost against 20–30 percent-of-total-spend potential recovery — before the June 23 wave.
