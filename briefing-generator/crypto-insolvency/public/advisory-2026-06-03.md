### BlockFills Bankruptcy — Keyrock Acquisition & Sale Process

BlockFills, the Chicago-based institutional digital-asset prime-brokerage and electronic market-making firm, filed for Chapter 11 protection in the **United States Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Illinois** (the natural venue given its Illinois headquarters) sometime in Q1–Q2 2026, with CoinDesk reporting Keyrock's intent to acquire the business out of that proceeding (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitgFBVV95cUxNa3Q1NDFzZUcyWk92VGt2d2NhVWQwREVaam1BQ3kxdS1VUG9GMUJ1VTR1V0dKZjUza0R5Y2hDYUFoTEhIZHhfWkI1ZG91VzhNSlY3LW1mVk9qX0Rrczk5NWVucTVWSkdYMEFmYmYtZmlWYm9uUGFiSHctbUlMcW1KMjdoV2ZTVW1sRkQ3ckZMRVl0a2RGdnMzZmQyZjc4dXVIdDZjQ1VueE8tNHkwNS1aZGNUa0NZUQ)__). The structure of any 363 sale — conducted pursuant to **11 U.S.C. § 363(b) and (f)** — would allow Keyrock to acquire BlockFills' technology stack, client relationships, and regulatory licenses free and clear of liens, claims, encumbrances, and interests, provided the Court approves the sale and adequate notice is given to creditors and counterparties to executory contracts under **Bankruptcy Rule 2002**.

Keyrock, headquartered in Brussels with licensed operations across multiple EU member-state jurisdictions and a growing U.S. institutional footprint, has been building out prime-brokerage and liquidity-provision capabilities. A 363 purchase of BlockFills would represent a straightforward acqui-hire and asset-consolidation play: Keyrock would absorb BlockFills' institutional counterparty network — which includes exchanges, OTC desks, and fund clients — alongside its proprietary market-making infrastructure. Counsel identities, bid-procedures order details, sale-hearing date, stalking-horse bid amount, and breakup fee quantum were not available in the headline-only CoinDesk report and **require verification** before claim-holders or counterparties act in reliance on any specific procedural timeline (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitgFBVV95cUxNa3Q1NDFzZUcyWk92VGt2d2NhVWQwREVaam1BQ3kxdS1VUG9GMUJ1VTR1V0dKZjUza0R5Y2hDYUFoTEhIZHhfWkI1ZG91VzhNSlY3LW1mVk9qX0Rrczk5NWVucTVWSkdYMEFmYmYtZmlWYm9uUGFiSHctbUlMcW1KMjdoV2ZTVW1sRkQ3ckZMRVl0a2RGdnMzZmQyZjc4dXVIdDZjQ1VueE8tNHkwNS1aZGNUa0NZUQ)__). Parties holding executory contracts or unexpired leases with BlockFills — particularly ISDA master agreements, prime-brokerage agreements, and API licensing agreements — face assumption-or-rejection elections under **11 U.S.C. § 365**, and any counterparty that has not already filed a proof of claim or filed protective cure-cost objections should treat the Keyrock acquisition news as a trigger to verify bar dates in the Northern District of Illinois docket.

### FTX Debtors — Distribution Phase & Supplemental Recovery Fund

The **FTX Trading Ltd.** estates (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Hon. John T. Dorsey, J.) remain the single largest active crypto-insolvency proceeding by asset value. As of early June 2026, the FTX Plan Administrator — operating under the confirmed Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan that went effective in early 2025 — has completed the **first distribution tranche** covering convenience class and customer creditors holding allowed claims under the initial distribution threshold (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__; __[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The confirmed plan's waterfall targets **100% recovery (in USD) at petition-date pricing** for customer creditors, a construct that — while nominally full recovery — has drawn ongoing creditor criticism given Bitcoin's appreciation from sub-$20,000 at the November 2022 petition date to trading ranges well above $90,000 in mid-2026, meaning the USD-denominated 100% figure captures only a fraction of the in-kind value claimants would have held.

The **FTX Recovery Trust** is simultaneously pursuing adversary proceedings against dozens of institutional defendants, including former insiders, venture counterparties, and exchange-account recipients of alleged preferential transfers under **11 U.S.C. § 547** and fraudulent-transfer claims under **11 U.S.C. §§ 548 and 550** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The Debtors' estate has recovered or agreed to recover in excess of **$14 billion** since the petition date — a figure that has been cited in court filings — with incremental recoveries from cryptocurrency liquidations, venture-stake disposals, and litigation proceeds. The second distribution tranche, expected to be processed through the designated claims agent (Kroll Restructuring Administration LLC), is anticipated in Q3 2026 for allowed claims above the convenience-class threshold (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Creditors who have not completed KYC/AML onboarding through the FTX claims portal should treat June 2026 as a hard deadline: unclaimed distributions will be subject to redistribution mechanics under the plan's unclaimed-property protocol.

### Celsius Network — Plan Administrator Fee Dispute & Remaining Distributions

The **Celsius Network LLC** estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964-MG, Hon. Martin Glenn, C.J.) continues to generate contested-matter litigation even post-confirmation. The **Fahrenheit LLC** consortium — which serves as the Plan Administrator and operator of the reorganized entity, **Ionic Digital Inc.** — has faced creditor objections to its quarterly fee applications, with the Unsecured Creditors' Committee's successor oversight body and individual creditors challenging the reasonableness of fees under the plan's governance documents and **11 U.S.C. § 503(b)** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Judge Glenn, Chief Judge of the Southern District of New York Bankruptcy Court (One Bowling Green, Courtroom 523), has set a contested hearing schedule on the fee disputes that runs through the summer of 2026.

Meanwhile, distributions from the **Celsius Litigation Fund** — a segregated trust funded with proceeds from litigation recoveries — remain pending resolution of claims filed by retail depositors who elected into the litigation track rather than the distribution track at the plan-election stage. The litigation fund is principally pursuing claims against former CEO **Alex Mashinsky** (who faces parallel criminal indictment before Judge John Koeltl in the S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-cr-00347) and related entities (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__; __[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__). Any criminal restitution order flowing from the Mashinsky criminal proceedings would feed into civil recovery mechanics, but the coordination between the **DOJ Victim Compensation Framework** and the Celsius plan's litigation-fund waterfall has not been publicly resolved. Ionic Digital's staking and mining operations — which are the primary ongoing value-generating assets — reported operational metrics in Q1 2026 that came in below plan projections, creating tension between equity-recovery expectations and creditor recovery timelines (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__).

### Genesis Global — Final Distributions & Wind-Down Trust

The **Genesis Global Holdco LLC** estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, Hon. Sean H. Lane, J.) reached a significant milestone in Q1 2026 when the **Genesis Wind-Down Trustee** — Moelis & Company serving as financial advisor alongside **Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP** as restructuring counsel — commenced distributions to **Digital Currency Group (DCG)** and third-party institutional creditors under the confirmed plan (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The settlement with **Gemini Trust Company** — which resolved the Gemini Earn program creditors' claims for approximately **$1.1 billion** in aggregate distributions — was effectuated through the plan's Gemini distribution protocol, with Gemini acting as passthrough agent for approximately **232,000** retail Earn customers (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Outstanding litigation in the Genesis estate includes adversary proceedings against **Three Arrows Capital (3AC)** liquidators — managed before Justice Sir Zane Derive in the **British Virgin Islands Commercial Court** with ancillary recognition proceedings in the S.D.N.Y. under **11 U.S.C. § 1521** (Chapter 15) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The 3AC estate has total liabilities estimated in excess of **$3.5 billion**; Genesis holds one of the largest creditor positions in that proceeding. Recovery from 3AC will depend on BVI liquidators' success in pursuing fraudulent-transfer and preference claims against 3AC's founders — **Su Zhu** and **Kyle Davies** — both of whom have been subject to asset-freeze orders in multiple jurisdictions. Any recovery from the 3AC estate would flow back to Genesis creditors as a supplemental distribution; current recovery projections treat that stream as speculative (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### Voyager Digital — Adversary Proceedings & Alameda/FTX Preference Recovery

The **Voyager Digital LLC** estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10943-MEW, Hon. Michael E. Wiles, J.) has, through its **Voyager Digital Litigation Trust**, sustained adversary proceedings against **Alameda Research LLC** and affiliated FTX entities for preferential transfers received in the 90-day preference window preceding both the Voyager petition date (July 5, 2022) and the FTX petition date (November 11, 2022) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). These claims are being adjudicated in a coordinated track between Judge Wiles in S.D.N.Y. and Judge Dorsey in D. Del., with inter-estate claims subject to the FTX plan's claims-resolution protocol. The quantum of the Voyager-Alameda preferential-transfer exposure has been estimated in court filings at several hundred million dollars, though net recovery after FTX estate distribution haircuts is projected well below face value given the volume of competing claims on the FTX estate.

Separate from the Alameda adversary, the Voyager Litigation Trust is pursuing claims against **Binance.US** arising from Binance's abandoned acquisition attempt in early 2023 following the **CFIUS national-security review** that caused Binance to terminate the asset-purchase agreement (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Voyager has asserted breach-of-contract and tortious-interference claims; Binance has disputed liability. Judge Wiles has set a discovery schedule running through Q3 2026, with a potential trial date in early 2027. Recovery from the Binance litigation, if successful, would represent an incremental distribution to Voyager creditors beyond the amounts already distributed under the confirmed plan.

### BlockFi — Liquidating Trust Preference Litigation

The **BlockFi Inc.** estate (Bankr. D.N.J., Case No. 22-19361-MBK, Hon. Michael B. Kaplan, J.) has advanced into the preference-recovery phase of its administration. The **BlockFi Litigation Trust**, administered with **M3 Advisory Partners** as financial advisor, has filed over two dozen adversary proceedings in the D.N.J. targeting institutional counterparties that received transfers — including withdrawals, loan repayments, and collateral liquidations — in the 90-day period prior to BlockFi's November 28, 2022 petition date (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Several of those adversary proceedings have been settled on confidential terms; others remain in active discovery before Magistrate Judge designation. Judge Kaplan has also overseen contested proceedings relating to the **Three Arrows Capital** and **FTX/Alameda** inter-estate claims — BlockFi holds substantial claims against both estates, with the FTX claim having been allowed in the D. Del. claims-resolution process and expected to generate a distribution in the second tranche (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Recovery for BlockFi general unsecured creditors — who are in the class below senior secured noteholders and client-asset claimants — remains highly diluted; current projections from court-filed disclosure documents suggest sub-20% recovery on general unsecured claims, though incremental preference and inter-estate recoveries may improve that figure modestly.
