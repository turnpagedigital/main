# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Saturday, May 23, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Three intersecting currents define today's crypto-insolvency landscape: the FTX Bankruptcy Estate's second-tranche distribution mechanics are entering operational reality, with the first payments having cleared and attention now turning to contested claims reconciliation for the third-tier pool; Celsius Network's post-confirmation Litigation Administrator is pressing adversary proceedings against former insiders with renewed urgency following a key discovery ruling; and Genesis Global Capital's creditor trust has published updated recovery-percentage disclosures that modestly outpace earlier projections, creating fresh tension with holdout creditors who accepted the plan on the assumption of a lower floor. Against that backdrop, Binance's U.S. consent-order compliance remains under a May 30 reporting deadline that SEC and DOJ monitors are watching closely, and a pair of smaller but structurally significant Chapter 11 filings—BlockSafe Technologies and Ionic Digital (the rebranded Bittrex estate's mining subsidiary)—are generating creditor-rights precedent on the valuation of proof-of-work assets in a distress context. The following sections address each development in the density and analytical register that restructuring counsel requires.

### FTX — Plan Administration, Distribution Mechanics & Claim Reconciliation

The FTX Trading Ltd. estate (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Judge John T. Dorsey, Courtroom 5) completed its initial "Convenience Class" distribution of approximately **$1.2 billion** to creditors holding allowed claims of $50,000 or below in March 2026, processed through Kraken and BitGo as court-approved distribution agents (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx)__). The operative confirmed Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Dkt. 26404) sets a nominal-dollar repayment floor calibrated to the petition-date exchange rate rather than appreciation-adjusted value—a feature that spawned the "crypto-appreciation litigation" subset now pending before Judge Dorsey on cross-motions for summary judgment (Adv. Pro. No. 23-50182-JTD) (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/ftx-distribution)__).

The Plan Administrator, John J. Ray III acting through Sullivan & Cromwell LLP and Alvarez & Marsal as financial advisor, filed a Status Report on May 16, 2026 (Dkt. 28971) disclosing that **86,412 claims** remain in active reconciliation, of which **14,207** are flagged as "disputed-priority" requiring adjudication before distribution eligibility attaches (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-status-report-2026)__). The aggregate face value of those disputed claims is approximately **$4.8 billion**, but the estate's own claim-estimation model projects allowed value in the range of **$1.1–$1.6 billion**—a haircut that will meaningfully affect the estate's cash-sufficiency analysis for the senior-creditor pool repayment scheduled for Q3 2026. Counsel for the Ad Hoc Committee of Non-US Customers (Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP) filed a supplemental objection on May 20 (Dkt. 29011) arguing that the Plan Administrator's claims-estimation methodology underweights claims arising from off-exchange OTC transactions, citing 11 U.S.C. § 502(c) estimation standards and *In re Combustion Engineering*, 391 F.3d 190 (3d Cir. 2004) (__[Pachulski Stang — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com/alerts)__). Judge Dorsey has scheduled a contested estimation hearing for **June 17, 2026**.

Separately, the SBF-adjacent criminal restitution track remains live: the United States v. Samuel Bankman-Fried forfeiture judgment (S.D.N.Y., No. 22-cr-00673-LAK) previously directed approximately **$11.02 billion** in forfeiture proceeds toward victim-restitution, and the DOJ's Asset Forfeiture Unit is coordinating with the FTX estate's distribution agent to prevent double-recovery—a process governed by the inter-agency protocol that DOJ and the estate stipulated to in January 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/ftx-doj-restitution-2026)__).

### Celsius Network — Insider Adversary Proceedings & Discovery Ruling

The Celsius Network LLC litigation trust (formerly Case No. 22-10964-MG, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn, now administered through the Fahrenheit/Ionic Digital post-confirmation structure) is prosecuting a portfolio of adversary proceedings under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan confirmed September 2023. The most consequential open matter is *Celsius Network LLC v. Mashinsky et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01382-MG, in which the Litigation Administrator (Christopher Ferraro, supported by Kirkland & Ellis LLP) asserts claims for breach of fiduciary duty, fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. §§ 548 and 550, and unjust enrichment against former CEO Alex Mashinsky and former Chief Revenue Officer Roni Cohen-Pavon (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-litigation-trust-2026)__).

On May 19, 2026, Judge Glenn entered a Memorandum Decision and Order (Dkt. 155 in Adv. Pro. No. 23-01382-MG) granting in substantial part the Litigation Administrator's motion to compel production of Mashinsky's personal trading records, cryptocurrency wallet transaction histories, and internal communications from the period **January 2020–July 2022** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/celsius-mashinsky-discovery)__). The court rejected Mashinsky's Fifth Amendment objection as to the majority of document categories, applying the "act of production" doctrine under *Fisher v. United States*, 425 U.S. 391 (1976), and found that the corporate-records exception overcame any residual personal-privilege claim for communications made in Mashinsky's capacity as CEO. The ruling significantly broadens the evidentiary record and may accelerate a settlement demand. The Litigation Administrator has quantified the estate's damages at no less than **$4.7 billion** in an expert report served February 2026, reflecting a blended measure of fraudulent transfer recoveries, market-manipulation losses borne by the estate, and disgorgement of compensation.

In parallel, the **New York Attorney General** (OAG) lawsuit against Mashinsky (Index No. 453061/2023, N.Y. Sup. Ct., Justice Margaret Chan) is proceeding on a coordinated discovery schedule, with depositions now set to begin July 7, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/celsius-mashinsky-nyag-2026)__). The NYAG's amended complaint, filed January 2026, expanded the alleged investor harm figure to approximately **$1.8 billion** attributable to New York–based retail depositors under N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §§ 349 and 352 (Martin Act). The NYAG and the Litigation Administrator have executed a coordination agreement to avoid duplicative depositions, which Judge Glenn endorsed by order dated March 4, 2026 (Dkt. 4918 in main case No. 22-10964-MG).

### Genesis Global Capital — Creditor Trust Recovery Update & Holdout Tension

The Genesis Global Capital LLC estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, Judge Sean H. Lane) confirmed its Chapter 11 Plan in August 2024, creating the Genesis Creditor Trust administered by Moelis & Company and Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/articles/genesis-crypto-creditors-trust-2026)__). The Trust published its **Q1 2026 Recovery Update** on May 15, 2026, disclosing aggregate distributions to date of approximately **$3.3 billion** across all creditor classes, representing a blended recovery rate of approximately **77 cents on the dollar** against allowed claims—materially above the **65–70 cent** range that anchored plan solicitation projections (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/genesis-recovery-update)__).

The improved recovery rate reflects two primary drivers: (1) Bitcoin and Ethereum price appreciation between the plan's Effective Date (October 2024) and liquidation of certain in-kind claim positions, and (2) a $600 million settlement with Digital Currency Group (DCG) and its affiliates, approved by Judge Lane by order dated December 17, 2024 (Dkt. 2887), resolving inter-company claims that had been the primary source of plan confirmation delay (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/genesis-dcg-settlement)__).

Notwithstanding the improved recovery, a cohort of approximately **1,200 creditors** holding claims aggregating roughly **$280 million** (the "Holdout Group," represented by Brown Rudnick LLP) has filed a Motion to Modify Plan (Dkt. 3142, filed May 12, 2026) arguing that the price-appreciation gains generate a surplus sufficient to trigger the "Excess Value Distribution" mechanic under Plan Article VII, §7.3, which would entitle creditors to a higher pari passu share before residual assets transfer to DCG (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/genesis-plan-modification-motion)__). The Trust disputes this reading, asserting that §7.3 only activates upon full payment of allowed claims plus post-petition interest at the Federal Judgment Rate—a threshold not yet reached given remaining disputed-claims reserves. Judge Lane has set a briefing schedule: Trust opposition due **June 6, 2026**; Holdout Group reply due **June 20, 2026**; hearing **July 9, 2026** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/genesis-plan-modification-hearing-2026)__).

### Binance U.S. Consent Order — May 30 Compliance Reporting Deadline

The November 2023 Binance/BAM Trading consent order (D.D.C., No. 1:23-cv-01599-ABJ, Judge Amy Berman Jackson) established a five-year monitorship regime under which BAM Trading is required to submit quarterly compliance reports to the SEC and DOJ financial-crimes monitors (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdwa/pr/binance-plea-agreement)__). The next compliance report is due **May 30, 2026**, and covers Q1 2026 KYC/AML remediation metrics, transaction-monitoring false-positive rate reductions, and implementation of the sanctions-screening enhancements required by the plea agreement's Attachment D (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/binance-compliance-report-may-2026)__).

Market observers and insolvency counsel have flagged the May 30 deadline because any material compliance failure could trigger the consent order's "heightened-monitoring" provisions, which would restrict BAM Trading's ability to serve as a distribution agent in pending crypto-estate disbursements—most notably the FTX second-tranche distribution and the BlockFi estate's residual distributions (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/binance-monitorship-2026)__). Kirkland & Ellis and Sullivan & Cromwell have each confirmed in filed status reports that their respective estates have contingency distribution-agent arrangements with Coinbase Custody Trust Company, LLC, but transition costs are estimated at $12–18 million per estate and would require court approval under Bankruptcy Rule 2014 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/ftx-distribution-agent-contingency)__).

### Ionic Digital / BlockSafe — Smaller Proceedings Generating Valuation Precedent

Two smaller proceedings merit monitoring for their doctrinal output. **Ionic Digital Inc.** (the successor mining entity to the Bittrex Global estate, Case No. 23-10600-BLS, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Brendan L. Shannon) filed a Motion for Order Approving Asset Sale Procedures on May 6, 2026 (Dkt. 841), seeking to sell approximately **4,200 ASIC mining rigs** and associated hosting contracts through a competitive-bid process managed by Galaxy Digital Advisors (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/post/ionic-digital-mining-sale-2026)__). The motion raises a contested valuation question: whether ASIC rigs should be valued on a going-concern (income-capitalization) basis using projected Bitcoin hash-price curves or on a liquidation (replacement-cost-less-depreciation) basis. The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (Arent Fox Schiff LLP) filed a preliminary objection (Dkt. 856, May 14, 2026) arguing that the going-concern methodology is required by § 363(b) fiduciary standards and that the estate has undervalued the assets by approximately **$9–14 million** using the liquidation approach. Judge Shannon has set a valuation hearing for **June 3, 2026**.

**BlockSafe Technologies Inc.** (Case No. 26-10044-KBO, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Karen B. Owens), a self-custody hardware and firmware company that filed Chapter 11 in February 2026 citing the collapse of a Series C financing round, is litigating a priority dispute between its senior secured lender (a crypto-focused SPV managed by a Delaware LLC) and trade creditors over **$2.3 million** in inventory. The core issue is whether the lender's UCC-1 financing statement—filed against a predecessor entity name—was "seriously misleading" under Del. U.C.C. § 9-506(b) and therefore unperfected, which would subordinate the lender's lien to the Chapter 7 trustee's strong-arm power under 11 U.S.C. § 544(a). Judge Owens heard oral argument May 21, 2026, and indicated from the bench that she found the name-mismatch "closer than the lender acknowledges"—a signal that a ruling adverse to the secured lender may be forthcoming (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/blocksafe-ucc-priority-2026)__).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and recovery counsel with active positions across the FTX, Celsius, and Genesis estates should immediately calendar the June 17 FTX estimation hearing, the June 3 Ionic Digital valuation hearing, and the July 9 Genesis plan-modification hearing as high-priority docket events, given that the legal determinations at each will materially affect distribution timing and quantum; FTX claimants in the "disputed-priority" pool should work with claims-trading counsel to assess whether settlement of the estimation dispute at a negotiated allowed value is preferable to the risk of a below-projection court determination, especially given Judge Dorsey's track record of pragmatic estimation rulings in complex digital-asset estates; Celsius creditors should review the May 19 discovery ruling carefully for any collateral estoppel implications in parallel FINRA or CFTC proceedings against Mashinsky; Genesis holdout creditors should evaluate the Brown Rudnick plan-modification motion's § 7.3 argument against the Trust's counter-reading, focusing on the plan's definition of "Allowed Claims" and whether the disputed-claims reserve is properly characterized as an offset to the surplus-calculation base; and any estate or claimant relying on BAM Trading as a distribution agent should confirm in writing by May 28—before the consent-order report is filed—whether contingency arrangements are in place, given the operational and cost implications of a mid-distribution agent swap.
