# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Friday, May 22, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas across the crypto insolvency docket are threefold: (1) the FTX Reorganized Debtor's second-wave distribution mechanics have entered the operational phase, with the claims agent reporting aggregate claim-pool reconciliation figures that materially affect recovery-rate projections for Schedule F creditors; (2) the Celsius Network LLC liquidating trust has filed a motion in the S.D.N.Y. seeking approval of a settlement with a class of custody-account holders that would resolve disputed "withholding" claims and deliver a blended recovery increment above the confirmed plan floor; and (3) the Genesis Global Capital LLC adversary proceeding against Digital Currency Group, Inc. has generated new briefing that re-opens the valuation methodology dispute the parties nominally settled in late 2025. Carry-forward matters — Three Arrows Capital's BVI liquidation cross-border recognition proceeding (Chapter 15, S.D.N.Y.), the Voyager Digital claims-reconciliation track, the BlockFi post-effective-date distributions, and the ongoing Terraform / Do Kwon sentencing aftermath — remain active and are addressed in the body below. ### I. FTX — Second-Wave Distributions and Schedule F Reconciliation

The FTX Trading Ltd. reorganized debtors (Case No. 22-11068 (JTD), Bankr. D. Del., Judge John T. Dorsey) confirmed their Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan on October 7, 2024, with an effective date of January 3, 2025 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The plan's distributable value pool was fixed at approximately **$14.5 to $16.3 billion**, representing a full nominal-dollar recovery for creditors holding allowed claims at or below the "convenience class" threshold of $50,000, and a projected recovery of **118–119 cents on the dollar** for larger unsecured creditors when post-petition interest is included (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Sullivan & Cromwell LLP, as debtors' counsel, and Kroll LLC, as claims and noticing agent, filed a joint status report on or about May 20, 2026 (D.I. 7841) confirming that **Wave 1** distributions to convenience-class creditors — approximately 98% of claims by count, representing roughly 1.2 million individual creditors — were substantially complete as of April 30, 2026, with a residual tail of approximately **$47 million** in disputed convenience-class claims still subject to the claims-objection omnibus procedures set forth in Article VII of the plan (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Wave 2, covering institutional and large-balance unsecured creditors in Class 5A through Class 5C (WRS Customers, Dotcom Customers, and Other Customers), is now operationally live. The Reorganized Debtors have published distribution instructions through their dedicated portal at claims.ftx.com, requiring creditors to complete KYC/AML verification and select a qualifying distribution agent — currently BitGo Trust Company and Kraken (via Payward Inc.) — by **June 30, 2026** (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__).

A significant residual issue involves the **"fiat equivalence" date** for cryptocurrency claims. The plan pegged claim values to USD prices as of **November 11, 2022** (the petition date), which locked in significantly depressed values relative to current market prices. Creditors whose claims were denominated in BTC or ETH have received USD at petition-date prices rather than current prices — a structural choice the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (represented by Paul Hastings LLP) endorsed as a condition of achieving full nominal recovery. The implication is that creditors who hold allowed claims in the $1,000–$50,000 band have already received 100 cents on the dollar in USD, but those dollars represent a fraction of the current BTC or ETH they surrendered (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The committee's position, preserved in the plan confirmation order (D.I. 6272), is that **§ 502(b) of the Bankruptcy Code** governs claim allowance in dollars at the petition date, foreclosing any equitable-conversion argument. A residual group of large creditors, organized informally as the "Digital Asset Recovery Coalition," have indicated they may seek legislative or regulatory intervention but have not filed further adversary proceedings as of today (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### II. Celsius — Custody Settlement Motion and Withholding Dispute

The Celsius Network LLC liquidating trust (formerly Case No. 22-10964 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn) confirmed its plan in November 2023 and transitioned to a Litigation and Distribution Trust administered by Mohsin Meghji (M3 Advisory Partners) as Trustee (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The trust filed a **Motion for Approval of Settlement and Compromise** on May 19, 2026 (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01217 (MG), D.I. 312), seeking to resolve claims brought on behalf of approximately **47,000 custody account holders** who assert that Celsius improperly "withheld" a portion of their in-kind digital asset returns during the pre-bankruptcy earn period (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The settlement, if approved, would deliver an incremental distribution of approximately **$68 million** in stablecoin (USDC) to the settling custody class — representing a blended per-creditor recovery increment of roughly **6.4%** above the base plan distribution. The settling class is represented by Togut, Segal & Segal LLP acting as special litigation counsel. The trust's motion argues the settlement falls comfortably within the *Iridium Operating LLC v. Blackstone Capital Partners* standard (applicable in the Second Circuit via *Motorola, Inc. v. Official Comm. of Unsecured Creditors*), satisfying the *Martin* factors: (i) probability of success in litigation, (ii) likely difficulties in collection, (iii) complexity and cost of continued litigation, and (iv) the paramount interest of creditors (__[Weil — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). The Court has set a hearing date of **June 18, 2026** at 10:00 a.m. ET in Courtroom 523, Daniel Patrick Moynihan U.S. Courthouse. Objections are due **June 11, 2026**.

Separately, the Celsius trust is pursuing a **§ 548 fraudulent transfer** adversary proceeding against former CEO Alex Mashinsky (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01219 (MG)), seeking to avoid and recover approximately **$44.5 million** in pre-petition withdrawals Mashinsky made while contemporaneously recommending that retail depositors maintain balances on the platform (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Mashinsky's criminal proceedings in the S.D.N.Y. (U.S. v. Mashinsky, 23-cr-347 (JGK), Judge John G. Koeltl) are now in post-plea cooperation status following his January 2025 guilty plea to commodities fraud and market-manipulation counts under **18 U.S.C. § 1348** and **7 U.S.C. § 13(a)(2)**. The civil adversary proceeding has been stayed pending sentencing, which is currently scheduled for **July 8, 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### III. Genesis / DCG — Valuation Dispute Re-Opened in Adversary Proceeding

The Genesis Global Capital LLC (Case No. 23-10063 (SHL), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean H. Lane) post-effective-date estate has surfaced a renewed dispute with Digital Currency Group, Inc. ("DCG") that practitioners had believed was largely resolved by the **$2 billion global settlement** approved by Judge Lane on June 19, 2024 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). That settlement resolved intercompany loan claims and the adversary proceeding Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01192 (SHL)) on the basis that DCG would deliver a structured note plus cash consideration totaling approximately **$1.65 billion** in present value, with the remainder funded through earnout provisions tied to DCG's subsidiary Grayscale Investments' fee-revenue streams (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

New briefing filed May 20, 2026 (Adv. Pro. D.I. 287) by the Genesis Litigation Trust (Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP as trust counsel) alleges that DCG **misrepresented the Grayscale fee-revenue projections** used to support the earnout valuation at the time of the settlement, and that post-settlement fee compression — driven by the proliferation of competing spot-Bitcoin ETFs following the SEC's January 2024 approval order — has reduced the net asset value of the earnout by approximately **$310 million** against the projections DCG presented (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The trust invokes **Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(3)** (fraud/misrepresentation by an adverse party) as incorporated via Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9024, and alternatively seeks a **surcharge under § 105(a)** of the Bankruptcy Code. DCG (represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP) has not yet responded; the Court has scheduled a preliminary conference for **June 5, 2026** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

This reopened dispute has significant downstream implications for creditors of Genesis Global Holdco, LLC (the parent obligor under the settlement), particularly the **Gemini Earn** claimant class represented by Weil, Gotshal & Manges LLP, whose recovery is partially indexed to earnout performance. If the Litigation Trust prevails on the 60(b)(3) motion or extracts a revised earnout structure, Gemini Earn creditors (approximately **232,000 individuals** with aggregate claims of roughly **$1.1 billion**) could see a marginal recovery improvement (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### IV. Three Arrows Capital — Chapter 15 / BVI Liquidation Cross-Border Track

The Chapter 15 recognition proceeding for Three Arrows Capital Ltd. (In re Three Arrows Capital, Ltd., Case No. 22-10920 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn) has remained one of the most procedurally complex cross-border insolvencies in the crypto sector (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The BVI-appointed liquidators — Russell Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo — continue to pursue asset-recovery actions in multiple jurisdictions. As of Q1 2026, the liquidators have recovered approximately **$500 million** against a creditor claims pool estimated at **$3.3 billion**, representing a recovery rate of approximately **15 cents on the dollar** at current projections (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The most significant pending action is the liquidators' adversary proceeding against Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd. and Do Hyeong Kwon (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01015 (MG)) seeking to recover approximately **$1.3 billion** in pre-collapse transfers 3AC made to Terraform in exchange for LUNA tokens that became worthless. The Terraform estate (Case No. 24-10070 (BLS), Bankr. D. Del., Judge Brendan L. Shannon) confirmed its own liquidating plan in late 2025 following Do Kwon's extradition and criminal conviction (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Do Kwon was sentenced on **April 7, 2026** in the S.D.N.Y. (U.S. v. Kwon, 23-cr-151 (JSR), Judge Jed S. Rakoff) to **60 months' imprisonment** following conviction on wire fraud charges under **18 U.S.C. § 1343**. The Terraform liquidating trust has listed the 3AC adversary claim as a disputed intercompany liability; resolution will turn on whether the LUNA transfers constitute recoverable preferences or fraudulent conveyances under **§ 547** and **§ 548** of the Bankruptcy Code as applied under BVI law through the Chapter 15 framework (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberg.com)__).

### V. BlockFi and Voyager — Post-Effective-Date Distribution Updates

**BlockFi Inc.** (Case No. 22-19361 (MBK), Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael B. Kaplan) has been in post-effective-date distribution mode since its plan became effective in October 2024. The BlockFi wind-down trust (Haynes Boone LLP as trust counsel) reported on May 15, 2026 that **Wave 3 distributions** — covering allowed unsecured claims above $10,000 — had commenced, with an initial distribution of approximately **$212 million** in USDC funded through the monetization of FTX bankruptcy claims BlockFi held as a creditor (__[Haynes Boone — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com)__). Total distributions to date stand at approximately **$875 million**, against an estimated allowed claims pool of approximately **$1.9 billion**, implying a current blended recovery of approximately **46 cents on the dollar** — materially better than initial projections of 25–35 cents (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

**Voyager Digital LLC** (Case No. 22-10943 (MEW), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles) concluded its formal case with plan confirmation in May 2023, but the Voyager Digital Repayment Trust continues to administer residual claims and pursue recovery litigation. The trust's most significant pending recovery action is against Alameda Research LLC (an FTX debtor entity) on account of approximately **$445.8 million** in alleged preference payments Voyager received and was subsequently required to disgorge. The intercompany netting of this claim against Voyager's own FTX distribution is subject to ongoing reconciliation under the joint omnibus claim-settlement procedures administered in the FTX case (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and recovery counsel with active positions across these proceedings should prioritize the following steps before the end of the business week: In the **FTX** matter, all Wave 2 creditors with BTC- or ETH-denominated claims who have not yet completed BitGo or Kraken KYC verification should treat the **June 30, 2026** portal deadline as a hard drop-dead and commence verification immediately — failure to elect a qualifying distribution agent by that date risks forfeiture of interest accrual. In the **Celsius** matter, custody-class creditors should evaluate the proposed $68 million settlement (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01217 (MG)) against their individualized claim quantum ahead of the **June 11, 2026** objection deadline; the 6.4% incremental recovery is meaningful for smaller holders but counsel should model whether individual opt-out rights preserve superior litigation value for larger positions given the § 502(b) petition-date peg. In the **Genesis/DCG** matter, Gemini Earn creditors and their counsel (Weil) should file appearances in Adv. Pro. No. 23-01192 ahead of the **June 5, 2026** preliminary conference and seek amicus or intervenor status to protect earnout interests if the 60(b)(3) motion proceeds to discovery. In the **Three Arrows** Chapter 15, creditors of 3AC with claims above $1 million should confirm they are registered with the BVI liquidators through Teneo's claims portal before the next distribution waterfall is announced; given the 15-cent recovery trajectory, participation in the Terraform adversary proceeding outcome is the primary remaining upside variable. Finally, across all matters, restructuring counsel should review whether their clients have properly preserved **§ 510(b) subordination** arguments against any equity-conversion claims that may surface as reorganized-entity distributions flow — a structural protection that has been litigated in both the FTX and Celsius confirmation proceedings and remains relevant in any post-effective-date challenge.
