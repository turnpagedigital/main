# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Friday, June 19, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills § 363 Sale Approved; Plan Confirmation Set for September — The Week's Decisive Move**


On Tuesday, June 16, 2026, a Delaware bankruptcy judge approved the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as the estate prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 The buyer is Keyrock SA, a Brussels-based market maker. 
Per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock confirmed on May 26, 2026 that it had been declared the "Successful Bidder" for the assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
The acquisition comes months after Keyrock raised a Series C round led by SC Ventures, Standard Chartered's venture capital arm, at a $1.1 billion valuation.


The § 363 sale approval is the structurally decisive event in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, 
Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan.
 
Reliz and its three affiliated debtors filed on March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 With the § 363 sale now approved, the case advances toward plan confirmation. 
The claims administrator, Veritaglobal, lists a September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) key date — the next significant procedural deadline on the confirmed calendar
 — which, consistent with the Law360 reporting, corresponds to the plan confirmation timeline flagged at the sale hearing. Creditors and claimants should note that while the docket logged 13 new entries in the 72 hours ending June 18 (docket entries 429–442), the substance of entries 441 and 442 was not publicly described in the CourtListener extract; that content warrants direct review at the Veritaglobal claims portal.

**The Customer-Property Question Remains Open**

The sale approval does not resolve the case's central legal question. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 That dispute maps directly onto 11 U.S.C. § 541's scope-of-estate analysis — the same question litigated in the Celsius terms-of-service proceedings (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 22-10964) and addressed in the Hong Kong Court of First Instance's *Gatecoin* ruling (HKCFI [2023] 914), where the court treated customer-deposited crypto as property of the estate absent a clear trust structure. BlockFills' institutional counterparty structure — 
the company focused solely on institutional, high-net-worth, and sophisticated customers and did not offer services to retail traders
 — distinguishes it from Celsius's retail-deposit model, but the § 541 analysis tracks the same framework: did BlockFills hold customer assets in segregated trust or commingle them into estate property?


The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 That commingling history is the evidentiary foundation for any § 541 contest: funds that flowed through counterparty defaults are harder to trace as identifiable customer property. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026)
 and, per the same filing, *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.). Both actions were stayed by the § 362 automatic stay on March 15, 2026. Whether the preliminary injunction obtained at the outset of the case has been extended through confirmation is unverified in publicly available docket summaries — that extension posture should be confirmed directly on PACER.

**The Dual-Track Structure and What Survives the § 363 Sale**


The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 The § 363 sale to Keyrock transfers the operational assets — technology, client lists, IP. The liquidating trust absorbs the residual estate claims, including any fraudulent-transfer or preference recoveries and the disputed customer-property pool. 
The deal would transfer "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets — along with certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, proprietary technology and intellectual property — to Keyrock, subject to court and regulatory approval.
 The gap between "substantially all assets" and the full $145 million general unsecured claims pool is where creditor recovery actually lives; the plan structure and the liquidating trust's claims-monetization strategy will determine how much of that gap closes.

**FTX Recovery Trust: $1.15B Clawback Against Genesis Digital Assets Voluntarily Dismissed**

A significant secondary development this week: 
the FTX Recovery Trust voluntarily dismissed its lawsuit against Bitcoin mining company Genesis Digital Assets Ltd., abandoning its attempt to recover $1.15 billion in funds alleged to have been fraudulently transferred by the bankrupt cryptocurrency exchange's co-founder; the trust dropped all claims with prejudice, according to a filing in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The case caption is *FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets Ltd.*, Bankr. D. Del., No. 1:25-ap-52358, dismissal filed June 15, 2026. 
The FTX trust is represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP and Landis Rath & Cobb LLP; Genesis Digital is represented by Latham & Watkins LLP and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor LLP.


The mechanics of the claim deserve precise attention. 
The lawsuit claimed Genesis Digital, along with its affiliates and co-founders, received commingled and misappropriated funds from FTX co-founder Sam Bankman-Fried between August 2021 and April 2022; the trust alleged that Bankman-Fried used his personal hedge fund, Alameda Research LLC, to purchase several tranches of shares in the Bitcoin mining company at heavily inflated prices.
 
According to the filing, between 2021 and 2022, Alameda spent over $500 million on 154 preferred shares of GDA and wired another $550.9 million directly to GDA's co-founders, Rashit Makhat and Marco Krohn.
 
Genesis Digital, a Cyprus-based company headquartered in Dubai, moved to dismiss the lawsuit in January.
 No settlement amount has been publicly disclosed; the with-prejudice dismissal means the trust cannot refile. The strategic rationale — whether this reflects a settlement, an evidentiary concession on the fraudulent-transfer elements, or a resource-allocation judgment — has not been stated publicly. 
Counsel for the FTX trust did not immediately respond to a request for comment about why it dropped the suit; counsel for Genesis Digital did not immediately respond either.


For the FTX creditor recovery arc, the dismissal is a setback of real magnitude. 
The recovery plan began with a $1.2 billion payout for claimants, followed by a $5 billion distribution in May; it is expected to unlock $1.6 billion for creditors on September 30.
 The $1.15 billion that would have flowed from a successful GDA recovery will not be available to that September distribution or any subsequent one, absent unforeseen developments.

## Recommended Actions

**For creditors and claimants in BlockFills / Reliz (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)):** The § 363 sale approval to Keyrock closes the operating-asset chapter of this case; attention now shifts entirely to the plan confirmation process and the liquidating trust's scope. Review the confirmed plan text (Docket No. 128 and any amendments) for the treatment of your claim class — specifically whether customer-deposited digital assets are classified as general unsecured claims or as separately administered property under a bailment or trust theory, because that classification drives both your recovery percentage and your distribution timing. The September 11, 2026 deadline at Veritaglobal is the next hard date; confirm whether that is a claims objection deadline, a plan supplement deadline, or a confirmation hearing date by pulling the scheduling order directly from PACER or the Veritaglobal portal at veritaglobal.net/BlockFills. If you hold an unliquidated or disputed claim arising from the prepetition customer asset shortfall, ensure that claim is filed and that any related Customer Action (Dominion Capital or Alberta Ltd./Bertram) has been properly coordinated with bankruptcy counsel — the automatic stay will affect those proceedings' trajectory through confirmation. **For FTX creditors:** The with-prejudice dismissal of *FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets* (No. 1:25-ap-52358) is final; factor the $1.15 billion reduction in prospective recoveries into your claim-valuation model. The September 30 distribution of approximately $1.6 billion remains on track per publicly available estate communications; confirm your claim-of-record date if you acquired FTX claims in the secondary market, as distribution eligibility may turn on transfer notice mechanics under Bankruptcy Rule 3001(e).
