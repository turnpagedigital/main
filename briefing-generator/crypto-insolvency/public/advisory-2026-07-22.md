### I. BlockFills (★ KEY FOCUS) — *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.

This matter remains the primary analytical focus for the customer-property question in 2026 U.S. crypto insolvency.


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), reporting approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 The case is assigned to Judge Thomas M. Horan, Courtroom No. 5, 824 Market Street, Wilmington, Delaware 19801.

The customer-property question crystallized at the first-day hearing. 
Debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That concession — echoing Celsius's contested terms-of-service litigation — places the entire $145 million unsecured pool on the estate side of §541, eliminating any trust or bailment carve-out absent adversary proceedings from individual customers. Creditors who believed they held segregated assets are, on the Debtors' framing, general unsecured creditors subject to plan recovery.


The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 That dual-track structure — §363 sale of operating assets, residual estate into a liquidating trust — is now materially advanced.

On the sale track: 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, assuming "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology. Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" in a bankruptcy court filing dated May 26, 2026.
 
A court hearing to approve the sale was held June 16, and the transaction has since closed following court and regulatory approval.
 At $3.25 million against $145 million in general unsecured obligations, the operating-asset sale proceeds cover less than 3 cents on the dollar — confirming that creditor recovery will depend almost entirely on the liquidating trust's monetization of remaining assets and any fraudulent-transfer or preference recoveries.

On the plan track: 
On May 28, 2026, the Court entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement and scheduling the confirmation hearing (Docket No. 388).
 
The voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline were July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the confirmation hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The tracked-case ground truth reflects a last docket entry of July 17, 2026 (Dkt. 530), with zero new entries in the 72-hour window before this morning's run — suggesting either a post-confirmation quiet period or a continuance not yet reflected in the CourtListener sync. Claims administration is running through Veritaglobal (f/k/a Kroll's KCC) at 222 N. Pacific Coast Highway, Suite 300, El Segundo, CA 90245.

The UCC, formed March 30, 2026 and staffed by members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs, Inc., 
appointed pursuant to a U.S. Trustee notice filed March 27, 2026 (Docket No. 104); FTI Consulting was retained as the Committee's financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.


Liquidity background worth holding: 
The company attributes its crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius; compounding these pressures were stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.

### II. MVMT Labs, Inc. (NEW) — *In re MVMT Labs, Inc.*, Case No. 26-11113, Bankr. D. Del.

This is a materially new filing as of yesterday evening, July 21, 2026 — the single freshest development on this beat.


MVMT Labs, Inc., the Delaware corporation historically associated with the Movement blockchain, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The case was filed under Subchapter V of Chapter 11 — a streamlined reorganization track reserved for smaller businesses — and assigned to the Honorable Thomas M. Horan.
 Same judge as BlockFills; different case number.


A company that was reportedly on the cusp of a $3 billion valuation in early 2025 now lists estimated assets of just $100,001 to $500,000, against liabilities of between $1 million and $10 million.
 
The original developer of the Movement blockchain listed between $100,001 and $500,000 in assets, up to $10 million in liabilities, and as many as 299 creditors.


The largest single creditor claim belongs to the Debtor's own former executive. 
Rushi Manche holds the largest unsecured claim, worth more than $1.6 million.
 
The project came under scrutiny after a market-making deal enabled the rapid sale of 66 million MOVE tokens, triggering a steep price drop and prompting investigations and a token buyback.
 
The firm's collapse was triggered after the controversial initial distribution of those 66 million MOVE tokens through a market-maker agreement that caused massive liquidations and bans from exchanges such as Binance.


The structural separation between the insolvent entity and the live ecosystem deserves precision. 
Move Industries CEO Torab Torabi has stated that his company, now behind the Movement ecosystem, is not part of the bankruptcy proceedings.
 
Move Industries assumed responsibility for development and operations from Movement Labs in December 2025, through a transfer described in a post on the Movement Network website.
 That transfer raises a threshold question creditors and the Subchapter V trustee should press: whether the December 2025 operational handoff constitutes a fraudulent transfer or an avoidable transaction under 11 U.S.C. §548 or applicable state law, particularly given the temporal proximity to the insolvency filing and the mismatch between the Debtor's balance sheet ($500K assets, $10M liabilities) and its former enterprise valuation.


The deadline for creditors to file proofs of claim is September 14, 2026, and a reorganization plan is expected around mid-October under Subchapter V's accelerated timeline.
 
The court expects a restructuring plan by October 13, 2026.


The MOVE token itself has been severely impaired. 
MOVE hit $0.0104 — a 94% drop from earlier levels — with a market cap of approximately $45 million, ranking 454 among tracked tokens.
 For any creditor whose claim was denominated in or pegged to MOVE, the petition-date valuation question (in-kind vs. fiat; which date governs) will be live in Subchapter V plan negotiations.

### IV. Pattern Recognition — The §541 / Customer-Property Fault Line

The three active dockets above all converge on the same threshold dispute that defined Celsius and will shape this entire wave: whether customer-deposited crypto is property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. §541 or held in trust or bailment outside it.

In BlockFills, the answer — at least on the Debtors' opening posture — is that everything is estate property, commingled at filing. In MVMT Labs, the December 2025 operational transfer to Move Industries will need to be stress-tested for the same question applied to IP, tokens, and ecosystem assets rather than customer cash. In FTX, the plan resolved the question on a petition-date fiat-value basis — MOVE token creditors in the new Subchapter V case should study the FTX plan's valuation methodology carefully, as the Subchapter V trustee's plan will almost certainly face the same in-kind vs. fiat election argument that drove years of Celsius litigation and the Gatecoin HKCFI No. 914 decision under Hong Kong's CWUMPO.

The 2026 pattern is now clear: as Layer-2 and infrastructure-layer blockchain projects cycle through the insolvency courts with hollow balance sheets and contested prior-entity transfers, the fraudulent-transfer avenue under §548 — and its offshore analogues under the BVI Insolvency Act 2003 and Cayman Companies Act Part V — becomes the primary recovery vector. The MVMT Labs/Move Industries split is the cleanest current example of that structure.

Creditors with claims traceable to the December 2025 Move Industries transition should preserve all communications relating to that transfer and flag them to counsel for §548 analysis; the six-year lookback under applicable Delaware fraudulent-transfer law may extend beyond Subchapter V's plan horizon. **FTX creditors** who completed the June 16 record-date procedures should receive funds through BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer by approximately August 3 — any creditor who has not yet completed KYC and tax documentation should do so immediately, as the sixth distribution timeline is not yet announced and the disputed-claims reserve reduction to $1.8 billion signals the estate is moving toward wind-down.
