### BlockFills (*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.*, No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., Judge Thomas M. Horan) — Sale Approved; Confirmation Passed

The dominant story on this beat this week. The docket reflects two new filings as of July 17 (Dkt. 530 and an unidentified entry filed the same day), but the structural developments driving creditor posture predate those entries and require consolidation here.

The pivotal June 16 milestone: 
Judge Horan approved the $3.25 million sale of BlockFills to Keyrock, a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume substantially all of BlockFills' assets.
 
The acquisition gives Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, which includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies.
 The sale approval came under § 363 of title 11, consistent with the dual-track structure the Debtors filed from the outset.

The confirmation hearing was calendared for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Horan at 824 Market Street, Wilmington. 
The Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET and the Confirmation Hearing was July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket ground truth shows two filings in the 72 hours ending July 17 — the most recent dated July 17 (no description yet posted) and Dkt. 530 dated July 15 — suggesting post-hearing confirmation order activity, though the order itself is not yet described in the publicly synced feed. Creditor counsel should pull the docket directly at CourtListener ID 72494188 for the confirmation order text.

The capital structure context remains critical for recovery sizing: 
the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings and three affiliated entities filed March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 With a $3.25 million sale price against $145 million of unsecured exposure, recovery math on the asset-sale proceeds alone is de minimis. The recovery thesis for general unsecured creditors rests on the liquidating trust and whatever the estate recovers through the adversary and insider-claims process.

The § 541 commingling issue — the central property-of-estate question — was resolved against customers at the first-day stage. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives had disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 That admission forecloses the bailment/trust argument that customers in Celsius and similar cases tried to press; the plan confirmation, if entered on or around July 13, cements the estate-property characterization and moves unsecured creditors into the pro-rata distribution queue rather than any preferential in-kind return track.

The pre-petition fraud exposure remains live. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y.).
 
A second action, *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al.*, N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:26-cv-02451, asserted similar claims against multiple Debtor entities and named individuals including Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace.
 Both matters were stayed by the automatic stay upon filing; how the plan treats those claims — released, vested in the liquidating trust, or preserved for individual prosecution — is the question creditors' counsel must verify in the confirmed plan text.


BlockFills attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 The Committee of Unsecured Creditors — comprising SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs, Inc. — 
retained FTI Consulting as financial advisor effective April 1, 2026, approved under Dkt. 264.
 FTI's ongoing engagement suggests the Committee will be active in reviewing the plan's insider-release provisions and any proposed distribution mechanics under the liquidating trust.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: $900M Commencing July 31

Yesterday's announcement is the second-most significant development this week. 
FTX Trading Ltd. and the FTX Recovery Trust announced that FTX will commence distributions on July 31, 2026 to holders of allowed claims in the Plan's Convenience and Non-Convenience Classes that have completed the pre-distribution requirements by the Record Date of June 16, 2026.


The class-by-class mechanics: 
Allowed Class 5A Dotcom Customer Entitlement Claims will receive an incremental 9% distribution (105% cumulative distribution to date); Allowed Class 5B U.S. Customer Entitlement Claims will receive a 5% distribution (105% cumulative distribution to date); Allowed Classes 6A General Unsecured Claims and 6B Digital Asset Loan Claims will each receive a 3% distribution (103% cumulative distribution to date); and Allowed Class 7 Convenience Claims will receive a cumulative 120% distribution.



FTX will begin distributing approximately $900 million to creditors on July 31, extending a bankruptcy repayment process that has now returned nearly $10 billion since payouts started in 2025.
 A separate $18 million payment flows concurrently: 
the Preferred Shareholder Remission Fund Trust will distribute $18 million to eligible preferred equity holders on July 31, bringing total payments from that fund to $95 million.


The claim-valuation-date issue warrants a standing note for this beat. 
FTX's official claims documentation states that customer balances are reflected as of November 11, 2022; their U.S. dollar values were calculated using the Digital Asset Conversion Table approved during the bankruptcy proceedings. A creditor who held an appreciating asset such as Bitcoin is therefore being repaid against its court-recognized claim value rather than receiving the original number of coins or their present market value. Passing 100% represents repayment of the allowed dollar claim plus applicable plan treatment, not full restoration of the investment position the customer would hold today.
 This is the precise tension — petition-date dollarization versus in-kind recovery — that Genesis resolved in creditors' favor and that FTX resolved against them. At current BTC prices, the delta between a petition-date dollar claim and the coin equivalent is substantial.

Transfer mechanics for the claim-trading aftermarket: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register as of future record dates, where the 21-day notice period has lapsed without objection.
 Any claim trader who acquired positions after the June 16 record date is out for the July 31 round.

### Genesis/DCG Wind-Down — Active Adversary Docket, July 21 Pre-Trial Scheduled

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC (*In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al.*, Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean Lane) remains active in its post-plan wind-down phase. 
An omnibus hearing ran July 15, 2026; a pre-trial conference is calendared for July 21, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET (ZoomGov).
 The active adversary proceedings before the court include multiple fronts against DCG: 
*Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053, and a related district court matter, No. 26-03230.
 Additional adversary proceedings include *Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Cap.*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129, and 
*Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.


The structural backdrop: 
the May 2024 confirmation decision broke new ground by allowing creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to the petition-date value; Genesis was authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors, even assets that appreciated after the petition date.
 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the confirmed plan.
 What remains is the litigation-recovery tail: adversary proceeds against DCG and insiders flow to further distributions under § 1129 plan mechanics. The July 21 pre-trial is presumably a scheduling conference in one of the active adversary proceedings; counsel should confirm which caption attaches to that ZoomGov setting.

### Cross-Case Pattern Note: The § 541 / In-Kind Valuation Fault Line

The three cases reviewed this week illustrate the beat's central tension cleanly. BlockFills: commingling admitted at first-day, estate property determined, customers in the general unsecured pool, petition-date (or near-petition-date) dollar recovery. FTX: claims dollarized at November 2022 values; class 5A/5B creditors reaching 105% of allowed dollar value but not recovering coin appreciation. Genesis: the exception — in-kind recovery approved over DCG's objection, 
with the court ruling that DCG lacked standing to object because its equity interests were junior to tens of billions of dollars in allowed claims, including at least $11 billion in governmental claims.
 
DCG did not appeal.
 That asymmetry — Genesis creditors recover coin appreciation, FTX and BlockFills creditors do not — is the structural result of how each debtor's plan treated claim valuation. Practitioners advising creditors in future insolvencies should treat Genesis as the high-water mark, not the baseline.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors in the BlockFills estate should immediately obtain the confirmation order from Dkt. 72494188 on CourtListener (the July 17 filing and Dkt. 530 likely encompass confirmation-related entries) and map their claim against the liquidating trust waterfall and the treatment of the insider fraud claims that Dominion Capital and Alberta raised prepetition — those claims' fate under the plan is the primary recovery lever for general unsecured creditors beyond the $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds; claims not expressly released should be preserved for assignment to the trust. FTX creditors who completed KYC, tax, and service-provider onboarding by the June 16 record date should confirm distribution-provider readiness through BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer for the July 31 payment window; those who missed the record date should begin the compliance queue now against future record dates, and claim-trading counterparties should verify 21-day transfer notice periods have cleared before assuming eligibility. Professionals monitoring Genesis should pull the July 21 pre-trial agenda for Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053 to determine the litigation schedule on the primary DCG adversary; that recovery will drive future distributions and is the material remaining value-creation event in the Genesis wind-down. On the Celsius clawback front, the July 13 damages framework order merits direct review for any counterparty to a Celsius clawback demand — the framework will likely set the methodology for settling outstanding preference and fraudulent-transfer demands across the Celsius defendant pool.
