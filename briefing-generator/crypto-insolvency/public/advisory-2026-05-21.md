## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas:** The FTX Debtors' fourth interim distribution window opened this week with confirmed wire transfers to approximately 98% of creditor accounts denominated below $50,000, while the estate's litigation trust simultaneously filed a $1.76 billion adversary proceeding against a cohort of venture capital co-investors; the Genesis Global Capital liquidating trust secured a contested order authorizing a second interim distribution of roughly $1.2 billion in cash and Bitcoin-equivalent value; and the Celsius Network post-effective-date debtor announced that a disputed $444 million clawback action against former CEO Alex Mashinsky advanced past a motion-to-dismiss ruling in the Southern District of New York. Separately, Voyager Digital's effective-date wind-down administrator filed its third and likely penultimate fee application, and regulators in the UK's Financial Conduct Authority issued a notice of investigation against two former Three Arrows Capital principals in connection with ongoing cross-border asset recovery efforts coordinated with the BVI liquidators.

### FTX Debtors — Fourth Interim Distribution and New Adversary Proceeding

The FTX Debtors (In re FTX Trading Ltd., et al., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Bankr. D. Del., Judge John T. Dorsey, Courtroom 5) confirmed this week that the fourth interim distribution tranche — targeting convenience-class creditors holding allowed claims under $50,000 — went live Monday, May 19, with approximately $872 million disbursed through the Kroll Restructuring Administration portal (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/05/19/ftx-fourth-distribution-tranche/)__). The Plan Administrator has confirmed that 98.3% of eligible accounts in this tranche received transfers within 48 hours of the distribution window opening, with remaining accounts held pending updated KYC/AML verification under the estate's obligations pursuant to the Bank Secrecy Act, 31 U.S.C. § 5318 (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/post/ftx-fourth-distribution-2026)__).

More consequentially for the broader creditor body, the FTX Litigation Trust — a vehicle established under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan — filed Adversary Proceeding No. 26-50129-JTD on May 19, 2026, naming as defendants a group of 11 venture capital and strategic investors that participated in FTX's Series B and Series C funding rounds during 2021 and 2022. The complaint seeks avoidance and recovery of approximately $1.76 billion in aggregate distributions, advisory fees, and preferential transfers under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547, 548, and 550, alleging that certain defendants received redemptions and fee payments within 90 days of the petition date while possessing constructive knowledge of the debtor's insolvency (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-litigation-trust-sues-vc-investors-1-76-billion-2026)__). The complaint also advances a state-law fraudulent transfer theory under Delaware's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act, 6 Del. C. § 1304, for transfers occurring outside the preference window. Counsel of record for the Trust is Sullivan & Cromwell LLP (James Bromley and Andrew Dietderich), with co-counsel Landis Rath & Cobb LLP. A scheduling conference has not yet been set; under the local rules, defendants have 30 days from service to respond (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-vc-adversary-proceeding-2026)__).

Creditors should note that the confirmed plan's post-effective-date reorganized entity continues to hold approximately $180 million in disputed-claims reserve, pending resolution of roughly 2,400 objected claims. The claims agent (Kroll) estimates that resolution of those contested claims could add between $0.04 and $0.11 per dollar of allowed claim value to ultimate recoveries at the high end of the sensitivity range (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/technology/ftx-disputed-claims-reserve-2026-05/)__).

### Genesis Global Capital — Second Interim Distribution and Trust Governance Dispute

In the Genesis Global Capital liquidating trust (In re Genesis Global Capital, LLC, et al., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean H. Lane, Courtroom 701), Judge Lane entered an order on May 20, 2026 authorizing the second interim distribution of approximately $1.2 billion, to be paid in a combination of U.S. dollars (estimated 68% of distribution value) and Bitcoin-equivalent consideration at a reference price of $94,200 per BTC as of May 16, 2026 (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/genesis-second-distribution-approved)__). The distribution covers creditors in the GGC, GAP, and GGH estates who filed timely proofs of claim and whose claims were not subject to pending objection as of the distribution record date of May 9, 2026.

A contested governance matter overshadowed the distribution hearing. A creditor group represented by Kirkland & Ellis LLP (Joshua Sussberg and Christine Okike) filed a motion on May 14 challenging the Liquidating Trustee's unilateral decision to invest approximately $340 million in short-duration U.S. Treasury instruments pending distribution, arguing that the trust agreement required affirmative consent of the Trust Oversight Committee before any reinvestment of liquid assets exceeding $50 million (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/genesis-trust-governance-dispute-2026)__). The Trustee (Derar Islim, former Genesis CEO, in a court-supervised capacity) countered that the trust agreement's investment guidelines, set out at Section 6.4(b) of the confirmed plan, granted discretion for "capital preservation" instruments without committee pre-approval. Judge Lane issued a bench ruling finding for the Trustee on the narrow governance question but directed the Trustee to provide 72-hour advance notice to the committee before any future reinvestment exceeding $25 million — a compromise that partially cabins discretion going forward (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/genesis-trust-governance-order-2026-05-20/)__).

Parent Digital Currency Group (DCG) continues to contest approximately $620 million in intercompany claims asserted by the GGC estate, with that adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 24-01070-SHL) currently in discovery through a September 30, 2026 fact-discovery cutoff. DCG's answer, filed March 15, 2026, invokes the equitable subordination doctrine under 11 U.S.C. § 510(c) as an affirmative defense — a posture that, if credited, would subordinate DCG's own cross-claims against the estate to the satisfaction of third-party creditors (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/post/genesis-dcg-adversary-discovery-2026)__).

### Celsius Network — Mashinsky Clawback Action Survives Motion to Dismiss

The post-effective-date Celsius debtor (In re Celsius Network LLC, et al., Case No. 22-10964-MG, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn, Courtroom 523) achieved a significant litigation milestone when Judge Glenn issued a 47-page opinion on May 19, 2026 largely denying defendant Alex Mashinsky's motion to dismiss the estate's $444 million clawback complaint (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-mashinsky-clawback-survives-motion-to-dismiss-2026)__). The adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01219-MG) alleges that Mashinsky received approximately $68.7 million in CEL token sales proceeds, $12.3 million in salary and bonus compensation, and caused the estate to make constructively fraudulent transfers totaling approximately $363 million to insiders and related parties within the two-year lookback period under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B).

Judge Glenn held that the estate adequately pleaded both the "transfer" and "benefit received" elements at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage, rejecting Mashinsky's argument that CEL token sales by him personally could not constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 because the tokens were purchased on third-party exchanges (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/celsius-mashinsky-mtd-denied-2026)__). The court did dismiss, without prejudice, two counts asserting violations of the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c), finding that the civil RICO pleading standard — requiring a "pattern of racketeering activity" with at least two predicate acts separated by both time and character — was not met on the current complaint. The estate has 30 days to replead. Counsel for the estate is Kirkland & Ellis LLP; Mashinsky is represented by White & Case LLP (Gregory Pesce and Keith Woffinden).

The dismissal ruling does not affect the parallel criminal proceedings: Mashinsky entered a guilty plea in the Southern District of New York (U.S. v. Mashinsky, Case No. 23-cr-347-JPC, Judge J. Paul Oetken) in July 2025 on one count of commodities fraud, 7 U.S.C. § 13(a)(2), and three counts of securities fraud, 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b), with sentencing currently scheduled for September 11, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/celsius-mashinsky-sentencing-scheduled-2026/)__). The collateral estoppel implications of the criminal plea for the civil adversary proceeding have not yet been briefed.

### Voyager Digital — Wind-Down Fee Application and Residual Recovery

The Voyager Digital wind-down administrator (In re Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc., et al., Case No. 22-10943-MEW, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles, Courtroom 617) filed its third interim fee application on May 20, 2026, seeking approval of $4.2 million in fees and $310,000 in expenses for the period January 1 through April 30, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/voyager-wind-down-fee-application-2026)__). The application, filed by Moelis & Company as financial advisor to the wind-down administrator, indicates that total estate disbursements since the plan effective date have reached approximately $1.33 billion across all creditor classes, representing a blended recovery of roughly 36 cents on the dollar against pre-petition claims at face value.

Approximately $47 million in disputed claims remain unresolved, primarily relating to crypto-asset valuation disputes where the debtors' estate calculated claims at petition-date prices (June 2022) and creditors assert entitlement to higher current-market valuations under their loan agreements. The administrator has moved for summary judgment on the valuation methodology issue, with briefing to close June 30, 2026 before Judge Wiles. The outcome turns on whether the loan agreements' force-majeure and default-valuation clauses are preempted by the allowance-of-claims framework under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b), a question on which courts in the S.D.N.Y. have not issued a definitive post-Voyager ruling (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/voyager-claims-valuation-dispute-2026)__).

### Three Arrows Capital — FCA Investigation and Cross-Border Asset Recovery

The joint liquidators of Three Arrows Capital Ltd. (In the Matter of Three Arrows Capital Ltd., BVI Commercial Court, Claim No. BVIHC(COM) 2022/0119, Liquidators: Russell Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo) reported on May 20, 2026 that the UK Financial Conduct Authority has opened a formal investigation into Su Zhu and Kyle Davies, the co-founders of 3AC, in connection with potential violations of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, sections 19 (general prohibition) and 89 (misleading statements) (__[FT — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/content/fca-three-arrows-capital-investigation-2026)__). The FCA investigation is the latest in a multi-jurisdictional enforcement picture that already includes parallel proceedings in Singapore (MAS, which revoked 3AC's fund management license in June 2022), the United States (CFTC, Docket No. 22-cv-08613, S.D.N.Y.), and the BVI liquidation.

The liquidators have to date recovered approximately $120 million in estate assets globally, against estimated creditor claims exceeding $3.3 billion — a recovery rate of approximately 3.6% on face claims (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/three-arrows-capital-liquidation-recovery-update)__). The BVI court granted a recognition order under the UNCITRAL Model Law on Cross-Border Insolvency as implemented in England under the Cross-Border Insolvency Regulations 2006, allowing the liquidators to enforce asset-disclosure orders against UK-domiciled counterparties without fresh proceedings. A similar Chapter 15 recognition order was entered in the S.D.N.Y. (In re Three Arrows Capital Ltd., Case No. 22-10920-MG, Judge Martin Glenn) in July 2022 and remains in effect (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/3ac-cross-border-recovery-2026)__).

## Recommended Actions

**Creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel** should prioritize the following across the active estates: (1) FTX convenience-class creditors who have not yet received fourth-tranche distributions should contact Kroll Restructuring Administration immediately to confirm KYC/AML compliance, as the window for expedited processing closes June 2, 2026; (2) FTX general-class creditors holding claims against entities named in the new Adversary Proceeding No. 26-50129-JTD should monitor docket developments — if you are a defendant or hold economic interests in defendant entities, you need conflicts-cleared restructuring counsel engaged before the 30-day response deadline; (3) Genesis creditors with pending objected claims should review the Trustee's claims-reconciliation filings carefully, as the trust's investment-governance dispute demonstrates that distribution timing remains contingent on contested intercompany matters with DCG, and your ultimate recovery rate in cash vs. BTC-equivalent form will depend on the reference-pricing date used at each distribution; (4) Celsius creditors should track the Mashinsky clawback replead deadline (June 18, 2026) and the RICO pleading supplement, as a successful amendment that survives a second motion to dismiss would materially increase the aggregate pool available for distribution; (5) Three Arrows Capital unsecured creditors should file or confirm updated claims in the BVI liquidation by any applicable bar dates and consult counsel regarding the FCA investigation's potential to unlock UK-sited assets not previously reachable through BVI process alone; and (6) Voyager claimants disputing petition-date valuation should prepare economic expert submissions for the June 30 briefing cutoff — if Judge Wiles rules in favor of the administrator's methodology, the window to appeal will be tight.
