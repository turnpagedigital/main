# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Thursday, June 25, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills / Reliz — Keyrock sale is past its June 16 hearing date; confirmation now the critical path.** The most consequential open question in the BlockFills case is what happened at and after the June 16 sale hearing. 
On March 15, 2026, four affiliated debtors — Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., Reliz Technologies LLC, Reliz LTD, and Reliz CI LTD — filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
Reliz reported assets of $50–$100 million against liabilities of $100–$500 million at filing.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.



Per a bankruptcy filing, Brussels-based Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million for substantially all of BlockFills' assets, including certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary Vision Trader technology and intellectual property; Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" in the court document filed May 26, 2026.
 
The debtors were pursuing a dual-track restructuring — sale process and plan path simultaneously — with the sale hearing set for June 16, 2026, and plan confirmation targeted for September 11, 2026.
 The June 16 hearing appears to have proceeded on schedule per the Veritaglobal case portal, which lists it as a noticed date. (__[Veritaglobal / BlockFills case page](https://www.veritaglobal.net/BLOCKFILLS)__) No post-hearing docket entry confirming approval has been independently verified this run; practitioners should pull Dkt. No. 457 (filed June 24) directly via CourtListener docket ID 72494188 to confirm the sale order's entry.

The structural tension that will govern residual creditor recovery deserves careful attention. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission tracks the same § 541 property-of-the-estate framing that drove the most contentious phase of the Celsius case. If the unsecured creditors' committee — 
comprised of SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs Inc. — (Dkt. 262, Apr. 29, 2026)
 — challenges that characterization in the plan or otherwise, it reopens the customer-property/trust-claim question at confirmation under 11 U.S.C. § 1129. 
The committee retained FTI Consulting as financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.


Two pre-petition customer lawsuits add adversary-proceeding complexity that survives the § 363 sale. 
In the weeks before filing, Dominion Capital LLC sued in *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram filed *1548199 Alberta Ltd. v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill., Mar. 5, 2026), each asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud; courts granted TROs in both actions.
 The debtors also commenced Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners LLC, (Dkt. filed Apr. 9, 2026), suggesting active fraudulent-transfer or turnover litigation against counterparties beyond the customer plaintiffs. (__[Veritaglobal Adv. Pro. 26-50230 filing](https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260409000000000003)__) Creditors should monitor whether sale-order language contains a broad "free and clear" release under § 363(f) that attempts to cut off these claims against the buyer — a point objectors including the Chicago Blackhawk Hockey Team, Inc. and Oracle raised at or before the June 16 hearing per the June 12 hearing agenda at Dkt. 424.


The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
According to BlockFills, trading volume exceeded $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase year-over-year; the firm said it served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 The $3.25 million purchase price against a $145 million general unsecured creditor pool signals deeply distressed recoveries absent successful avoidance-action litigation.

**FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth distribution record date has passed; July 31 payout approaching.** The FTX wind-down has now crossed into its fifth distribution cycle. 
The record date for the fifth distribution to holders of allowed claims was June 16, 2026 — which has now passed — with distributions expected to commence July 31, 2026; payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders are also scheduled for July 31.
 
FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.


For context on cumulative recovery: 
distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion to Convenience Class claimants, followed by a larger institutional payout in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion in September, and a fourth round launched March 31, 2026.
 
The fourth distribution totaled approximately $2.2 billion; it pushed many customer claim classes — including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B — to 100% recovery, while Class 7 reached a cumulative 120%.
 
By mid-2026, creditors had been paid roughly $10 billion across four distributions.


The petition-date valuation issue — the structural wound the FTX plan has not cured — remains live for Class 5A Dotcom claimants. 
The main Classes 5B and 6A/6B are at 100% recovery; Class 5A (the Dotcom group) has been paid approximately 96% of principal so far, with a projected total recovery of roughly 118–120% once the 9% annual Consensus Rate interest is added.
 
Every percentage is measured against the dollar value of assets at November 2022 — meaning that for holders who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ in cash returns less purchasing power in today's terms.
 That gap — between nominal claim recovery and in-kind token appreciation — is the structural cost of the petition-date dollarization approach, now crystallized for comparison against the Genesis in-kind model.


For holders of transferred claims, distributions will be made only where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 Claim purchasers who missed the June 16 deadline will wait until the sixth distribution cycle. The FTX Recovery Trust has continued its clawback posture: 
Genesis Digital Assets, a Bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion; the case is part of the broader clawback strategy, with Genesis Digital Assets pushing to dismiss, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.

**Genesis / DCG Wind-Down — Litigation adversary proceedings active; four distributions completed.** The Genesis Global Holdco wind-down (Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) has now completed four distributions to creditors since the plan's August 2, 2024 effective date. (__[Kroll Genesis restructuring page](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__) 
The wind-down estates are managed by Mark Renzi as Plan Administrator under the oversight of the Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) and the Litigation Oversight Committee (LOC), with Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP and Selendy Gay PLLC serving as their respective legal advisors.


The active adversary docket is the operative story now. The Kroll case page lists several active proceedings: 
Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230, and DCG v. Genesis Global Capital, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129
 — cross-litigation between the wind-down estate and its former parent. Additional insider-defendant and redacted-defendant adversary proceedings (Adv. Pro. Nos. 25-01097 and 25-01028) remain sealed or pseudonymous. The litigation posture extends the estate's tail considerably beyond the distribution timeline.

The doctrinal significance of Genesis's in-kind recovery model — which broke with the petition-date dollarization default adopted in FTX and most prior crypto cases — continues to reverberate. 
The May 2024 confirmation decision allows creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to petition-date values; Genesis was authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors, even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the confirmed plan.
 
Per the initial distribution, Bitcoin creditors received 51.28% in-kind recoveries, Ethereum creditors received 65.87%, and Solana creditors received 29.58%.
 Subsequent distributions have incrementally closed the gap. 
As part of the bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund — comprising $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD — to pursue legal action against third parties including DCG.

**Cross-Docket Pattern: Customer Property and the Commingling Problem.** The recurring fault line across BlockFills, Celsius, and Genesis is whether crypto deposits constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or whether they were held in trust or bailment such that customers have a priority claim to specific assets rather than a rateable share of the general estate. BlockFills debtors have taken the Celsius position — all funds are estate property — while the committee has retained independent financial advisors who will presumably scrutinize that characterization. The Celsius TOS litigation (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) and the HKCFI decision in *Gatecoin* (HKCFI 914) established that TOS language and commingling practices are outcome-determinative; BlockFills' confirmed commingling (per first-day admissions) tracks the Celsius "earn account" fact pattern more closely than the segregated-custody model that might support a trust or bailment claim. Customer-claimants in BlockFills should review the operative account agreements and custodial terms before conceding the estate-property framing in plan negotiations.
