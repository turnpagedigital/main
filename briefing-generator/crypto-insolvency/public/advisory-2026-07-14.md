### I. BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Confirmation Window Opens


On March 15, 2026, all four Reliz debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan, U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, 824 Market Street, Courtroom No. 5, Wilmington.



The petition disclosed $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty exposure — specifically an $8.5 million impairment from the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.


The docket reached Entry 527 on July 13, three new entries within 72 hours per the live case data, against a backdrop set by the May 28 solicitation approval. 
On May 28, 2026, Judge Horan entered the order approving the disclosure statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, and establishing all deadlines for plan objections, claim objections, voting procedures, and the record date (Docket No. 388).
 The Veritaglobal calendar shows 
September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET)
 as the remaining active future date — suggesting confirmation has not yet occurred as of this writing or has been rescheduled to September.

The structural profile of the case remains distinctive and analytically important. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt.
 That framing — institutional brokerage rather than retail lending — distinguishes BlockFills from Celsius, but the § 541 customer-property question under 11 U.S.C. is structurally identical: if customer crypto deposits are property of the estate, they waterfall alongside general unsecured obligations; if held in trust or bailment, they return in kind ahead of the estate.


BlockFills served exclusively institutional, high-net-worth, and sophisticated traders — no retail customers.
 That fact shapes the litigation posture on the customer-property question. Unlike Celsius, where a fragmented retail base litigated TOS language through the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, BlockFills' counterparties are sophisticated entities with independent contract claims.


In the weeks immediately preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits asserted misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill., Mar. 5, 2026); both courts granted TROs that significantly restricted the debtors' operations before the Chapter 11 filing stayed them.



On March 27, 2026, the U.S. Trustee for Region 3 appointed the Committee of Unsecured Creditors (Docket No. 104); committee members include SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 FTI Consulting serves as the Committee's financial advisor. The committee structure is notable — Dominion Capital is simultaneously a committee member and a pre-petition plaintiff in the S.D.N.Y. customer-fraud action, a conflict posture that committee counsel will need to manage through the confirmation process.

**Auction cancellation and § 363 disposition.** 
The Veritaglobal case page reflects a Notice of Cancellation of Auction, a Notice of Further Rescheduled Auction, a Notice of Rescheduled Auction, and a Notice of Sale Hearing.
 That sequence — cancellation followed by reschedulings — is consistent with a contested § 363 process where initial bidding failed to clear minimum requirements, followed by renewed marketing. 
The June 12 hearing agenda (Dkt. 424) confirmed that the sale of substantially all assets was going forward at the June 16 hearing, while a protective-order matter was adjourned and objections remained from the Chicago Blackhawks Hockey Team and Oracle.
 The status of those objections as of today's date has not been separately confirmed in the 72-hour scan; practitioners should pull the post-June 16 docket for the sale approval order and any asset-purchase agreement terms.

**Phishing alert — active risk for creditors.**

### II. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Commences July 31, 2026; Record Date Closed; Disputed Reserve Cut $600 Million

The FTX docket (In re FTX Trading Ltd., Bankr. D. Del.) has moved from plan administration to distribution mechanics. The next distribution window is now 17 days away.


The fifth distribution is expected to commence on July 31, 2026; the next payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders will also be made on July 31, 2026, with a June 16, 2026 record date.
 
FTX also filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion — from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion — which, subject to court approval, would release cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims.



Cumulative distributions now exceed $10 billion.
 
Distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion in September, and a fourth round launched on March 31.


The claim-transfer aftermarket carries a hard process risk for this distribution window. 
For transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 That window has now closed. Any claim transferred after June 16 — or any transfer for which the 21-day notice period had not run by that date — will not participate in the July 31 distribution. The transferee holds a claim that participates in the next subsequent distribution only.


Under § 7.14 of the confirmed Plan, holders of Allowed Claims who do not submit a completed valid tax form will be required to do so within the timeframe provided in that section or forfeit their distribution; additionally, if a holder does not successfully onboard with a distribution service provider within six months from the July 31 distribution date, such holder may forfeit their right to receive distributions on their Allowed Claims.


The petition-date valuation constraint remains the central economic fact. 
The "120% recovery" headline is calculated against the USD value of assets on November 11, 2022, when FTX filed for bankruptcy — on that date, Bitcoin was trading at approximately $16,871.
 With BTC now well above $80,000, holders who were paid in dollars rather than in kind received a structurally diminished recovery relative to current market value. That valuation delta continues to drive secondary-market activity and reinforces why claim sellers have been pricing at discounts to face even at headline recovery rates exceeding par on a dollar basis.


FTX is represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP as legal counsel, Alvarez & Marsal North America, LLC as financial advisor, Perella Weinberg Partners LP as investment banker, Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan, LLP as special counsel, and Landis Rath & Cobb LLP as Delaware counsel.

### III. Genesis Global Holdco / DCG — Post-Plan Adversary Litigation Proliferates; Omnibus Hearing July 15

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063) is past its August 2024 plan effective date but operating an expanding portfolio of adversary proceedings against Digital Currency Group and its affiliates.


The Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) is prosecuting Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053 (filed 2026), and a parallel action at Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230.
 
DCG is itself a counterclaimant in Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129.
 Additional insider-recovery proceedings are pending at 
Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097,
 and 
Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028.
 The $33 million adversary proceeding against DCG related to Three Arrows Capital has been a separate litigation track since the plan's confirmation.


An omnibus hearing is set for July 15, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 That hearing falls tomorrow. Practitioners monitoring the DCG adversary proceedings should track the agenda for any motion-to-dismiss rulings or discovery scheduling orders in 26-01053 or 26-03230.

The Genesis plan's in-kind distribution structure remains analytically significant for the broader beat. 
Genesis obtained court approval of a Chapter 11 plan that provided in-kind recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values; because some crypto prices like Bitcoin nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected creditor recoveries.
 That approach — distinguishing Genesis from the dollarized-cap methodology used in FTX and Celsius — is the template that institutional creditors in future crypto insolvencies will cite when opposing petition-date valuation caps. The WDOC's continued recovery litigation against DCG under the $70 million litigation fund established at confirmation is the mechanism for funding residual distributions. 
That fund comprises $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.

### IV. Cross-Case Pattern: Customer-Property, Valuation-Date, and Plan-Mechanics Convergence

Three vectors from this week's docket activity are worth holding together analytically:

**Customer-property (§ 541).** BlockFills presents the institutional version of a question first litigated acutely in Celsius: are crypto deposits held on account for customers property of the estate or a trust/bailment? 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested in Case No. 26-10371.
 The NewCo transaction structure Reliz is pursuing attempts to sidestep the § 541 dispute by migrating customer accounts to a reorganized entity — but if the sale fails or the plan is not confirmed, that question will need to be adjudicated. Counsel should expect the committee's FTI financial advisors to be focused on this issue through the September 11 confirmation window.

**Valuation-date.** The FTX distribution mechanics confirm that the petition-date dollarization cap — Bitcoin at $16,871 on November 11, 2022 — governs all claim recovery in that estate, regardless of current prices. 
The FTX estate will never pay based on current market prices; it is legally settled at petition-date values.
 That constraint is built into the confirmed Plan and is not appealable at this stage. By contrast, Genesis's in-kind framework produced materially better nominal recoveries for BTC and ETH creditors. The divergence between the two architectures will govern secondary-market pricing of any remaining disputed or unresolved claims in either estate.

**Plan mechanics — forfeiture risk.** The six-month onboarding deadline under § 7.14 of the FTX Plan creates a forfeiture cliff approximately around January 31, 2027, for any holder who receives a July 31 distribution but fails to complete DSP onboarding. 
Holders who do not onboard with a distribution service provider within six months from the July 31 distribution date may forfeit their right to receive distributions.
 That deadline will begin running in 17 days for the July 31 tranche and is a practical compliance issue for claim purchasers who have completed transfer but not yet completed KYC/DSP onboarding downstream.
