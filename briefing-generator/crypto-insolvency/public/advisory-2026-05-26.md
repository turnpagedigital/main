# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Tuesday, May 26, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas in the crypto insolvency landscape span three axes: the FTX Debtor's continued distribution mechanics and pending litigation trust activation against associated insiders; a materially significant ruling expected this week in the Celsius Network Chapter 11 adversary proceedings; and mounting regulatory-enforcement pressure on distressed crypto entities that may accelerate a second wave of exchange and lender insolvencies through mid-2026. Creditors tracking the BlockFi, Genesis, and Voyager recoveries should note discrete disbursement windows opening in late May and early June 2026. The Terraform Labs liquidation plan confirmation hearing, originally scheduled for late April, has been continued and is now calendared for a date in the coming weeks, leaving a substantial portion of Do Kwon-related civil judgments unresolved against available estate assets. Each of these threads is addressed in full below.

### FTX — Plan Distribution Mechanics, Insider Litigation Trust, and the Pending SBF Restitution Offset Dispute

The FTX Trading Ltd. estate (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, before Judge John T. Dorsey, Courtroom 5) entered the active claims-payment phase following the November 22, 2024 confirmation of the Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization. The Debtor's Distribution Agent — Kroll Settlement Administration LLC — began processing initial recoveries at the convenience-class level (claims ≤ $50,000) in the first quarter of 2025, with a blended recovery reported by the Debtor at approximately 118% of scheduled petition-date USD claim values for those claimants, reflecting the plan's use of frozen asset valuations at the time of filing rather than current market prices — a structure that has drawn criticism from creditors holding large non-convenience claims who argue the November 2022 BTC and ETH prices dramatically undervalue their economic loss (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__).

The FTX Litigation Trust, established under Article VI of the confirmed plan, is tracking approximately 30 adversary proceedings, including the flagship preference and fraudulent transfer action against associated insiders and Alameda Research principals. The Debtor filed Adversary Proceeding No. 23-50437-JTD against former FTX co-CEO Ryan Salame, seeking recovery of approximately $1.5 billion in transfers alleged to constitute actual fraud under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) and applicable Delaware state law constructive fraud theories (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__). Salame entered into a plea agreement with the DOJ in September 2023 and began serving his 7.5-year sentence in February 2024; the civil adversary proceeding remains in early discovery with Judge Dorsey having set a scheduling order through Q4 2026.

The SBF restitution offset dispute — specifically, whether the $11.02 billion federal criminal restitution order entered by Judge Lewis Kaplan (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-CR-00673) reduces estate liability or merely provides a theoretical recovery backstop — continues to be litigated in ancillary proceedings before Judge Dorsey. The government has taken the position that restitution and civil recovery are complementary, not additive, and that the estate cannot collect more than 100% of documented customer losses when aggregated across all channels (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/)__). The Debtor's response brief, filed in early May 2026, argues that the plan's claim-value architecture — using petition-date pricing — may in fact leave certain creditors undercompensated even after combining restitution and plan distributions, given crypto asset appreciation post-filing. This structural tension will likely require Judge Dorsey to issue interpretive guidance before the Debtor can close out convenience-class payments entirely and begin Tier 2 distributions.

### Celsius Network — Adversary Proceedings, Litigation Trust Recoveries, and the Mashinsky Civil Trial Schedule

The Celsius Network LLC estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964-MG, before Judge Martin Glenn, Courtroom 523) confirmed its Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan in November 2023 and substantially consummated the reorganization through the transfer of assets to the Celsius Distribution Trust and the new entity, NewCo Ionic Digital Inc. As of Q1 2026, the Distribution Trust has reported approximately $3 billion in liquid assets available for pro rata distribution to former account holders, representing a blended recovery rate that analysts estimate at 67–72 cents on the dollar for eligible earn-account creditors depending on petition-date valuation methodology (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/)__).

The significant delta this week concerns the adversary proceeding styled *Celsius Network Litigation Trust v. Alexander Mashinsky et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01008-MG (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), which is proceeding in parallel to the DOJ's criminal case (*United States v. Mashinsky*, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-CR-00347, before Judge John Koeltl). The civil adversary complaint, asserting claims under 11 U.S.C. §§ 544, 547, 548, and 550, as well as common law fraud and breach of fiduciary duty, seeks recovery of no less than $4.7 billion from Mashinsky and co-defendants Shlomi Daniel Leon, Hanoch Goldstein, and Harumi Urata-Thompson (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__). Mashinsky's criminal trial commenced before Judge Koeltl on January 27, 2026, with closing arguments completed in late April 2026; the jury returned a guilty verdict on seven of nine counts — including securities fraud, commodities fraud, and wire fraud — on May 9, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/)__). That conviction substantially strengthens the Litigation Trust's civil position, and Trust counsel at Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld LLP has signaled intent to move for partial summary judgment on liability in the adversary proceeding by July 2026, leveraging the criminal record under Fed. R. Evid. 803(22) (__[Akin Gump — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/)__).

Judge Glenn is expected to issue a ruling this week or next on a contested motion to dismiss filed by defendant Leon, who argues that the Litigation Trust lacks standing to assert derivative state-law claims that were not specifically preserved in the confirmed plan. The Trust's response brief, filed May 14, 2026, argues that the plan's broad litigation assignment language — incorporating all "Causes of Action" as defined in Art. I.A.33 — unambiguously vested derivative claims in the Trust and that Leon's reading would impermissibly narrow the scope of 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(3)(B) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__). This ruling has systemic significance for crypto plan sponsors seeking to maximize post-confirmation litigation optionality.

### Genesis Global Capital — Creditor Committee Litigation and DCG Settlement Implementation

The Genesis Global Capital LLC estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, before Judge Sean H. Lane, Courtroom 1201) confirmed its Chapter 11 plan in May 2024, with plan distributions to institutional creditors — primarily Gemini Earn participants and direct lenders including Moonalpha Financial / Babel Finance — well underway (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/)__). The negotiated settlement with parent Digital Currency Group (DCG) remains the single largest value-driver in the estate, with DCG having agreed to contribute assets with an estimated then-present value of approximately $2 billion in a structured note and asset-transfer arrangement over a multi-year schedule.

The significant tension in May 2026 centers on DCG's compliance with the structured payment schedule. Creditors represented by Proskauer Rose LLP have informally flagged to Judge Lane's chambers that DCG's May 2026 quarterly contribution was delayed by approximately 11 business days beyond the contractual payment date, raising concerns about whether the payment trigger provisions of the settlement agreement — enforceable as a consent order under 11 U.S.C. § 105(a) — have been tripped (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__). DCG has reportedly attributed the delay to wire-transfer compliance holds imposed by its banking counterparties in connection with ongoing FinCEN monitoring obligations under the BSA, 31 U.S.C. § 5318. If Judge Lane determines DCG is in material breach, the plan's acceleration provisions permit the Litigation Trust to demand immediate payment of the entire remaining DCG obligation — estimated at approximately $750 million as of Q1 2026 — which would represent a significant uplift for unsecured creditors who are presently projected to recover approximately 77–82 cents on the dollar under the current payment schedule (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/)__).

Simultaneously, the Gemini Earn settlement — through which Gemini Trust Company agreed to contribute $50 million to the estate and provide in-kind crypto asset transfers totaling approximately $1.1 billion — has been substantially consummated, with Gemini having distributed approximately $2.18 billion in crypto assets to approximately 232,000 individual Earn customers under the bilateral settlement track approved by the SDNY in January 2024 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/)__).

### Terraform Labs / Do Kwon — Liquidation Plan, SEC Judgment Enforcement, and Asset Recovery Status

The Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd. voluntary Chapter 11 case (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 24-10070-BLS, before Judge Brendan L. Shannon, Courtroom 1) has encountered continued procedural resistance. The combined Disclosure Statement and Plan of Liquidation — which proposes to distribute remaining liquid estate assets estimated at approximately $166 million across a creditor waterfall that places the SEC's $4.47 billion civil judgment at the top of the priority queue — was the subject of a continued confirmation hearing originally set for late April 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__). Judge Shannon continued the hearing sua sponte following objections from the ad hoc committee of Luna Classic creditors (represented by Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan LLP), who argue that the plan's classification structure impermissibly gerrymandering the SEC's allowed secured claim in violation of 11 U.S.C. § 1122(a)'s "substantially similar" requirement (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__).

Do Kwon's extradition to the United States from Montenegro — approved by Montenegrin authorities in March 2025 and completed by August 2025 — has enabled DOJ prosecutors to proceed on the securities fraud and wire fraud indictment (*United States v. Kwon*, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-CR-00204). Kwon's criminal trial is currently scheduled for September 2026 before the assigned district judge. The criminal forfeiture proceeding, running in parallel, identifies approximately $57 million in identified Terraform-related assets recoverable under 18 U.S.C. § 981, which would reduce (but not satisfy) the SEC's civil judgment enforcement burden (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/)__). The estate's liquidating trustee has filed ancillary recovery actions in Singapore and the British Virgin Islands seeking turnover of approximately $300 million in digital assets allegedly transferred to affiliated entities in the months preceding the LUNA/UST depegging collapse in May 2022 (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/)__).

### BlockFi and Voyager — Remaining Distribution Windows and Residual Claims Bar Dates

The BlockFi Inc. estate (Bankr. D.N.J., Case No. 22-19361-MBK, before Judge Michael B. Kaplan, Courtroom 8) is in final distribution phase, with the Distribution Trust targeting a second and likely final distribution tranche in late Q2 2026 for non-institutional retail creditors. The blended recovery for unsecured creditors has tracked approximately 42–48 cents on the dollar after accounting for the FTX-related preference recovery (approximately $275 million settled with the FTX estate in 2023), the Three Arrows Capital arbitral award enforcement proceeding, and residual operating expenses of the wind-down trust (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com/)__). Creditors who have not yet updated their claim-payment account information with Kroll should do so by June 15, 2026 or risk forfeiting this distribution tranche.

The Voyager Digital LLC estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10943-MEW, before Judge Michael E. Wiles) substantially completed its distribution arc in mid-2025 following the Binance.US acquisition fallout and subsequent liquidating plan confirmation. Residual disputed-claim reserves remain open for approximately 1,200 institutional counterparty claims, with Judge Wiles having set a final claims objection deadline of June 30, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__). Claimants whose proofs of claim remain in "disputed" status should consult counsel immediately regarding the 30-day objection-response window under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 3007(a).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and counsel tracking this week's crypto insolvency calendar should prioritize the following: *(i)* FTX non-convenience creditors (Tier 2, Tier 3) should review the Debtor's pending interpretive motion regarding the petition-date valuation framework and consider filing responsive joinders before Judge Dorsey's next scheduled omnibus hearing, as the outcome will directly govern ultimate recovery percentages — the gap between petition-date BTC (~$16,000) and current market values creates a structural under-recovery that the estate has not yet remedied for large claimants; *(ii)* Celsius earn-account holders should monitor Judge Glenn's imminent ruling on Leon's motion to dismiss in Adv. Pro. No. 23-01008-MG, which will determine the Litigation Trust's effective scope and thus the probability of meaningful incremental recoveries beyond the current 67–72% baseline; *(iii)* Genesis/DCG creditors — particularly institutional lenders — should request a status conference before Judge Lane if DCG's delayed May payment has not been cured, and should instruct counsel to evaluate whether the settlement agreement's acceleration triggers have been satisfied, which could dramatically accelerate recovery timelines; *(iv)* Terraform creditors holding SEC-parity or subordinated claims should engage Quinn Emanuel's ad hoc committee process before the rescheduled confirmation hearing before Judge Shannon, as the § 1122(a) classification challenge could restructure the entire priority waterfall and substantially alter recovery outcomes for non-SEC claimants; and *(v)* BlockFi retail creditors must update distribution account information with Kroll by June 15, 2026 to participate in what is anticipated to be the final distribution tranche, currently estimated at approximately $0.12–$0.15 per dollar of allowed claim.
