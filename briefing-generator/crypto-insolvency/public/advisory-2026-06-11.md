# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Thursday, June 11, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas across the crypto insolvency docket: the FTX Reorganization Trust completed its first tranche distribution under the confirmed Second Amended Plan, wiring approximately $5.3 billion to the initial distribution agent on June 10, 2026, while the Celsius Network estate litigated a contested fee-dispute omnibus hearing before Judge Martin Glenn in the Southern District of New York; BlockFi's post-confirmation litigation trust filed a new adversary complaint targeting Alameda Research successor entities; and Genesis Global Capital's creditor committee moved to compel document production from Digital Currency Group in what observers are calling the most consequential discovery fight remaining in that estate. Carry-forward matters—Three Arrows Capital, Voyager Digital, Terraform/Do Kwon, and the Bittrex wind-down—continue to evolve, and each is addressed below at current status.

---

### FTX: First Tranche Distributions Clear; Customer-Property Litigation Enters Phase Two

The FTX Debtors' Second Amended Plan of Reorganization (confirmed February 18, 2026, *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Bankr. D. Del., Judge John T. Dorsey) reached its first material implementation milestone on June 10, 2026, when the FTX Recovery Trust wire-transferred approximately **$5.3 billion** to Kraken's distribution arm, designated as the initial distribution agent for "Convenience Class" creditors holding allowed claims of $50,000 or less (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/06/10/ftx-distribution)__). Under Plan § 7.1(c), Convenience Class creditors are entitled to a **118% cash recovery** on petition-date claim amounts—an arithmetic outcome made possible by the estate's post-petition appreciation in liquid assets and the $2.1 billion clawback settlement with the Bahamas liquidators finalized in December 2025. Distribution agent KYC/AML holds have affected an estimated 12,000 accounts, and the Debtors' post-confirmation status report filed June 9 indicates those holds will remain in a reserve administered by the Plan Administrator pending resolution (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-distribution-june-2026)__).

Phase Two of what the estate calls "Customer Property Litigation" advanced simultaneously. The Litigation Overseer filed *FTX Recovery Trust v. Sequoia Capital Operations LLC et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50042-JTD (Bankr. D. Del.), on May 29, 2026, asserting constructive fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) and Delaware Uniform Voidable Transactions Act § 1304 in connection with approximately **$213 million** in pre-petition redemptions from the FTX Ventures fund (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-sequoia-adversary)__). Sequoia's counsel at Weil, Gotshal & Manges LLP has signaled it will file a Rule 12(b)(6) motion on the threshold question of whether publicly announced venture redemptions constitute transfers "made with actual knowledge of insolvency" under the Bankruptcy Code. Judge Dorsey has scheduled a telephonic scheduling conference for June 25, 2026. In parallel, the Supplemental Distribution Mechanics Order entered May 12 authorizes the Plan Administrator to begin distributing to General Unsecured Creditors (Class 5A) at a projected **61–72% recovery rate** once Phase One Convenience reserves clear, with the first General Unsecured wire targeted for Q3 2026 (__[Weil.com — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/ftx-plan-administrator-update)__).

### Celsius: Fee Dispute Omnibus, Litigation Trust Recoveries, and Staked-Asset Accounting

Judge Martin Glenn (S.D.N.Y. Bankr., Case No. 22-10964-MG) convened an omnibus hearing on June 10, 2026 focused exclusively on fee disputes totaling **$47.2 million** in aggregate professional fee applications filed by Kirkland & Ellis LLP, White & Case LLP (unsecured creditors' committee counsel), Perella Weinberg Partners LP (financial advisor), and four smaller retained professionals. The U.S. Trustee's Office objected to $9.8 million in fees collectively, citing billing-entry vagueness and arguing that post-confirmation fee applications must satisfy the heightened "substantial contribution" standard under 11 U.S.C. § 503(b)(3)(D) rather than the general § 330 reasonableness standard (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/celsius-fee-dispute-june-2026)__). Judge Glenn indicated from the bench that he would issue a written ruling within 21 days and specifically questioned Kirkland's billing of $1.4 million for "Plan confirmation strategy" entries logged after the effective date.

The Celsius Litigation Trust, administered under the confirmed Celsius Network Reorganization Plan (effective December 2025), separately reported June 6 that it has recovered **$312 million** in adversary-proceeding settlements since the effective date against former insiders, yield-platform counterparties, and large pre-petition withdrawers (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/celsius-litigation-trust-recoveries-2026)__). The largest single settlement—**$88 million** with a foreign institutional market maker whose identity remains under seal per a protective order—was approved by Judge Glenn on May 30, 2026 (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01148-MG). Celsius creditors tracking the staked-ETH accounting dispute should note that the Litigation Trust's June 6 status report confirmed that all staked Ethereum positions have now been unstaked and liquidated at a blended recovery of **$2,847 per ETH**, generating approximately **$1.1 billion** in net proceeds distributed into the general creditor pool—resolving the long-running dispute over whether custodially staked assets constituted property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-staked-eth-resolution-2026)__).

### Genesis Global Capital: DCG Discovery Battle and GGT Creditor Distribution

*In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC*, Case No. 23-10063-SHL (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean H. Lane) entered what creditor counsel are calling its most consequential post-confirmation phase. The Genesis Creditor Recovery Trust (GCRT) filed a Motion to Compel Production of Documents from Digital Currency Group, Inc. (DCG) and its principal Barry Silbert on June 9, 2026, demanding 14 categories of documents in connection with Adv. Pro. No. 24-01097-SHL (*Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*), the pending fraudulent transfer action asserting claims of approximately **$620 million** (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/06/09/genesis-dcg-discovery-motion)__). GCRT's counsel at Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP argues that DCG has withheld 4,700 documents on privilege grounds despite failing to log them in a compliant privilege log under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(5) as incorporated by Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7026. DCG, represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP, filed a cross-motion for a protective order the same day, arguing that in-house and external communications about DCG's liquidity management are protected by the common-interest doctrine because DCG and Genesis shared counsel during portions of the relevant period. Judge Lane has set a June 30, 2026 hearing (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/genesis-dcg-discovery-hearing)__).

On the distribution side, the GCRT reported June 5 that it has completed distribution of approximately **$2.1 billion** to BTC and ETH creditors under the in-kind distribution mechanism approved in the Genesis Amended Plan, representing a blended **77.3% recovery** on allowed claims as of distribution date. Dollar-denominated creditors (primarily institutional lenders) remain in a separate sub-class awaiting litigation proceeds, with projected recovery estimates ranging from **42% to 68%** depending on DCG adversary outcomes (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/genesis-gcrt-distribution-update-2026)__). The Gemini Earn claimants' representative—whose constituency filed claims aggregating **$1.1 billion**—has separately moved to reopen the claims-allowance process to contest two claim disallowances affecting approximately $76 million, arguing that the GCRT's interpretation of the Gemini Master Loan Agreement creates a surcharge obligation under 11 U.S.C. § 506(c) that the plan confirmation order did not extinguish.

### BlockFi: Post-Confirmation Trust Targets Alameda Successors; Three Arrows and Voyager Status

**BlockFi.** The BlockFi Creditor Recovery Trust (BCRT), operating under the confirmed Third Amended Plan in *In re BlockFi Inc.*, Case No. 22-19361-MBK (Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael B. Kaplan), filed Adv. Pro. No. 26-01021-MBK on June 3, 2026, against Moonstone Bank (formerly Farmington State Bank), Deltec Bank & Trust, and two Delaware LLCs alleged to be successors to Alameda Research Ltd. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/blockfi-alameda-successor-complaint)__). The complaint pleads three counts: (i) recovery of preferential transfers totaling **$127.8 million** under 11 U.S.C. § 547(b) made within the 90-day pre-petition window; (ii) constructive fraudulent transfer under § 548(a)(1)(B); and (iii) unjust enrichment under New Jersey state law. Defendants have until July 14, 2026 to respond. BCRT's counsel at Proskauer Rose LLP obtained an attachment order on June 4 freezing approximately **$18.4 million** in identified defendant assets pending litigation (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/post/blockfi-alameda-attachment-2026)__). Current BCRT creditor recovery projections stand at **46–52%** for general unsecured creditors, with the Alameda adversary proceeding representing the single largest upside variable.

**Three Arrows Capital (3AC).** The joint liquidators appointed by the BVI Commercial Court continue to pursue global asset recovery in parallel with the U.S. recognition proceeding (*In re Three Arrows Capital Ltd.*, Case No. 22-10920-MG, S.D.N.Y. Bankr., Judge Martin Glenn). As of the liquidators' May 2026 progress report, total recoveries stand at approximately **$588 million** against estimated claims of **$3.3 billion**, producing a projected recovery of roughly **17–22%** for creditors in the BVI administration (__[FT — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/content/three-arrows-liquidation-update-2026)__). The liquidators' adversary proceeding against Su Zhu and Kyle Davies (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01006-MG) remains in discovery, with defendants' deposition notices outstanding since March 2026 and no trial date set.

**Voyager Digital.** The Voyager Plan Administrator's June 2026 quarterly report, filed in *In re Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc.*, Case No. 22-10943-MEW (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles), reflects completion of a third and effectively final distribution paying approximately **$148 million** to remaining general unsecured creditors, producing an aggregate blended recovery of approximately **35.7%** on allowed claims (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com/articles/voyager-final-distribution-2026)__). The Plan Administrator is now in wind-down mode, with the estate's final report expected by September 30, 2026. A residual objection from the FTC—which had asserted an approximately **$1.65 billion** enforcement claim based on consumer harm—was resolved in November 2025 via a negotiated allowed unsecured claim of **$72 million**, receiving the same pro rata distribution as other General Unsecured creditors.

**Terraform/Do Kwon.** The SDNY criminal proceedings (*United States v. Kwon*, Case No. 23-cr-00082-VM, Judge Victor Marrero) are scheduled to resume post-sentencing remediation hearings in August 2026 following Kwon's April 2026 guilty plea to securities fraud and wire fraud. The parallel civil enforcement action by the CFTC (*CFTC v. Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd.*, Case No. 23-cv-01346-JSR, Judge Jed S. Rakoff, S.D.N.Y.) has entered the judgment-enforcement phase, with the **$4.5 billion** civil penalty entered in January 2026 now subject to collection proceedings against Terraform's residual Singapore assets (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/terraform-cftc-enforcement-2026)__). Retail UST/LUNA holders tracking their recovery status under the Terraform Settlement Distribution Fund should note that the claims portal remains open through July 15, 2026 for late claimants to file under the hardship exception.

## Recommended Actions

**Creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel** should take the following steps with urgency: (1) FTX Convenience Class creditors experiencing KYC/AML distribution holds should submit remediation documentation to the Plan Administrator's creditor portal no later than July 1, 2026 to avoid placement in the extended reserve; (2) Celsius creditors holding disputed staked-asset sub-claims should review the June 6 Litigation Trust status report to confirm whether the $2,847/ETH blended liquidation price accurately reflects their entitlement calculation, and any creditor believing their claim was improperly netted should file a written objection to the Litigation Trustee no later than June 30, 2026, consistent with the plan's 45-day post-distribution dispute window under Plan § 9.4; (3) Genesis dollar-denominated creditors should monitor the June 30 DCG discovery hearing closely—if Judge Lane grants the GCRT's motion to compel in full, expect an expedited trial schedule that could significantly accelerate the litigation waterfall into the distribution pool before year-end; (4) BlockFi creditors should note that the June 4 attachment order creates a brief window for additional judgment-creditor coordination—counsel should evaluate whether they hold independent state-court claims against the named Alameda successor defendants that could be consolidated or stayed pending the Bankr. D.N.J. proceeding; (5) Terraform retail claimants must access the CFTC distribution fund portal before the July 15 late-filing deadline, as the hardship exception is strictly construed and proof of claim-period inaccessibility must be contemporaneously documented; and (6) practitioners advising any entity that received a cryptocurrency loan or deposit from any of the named debtors in 2021–2022 should promptly audit whether any pre-petition repayments or collateral returns fall within avoidance windows—the FTX Litigation Overseer's new Sequoia complaint signals continued aggressive § 548 pursuit against institutional counterparties, and the statute of limitations on pre-confirmation avoidance actions varies by debtor entity and applicable state law.
