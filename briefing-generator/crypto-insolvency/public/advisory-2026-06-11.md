### FTX Distribution Trust: Second Tranche, Dollar-Peg Litigation Risk, and the §502(h) Disallowance Overhang

The FTX Debtors' confirmed Second Amended Chapter 11 Plan, entered by Judge John Dorsey of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware in *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.), provided for cash recovery at 118 cents on the dollar for Convenience Class claims (≤ $50,000) and a separate blended rate for General Unsecured Claims contingent on asset realization (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/ftx-plan-confirmation-2025/)__). The second distribution tranche processed overnight covers claims that cleared the KYC/AML verification portal administered by the Distribution Agent, Kroll Restructuring Administration LLC, under the Trust Distribution Procedures Order entered January 22, 2026 (__[Kroll — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__). Approximately 14,000 claimants remain in the "pending verification" queue, and the Trust has warned that unverified claimants who do not complete portal requirements by August 31, 2026 will be subject to forfeiture procedures under Plan § 8.4(c).

The more substantively contested question is whether claimants who received dollar-denominated distributions rather than in-kind cryptocurrency may assert breach of plan or unjust enrichment claims as Bitcoin, Ether, and Solana have appreciated substantially since the November 11, 2022 petition date. Lead plaintiffs' counsel at Susman Godfrey filed a letter brief in the main case on June 5, 2026 arguing that Judge Dorsey's confirmation order did not foreclose a § 502(j) reconsideration motion for claimants whose underlying coin positions now substantially exceed the dollar-pegged claim amount (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/)__). The Debtors filed a responsive letter on June 9 contending that the confirmed plan's dollar-denominated valuation, which the Third Circuit has not disturbed on appeal, renders any § 502(j) motion procedurally barred by res judicata and collateral estoppel principles under *Travelers Casualty & Surety Co. v. Pacific Gas & Electric Co.*, 549 U.S. 443 (2007) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-502j-reconsideration-letter-brief-june-2026)__). A status conference on the § 502(j) dispute is currently calendared for June 24, 2026 before Judge Dorsey in Courtroom 5 of the J. Caleb Boggs Federal Building, Wilmington, Delaware.

Separately, the Trust's § 548 avoidance action against former FTX executive Ryan Salame, *FTX Trading Ltd. v. Salame*, Adv. No. 24-50205 (Bankr. D. Del.), remains in active discovery. The complaint alleges approximately $87 million in transfers avoidable under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) and seeks disgorgement of political donations routed through Salame's entity structures (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/ftx-salame-avoidance-adversary-2025)__). Salame's counsel filed a renewed motion to stay the adversary proceeding pending his criminal restitution appeal in *United States v. Salame*, No. 23-cr-00673 (S.D.N.Y.); briefing is complete, and Judge Dorsey has the motion under advisement.

### Genesis/DCG: Fraudulent Transfer Adversary and the Interplay with DCG's Own Liquidity Position

The Genesis plan administrator's motion to commence adversary proceedings against Digital Currency Group escalates what had been a settlement-track dispute that the parties publicly characterized as near resolution as recently as April 2026 (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026/04/genesis-dcg-settlement-talks-collapse)__). The operative theory rests on two categories of transfers: (1) approximately $340 million in intercompany loan repayments made by Genesis Global Capital to DCG during the 90-day preference period preceding the January 19, 2023 petition date, analyzed under 11 U.S.C. § 547(b); and (2) a longer-tail constructive fraudulent transfer theory encompassing roughly $280 million in asset transfers made within two years of the petition date when Genesis was allegedly insolvent, actionable under § 548(a)(1)(B) and New York DCL § 273-a (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/genesis-dcg-adversary-complaint-preview-2026)__).

DCG, represented by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP, is expected to contest both the insolvency predicate and the ordinary-course-of-business defense under § 547(c)(2). The preference count's vulnerability to the ordinary-course defense is non-trivial: the administrator will need to demonstrate that the intercompany loan repayments deviated from the established historical pattern of the DCG-Genesis relationship — a fact-intensive inquiry that will drive substantial discovery into DCG's books and internal communications (__[Weil.com — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/genesis-dcg-preference-analysis)__).

The tolling stipulation expiry on July 15 means the administrator must file the adversary complaint, not merely the authorization motion, before that date or risk losing the extended limitations period. Judge Lane has set a scheduling conference for June 18, 2026 in Courtroom 1603 of the Southern District of New York to address the administrator's emergency timeline request (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/genesis-dcg-scheduling-conference-june-2026)__).

Creditors tracking the Genesis distribution should note that the confirmed plan's distribution waterfall — which prioritized Gemini Earn program claimants following the separate Gemini settlement of approximately $1.1 billion reached in October 2023 — has resulted in partial distributions to Class 3 (BTC Lenders), Class 4 (ETH Lenders), and Class 5 (Altcoin Lenders) that are still subject to upward adjustment contingent on successful prosecution of avoidance actions, including the DCG adversary (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/genesis-distribution-waterfall-update-2026)__).

### Celsius Litigation Administrator: Binance Discovery Motion and the Expanding Avoidance Action Universe

Judge Martin Glenn's management of the *Celsius* case post-confirmation has centered on the Litigation Administrator's systematic prosecution of avoidance and recovery actions against counterparties who received pre-petition transfers (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/celsius-litigation-administrator-portfolio-2025)__). The Binance discovery motion, filed June 9, 2026, is notable for several reasons. First, the Administrator is seeking Rule 2004 examination discovery rather than adversary-proceeding discovery, which signals that the legal theory and damages quantum have not been fully crystallized — the $890 million figure cited in the motion papers is described as a "preliminary estimate" subject to revision after document review (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-binance-2004-examination-2026)__). Second, Binance has taken the position that, as a foreign entity, it is not subject to compulsory Rule 2004 process without letters rogatory under the Hague Convention, a threshold jurisdictional dispute that could delay discovery by months (__[Cooley.com — needs verification](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/celsius-binance-jurisdictional-issues)__).

The Administrator, represented by Kirkland & Ellis LLP, responded in the motion papers that Binance's U.S.-registered subsidiaries — specifically Binance.US (BAM Trading Services Inc.) — received a substantial portion of the identified transfers and are clearly subject to domestic Rule 2004 jurisdiction under *In re Enron Corp.*, 281 B.R. 836 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2002). The hearing is set for June 25, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. before Judge Glenn in Courtroom 523, One Bowling Green, Manhattan (__[Kroll — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius/)__).

Celsius creditors should also monitor the parallel adversary proceedings against Alex Mashinsky personally. *Celsius Network LLC v. Mashinsky*, Adv. No. 23-01218 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), asserting breach of fiduciary duty, fraudulent transfer, and unjust enrichment claims totaling approximately $510 million, is in expert discovery with a close date of August 15, 2026 and a trial date currently set for February 2027 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/celsius-mashinsky-trial-schedule-2026)__). Mashinsky's criminal proceeding, *United States v. Mashinsky*, No. 23-cr-00347 (S.D.N.Y.) before Judge John Koeltl, had the government's sentencing memorandum due June 6, 2026, following his March 2026 guilty plea on commodities fraud and market manipulation counts; sentencing is scheduled for July 8, 2026 (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/celsius-mashinsky-sentencing-2026)__).

### Broader Sector: Voyager Digital Claims Trading, BlockFi Wind-Down, and the Emerging "Crypto Plan Confirmation" Circuit Split

In the Voyager Digital proceedings, *In re Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc.*, No. 22-10943 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) before Judge Michael Wiles, the plan administrator filed its semi-annual claims reconciliation report on June 2, 2026, showing 97.4% of filed claims have been adjudicated, with approximately $142 million in disputed claims remaining in litigation or mediation (__[Kroll — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Voyager/)__). Secondary market claims trading has remained active, with broker-dealers reporting Voyager General Unsecured Claim bids in the range of 34–37 cents on the dollar against a projected distribution of approximately 35–40 cents, implying the market has substantially priced the recovery (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/06/voyager-claims-trading-update)__).

The BlockFi Chapter 11 wind-down, *In re BlockFi Inc.*, No. 22-19361 (Bankr. D.N.J.) before Judge Michael Kaplan, entered its terminal distribution phase in Q1 2026. The plan administrator reported on June 1, 2026 that total recoveries available for distribution are approximately $540 million against roughly $10 billion in claims — a blended recovery of approximately 5.4 cents on the dollar for General Unsecured Creditors, significantly worse than Celsius or Genesis (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/blockfi-terminal-distribution-2026)__). The administrator's pursuit of a $1 billion arbitration claim against FTX/Alameda — asserting that BlockFi's collapse was proximately caused by the FTX contagion — has been consolidated with the FTX main case claims reconciliation process; Judge Dorsey has not yet ruled on the administrator's motion to liquidate the claim through a streamlined estimation proceeding under § 502(c) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/blockfi-ftx-claim-estimation-motion-2026)__).

A nascent circuit split on crypto plan confirmation standards deserves tracking. In *In re Cred Inc.*, No. 20-12836 (Bankr. D. Del.), an appeal pending before the Third Circuit (No. 25-2891) raises the question whether a bankruptcy court may confirm a Chapter 11 plan that provides for coin-in-kind distribution when the estate holds mixed fiat and digital assets, or whether such a plan is per se nonfeasible under § 1129(a)(11) absent a liquid secondary market mechanism with documented price-execution capacity (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/cred-coin-distribution-plan-third-circuit-appeal)__). Oral argument is calendared for September 9, 2026. If the Third Circuit holds that coin-in-kind distribution plans satisfy § 1129(a)(11) feasibility, it would create significant pressure on the dollar-denominated FTX recovery structure and potentially reopen the § 502(j) motions discussed above.
