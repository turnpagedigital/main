### I. BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Confirmation Hearing: July 13, 2026

This case arrives at its § 1129 moment on Monday. 
The confirmation hearing is set for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET
 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in Courtroom 5 at 824 Market Street, Wilmington. 
The voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline were both July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 The docket shows 15 new filings in the 72 hours ending July 9 (through Dkt. 509), reflecting active pre-confirmation motion practice — content of those entries is not yet available from public sources reviewed this run; monitor CourtListener docket 72494188 directly.

**Petition and capital structure.** 
On March 15, 2026, all four Debtor entities filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Horan in the District of Delaware.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse Celsius arbitration obligation and stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment.


**The sale leg closed first; confirmation now resolves the plan leg.** 
Per a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and was declared the "Successful Bidder" for substantially all of BlockFills' assets per the official court document filed May 26, 2026.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Court entered the Disclosure Statement Order (Dkt. 388), scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing voting and objection deadlines, and approving solicitation packages.
 
The case has been operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track structure built around both the sale and a plan path, with confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.


**The customer-property question is not resolved by the sale alone.** The most consequential unresolved issue for general unsecured creditors — who are primarily customers — is whether their digital asset deposits constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in trust/bailment outside the estate. 
On January 16, 2026, counterparty Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; those assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' own representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 
The Debtors have noted a procedural deficiency in customer-creditor arguments: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds. If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, distributed pro rata under the Plan.
 This is the Celsius commingling problem, redux: without a successful tracing action in an adversary proceeding, customers who deposited crypto hold general unsecured claims — not segregated property — and recover alongside trade creditors in a pool that the $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price does little to fill on its own.

**Prepetition customer litigation stays live.** 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, two customer actions named Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers alleging misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026) and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026). The plaintiffs obtained TROs that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business.
 The automatic stay does not extinguish prepetition claims against non-debtor officers and directors; those defendants remain exposed outside the plan process.

**Official Committee.** 
On March 27, 2026, the U.S. Trustee for Region 3 filed the Notice of Appointment of Committee of Unsecured Creditors (Dkt. 104). Committee members are: SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
FTI Consulting serves as financial advisor to the Committee, effective April 1, 2026.
 McDermott Will & Emery serves as Debtors' lead counsel; Katten Muchin Rosenman serves as co-counsel. 
Berkeley Research Group (BRG), with Managing Director Mark Renzi as CRO, serves as the Debtors' financial advisor.


**What to watch at the July 13 hearing.** The central confirmation questions under § 1129 are: (1) whether the plan's treatment of customer claims as general unsecured — rather than trust/bailment interests — is legally defensible given the commingling evidence; (2) whether the $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price, supplemented by whatever estate causes of action survive closing, generates recoveries sufficient to satisfy the § 1129(a)(7) "best interests" test; and (3) whether any objecting creditor filed a formal adversary proceeding asserting property-of-the-estate claims in time to be heard at confirmation. A claims bar date of September 11, 2026 is also on the docket — relevant for any creditors who have not yet filed proofs of claim.

### II. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: July 31, 2026


The anticipated record date for the fifth distribution to holders of allowed claims was June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 That record date has now passed. 
As part of ongoing claims reconciliation, the Trust filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reserve reduction — if approved — flows directly to claimants in the July 31 round. 
The fifth distribution has a record date of June 16, 2026, announced May 26, 2026; the fourth distribution on March 31, 2026 brought cumulative distributions above $10 billion.



For transferred claims, the July 31 distribution is paid to the transferee only if the transfer has been processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16 record date and the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection. If those conditions are not met by the record date, the distribution follows the holder of record as of that date.
 Anyone who traded FTX claims in the secondary market after May 26 but before July 31 needs to confirm transfer registration status immediately.


Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims became eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026.
 That is now a live window. The petition-date valuation dispute — capping claims at November 2022 dollar values rather than current crypto prices — remains embedded in the plan's architecture and generates residual creditor dissatisfaction, but is not a live appellate issue at this stage.

**Clawback posture.** 
The FTX estate is pursuing a $1 billion lawsuit against Genesis Digital Assets as part of its broader clawback strategy.
 Resolution of that action, if successful, would supplement future distribution rounds.

The Genesis/DCG litigation carries forward the valuation-date precedent set at confirmation. 
When the plan went effective in August 2024, BTC creditors received 51% of their in-kind coin-for-coin value (equivalent to 166% of petition-date value), ETH creditors received 66% of in-kind coin-for-coin value (153% of petition-date value), and US dollar creditors received 100% of their in-kind and petition-date value.
 
Further distributions are expected from the proceeds of litigation and settlements under the plan.
 The WDOC's adversary proceedings against DCG are the mechanism for generating those additional distributions; their progress at the July 15 omnibus will be the next public data point.


Among the accomplishments of the restructuring: the WDOC obtained more than $630 million in loan repayments from DCG through a novel turnover proceeding and consent judgment, commenced a $40 million arbitration against DCG regarding late fees and enforcement costs related to certain prepetition loans, and commenced a $33 million adversary proceeding against DCG seeking repayment of amounts paid to Three Arrows Capital.

### IV. Cross-Cutting Theme: The Commingling/Segregation Question

The BlockFills commingling facts — assets swept from deposit wallets within 90 minutes, explicit management disclosures that no per-customer segregation existed — make that case the clearest post-Celsius test of the § 541 property-of-estate analysis in an institutional crypto context. The Celsius litigation resolved this question against customers who held assets in interest-bearing "Earn" accounts (finding they transferred title to Celsius), but the analysis for customers holding assets in pure custody or trading margin accounts remains more contested. BlockFills operated as an institutional OTC brokerage and prime-services desk, not a retail lending platform; whether customer assets deposited for trading purposes — as distinct from lending — carry a different bailment or trust analysis is a question Judge Horan may or may not reach at Monday's confirmation hearing, depending on whether any creditor filed an adversary proceeding or plan objection squarely raising it. The docket activity (15 new entries in 72 hours) suggests the pre-confirmation period is live; the specific filings are not yet available through the sources reviewed this run.

Creditors and rights-holders across this beat face the following concrete next steps, in order of immediacy: **BlockFills claimants** should confirm whether any adversary proceeding or plan objection raising the customer-property issue was filed before the July 1, 2026 deadline, and prepare to monitor the July 13, 2026 confirmation hearing (Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371, Courtroom 5, 824 Market Street, Wilmington) for Judge Horan's ruling on treatment of customer claims as general unsecured versus trust/bailment interests; any creditor who did not file a timely objection is bound by the plan if confirmed, subject only to the September 11, 2026 claims bar date for proof-of-claim filings. **FTX claimants** in the claim-trading market should verify that any transfer executed after May 26, 2026 appears on the official Kroll register as of the June 16 record date; the 21-day objection window controls distribution eligibility for the July 31 round, and late-registered transfers will default to the holder-of-record as of that date. NFT Customer Entitlement Claim holders should have initiated the separate distribution process through the FTX Customer Portal by now; that window opened June 30. **Genesis/WDOC creditors** expecting supplemental distributions from the DCG adversary proceedings should track the July 15 omnibus hearing in Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 23-10063 for any rulings on the DCG motion to dismiss (Adv. Pro. Nos. 26-01053, 26-03230), as dismissal would impair the pool available for future distributions. Across all three matters, claim-valuation-date disputes — in-kind versus fiat, petition-date versus distribution-date — remain the structural variable that most affects actual recovery dollars; the Genesis model (in-kind at distribution-date value) remains the outlier; BlockFills and FTX both operate under petition-date dollarization frameworks.
