First, Ionic Digital (Nasdaq: IOND) — the Bitcoin miner assembled from Celsius Network's mining assets — completed its Nasdaq direct listing on July 28, giving former Celsius claimholders their first liquid exit. ### I. Celsius / Ionic Digital: Creditor Exit Opens, Avoidance Docket Intensifies


Ionic Digital (IOND), the Bitcoin miner formed out of Celsius Network's bankruptcy, rose 26% on its Nasdaq debut on July 28, valuing the company at $2.8 billion — the exchange's largest direct listing since 2021.
 
The listing proceeded under the Nasdaq Global Select Market after the company secured SEC approval.
 
Ionic did not issue new shares or raise new capital; the listed shares belong to existing stockholders. Ionic had separately closed a $400 million Series A convertible preferred round in June 2026 at a reference price of $53.00 per share.


For former Celsius claimholders, the listing is a liquidity event — not a windfall. 
Ionic was formed in January 2024 to acquire Celsius Mining's assets through the court-approved restructuring, and as part of that recovery process, Ionic issued 37 million Class A shares to eligible Celsius claimholders.
 
The first trade on July 28 opened at $50 — below the $53 Nasdaq reference price.
 
Registered stockholders plan to sell up to 10.8 million shares through the listing.


The operating pivot is relevant to long-term value. 
Ionic's flagship asset is a 234 MW facility in Ward County, Texas, leased to Nscale under a 126-month triple-net agreement.
 
Ionic decommissioned Bitcoin mining at the Ward County site in December 2025 and committed the site's 234 megawatts to AI infrastructure provider Nscale under a lease carrying approximately $1.95 billion in contracted revenue.
 
The company projects $195 million in revenue for 2026.


Ionic's listing does not close the Celsius avoidance docket. 
A New York bankruptcy judge established a first-of-its-kind framework for valuing cryptocurrency in Celsius avoidance actions — a ruling White & Case says will guide nearly 1,000 pending cases and hundreds of defaults.
 The Law360 docket entry for July 13, 2026 confirms the court set a damages framework for Celsius Ch. 11 clawbacks (Law360, *Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.*, Jul. 13 docket item). 
White & Case obtained a sweeping victory on the jurisdictional question, with Bloomberg Law reporting that international customers who withdrew over $100,000 ahead of the collapse remained subject to U.S. clawback litigation.
 
In April 2025, the administrator reached a mass-settlement framework covering more than 750 defendants.
 
A Fourth Distribution of approximately $344.4 million began in February 2026.


The GENIUS Act is an emerging variable for stablecoin-adjacent clawback arithmetic. 
The GENIUS Act, signed into law on July 18, is one of the most significant changes to U.S. bankruptcy law since 2005; it requires permitted stablecoin issuers to back their coins one-to-one with liquid assets and treat those reserves as property of the customer, not the issuer.
 The Act's customer-property treatment does not apply to the pre-enactment Celsius estate, but it reshapes the legal baseline for any crypto-insolvency filed after July 18 — including any platform that held stablecoin customer deposits.

### II. BlockFills / *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings* — Post-Sale, Post-Confirmation Posture


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371, before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The debtors reported $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The § 363 sale closed on July 16. 
Keyrock completed its acquisition of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business on July 16, 2026; the deal follows BlockFills' Chapter 11 case and expands Keyrock's regulatory footprint into the Cayman Islands and the United Kingdom.
 
Per bankruptcy filings, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and assumed "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
The deal folds in a CIMA-registered entity in the Cayman Islands, acquired outright, plus an FCA-authorized entity in the United Kingdom still awaiting regulatory approval.


The $3.25 million sale price against ~$145 million in general unsecured claims signals a thin recovery pool. 
The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 Confirmation was originally calendared for July 13, 2026. 
The Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Hearing was set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket logged six new entries in the 72 hours through July 29 (CourtListener Docket ID 72494188, fetched July 30, 2026) — the substance of those entries has not been publicly described in available sources and should be reviewed directly.

The core structural dispute — whether customer digital assets held at BlockFills constitute estate property — was flagged at filing and remains the central creditor-rights question. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursued a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remained contested.
 Resolution of that question will determine whether institutional counterparties are general unsecured creditors at pennies on the dollar or retain priority property claims. The Celsius/Glenn precedent (Earn account assets = estate property under the ToS) is the analytical anchor, but BlockFills served a different institutional counterparty profile — hedge funds, broker-dealers, and investment managers — whose agreements may support different property characterizations under §§ 541 and 362.


The company attributed its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 Two pre-petition misappropriation lawsuits also preceded the filing: 
certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two Customer Actions asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims — including *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026).
 These adversarial threads will likely resurface in the liquidating trust's causes-of-action schedule.

### V. Cross-Cutting: Valuation-Date Disputes and the § 547 Preference Frontier


Crypto-industry bankruptcies increasingly involve preference actions where account-holders received or moved value shortly before the insolvency event. Valuation questions are acute: which date governs — the petition date or the withdrawal date? And if the transferred value was in a volatile token, how should gains or losses be measured for purposes of a clawback? These issues challenge traditional avoidance frameworks and force adaptation by trustees and creditors alike.


The Celsius damages-framework ruling of July 13, 2026 — now confirmed by multiple whitelisted sources — is the most consequential recent ruling on this question. 
A New York bankruptcy judge established a first-of-its-kind framework for valuing cryptocurrency in Celsius Network avoidance actions, a ruling that White & Case says will guide nearly 1,000 pending cases and hundreds of defaults.
 The framework's methodological choices (petition-date pricing vs. distribution-date pricing; dollar-denominated vs. in-kind recovery) will directly govern the quantum of clawback exposure for the hundreds of defendants still in active litigation. Claims-purchasers pricing Celsius avoidance-defendant positions need to update their models.

The GENIUS Act's customer-property rule for stablecoin reserves (
signed July 18
) does not apply retroactively to the existing insolvency cohort, but it will materially change the opening posture of any crypto-platform insolvency filed after enactment — including platforms that commingled customer stablecoin balances with operating capital.
