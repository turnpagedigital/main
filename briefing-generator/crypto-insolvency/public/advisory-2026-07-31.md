**BlockFills — *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., Judge Thomas M. Horan**


On July 13, 2026, Judge Horan confirmed the debtors' Chapter 11 plan, just 122 days after the March 15, 2026 filing date.
 McDermott Will & Emery published a client alert today characterizing the timeline as record-fast for a crypto Chapter 11. 
BlockFills was the first crypto derivatives platform focused on eligible contract participants — institutions and sophisticated high-net-worth individuals — to enter Chapter 11, and the case combined contested estate-property disputes, D&O litigation, and a going-concern sale, all resolved within a single compressed proceeding.


The confirmation order (Dkt. 527) follows a plan structure built around two tracks. 
The four debtor entities pursued a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive § 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remained contested.
 That contest had real money at stake: 
the first-day record identified roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
As disclosed on February 11, 2026, the debtors' representatives had told clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," not segregated on separate wallets, and were commingled into "one balance sheet"
 — the central fact driving the estate-property dispute and the principal creditor-rights issue throughout the case.

The Certificate of No Objection filed at Dkt. 552 (July 30, 2026, 4:28 p.m. ET) has not been separately described in any whitelisted source; its substance is drawn from the docket ground truth. The most plausible reading — consistent with CNO practice in D. Del. after confirmation — is that it covers a fee application or professional retention matter for which the objection period has run without opposition. That inference is unverified as to the specific relief; creditors and claim-trading counterparties should pull the docket directly. 
The next posted key date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 — likely the plan effective date or a related distribution trigger, though the nature of that milestone is unverified from available whitelisted sources.

The background creditor-rights issues embedded in this confirmation remain worth tracking at the pattern level. 
The debtors noted a procedural deficiency against objecting creditors: the objectors had not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and had not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 
If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, to be distributed pro rata under the plan, and to serve as the basis for the cash collateral arrangement.
 Judge Horan's confirmation order (Dkt. 527) will be the primary document for understanding how that dispute resolved — whether the court ruled on estate-property status or whether the plan mooted the question through a consensual structure. That detail is unverified from available sources and should be confirmed by reviewing Dkt. 527 directly.
