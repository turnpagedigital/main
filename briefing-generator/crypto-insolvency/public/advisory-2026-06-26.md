### BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371 · Bankr. D. Del. · Judge Horan): Sale Approved, Plan Confirmation Now the Fulcrum

The case remains the most consequential open docket on this beat because it crystallizes the customer-property question at institutional scale. 
On March 15, 2026, each of the four debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The debtors are Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (EIN x6265), Reliz Technologies LLC (x1968), Reliz LTD, and Reliz CI LTD, each with a service address at 401 West Ontario St., Suite 400, Chicago, IL 60654. 
The debtors in these chapter 11 cases include Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (6265), Reliz Technologies LLC (1968), Reliz LTD (N/A), and Reliz CI LTD (N/A).


The balance-sheet picture has not changed: 
the court filing showed Reliz reporting assets between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million.
 The first-day record adds precision: 
the capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt, with roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The liquidity collapse traces to a sequential chain: 
the company attributes its crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 Losses disclosed in connection with the filing total approximately $75 million.

**The Keyrock deal:**

 The sale consideration — $3.25 million for a platform that reported $60 billion in 2025 trading volume — reflects the estate's zero-going-concern leverage once customer litigation TROs froze operations. 
Trading volume exceeded $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and the firm served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.

The acquisition will give Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, which includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies.

### Cross-Case Pattern: The § 541 Question and Petition-Date Valuation

The thread connecting BlockFills, Genesis, and the broader wave of institutional crypto insolvencies is the same statutory question: whether customer crypto deposits are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541, or whether platform terms of service create a trust or bailment that places customer assets outside the estate. In Celsius (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964, Judge Martin Glenn), the court found that earn-account terms transferred title to the estate — a result that consumer-facing platforms have structurally replicated in custody and trading agreements ever since. BlockFills' institutional trading agreements — not yet publicly available in full — will determine whether the ~$145 million in customer and trade obligations are treated as general unsecured claims or as segregated customer property. The FTX plan's decision to value claims at petition-date fiat (November 11, 2022) rather than in-kind illustrates the stakes: 
every percentage in FTX's recovery is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.
 Genesis's in-kind recovery framework — negotiated with the NYAG and embedded in the plan — stands as the counterfactual model for creditors who can demonstrate a bailment or trust relationship.
