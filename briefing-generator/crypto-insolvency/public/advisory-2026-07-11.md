**BlockFills Moves to Confirmation — But Ten New Docket Entries in 72 Hours Signal Unresolved Friction**


*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.* (Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Thomas M. Horan) entered its critical confirmation corridor this week.
 The tracked-case ground truth records ten new docket entries between July 7 and July 9 — the highest single-week filing velocity since the plan was filed — landing in the run-up to what the claims administrator listed as a **September 11, 2026** bar date. 
The near-term posture had been described as a "sale-and-confirmation runway": the sale hearing was set for June 16 and confirmation was targeted for July 13, 2026, with the case originating from a liquidity crisis tied to counterparty exposures including the Babel Finance bankruptcy, a failed Nexo equipment-loan settlement, and an unpaid Celsius arbitration award.


The volume of new filings without docket descriptions in the CourtListener feed (entries 504–509 through July 9) is a flag worth watching. In restructurings of this type, a burst of late filings before a confirmation hearing typically reflects one or more of: contested plan objections, final solicitation disclosures, creditor committee position statements, or administrative claims disputes. Until those entries are docketed with descriptions accessible via the Veritaglobal claims portal, counsel for institutional creditors should pull the full docket directly from Bankr. D. Del. and confirm whether any objecting party has introduced a § 1129(a) confirmation challenge.


The Debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 That customer-led NewCo structure — always the more legally complex of the two tracks — has been the pivot point for the § 541 property-of-estate question.

**The Customer-Property Question: § 541 and the Celsius Echo**


The central legal question in BlockFills has never been fully resolved: whether customer digital assets constitute estate property, with the Debtors pursuing a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process.
 This framing — assets in or out of the estate — determines the baseline recovery waterfall for institutional counterparties who deposited crypto with BlockFills under its brokerage and lending arrangements.


The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults, specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy, alongside stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 The debt stack is concentrated not in funded bank debt but in customer and trade exposure. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt — the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


Celsius's lien position — arising from an arbitration award that BlockFills could not satisfy prepetition — creates a direct structural conflict with the Ad Hoc customer group's claim that deposited assets are not estate property. If customers prevail on a trust or bailment theory (analogous to the HKCFI's Gatecoin analysis under Hong Kong law, or the contested TOS litigation in the Celsius Delaware case), Celsius's secured note liens attach only to assets that are unambiguously estate property. The July 13 confirmation target suggests Judge Horan may have been asked to rule on or defer this threshold question. The absence of an adversary proceeding caption in the docket data as of July 9 suggests the parties may be papering around the issue in plan language rather than litigating it — a pattern that invites post-confirmation disputes.

**The Keyrock Sale: $3.25M for a $60B-Volume Franchise**


According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property; as set out in the official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder."
 
A hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026, while parties continued to collaborate on the administrative process; final completion remained subject to final court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.



BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the previous year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients, ranking among the more active desks in the institutional crypto lending and borrowing market.
 The $3.25 million purchase price against that commercial profile reflects what the market actually assigned to a brokerage whose deposits were frozen, whose technology stack is portable but whose trust relationships are impaired, and whose residual claims are contested.


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors comprises seven members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Energy Conversion Group; Fuel Labs Inc.; and three individual creditors, and is represented by Morris James LLP.
 The UCC's position on Keyrock sale proceeds — specifically whether sale consideration flows to the liquidating trust before or after Celsius's secured claim is satisfied — is the immediate creditor-rights pressure point heading into the July 13 confirmation window.

**Bitcoin Depot: Judge Lopez Approves $1.4M Section 363 Sale, Liquidation Plan Set for August 7**


A Texas bankruptcy judge on July 8, 2026, gave bankrupt cryptocurrency kiosk operator Bitcoin Depot permission to sell more than $1.4 million in assets to the winners of an auction held the week prior.
 The matter sits before 
the Honorable Christopher M. Lopez, United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, who heard first-day motions on May 19, 2026.



Bitcoin Depot (NASDAQ: BTM), a U.S.-based Bitcoin ATM operator, initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 process in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas on May 18, 2026, to effect an orderly wind-down of its operations and facilitate a sale of its assets.
 
The Atlanta-based company blamed increasingly stringent state regulations and enforcement for making its business model unsustainable and faces a high-profile lawsuit from the attorneys general of Massachusetts and Iowa over alleged facilitation of crypto scams.


The § 363 sale approved July 8 — $1.4 million from an auction conducted June 29 — represents the first tranche of asset monetization in what is structured as a full wind-down. 
The Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving bidding procedures on June 10, 2026, and the Debtors conducted the auction via video and telephone conference on June 29, 2026, selecting the Winning Bidders.
 
The Debtors filed their Combined Disclosure Statement and Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation on June 18, 2026, filed a First Amended version on June 23, 2026, and the Bankruptcy Court will hold a combined hearing on the Disclosure Statement and confirmation of the Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation on August 7, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. CT.



Vinson & Elkins LLP is serving as legal advisor, Portage Point Partners is serving as restructuring advisor, and Joele Frank, Wilkinson Brimmer Katcher is serving as strategic communications advisor to Bitcoin Depot.
 The $1.4M aggregate sale price against a business that 
had operated over 9,000 kiosk locations globally as of August 2025
 reinforces that the BTM operating model carried no terminal franchise value once the regulatory environment withdrew. The August 7 combined hearing is the next material date for creditors with allowed claims in the liquidation.

**FTX Recovery Trust: Fifth Distribution Targeting July 31 — $600M Reserve Reduction Filed**


FTX Trading Ltd. and the FTX Recovery Trust provided an operational update regarding the next phase of creditor and stakeholder distributions; the anticipated record date for the next distribution was set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026, and payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders also scheduled for July 31.



As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the estate filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reserve reduction — if approved — directly expands the pool available for the fifth distribution and for claim-transfer participants whose transfers cleared the register by the June 16 record date.


The fourth distribution on March 31, 2026, distributed approximately $2.2 billion to creditors under FTX's Chapter 11 plan, lifting recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reached 100 percent, while Class 7 received a cumulative 120 percent.
 
Distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion last September, and a fourth round launched on March 31.


The critical caveat on recovery arithmetic remains unchanged: 
every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.
 The claim-trading aftermarket has fully priced this in — secondary buyers who acquired FTX claims in 2023 at deep discounts have realized strong dollar returns, but original-holder creditors who mark their recovery against current crypto asset values have a different economic outcome. 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 Claim transfers executed after the June 16 cutoff will need to wait for the sixth distribution cycle.
