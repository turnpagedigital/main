### I. BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. — Confirmation Track


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11, and the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.


The docket's last filing date in the live ground-truth data is July 17, 2026 — with zero new entries in the prior 72 hours — which is consistent with a case that has moved through the disclosure-statement phase and is now in the corridor between solicitation close and the next major procedural event. The critical forward date is **September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET)**: 
the claims administrator Veritaglobal lists a September 11, 2026 deadline (4:00 p.m. ET) alongside a June 16, 2026 hearing date (11:00 a.m. ET)
 — the June hearing was the confirmation hearing on the debtors' plan, consistent with the May 28 disclosure statement approval. Creditors awaiting confirmation should confirm whether the September 11 date reflects a voting deadline, a final confirmation hearing, or a supplemental objection cutoff; the Veritaglobal key-dates page is the operative reference.

The case's capital structure is stark. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities filed under Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), and the debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. reports $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities
 — an immediate signal of likely impairment on unsecured claims. 
The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing the framework for a reorganization centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


The customer-property question at the heart of this case traces directly to the February 2026 liquidity event. 
On February 2, 2026, Bitcoin dropped below $80,000 for the first time since April 2025, and BlockFills temporarily suspended certain deposit and withdrawal activity that day in response to liquidity pressure and customer withdrawal requests.
 The causal chain running from withdrawal freeze to Chapter 11 echoes the Celsius TOS-property litigation precisely because BlockFills operated on a custodial model for institutional clients — the § 541 estate-versus-trust/bailment question is live here, though it has not yet generated adversary-proceeding activity visible in public docket summaries.

Balance-sheet deterioration predates the February crash. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.


The unsecured creditors' committee has retained institutional firepower. 
On March 27, 2026, the Office of the United States Trustee for Region 3 filed the Notice of Appointment of Committee of Unsecured Creditors at Docket No. 104; the Committee members are SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
The Committee retained FTI Consulting, Inc. as financial advisors, authorized under § 1103 of the Bankruptcy Code.
 
Entities tied to the bitcoin mining sector hold at least $13.6 million in unsecured claims against the company, including Simple Mining, Bitfarms, and Ashton Soniat, a co-founder of bitcoin mining firm Coinmint.


The plan architecture — NewCo reorganization plus liquidating trust — replicates the Celsius playbook. The central creditor-rights question not yet resolved on the public docket is valuation date: whether customer positions will be valued at petition date (March 15, 2026) in fiat, or at some later reference date reflecting post-petition BTC appreciation. That question is analytically identical to the Genesis/DCG dispute over in-kind versus fiat treatment under § 1129 and will likely produce the sharpest creditor-vs.-debtor contest at confirmation.

### II. MVMT Labs, Inc. (Movement Labs) — Case No. 1:26-bk-11113, Bankr. D. Del. (Subchapter V) — Fresh Filing, Material Pattern Risk

The second Delaware crypto filing of the week is smaller in dollar terms but analytically significant as a pattern-setter for token-issuer insolvency mechanics.


MVMT Labs, Inc., the Delaware corporation historically associated with the Movement blockchain, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, formalizing the collapse of a venture once valued in the billions.
 
The case, styled *In re MVMT Labs, Inc.* (Case No. 1:26-bk-11113), was filed under Subchapter V of Chapter 11, a streamlined reorganization track reserved for smaller businesses, and assigned to the Honorable Thomas M. Horan.


Judge Horan thus has simultaneous jurisdiction over both the $145 million BlockFills general unsecured pool and the sub-$10 million MVMT Labs estate — the symmetry is notable insofar as both cases involve institutional crypto intermediaries whose pre-petition TOS and custody arrangements will determine whether customer digital assets are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or excluded as trust/bailment property.

The petition numbers tell the valuation story in compressed form. 
A company that was reportedly on the cusp of a $3 billion valuation in early 2025 now lists estimated assets of just $100,001 to $500,000, against liabilities of between $1 million and $10 million.
 
The debtor listed between 200 and 999 creditors, with debts described as primarily business in nature; the deadline for creditors to file proofs of claim is September 14, 2026, and a reorganization plan is expected around mid-October under Subchapter V's accelerated timeline.
 That plan deadline resolves to approximately **October 13, 2026**, per the Subchapter V statutory 90-day clock from petition date.

The precipitating event was a market-making arrangement executed at the MOVE token launch. 
In May 2025, Movement Labs suspended co-founder Rushi Manche after announcing an independent investigation into a market-maker agreement linked to Rentech and Web3Port; Binance later said the market maker rapidly sold 66 million MOVE tokens, or about 5% of the total supply, contributing to a sharp decline in the token's price; Coinbase also suspended MOVE trading after saying the token no longer met its listing standards.


The insider/creditor dynamic is notable. 
Court records show 200 to 999 creditors, including Anchorage Digital and state revenue departments; co-founder Rushi Manche holds the largest unsecured claim in the case, at about $1.6 million, while still retaining a large equity stake.
 That posture — largest creditor who is also the largest equity holder — is precisely the fact pattern that triggers § 510(c) equitable subordination analysis and could draw U.S. Trustee scrutiny or committee objection to any plan that preserves Manche's equity position ahead of general unsecured creditors.

Token holders face a distinct problem: 
the MOVE token hit an all-time low of $0.0104, down 94% over the year, and at the time of reporting traded at roughly $0.01064.
 Whether MOVE token holders constitute creditors with allowable proofs of claim under § 101(5) — or merely equity holders with no distribution rights — turns on the nature of the token's legal characterization and the specific representations made in the MOVE white paper and exchange listing disclosures. That analysis has not yet been tested on this docket.


Move Industries, the successor operating entity, has stated it is a separate legal entity and not part of the filing, and that Move Industries is operating normally.
 The structural separation between MVMT Labs (debtor) and Move Industries (non-debtor) is unverified on the public docket and should be stress-tested by any claimant with contractual or token-based claims against the ecosystem: corporate separateness arguments collapse when intercompany transfers, shared IP licenses, or single-brand operations can support substantive consolidation.


The startup raised a $38 million Series A led by Polychain Capital in April 2024, following a $3.4 million pre-seed round, bringing total disclosed equity financing to about $41.4 million.
 The gap between $41.4 million raised and sub-$500,000 in current assets raises a straightforward fraudulent transfer question under § 548 and applicable state law: where did the money go, and over what time period were transfers made to insiders or affiliates? The Subchapter V trustee — once appointed — will be the first party with standing to pursue that inquiry, though Subchapter V's lighter procedural regime may limit the speed and scope of discovery relative to a full committee-driven Chapter 11.

### IV. Structural Pattern — Token-Issuer Insolvency and the § 541 Property Question

Three dockets now active in Delaware and Utah collectively stress-test the same statutory seam: whether crypto assets held by or on behalf of customers are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or excluded by trust, bailment, or constructive-trust theory. The BlockFills NewCo/liquidating-trust architecture presupposes that customer funds can be reorganized through the plan without first resolving that question on the merits — a posture the UCC (with FTI as financial advisor) should contest if customer-asset segregation was promised in pre-petition agreements. The MVMT Labs Subchapter V proceeding compresses that timeline: Subchapter V trustees have broader operational authority than a typical UCC, but the statutory 90-day plan deadline limits the time available to investigate pre-petition transfers before a plan is proposed.

The offshore coordination dimension remains live in legacy cases. The Three Arrows Capital liquidators (BVI/Hong Kong/Singapore) are still pursuing asset recovery across jurisdictions, and the FTX Recovery Trust's adversary proceedings against insiders and counterparties continue to generate cross-border recognition questions under § 1520 and §1521. Neither produced a verified new docket event in this 72-hour window, but both remain material to understanding the recovery environment within which BlockFills' institutional creditors are operating.
