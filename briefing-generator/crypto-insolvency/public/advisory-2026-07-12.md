### I. BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Confirmation Hearing Tomorrow


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtor entities filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The filing disclosed $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The confirmation posture as of today is this: 
the Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and the Confirmation Hearing is set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Disclosure Statement and Solicitation Procedures Order at Docket No. 388, establishing the voting and solicitation framework that has now concluded.
 The docket as of July 11 reflects fifteen new filings in the prior seventy-two hours — the specific descriptions of Dkt. Nos. 510 and 515 were not available in the Verita feed as of this morning, but the volume is consistent with pre-confirmation submissions (reply briefs, voting tabulation certifications, and plan supplement amendments).

The sale process that ran parallel to the plan track has resolved. 
Keyrock, a Brussels-based digital asset services firm, is acquiring BlockFills for $3.25 million, subject to court approval; BlockFills reported between $100 million and $500 million in liabilities against $50 million to $100 million in assets at filing.
 
The sale hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 
The acquisition gives Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, which includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies.
 The $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in unsecured obligations implies a deeply distressed recovery without supplemental plan distributions; the plan's treatment of the shortfall is what tomorrow's hearing will test.

**The structural issue that predates confirmation and will define its legal significance:** 
on January 16, 2026, counterparty 1548199 Alberta Ltd. transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; those assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 
The Debtors note a procedural deficiency: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001(b) for a determination of property of the estate and have not attempted tracing of specific funds. If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property under 11 U.S.C. § 541, they are available to fund the reorganization and be distributed pro rata under the Plan.


This is the exact commingling/§ 541 question the Celsius case litigated for eighteen months — the BlockFills plan appears to resolve it by structural fiat (treating customer assets as estate property and distributing pro rata) rather than through adversary-proceeding adjudication. Whether objecting customers who appear at tomorrow's hearing can re-open that question through a § 1129 objection is the core confirmation risk. Creditor-side professionals should arrive with that argument fully briefed.


On March 27, 2026, the U.S. Trustee for Region 3 filed the Notice of Appointment of the Committee of Unsecured Creditors at Docket No. 104. The Committee members are: SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
FTI Consulting, Inc. was retained as financial advisor for the Committee, effective April 1, 2026.
 The Committee's pre-confirmation investigation record — visible through FTI fee filings — will inform any § 1129(a)(7) "best interests" objection.

**Background on the liquidity crisis:** 
BlockFills attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius. Stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials compounded these pressures.
 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.
The plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and the courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.

### II. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Set for July 31, 2026 (FTX Trading Ltd., Bankr. D. Del. No. 22-11068)


FTX Trading Ltd. and the FTX Recovery Trust have confirmed that the record date for the next distribution was set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
Four distributions have already been made, the latest on March 31, 2026. The fifth is set for July 31, 2026; the FTX Recovery Trust has not yet disclosed the specific distribution amount.



As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reserve reduction is the mechanical lever that unlocks capital for the July 31 distribution — the amount flowing to creditors tracks directly to claims reconciled and removed from the reserve.


The March 31 fourth distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reached 100 percent, while Class 7 received a cumulative 120 percent.
 
Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan became eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026.


The petition-date valuation dispute remains structurally live even as nominal recovery percentages reach 100%+. 
All payouts are based on crypto values from November 2022 — not current prices. Bitcoin was approximately $16,871 at that time.
 
The estate's counterargument is that bankruptcy-date valuations provide a legally consistent baseline.
 For transferred-claim holders in the secondary market, the more immediate operational risk is settlement timing: 
for holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 Any transfer not cleared by June 16 misses the July 31 distribution entirely.

On the litigation-recovery side, 
Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion; Genesis is pushing to dismiss the suit, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.
 
Upcoming omnibus hearings are scheduled at the FTX docket for July 23, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. ET and August 19, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. ET.

### III. Genesis Global Holdco / DCG — Wind-Down Adversary Docket Active (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 23-10063)

Genesis's primary restructuring closed in August 2024. 
On the plan effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the Chapter 11 plan confirmed by Judge Sean Lane of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on May 21, 2024. That distribution added to the approximately $2.2 billion Genesis had distributed to Gemini Earn users between May and June 2024.


What distinguishes Genesis from the FTX/Celsius/BlockFills cohort is its in-kind distribution structure: 
unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition-date value; instead, Genesis sought restitution through the Chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the New York Attorney General, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 
As part of Genesis's bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action against various third parties, including parent company DCG. The fund comprises $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.


The adversary docket against DCG remains active and is the primary watch item for the Genesis wind-down estate. 
Active adversary proceedings include *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053, and a parallel proceeding at No. 26-03230.
 
A hearing on a motion to dismiss was held March 25, 2026 at 2:00 p.m. ET.
 
A pre-trial conference was held June 9, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 
A Wind-Down Oversight Committee creditor town hall was held July 7, 2026, and a July 15 omnibus hearing is scheduled.
 The $33 million adversary proceeding against DCG seeking repayment of amounts paid to Three Arrows Capital remains one of the active subsidiary claims within the litigation fund's mandate.

The pattern-recognition point across Genesis and BlockFills: when a crypto estate's plan rests on an in-kind / coin-for-coin distribution mechanism (Genesis), customer recovery scales with asset appreciation; when it rests on fiat-value-at-petition treatment (FTX, and implicitly BlockFills through its pro-rata cash distribution), customers who held appreciating tokens absorb the valuation loss in silence. Confirmation of BlockFills' plan tomorrow crystallizes that choice for BlockFills customers without adversary-proceeding adjudication.
