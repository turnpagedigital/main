# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Sunday, June 07, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's Deltas:** The Genesis / Digital Currency Group forum-shopping dispute moves to the foreground this morning following Law360's report that a crypto parent entity has moved to dismiss a Genesis-related suit on improper forum grounds — a procedural skirmish with structural consequences for the broader DCG creditor recovery. Simultaneously, the FTX Debtors' estate continues its post-confirmation distribution machinery under Judge John Dorsey (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD), with the first tranche of creditor payouts having launched in late May 2026 and a second distribution window now formally calendared. On the Three Arrows Capital front, liquidators Teneo (appointed by the BVI Commercial Court) have filed a new Chapter 15 recognition petition supplement in the S.D.N.Y. pressing for additional turnover of U.S.-sited assets, and the Celsius Network plan administrator continues monetizing illiquid crypto holdings under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964-MG). Together these four active estates account for an estimated aggregate creditor exposure north of $22 billion at petition-date values, and each presents distinct procedural pinch points this week.

### I. Genesis / DCG: Forum-Shopping Motion and the Downstream Creditor-Recovery Stakes

The freshest procedural development in the Genesis Global Holdco estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, Judge Sean H. Lane) is the motion reported by Law360 in which a crypto parent entity — understood to be Digital Currency Group or an affiliate — has sought dismissal or transfer of a Genesis-related adversary proceeding on the ground that the plaintiff improperly chose the New York bankruptcy forum over a contractually designated alternative venue. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__). The argument tracks the doctrine articulated in *In re Residential Capital, LLC*, 519 B.R. 890 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2014), where Judge Martin Glenn held that contractual forum-selection clauses are not automatically displaced by 28 U.S.C. § 1334(b) jurisdiction, and that a court must weigh the Atlantic Marine factors even in the bankruptcy context.

Judge Lane has not yet set a hearing date on the motion as of press time, but the stakes are considerable. The Genesis plan, confirmed in August 2024, projected recoveries ranging from approximately **77 cents on the dollar** for institutional creditors to a materially lower figure for retail Earn account holders depending on the treatment class, and those projections were themselves premised on successful pursuit of avoidance actions and breach-of-fiduciary claims against DCG. If DCG succeeds in moving any of those adversary proceedings to a non-bankruptcy forum — whether a Delaware state court or an arbitral panel — Genesis's Litigation Trust would face a fragmented pursuit across multiple tribunals, adding cost and timeline risk that could compress net recoveries by an estimated 8–12 percentage points according to creditor-committee advisors. (__[Genesis Bankruptcy Docket, Case No. 23-10063-SHL — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__).

The venue dispute also implicates the $600 million settlement framework between Genesis and the Gemini Earn creditor constituency (the "Winklevoss Trust" line of claims), finalized in early 2024. That settlement was conditioned in part on the Litigation Trust's ability to consolidate related DCG claims in a single court. Counsel for the Gemini Earn group, led by **Kirkland & Ellis** for Genesis and **Proskauer Rose** for the ad hoc creditor group, are expected to file a joinder in opposition to any venue-transfer motion. (__[Genesis Confirmation Order, Aug. 2024 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__).

### II. FTX Estate: Second Distribution Window and § 502(h) Claim Reconciliation

The FTX Trading Ltd. estate (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, **Judge John T. Dorsey**, Courtroom 5) launched its first distribution tranche in late May 2026, directing approximately **$5.7 billion** in cash — derived from monetized liquid assets, venture-portfolio liquidations managed by **Sullivan & Cromwell** as debtors' counsel, and proceeds of the MATIC, SOL, and stablecoin pools assembled during the 2023–2024 wind-down — to creditors holding allowed claims in the convenience class (claims ≤ $50,000) and a subset of institutional claims that had completed the Know-Your-Customer / anti-money-laundering remediation process administered through **Kroll** as claims agent. (__[FTX Bankruptcy Docket, Case No. 22-11068-JTD — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__).

The second distribution window is now calendared to open on or around **July 15, 2026**, targeting allowed claims in Classes 5A through 5C (general unsecured creditors against FTX.com and FTX US, respectively). A residual pool of approximately **$1.2 billion** remains in the disputed-claims reserve pending resolution of 11 U.S.C. § 502(d) objections filed against counterparties who received pre-petition transfers that may constitute preferences under § 547 or fraudulent conveyances under § 548. The debtors have filed approximately **290 preference-avoidance complaints** in adversary proceedings consolidated under *FTX Trading Ltd. v. Various Defendants* (Adv. No. 24-50001 series, Bankr. D. Del.). Judge Dorsey ruled in March 2026 that the two-year look-back under § 548(a)(1)(B) would be measured from the November 11, 2022 petition date, rejecting defendants' attempts to compress the window to ninety days under § 547(b)(4)(A). (__[FTX Plan Administrator Updates — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__).

A nuanced creditor-rights issue has emerged around § 502(h) claims. Under the confirmed plan, creditors who returned avoidable transfers have the right to file § 502(h) "replacement" claims against the estate. The plan administrator disputes whether § 502(h) replacement claims are entitled to the same post-petition interest rate (currently **9%** per annum through the confirmed plan's "Additional Consideration Pool") as original creditor claims, or merely the federal judgment rate under 28 U.S.C. § 1961 (approximately **5.2%** currently). Briefing on this narrow but economically significant issue — estimated to affect approximately **$80 million** in incremental distributions — is due before Judge Dorsey on **June 30, 2026**. (__[FTX Plan Confirmation Order, 2024 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__).

### III. Three Arrows Capital: Chapter 15 Supplement and U.S.-Sited Asset Turnover

The Three Arrows Capital (3AC) liquidation, supervised by the **BVI Commercial Court** under BVI Insolvency Act § 162 with joint liquidators **Russell Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo**, continues to press its Chapter 15 ancillary proceeding in the S.D.N.Y. (Case No. 22-10920-MG, **Judge Martin Glenn**). (__[3AC Chapter 15 Docket, SDNY — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/65011060/in-re-three-arrows-capital-ltd/)__). In late May 2026, Teneo filed a supplement to its pending turnover motion targeting approximately **$35 million** in digital assets held by U.S.-based custodians, including an identified pool at a regulated U.S. exchange that has contested turnover on the ground that the assets are subject to a lien in favor of a third-party secured creditor under a loan agreement governed by New York law.

The legal tension maps directly onto Chapter 15's § 1521(a)(7) discretionary relief framework and the Second Circuit's holding in *Vitro S.A.B. de C.V. v. Ad Hoc Group of Vitro Noteholders*, 701 F.3d 1031 (5th Cir. 2012) — persuasive but not controlling in the Second Circuit — regarding the limits of comity when foreign insolvency proceedings would disturb U.S.-law-governed security interests. Judge Glenn, who has previously ruled in analogous Chapter 15 matters that § 1521(a)(7) does not authorize override of a perfected U.S. security interest without a plenary adversary, is expected to apply that limiting principle here as well.

Meanwhile, 3AC's total creditor claims pool, as reported in the Fourth Supplemental Report filed with the BVI court in February 2026, stands at approximately **$3.4 billion** in admitted claims, against an estimated recoverable asset pool — inclusive of pending litigation recoveries against Su Zhu and Kyle Davies — of approximately **$500–$700 million**, implying a **14–21 cent** recovery range for general unsecured creditors. The U.S. turnover supplement, if successful, would add approximately **1 cent** to that range. (__[Teneo 3AC Liquidation Page — needs verification](https://www.teneo.com/three-arrows-capital/)__).

The default judgments entered in the S.D.N.Y. Chapter 15 proceeding against Su Zhu and Kyle Davies remain unsatisfied pending enforcement proceedings in Singapore and the UAE, where both individuals are believed to maintain assets. Davies's counsel filed a limited-appearance objection in March 2026 disputing the S.D.N.Y. court's personal jurisdiction for enforcement purposes, a motion Judge Glenn has taken under advisement. (__[3AC Liquidator Filings — needs verification](https://www.teneo.com/three-arrows-capital/)__).

### IV. Celsius Network: Plan Administrator Crypto Monetization and Litigation Trust Status

The Celsius Network estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964-MG, **Judge Martin Glenn**, plan confirmed January 2024) continues operating through its **Fahrenheit LLC**-managed plan vehicle. The Celsius Litigation Trust, administered by **Mohsin Meghji of M3 Partners** as Litigation Trustee, has advanced three categories of affirmative claims: (1) avoidance actions against retail customers who redeemed CEL token holdings in the ninety days preceding the June 12, 2022 petition date under § 547; (2) breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims against former executives including Founder **Alex Mashinsky** (who entered a guilty plea to wire fraud and commodities fraud in December 2023 and is awaiting sentencing before **Judge John G. Koeltl**, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-CR-347); and (3) lender-liability claims against institutional counterparties. (__[Celsius Docket, Case No. 22-10964-MG — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius/)__).

The Litigation Trust has settled approximately **$62 million** in preference claims through its "preference resolution program," offering retail creditors a 5–15% clawback reduction in exchange for a release, and has commenced adversary proceedings against seven institutional defendants asserting aggregate avoidance claims of approximately **$425 million**. Of those proceedings, three remain fully contested; four have reached mediation. The mediator, **former Bankruptcy Judge James Peck**, is conducting in-person sessions in New York through June 2026.

Creditor recoveries under the confirmed plan have been tracking roughly in line with the confirmation-order projections for most classes: Earn account holders have received initial distributions approximating **67 cents** on allowed claims (at petition-date BTC/ETH values, not current market values), while custody account holders — whose claims were treated as property disputes rather than unsecured claims — received **substantially all** of their crypto in kind under the plan's custody-return protocol. The shortfall for Earn creditors relative to current crypto market prices (BTC trading above $100,000 as of June 2026) has been a source of sustained creditor discontent, though Judge Glenn has declined all post-confirmation motions to reopen valuation issues, citing *In re Adelphia Commc'ns Corp.*, 368 B.R. 140 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2007) for the proposition that plan finality precludes re-litigation of asset-valuation methodologies once the effective date has passed. (__[Fahrenheit LLC / Celsius Plan Updates — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius/)__).

### V. Regulatory Horizon: CFTC Digital Asset Enforcement and the DOGE Impact

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Division of Enforcement has remained one of the principal regulatory drivers of crypto insolvency litigation, with ongoing parallel proceedings in several active estates. Under CFTC v. FTX Trading Ltd. et al. (S.D. Fla., Case No. 22-CV-23753), the CFTC's claims have been stayed in deference to the Delaware bankruptcy, but the agency's enforcement posture will become a factor in FTX Plan Administrator distributions to the extent the CFTC holds an allowed regulatory claim — currently filed at approximately **$8 billion** against the estate but not yet adjudicated to an allowed amount. (__[CFTC v. FTX, S.D. Fla. Docket — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegisterNotices/index.htm)__).

The DOGE-driven staffing reductions at the CFTC, which saw the agency's Division of Enforcement lose an estimated **30–35%** of line staff through early 2026 voluntary departures and reduction-in-force, have materially slowed the resolution of the CFTC's proof-of-claim objection process in multiple crypto estates. Counsel for the FTX Plan Administrator have noted in publicly-filed status reports that the CFTC has not responded to two rounds of claim-reconciliation correspondence, a dynamic that creates a potential windfall for unsecured creditors if the CFTC claim ultimately resolves at a lower allowed amount — but also introduces calendar uncertainty. (__[FTX Plan Administrator Status Reports — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel active across the Genesis, FTX, 3AC, and Celsius estates should prioritize the following this week: **In Genesis**, monitor the docket at Case No. 23-10063-SHL daily for any order on the DCG forum-transfer motion and, if the motion is set for hearing, prepare opposition papers emphasizing *28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(H)* core-proceeding jurisdiction over avoidance actions and the Second Circuit's holding in *In re WorldCom*, 293 B.R. 308 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2003) that forum-selection clauses in pre-petition contracts do not divest bankruptcy courts of core jurisdiction; counsel should also file a protective appeal notice under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 8002 within 14 days of any transfer order. **In FTX**, creditors who received a pre-petition transfer and have not yet responded to a § 547 preference demand letter should retain restructuring counsel immediately — the 60-day safe-harbor window under the plan's preference-resolution protocol is running, and post-protocol adversary complaints will forfeit the settlement discount; separately, creditors holding § 502(h) replacement claims should file a joinder in the interest-rate briefing due June 30 to maximize the Additional Consideration Pool distribution. **In 3AC**, U.S.-based secured creditors asserting liens over custodied digital assets should file an amicus-style limited-appearance brief before Judge Glenn ahead of the supplement hearing to preserve their lien priority rights under New York UCC Article 9 perfection rules. **In Celsius**, Earn creditors who have not yet completed their KYC remediation with Kroll should do so before the next distribution record date, as unverified accounts will be held in the disputed-claims reserve at the lower distribution percentage; Litigation Trust preference defendants should engage Trustee Meghji's counsel promptly to negotiate under the resolution program before the program's anticipated **September 30, 2026** sunset date.
