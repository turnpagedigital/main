### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. — Bankr. D. Del. 26-10371 (TMH))

**Sale approved; confirmation four days out.** 
On June 16, 2026, Judge Horan approved the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 The buyer is Keyrock SA. 
According to the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
The acquisition gives Keyrock access to around 2,000 institutional clients, along with BlockFills' technology and intellectual property.


The sale order clears the way for confirmation, though it does not resolve the case's most consequential creditor-rights question: whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate. 
The plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a Section 363 competitive sale process.
 With the Keyrock sale now court-approved, the NewCo toggle has effectively collapsed; what remains is plan confirmation over the liquidating trust structure.

**The § 541 commingling problem remains live.** This question was not resolved at the June 16 sale hearing and carries directly into July 13 confirmation. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
On January 16, 2026, Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 The Debtors also flagged a procedural barrier for objectors: 
the objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds.


This is the core creditor-property dispute the beat tracks across every generation of crypto insolvencies — the same question that drove years of contested TOS litigation in Celsius and that HKCFI addressed in *Gatecoin* (HKCFI 914). If Judge Horan accepts the estate-property theory at confirmation, unsecured claimants receive pro rata treatment under 11 U.S.C. § 1129; if a customer successfully argues in-kind title through an adversary proceeding, that claimant jumps ahead of the general estate. No adversary has been filed as of this writing, meaning that tracing argument is likely waived for the confirmation record.

**Capital structure.** 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that sits the $3.25 million Keyrock sale price — a recovery rate on enterprise value that is deeply distressed. Unsecured creditor recoveries under a liquidating trust will depend entirely on what the plan's liquidating trust realizes from remaining assets and litigation proceeds.

**Genesis/Celsius connection.** 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis in part to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 That Celsius arbitration award is the same secured Celsius claim now sitting at the top of the BlockFills capital structure, demonstrating how the 2022–2023 contagion wave continues generating downstream obligor effects in 2026.

**Opt-out release dispute.** 
The Delaware bankruptcy judge granted conditional approval to the Chapter 11 plan disclosure statement, saying issues over the opt-out release mechanism in the proposal can be argued at the confirmation hearing.
 That deferred dispute lands on July 13. Opt-out releases have been an active fault line in Delaware confirmation practice post-*Purdue*, and Judge Horan's ruling on the mechanism will directly affect how broadly non-debtor parties — including BlockFills' directors and officers — are insulated from customer fraud claims.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution & Clawback Posture

**Fifth distribution queued for July 31.** 
The anticipated record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims and interests has been set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan became eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026.
 
As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the estate filed an amended notice seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.


**Cumulative recovery context.** 
Four distributions have been made, the latest on March 31, 2026. The fifth is set for July 31, 2026 (record date June 16, 2026).
 
The fourth distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reach 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent.
 By mid-2026, the estate had distributed approximately $10 billion across four rounds. 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 That deadline has now passed; claim transfers recorded after June 16 do not qualify for the fifth distribution.

**The $1.15 billion Genesis Digital clawback is gone.** 
The FTX Recovery Trust voluntarily dismissed its lawsuit against Bitcoin mining company Genesis Digital Assets Ltd., ditching its attempt to gain back $1.15 billion in funds it said were fraudulently transferred. The trust dropped all claims with prejudice, according to a filing in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The lawsuit had alleged Genesis Digital, along with its affiliates and co-founders, received commingled and misappropriated funds from FTX co-founder Sam Bankman-Fried between August 2021 and April 2022, with Bankman-Fried allegedly using Alameda Research to purchase tranches of shares in the Bitcoin mining company at heavily inflated prices.
 Dismissal with prejudice means the estate cannot refile. The recovery shortfall that dismissal creates is partially offset by the reserve reduction, but the structural question — whether the petition-date valuation methodology forecloses meaningful recovery for creditors who held appreciating crypto — persists.

**Petition-date valuation tension.** 
The 119% recovery figure is pegged to November 11, 2022. Since the petition date, Bitcoin has surged by over 500%, and Solana has seen even more dramatic gains. Creditors are essentially receiving principal plus interest in USD, but have missed out on the historic appreciation of the underlying digital assets.
 The FTX approach — dollar-denominated petition-date cap — stands in direct contrast to the Genesis model. 
Virtually all crypto debtors "dollarized" claims and capped crypto claims at petition date values; Genesis, however, obtained court approval of a Chapter 11 plan that provided "in-kind" recoveries without capping claims at their petition date values.
 The *Genesis* anomaly remains the exception; FTX is the rule.

### Celsius — Fourth Distribution in Progress


On January 22, 2026, Celsius announced the fourth distribution, projected to begin in February 2026, allocating $344.4 million to eligible creditors.
 
This distribution is the result of approximately $257 million from the Tether Adversary Proceeding settlement, $73.7 million in assets associated with claims released from the Disputed and Contingent Claims Reserve, and $9.4 million from forfeited claims.
 
This is expected to be the final distribution made in BTC for eligible creditors, as Celsius plans to transition future distributions to U.S. dollars and stablecoins.
 The transition away from in-kind crypto distribution is a material change in creditor economics for those who expected coin-for-coin recovery; it also forecloses any upside from continued crypto appreciation post-distribution date.

Eligible classes include Class 2 (Retail Borrower Deposit), Class 5 (General Earn), Class 7 (Withhold), Class 8 (Unsecured Loan), and Class 9 (General Unsecured). 
Creditors with Class 4 Convenience Claims, Class 6A General Custody Claims, Class 6B Withdrawable Custody Claims, and Class 11 De Minimis Claims are not eligible for this distribution.
 KYC completion remains a prerequisite; creditors who have not completed AML/KYC through the Celsius Claims Portal should do so immediately — Celsius noted rolling distribution attempts continue on an ongoing basis.

### Cross-Case Pattern Recognition: The Commingling-to-Confirmation Arc

The BlockFills confirmation on July 13 crystallizes a pattern that has now repeated across every major post-2022 crypto Chapter 11. At filing, the debtor asserts estate-property status over commingled customer assets (§ 541); customers resist and attempt tracing claims (§ 541(d), state-law trust or bailment theories, FRBP 7001(b) adversary); the court typically defers the tracing question absent a filed adversary proceeding; and the plan proceeds on the estate-property assumption. In BlockFills, the adversary was never filed. In Celsius, the TOS litigation ran a lengthy parallel track but ultimately the estate-property theory prevailed for most earn account holders. The institutional clients of BlockFills — hedge funds, broker-dealers, and high-net-worth counterparties who deposited digital assets into the commingled "one balance sheet" — are in the same doctrinal position Celsius earn creditors were in before Judge Martin Glenn's ruling. That reading of the trajectory suggests unsecured pro-rata treatment at confirmation, not in-kind recovery.
