### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Sale Approved; Confirmation Runway Opens


A Delaware bankruptcy judge on June 16, 2026, agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.


The acquirer is Keyrock SA, a Brussels-based digital asset services firm. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 Keyrock was declared the successful bidder in a court document filed 
on May 26, 2026.


The creditor-recovery math is stark. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt. The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 A $3.25 million gross purchase price — against liabilities the petition schedules placed at $100 million to $500 million — signals a recovery environment for unsecured creditors that will depend almost entirely on plan treatment, litigation recoveries, and avoidance-action proceeds rather than the § 363 sale itself.

The dual-track restructuring has been coordinated throughout. 
Reliz has been operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with a sale hearing set for June 16, 2026 and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.


The case has an active adversary-proceeding posture. 
An adversary proceeding was filed in April 2026 as Case 26-50230-TMH against Artha Investment Partners LLC under the jointly administered Case No. 26-10371 (TMH).
 That proceeding is one of multiple pending; the docket also reflects Adv. Pro. No. 26-50224 (TMH), initiated in late March 2026. 
The U.S. Trustee for Region 3 filed the notice of appointment of the Committee of Unsecured Creditors on March 27, 2026 at Docket No. 104. The Committee members are: SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd; Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 The Committee retained FTI Consulting as its financial advisor effective April 1, 2026. (__[Veritaglobal.net — Docket No. 264, BlockFills Case No. 26-10371](https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260429000000000006)__)

Creditors and claim traders need to hold this procedural sequence clearly: court approval of the § 363 sale under 11 U.S.C. § 363(b) is distinct from plan confirmation under § 1129. The sale resolves disposition of operating assets; the plan will govern treatment of allowed unsecured claims, including the large customer book. Absent a robust plan recovery mechanism — funded by avoidance-action proceeds or post-closing litigation — holders of customer and trade claims face a material impairment at any plausible recovery rate against the $145 million unsecured pool.

Two pre-petition customer actions shadow the confirmation process. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026) and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026). The plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and the courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 The automatic stay displaced those TROs upon filing; their disposition in the plan and adversary proceedings will define whether D&O claims survive for creditor benefit.


The proof-of-claim bar date is September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET).
 Claims transferees should confirm their Rule 3001(e) transfer notices are docketed and processed before any future claims-register record date tied to plan distributions.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution (July 31) and Claims-Register Record Date (June 16) Now Passed

The FTX case, *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (JTD), Bankr. D. Del., moves to its fifth distribution phase this month with the controlling record date already behind us.


The anticipated record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims and interests has been set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan will become eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026.


This is the fifth tranche in a structured, phased program. Cumulatively: 
distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion last September, and a fourth round launched on March 31.
 The March 31 fourth distribution was $2.2 billion. 
In total, roughly $10 billion has already gone back to creditors. The next, fifth distribution is set for July 31, 2026 (record date June 16, 2026); the Recovery Trust has not yet disclosed the amount.



The fourth distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reach 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent.
 
The main classes — 5B and 6A/6B — are at 100%, and the small convenience claims (Class 7) are at 120%. Class 5A, the Dotcom group, has been paid about 96% of principal so far, with a projected total of around 118–120% once the same 9% annual interest is added — paid in stages.
 The critical valuation caveat remains operative: 
every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.


The estate also moved aggressively on reserve reduction. 
As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the entity filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reduction, if approved, will directly expand the pool available for the July 31 tranche.

For claim transferees, the operative timing rule is exacting: 
for a transferred claim, the distribution goes to the transferee only if the transfer has been processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date, and the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 Any transfer filed after that record date will be reflected in the sixth distribution, not the fifth. The omnibus hearing schedule shows the next calendared hearing is July 23, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. ET. (__[Kroll Restructuring — FTX Case Calendar](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/ftx/)__)

Ongoing FTX clawback litigation continues to run parallel to distributions. 
Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion. The case is part of the broader clawback strategy — not just identifying what assets remain, but challenging transfers made before the exchange's collapse. Genesis is pushing to dismiss the suit, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.

### Genesis Global Holdco — Wind-Down Adversary Posture; DCG Litigation Active

*Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al.*, Case No. 23-10063 (SHL), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., is now in active wind-down following its August 2, 2024 effective date, with the estate overseen by Plan Administrator Mark Renzi and coordinated litigation managed through the Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) and Litigation Oversight Committee (LOC). (__[Kroll — Genesis Wind-Down](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__)


The WDOC and the LOC have retained Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP and Selendy Gay PLLC respectively as legal advisors. From the Effective Date through the present, the Wind-Down Debtors have made four distributions to creditors pursuant to the Plan.


The active adversary docket is extensive and consequential for ongoing claim-recovery analysis: 
Case No. 23-10063 carries active adversary proceedings including *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230.
 That case — the estates' affirmative action against DCG — sits alongside DCG's own adversary proceeding against Genesis Global Capital (Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129), insider-defendant actions at Adv. Pro. Nos. 25-01097 and 25-01028, and a legacy DCG loan recovery action at Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.

The Genesis plan's valuation methodology remains the most consequential precedent from the 2022–2024 crypto-bankruptcy cohort for current recovery analysis. 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value. Instead, Genesis sought to provide restitution to creditors through the chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the New York Attorney General, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 
Breaking new ground, the decision allows creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to the value as of the petition date. As a result of the decision, Genesis is authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors, even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 That approach — which contrasts sharply with the petition-date-value cap applied in FTX — remains the analytical pole star for any claim-holder evaluating in-kind vs. fiat recovery optionality in active cases.

### Cross-Case Pattern: Valuation-Date Tension and § 541 Customer-Property Claims

The central doctrinal tension persisting across BlockFills, FTX, and Genesis — and foregrounded in the prior day's advisory — has not resolved. The operative question under 11 U.S.C. § 541 is whether customer crypto deposits constitute property of the estate or are held in trust or bailment, placing them outside the estate's reach. In BlockFills, the terms of service dispute closely tracks the Celsius / *In re Celsius Network LLC* (22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) line, where the presence of lending and "rewards" program agreements created a strong argument for estate inclusion. The BlockFills petition record characterized the debtors as an institutional brokerage running collateralized lending, OTC execution, and derivatives services — businesses where segregation of customer assets was operationally unsupported, much as in Celsius.

The FTX model resolved this question through its plan: all customer property was treated as estate property, distributed at petition-date values in fiat, with the plan providing a surplus recovery above 100% as a backstop. Genesis took the opposite valuation approach (in-kind at current value) but similarly treated assets as estate property for distribution purposes. BlockFills creditors evaluating their § 541 positioning should take little comfort from either precedent — both estates distributed on an estate-property basis. The only live trust/bailment arguments in BlockFills arise from specific earmarking or segregation commitments in individual customer agreements, which the Committee and its FTI financial advisor are presumably evaluating.
