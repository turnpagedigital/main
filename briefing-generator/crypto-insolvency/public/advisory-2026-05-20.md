# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Wednesday, May 20, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas in the crypto insolvency docket: the FTX Reorganized Debtor trust distributed its second tranche of convenience-class payments overnight, with aggregate disbursements now confirmed above the $1.2 billion threshold for the first rolling window; the Celsius Network liquidating trust filed a supplemental objection in the Southern District of New York targeting a cluster of institutional counterparty claims it characterizes as duplicative preference-recovery setoffs; and the Voyager Digital estate's wind-down administrator filed a motion seeking approval of a $484 million final distribution to general unsecured creditors, closing out what has become one of the cleaner Chapter 11 wind-downs in the 2022 crypto-collapse cohort. Offshore, the BVI liquidators of Three Arrows Capital (3AC) published their Q1 2026 progress report disclosing total recoveries to date of approximately $490 million against a claims pool that peaked above $3.5 billion, a recovery rate that continues to trail early creditor expectations. Each of these developments is analyzed in full below, together with continuing coverage of the Genesis Global Capital plan consummation, the Terraform Labs SEC disgorgement enforcement, and ongoing litigation in the BlockFi/FTX cross-claim corridor.

### FTX Reorganized Debtor — Second Tranche Distributions and Claim-Portal Mechanics

The FTX Reorganized Debtor trust (administered post-confirmation under the Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan confirmed by Judge John Dorsey, U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, Case No. 22-11068 (JTD)) executed its second scheduled distribution to holders of allowed convenience-class claims on the night of May 19–20, 2026 (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/05/20/ftx-second-tranche-distribution/)__). The convenience-class pool, comprising claims between $1 and $50,000 valued at petition-date prices, was projected in the confirmation order to receive 118 cents on the dollar in the aggregate, reflecting the estate's remarkable post-confirmation liquidity position built on the liquidation of Anthropic equity, locked venture positions, and the FTT token recapture program (__[Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-plan-confirmation-anthropic-equity-recovery-2025)__).

According to the trust's published payment dashboard, cumulative disbursements across the first two tranches now exceed $1.2 billion, with approximately 97% of eligible convenience-class creditors having completed KYC/AML verification through the Kroll portal (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/ftx-distributions-pass-1-billion-2026-05-20/)__). Remaining holdbacks relate primarily to creditors domiciled in jurisdictions subject to OFAC designation or where local regulatory approval for crypto-to-fiat conversion has not been obtained — a category the trust counsel at Sullivan & Cromwell has flagged as likely requiring a supplemental distribution protocol. Judge Dorsey has set a June 17, 2026 status conference to address the holdback disposition and the timeline for the first distribution to general unsecured creditors, whose recovery is projected in the disclosure statement at 100 cents plus post-confirmation interest accruing at the Federal Judgment Rate from the petition date (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-dorsey-june-status-conference-2026)__).

The mechanics of the general-unsecured distribution remain contested. The Ad Hoc Committee of Non-U.S. Customers (represented by Hogan Lovells) has renewed its objection to the trust's proposed FX conversion methodology, arguing that using the petition-date USD value of non-USD denominated claims systematically undercompensates creditors whose home-currency purchasing power declined against the dollar during the pendency of the case (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-fx-conversion-objection-2026)__). The trust has opposed, citing the plain language of 11 U.S.C. § 502(b) and the confirmed plan's USD-denomination election, and the dispute has been fully briefed ahead of the June 17 hearing.

### Celsius Network — Liquidating Trust Preference Litigation and Institutional Claim Objections

The Celsius Network Liquidating Trust, operating under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan administered in the Southern District of New York (Case No. 22-10964 (MG), Judge Martin Glenn) filed on May 16, 2026 a supplemental objection (Dkt. 7,841) targeting 23 institutional counterparty claims aggregating approximately $318 million (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-liquidating-trust-institutional-claim-objections-2026)__). The objection characterizes these claims as attempts by institutional lenders to assert gross-notional balances without netting against amounts owed to the estate under pre-petition crypto-lending agreements — a setoff dispute that implicates 11 U.S.C. § 553 and the forward-contract safe-harbor provisions of 11 U.S.C. § 546(e) and § 556.

The trust, represented by Kirkland & Ellis, argues that the institutional counterparties' pre-petition agreements with Celsius constituted unregistered securities lending arrangements subject to avoidance under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and the applicable two-year look-back window, and that any allowed claim must be subordinated under 11 U.S.C. § 510(b) to the extent the underlying transaction reflects an equity-like return profile (__[Kirkland & Ellis Client Alert — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/alert/celsius-institutional-claims-2026)__). The § 510(b) subordination theory, if sustained, could eliminate or substantially reduce the institutional creditors' standing to receive distributions ahead of retail account holders — a result that would benefit the retail creditor constituency, which has been the plan's political center of gravity throughout the case.

Separately, the trust has now commenced 47 adversary proceedings seeking to recover preferential transfers under 11 U.S.C. § 547, targeting withdrawals of $100,000 or more made in the 90-day window before the June 12, 2022 withdrawal freeze (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/celsius-preference-suits-2026-05/)__). Aggregate preference exposure across filed adversaries is approximately $210 million. Judge Glenn has consolidated the adversaries into three scheduling tracks by defendant category (retail insiders, institutional counterparties, and affiliated entities), with the institutional track set for summary-judgment briefing beginning September 2026.

### Voyager Digital — Final Distribution Motion and Wind-Down Completion

The Voyager Digital wind-down administrator, operating post-confirmation under the Third Amended Chapter 11 Plan (Case No. 22-10943 (MEW), Judge Michael Wiles, S.D.N.Y.), filed on May 19, 2026 a motion for approval of a final distribution of approximately $484 million to holders of allowed general unsecured claims (Dkt. 1,512) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/voyager-final-distribution-motion-2026)__). The proposed distribution would bring aggregate estate payments to general unsecured creditors to roughly 36 cents on the dollar, a figure that represents a modest but meaningful improvement over the 30–34 cent range disclosed in the plan's liquidation analysis, due primarily to above-projection recoveries on Voyager's residual VGX token position and a favorable resolution of its FTX administrative-expense claim.

The Voyager estate's FTX cross-claim — arising from FTX's pre-petition acquisition agreement that was terminated when FTX itself filed for bankruptcy in November 2022 — was resolved in early 2026 for a net allowed general unsecured claim of approximately $445 million against the FTX estate, which at projected FTX recovery rates translates to a nominal recovery of roughly $445 million at 100 cents (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/voyager-ftx-cross-claim-resolution-2026)__). The Voyager estate's ability to monetize that FTX claim will depend on the timing and quantum of the FTX general-unsecured distribution. Administrator counsel at McDermott Will & Emery has proposed a two-step final distribution: an immediate distribution of liquid assets on hand, followed by a deferred supplemental distribution once the FTX proceeds are received.

Judge Wiles has scheduled a hearing on the final distribution motion for June 9, 2026. No objections have been filed as of the date of this advisory, though the Unsecured Creditors' Committee's counsel (Kramer Levin) has reserved rights pending review of the administrator's final reconciliation of disputed claims (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/voyager-final-distribution-hearing-june-2026/)__).

### Three Arrows Capital — BVI Liquidation Progress and Cross-Border Enforcement

The BVI liquidators of Three Arrows Capital Ltd. (liquidators Russel Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo, appointed by the Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court, British Virgin Islands Commercial Division) published their Q1 2026 progress report on May 15, 2026, disclosing total recoveries to date of approximately $490 million against a filed-claims pool that peaked above $3.5 billion (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/post/3ac-bvi-liquidation-q1-2026-report/)__). The recovery rate of roughly 14 cents on the dollar is materially below early-case projections that had posited 20–30 cent recoveries, driven primarily by: (i) the near-total evaporation of 3AC's venture portfolio valuations; (ii) protracted litigation with Su Zhu and Kyle Davies over asset concealment and cooperation obligations; and (iii) the substantial legal and administrative costs of maintaining parallel recognition proceedings in the Southern District of New York (Chapter 15, Case No. 22-10920 (MG), Judge Martin Glenn) and in Singapore.

The liquidators' Chapter 15 proceedings remain active, with Judge Glenn having recently granted supplemental relief authorizing the BVI liquidators to issue subpoenas to U.S.-domiciled counterparties who received collateral from 3AC in the 90 days preceding the BVI winding-up order (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/3ac-chapter15-subpoena-relief-2026)__). The subpoena targets include at least three major U.S.-regulated prime brokers whose identities have not been publicly disclosed in the redacted court filings. The liquidators have separately pursued recognition in Singapore, where the High Court has entered a recognition order permitting examination of witnesses; Su Zhu, who was released from Changi Prison in late 2025 after serving a portion of a four-month sentence for obstruction of the Singapore liquidation proceedings, remains a compelled witness subject to ongoing deposition obligations (__[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/su-zhu-singapore-deposition-3ac-2026/)__).

The Q1 2026 report also discloses an active adversary proceeding against Genesis Global Capital in the S.D.N.Y. seeking to claw back approximately $200 million in alleged preference payments made by 3AC to Genesis in the weeks before the BVI winding-up order (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/05/15/3ac-genesis-preference-adversary-2026/)__). Genesis, itself operating under its own confirmed Chapter 11 plan (Case No. 23-10063 (SHL), Judge Sean Lane, S.D.N.Y.), has answered the complaint and asserted that the transfers qualify as settlement-payment safe-harbor transactions under 11 U.S.C. § 546(e). The safe-harbor defense is analytically significant given the Second Circuit's 2023 Merit Management clarifications and the ongoing circuit-level debate over whether crypto-asset transfers constitute "securities" within the meaning of § 546(e) — a question the Second Circuit has not definitively resolved in the crypto-native context (__[Weil Restructuring Blog — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/3ac-genesis-546e-crypto-2026)__).

### Genesis Global Capital — Plan Consummation and Creditor Recovery Tracking

The Genesis Global Capital estate (Case No. 23-10063 (SHL), Judge Sean Lane, S.D.N.Y.) has now substantially consummated its confirmed Chapter 11 plan, with the distribution agent (Alvarez & Marsal) having completed the first major distribution to in-kind crypto creditors in March 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/genesis-plan-consummation-march-2026)__). Recovery rates for the largest creditor class — holders of Bitcoin-denominated lending claims — have benefited dramatically from the BTC price appreciation since the petition date, with in-kind distributions translating to approximate dollar recoveries well above the original dollar-denominated proof-of-claim value for creditors who elected the crypto-denominated treatment.

The estate continues to litigate its contribution claims against Digital Currency Group (DCG) and Barry Silbert, arising from allegations that DCG caused Genesis to extend the maturity of an intercompany $1.1 billion promissory note in circumstances constituting a constructive fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and N.Y. Debtor & Creditor Law § 273 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/genesis-dcg-fraudulent-transfer-2026)__). The adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 24-01020 (SHL)) is in discovery; Judge Lane has set a fact-discovery cutoff of August 29, 2026, with expert reports to follow in the fall. The outcome of this litigation is a material variable in the estate's ability to fund a supplemental distribution to creditors who received less than 100 cents on their claims.

### Terraform Labs / Do Kwon — SEC Disgorgement Enforcement and Asset Recovery

The SEC's disgorgement and civil-penalty judgment against Terraform Labs and Do Kwon — arising from the May 2022 collapse of the Terra/LUNA ecosystem, which wiped approximately $40 billion in market value within 72 hours — entered the enforcement phase in earnest during Q1 2026. The judgment, entered by Judge Jed Rakoff in the Southern District of New York (SEC v. Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd. and Do Kwon, Case No. 23-cv-01346 (JSR)), imposes disgorgement of approximately $4.47 billion, prejudgment interest of approximately $150 million, and civil penalties of $420 million against Terraform Labs, plus disgorgement of approximately $110 million and penalties of $80 million against Do Kwon personally (__[SEC Litigation Release — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/terraform-kwon-judgment-2025)__).

Enforcement coordination between the SEC, the Terraform Chapter 11 estate (currently being administered in the District of Delaware, Case No. 24-10070), and the Montenegro criminal proceedings against Do Kwon has been complicated by competing sovereign claims on available assets (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/terraform-kwon-enforcement-coordination-2026/)__). The Terraform estate's plan of liquidation, confirmed in late 2025, provides for a SEC-claim treatment mechanism negotiated under 11 U.S.C. § 1129(a)(9) that allocates a portion of estate assets to a regulatory-settlement reserve — though the quantum of that reserve remains subject to ongoing negotiations between estate counsel and the SEC's Division of Enforcement.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and rights-holders with active positions across the 2022–2024 crypto-insolvency cohort should immediately prioritize the following: (1) **FTX creditors** who have not yet completed Kroll portal KYC verification should do so within 72 hours given the June 17 status conference — OFAC-implicated holdbacks may require specialized counsel, and failure to clear verification before the general-unsecured distribution timeline is set risks further delays; (2) **Celsius institutional counterparties** facing the trust's supplemental objection (Dkt. 7,841) should retain restructuring counsel immediately to respond to the § 510(b) subordination and § 548 avoidance theories, which carry asymmetric downside if unopposed — the § 546(e) safe-harbor analysis in the crypto context is fact-intensive and requires expert-level briefing; (3) **Voyager unsecured creditors** should monitor the June 9 final-distribution hearing and confirm their claim reconciliation status with the administrator, paying particular attention to the deferred FTX-proceeds component, which may require a separate election or hold-back acknowledgment; (4) **3AC creditors** who have not yet submitted claims in the BVI liquidation or filed proofs of claim in the Chapter 15 proceeding should consult with BVI-qualified insolvency counsel without delay, as bar-date issues in offshore liquidations can be jurisdictionally fatal; (5) **Genesis creditors** who elected dollar-denominated rather than crypto-denominated treatment should evaluate whether any post-consummation election-modification procedure is available through the distribution agent, given the material in-kind recovery premium; and (6) all parties monitoring the **Terraform/DCG enforcement corridor** should model their recovery assumptions against the competing sovereign-claim dynamic and the possibility that a substantial portion of the disgorgement pool will be subject to extended litigation before being available for distribution to harmed investors.
