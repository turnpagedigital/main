# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Monday, July 27, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The week just closed with two new Chapter 11 filings — Storj Labs (July 26) and MVMT Labs/Movement (July 15) — both in Delaware or West Virginia federal courts, against a background of 95 crypto project shutdowns year-to-date and the BlockFills plan confirmation already in Judge Horan's rearview.** The common thread across all three: a post-2022 insolvency wave that has now migrated beyond exchanges and lenders into token-ecosystem infrastructure companies carrying legacy obligations that growth never extinguished. The central creditor-rights question shifts with each new case — from "whose property are these customer deposits under § 541?" in BlockFills, to "what do STORJ token holders have legally in a Chapter 11 reorganization?" in Storj. The answer in both instances, as in Celsius before them, is that the Bankruptcy Code does not bend to community expectations.

### BlockFills (*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Post-Confirmation Posture

The key procedural milestone for this case has now passed. 
On May 28, 2026, Judge Horan entered the Disclosure Statement Order (Dkt. No. 388), approving the solicitation version of the plan, setting a voting deadline of July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, a confirmation objection deadline of July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and a confirmation hearing of July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket last updated July 24 (Dkt. No. 538), with one new filing in the prior 72 hours — almost certainly a post-confirmation order or related administrative entry, though the entry description was not populated in the CourtListener sync as of the July 26 fetch. **That entry (Dkt. 538, filed July 24) is the single most consequential unverified fact in this advisory; creditors should pull it directly from PACER or the Veritaglobal claims administrator portal at veritaglobal.net/blockfills.**

The plan was built around a dual-track sale-plus-reorganization structure. 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million for substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and IP; it was declared the successful bidder per a court filing dated May 26, 2026.
 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve that $3.25 million sale to the Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepared for the plan confirmation hearing.


The capital structure context matters for recovery modeling. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that $145 million unsecured pool, a $3.25 million sale price implies de minimis recovery for general unsecured creditors absent meaningful claims against insiders — and the pre-petition litigation record suggests that avenue is live.


In the weeks before the Chapter 11 filing, two customer actions asserted misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.*, No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. Mar. 5, 2026); both courts granted TROs that significantly restricted the debtors' ability to operate and exercise control over assets.
 Those pre-petition actions are now paused by the automatic stay, but the underlying fraudulent-transfer and D&O exposure they represent feeds directly into plan litigation trust mechanics and any post-confirmation avoidance action calendar.

The § 541 customer-property question — the central structural question that echoes Celsius's TOS battle — 
remains contested, with the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property still unresolved in the four debtor entities' dual-track restructuring.
 Judge Horan's confirmation order, whenever docketed, should address how that question was resolved or deferred.

### Storj Labs (*In re Storj Labs, Inc.*, No. 5:26-bk-00512, Bankr. N.D. W. Va.) — New Filing, July 26, 2026

This is the week's lede development. 
Storj Labs, Inc. voluntarily filed Chapter 11 on July 26, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of West Virginia, Case No. 5:26-bk-00512, to address certain legacy obligations while preserving ongoing business operations; the company states it will continue operating in the ordinary course throughout the Chapter 11 process.
 Judge assignment is not yet confirmed in available sources. No DIP financing arrangement has been publicly disclosed.


Storj Labs had raised approximately $35 million through venture funding, grants, and its 2017 STORJ token sale prior to the filing.
 
Storj runs a decentralized cloud storage network that pays individuals and businesses to rent out unused disk space; it was acquired last year by Inveniam, which the company says endorses the reorganization; Storj is disposing of previous acquisitions and non-essential operations as part of the process.
 
Those prior acquisitions include Valdi, a supplier of on-demand GPUs, and PetaGene, a provider of cunoFS file-access products — both purchased in 2024.


The token-recovery structure proposed here is genuinely novel — and legally precarious. 
The restructuring proposal contains a provision rarely seen in bankruptcy: Storj says it plans to share ownership of the reorganized company among management, token holders, and investors; token holders normally have no legal claim on an issuer and receive nothing in a Chapter 11 process.
 
Management has announced intentions to develop a framework enabling STORJ token holders to acquire equity stakes in the post-reorganization entity, with a proposed ownership structure that may include existing management, current investors, community stakeholders, and potentially new capital partners; details regarding eligibility criteria, token snapshot requirements, lockup provisions, or the percentage of equity allocated remain undisclosed.


The market read this immediately. 
The STORJ token fell 16% to approximately $0.06; almost $20 million worth of the token changed hands against a market value of approximately $27 million, meaning close to the entire circulating supply turned over in a single day.


For creditors and token holders, the structural questions are severe. 
Reorganizations can involve significant changes to ownership structures, equity stakes, and the priority of different classes of stakeholders; in traditional bankruptcies, equity holders — the closest analogy to token holders — sit at the bottom of the creditor hierarchy.
 The STORJ token does carry genuine utility: 
it serves a genuine utility function in that users pay for storage and bandwidth with it and node operators earn it as compensation for providing network capacity
 — which could support an argument for some class treatment. But that argument has to survive the absolute priority rule under 11 U.S.C. § 1129(b) if any senior class rejects the plan. An additional overhang: 
in May 2026, Binance placed STORJ under a "monitoring tag" as part of a review covering nine tokens, a designation that signals potential delisting risk.
 A Binance delisting mid-case would further erode any equity value the token-to-equity conversion might generate.

### MVMT Labs, Inc. (*In re MVMT Labs, Inc.*, No. 26-11113, Bankr. D. Del.) — Subchapter V Filing, July 15, 2026


The case, filed as 26-11113, was assigned to Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 Judge Horan, notably, is simultaneously presiding over BlockFills (No. 26-10371). 
MVMT Labs, Inc., the corporate entity behind the Movement blockchain, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 15, 2026; the company selected Subchapter V, a streamlined restructuring path for eligible small businesses.


The balance sheet is stark. 
The petition lists estimated assets of between $100,001 and $500,000, set against liabilities reaching as high as $10 million — figures that fall within the Subchapter V limits for small-business debtors.
 
Co-founder Rushikesh "Rushi" Manche holds the largest unsecured claim, exceeding $1.6 million; Manche separated from the company in May 2025 after internal questions over undisclosed agreements, and he retains a 34.25% equity stake.
 
Other notable creditors include the Delaware Division of Corporations, allegedly owed $459,000, along with Move Industries, Anchorage Digital, and auditing firm Ottersec.


The proximate cause of the filing is well-documented. 
The bankruptcy follows the MOVE token scandal; a CoinDesk investigation found that an intermediary named Rentech, connected to market maker Web3Port, held an arrangement that allowed 66 million MOVE tokens — about 5% of supply — to reach the market one day after the token debuted.
 
Rentech dumped the entire 5% supply (approximately 66 million MOVE), shortly after the token launched, profiting approximately $38 million.


Key dates: 
a formal creditors' meeting is scheduled for August 20, 2026, and all parties holding claims must submit a Proof of Claim before the court-mandated deadline of September 14, 2026.
 
Movement Labs must present its formal restructuring plan by October 13, 2026.
 
On July 20, the court allowed Movement Labs to maintain its bank accounts and existing cash management system, and the company received preliminary approval for DIP financing; the DIP creditor is MNF DIP SPV Ltd.


One corporate-structure point that complicates recovery analysis: 
CEO Torab Torabi has stated that Move Industries remains legally separate and continues operating normally, and the bankruptcy does not automatically place the blockchain or its current developer under court protection.
 Claims running against token launch mechanics may require piercing that legal separation, which will likely be contested.

### Sector Context: 95 Project Shutdowns in 2026 Through July 24


Several crypto businesses have been forced to close amid the bear market this year; as of July 24, 2026, 95 projects have shut down, with recent closures including SecondFi, BitMEX, and BitMart.
 
BitMEX, the exchange that invented the perpetual swap, announced on July 23 it would shut down after 11 years, with daily volume down to roughly $400,000 and its BMEX token falling more than 90%.
 The operational-wind-down model (BitMEX, BitMart) produces a materially different creditor posture than a reorganization case: no automatic stay, no plan mechanics, and customer recovery dependent entirely on platform-specific withdrawal procedures and, in BitMEX's case, potentially Seychelles or Cayman regulatory oversight of the distribution process. Neither BitMEX nor BitMart has a publicly visible U.S. Chapter 11 docket at this time — this advisory will track whether either entity files in a U.S. forum.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and rights-holders across all three active dockets should take the following concrete steps this week: In **BlockFills** (No. 26-10371), pull Dkt. No. 538 directly from PACER or the Veritaglobal administrator portal immediately — this is likely the confirmation order or a post-confirmation administrative entry, and its content will determine whether the plan is now effective, what the liquidating trust mechanics are, and whether any post-confirmation avoidance action calendar has been set; claimants who voted or hold claims should confirm their treatment class under the Amended Joint Plan (Dkt. No. 391) and monitor for any distribution notice from Veritaglobal at veritaglobal.net/blockfills. In **Storj Labs** (No. 5:26-bk-00512), token holders and network node operators should resist treating the company's equity-conversion proposal as a legal entitlement — it is not; under 11 U.S.C. § 1129(b), any distribution to equity or token-equivalent holders requires either (a) senior class acceptance or (b) a finding that no senior claimant is impaired; the creditor pool and priority waterfall are not yet disclosed, and the absence of a filed creditor matrix or asset schedule means no recovery model is possible at this stage; those with contractual claims against Storj (vendor agreements, storage contracts) should file proofs of claim at the first opportunity and retain counsel experienced in West Virginia bankruptcy practice. In **MVMT Labs** (No. 26-11113), the September 14 bar date is the operative deadline; creditors with token-related fraud or misrepresentation claims should evaluate whether those claims run against MVMT Labs alone or whether Move Industries (the current blockchain developer) is a proper defendant under alter-ego or successor-liability theories before the reorganization plan constrains that analysis in October; DIP creditor MNF DIP SPV Ltd.'s identity and terms should be examined once the DIP order is publicly available on PACER, as DIP lenders in Subchapter V cases often have outsized influence on plan terms.
