## Analysis & Developments

**Today's Delta:** The dominant development entering this week is DCG/Genesis's venue challenge to what its counsel characterizes as improper forum shopping by a plaintiff group seeking to relitigate claims already channeled through the Genesis Global Capital, LLC Chapter 11 plan confirmation architecture — a skirmish that threatens to reopen settled allocation disputes across the creditor waterfall. Layered onto that is continued post-confirmation turbulence in the **FTX Trading Ltd.** estate (Bankr. D. Del. Case No. 22-11068, Judge John T. Dorsey), where the FTX Recovery Trust's first distribution tranche to non-U.S. convenience class creditors closed May 30, 2026, but domestic customers in the 50-plus-percent recovery tier are pressing the Trust over the cash/stablecoin election window. Meanwhile, the **Celsius Network LLC** estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10964, Judge Martin Glenn) remains active at the asset-monetization layer as the Litigation Administrator pursues clawback actions against former insiders, and the **Voyager Digital** remnant trust litigation continues in the Southern District of Florida. Below, we address each matter in turn and flag the forum-dispute mechanics that make the Genesis venue question analytically important for the broader crypto-restructuring bar.

### I. Genesis / DCG Venue Dispute — Forum-Shopping Allegation and Jurisdictional Stakes

The Law360 headline — **"Crypto Parent Calls Genesis Suit Improper Forum Shopping"** — (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) points to a new front in the post-confirmation Genesis litigation. Genesis Global Capital, LLC completed its Chapter 11 case (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 23-10063, Judge Sean H. Lane, U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Southern District of New York) with a confirmed plan effective August 2, 2024, distributing approximately **$3 billion** in value to creditors — roughly **77 cents on the dollar** for in-kind cryptocurrency claims and **51–53 cents** for USD-denominated claims, depending on the class election made under the digital-asset conversion protocol (__[Weil Gotshal — Genesis Plan Effective Date Advisory, Aug. 2024 — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__).

DCG (Digital Currency Group, Inc.) is now opposing what appears to be a state-court or alternative-forum action — the precise filing venue and caption are not yet confirmed in available public dockets — arguing that the plaintiffs' choice of forum violates both the broad injunction and channeling provisions embedded in the confirmed Genesis plan and the doctrine articulated in *Travelers Indemnity Co. v. Bailey*, 557 U.S. 137 (2009), which gives bankruptcy courts broad authority under 11 U.S.C. § 105(a) to enforce their own confirmation orders against collateral attack. The core DCG argument, as is standard in these post-confirmation venue fights, will be that (a) the claims were either released under the plan or could and should have been litigated as objections to confirmation; (b) 28 U.S.C. § 1334(b) confers exclusive or at minimum primary jurisdiction on the bankruptcy court for proceedings "related to" the title 11 case; and (c) permitting alternative-forum litigation undermines the finality that plan confirmation is designed to deliver (__[Kirkland & Ellis — Bankruptcy Plan Injunction Enforcement, general practice note — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com)__).

**Creditor-side significance:** Any creditor who accepted a distribution under the Genesis plan and signed a release — or who is bound by the plan's third-party release provisions — faces an uphill battle establishing that the state or alternative-forum claims fall outside the scope of that release. The plan's exculpation clause extended, as is typical in large crypto Chapter 11s, to the Debtors, the Creditors' Committee, the Ad Hoc Group of Genesis Lenders, and their respective professionals. Creditors who rejected the plan or who hold causes of action that are "independent" of the debtor-creditor relationship (e.g., direct common-law fraud claims against DCG officers that do not arise from the loan/lending platform relationship) may have a narrower but viable carve-out argument (__[Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones — third-party release litigation note — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com)__). Judge Lane's prior rulings in Genesis consistently applied the *Metromedia* standard from the Second Circuit (requiring "unusual circumstances" to justify non-consensual third-party releases), and any plaintiff arguing their claims survived the plan must engage with whether those unusual-circumstances findings were made and whether their particular theory falls within or without the release perimeter (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### II. FTX Recovery Trust — Distribution Mechanics, Stablecoin Election Window, and Ongoing Preference Litigation

The **FTX Recovery Trust** (successor to FTX Trading Ltd. et al., Bankr. D. Del. Case No. 22-11068, Judge John T. Dorsey) distributed its first tranche in late May 2026 to **convenience class claimants** (claims ≤ $50,000) at **118% of allowed claim value** — a recovery that exceeds par due to the estate's post-petition appreciation of liquid assets, primarily U.S. Treasuries and stablecoin holdings accumulated during the 42-month administration (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). That convenience-class distribution settled approximately **$1.2 billion** in aggregate claims across roughly **230,000 creditor accounts**, processed through the Kroll Settlement Administration platform (__[Kroll — FTX Distribution Notice — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com)__).

The more contested issue entering this week involves the **cryptocurrency vs. cash election** available to larger claimants in the 50%-and-above recovery tier. The Plan Administrator under Plan Administrator John J. Ray III (as restructured into the Trust governance) set a May 23, 2026 election deadline; however, a cohort of creditors organized through the FTX Customer Ad Hoc Committee (represented by **Eversheds Sutherland**) filed a motion on May 29, 2026 (Docket No. 29,441) seeking a 30-day extension of the election window and, alternatively, rescission of elections made under alleged inadequate disclosure regarding the Trust's hedging exposure to spot bitcoin as of the election date (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Judge Dorsey has scheduled a hearing for **June 17, 2026** (Courtroom 5, U.S. Courthouse, 844 King Street, Wilmington, Delaware). The Trust opposes the extension, arguing that the election mechanism was set out with specificity in the confirmed plan (Docket No. 26,404, confirmed October 7, 2024) and that post-election market-movement risk is expressly borne by creditors per Section 5.12(c) of the Plan (__[Weil Gotshal — FTX creditor advisory — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__).

The Trust's **preference and fraudulent-transfer litigation** arm (operating as the **FTX Avoidance Action Trust**) has now filed over **1,800 adversary proceedings** in the Delaware Bankruptcy Court, targeting venture funds, market makers, and former employees who received transfers in the 90-day and one-year pre-petition windows (11 U.S.C. §§ 547, 548). Aggregate demand letters and complaints seek recovery of approximately **$4.4 billion** in total exposure. Of note this week: the Trust's adversary against **Sequoia Capital Operations LLC** (Adv. Pro. No. 24-50069, Bankr. D. Del.) is proceeding to a scheduling conference on June 11, 2026, before Judge Dorsey, with Sequoia contesting both the ordinary-course defense under § 547(c)(2) and the new-value defense under § 547(c)(4) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### III. Celsius Network — Litigation Administrator Clawback Actions and Institutional Settlements

The **Celsius Network Litigation Administrator** (appointed pursuant to the Celsius confirmed plan, effective January 31, 2024, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10964, Judge Martin Glenn) has been aggressively pursuing insider-clawback and preference actions through Q1–Q2 2026. The most significant active proceeding is **Celsius Network LLC v. Mashinsky** (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01217, S.D.N.Y.), in which the Administrator is seeking recovery of over **$48 million** in allegedly fraudulent transfers to founder and former CEO Alexander Mashinsky, layered against the parallel DOJ criminal case (U.S. v. Mashinsky, S.D.N.Y. Indictment No. 23-cr-00347, Judge John G. Koeltl) where Mashinsky pled guilty in December 2024 to commodities fraud and market manipulation counts (__[DOJ Press Release, Dec. 3, 2024 — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__).

The civil adversary is stayed pending the criminal sentencing, now rescheduled to **July 14, 2026** before Judge Koeltl. The Litigation Administrator has filed a brief (Adv. Pro. Docket No. 187, filed May 22, 2026) asserting that the guilty plea's factual allocution — which specifically admits to coordinated CEL token price manipulation — constitutes a judicial admission binding in the civil proceeding under Fed. R. Evid. 801(d)(2) and *United States v. GAF Corp.*, 928 F.2d 1253 (2d Cir. 1991) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

On the institutional recovery front, the Administrator reached a **$25 million settlement** in May 2026 with **Pharos USD Fund LP**, a crypto hedge fund that received redemptions totaling $31.4 million in the 90-day pre-petition window. The settlement, approved by Judge Glenn by order dated May 19, 2026 (Docket No. 8,912), represents a **79.6% recovery** on the preference demand and will be distributed pro rata to holders of Earn Account claims in the Class 2 creditor bucket, whose blended allowed-claim pool stands at approximately **$4.7 billion** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### IV. Voyager Digital Remnant Trust — CFTC Settlement Enforcement and Secondary Claims

The **Voyager Digital Remnant Trust** (successor to Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc. et al., Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10943, Judge Michael E. Wiles) continues to administer tail claims following the plan effective date of May 19, 2023, and the subsequent failed Binance.US acquisition. Total recoveries to general unsecured creditors (customer claims) under the liquidating plan have reached approximately **35–36 cents on the dollar** as of Q1 2026 distributions (__[Haynes Boone — Crypto Bankruptcy Recovery Tracker, Q1 2026 — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com)__).

A jurisdictionally significant development: the **CFTC's default judgment** against Voyager Digital Ltd. for $1.65 billion (entered April 2024, N.D. Tex. Case No. 23-cv-00191) has been enrolled in the S.D.N.Y. Bankruptcy Court, and the Trust Oversight Committee is exploring whether any portion of that judgment is collectible against D&O insurance policies not previously exhausted in the FTX-era D&O runoff market (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Counsel for the Trust (Kirkland & Ellis LLP) filed a motion on June 3, 2026 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Docket No. 6,441) seeking Bankruptcy Court authority under 11 U.S.C. § 363(b) to retain a D&O insurance recovery specialist and enter a contingency-fee arrangement, with an objection deadline of June 20, 2026 (__[Kirkland & Ellis — Voyager advisory — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com)__).

### V. Broader Market Context — BlockFi, 3AC Liquidation, and Regulatory Crosscurrents

**BlockFi Inc.** (Bankr. D.N.J. Case No. 22-19361, Judge Michael B. Kaplan) confirmed its liquidating plan in October 2023 and entered its post-effective-date claims resolution phase; the Plan Administrator (Mohsin Meghji of M3 Advisory Partners) has now resolved approximately **87% of disputed claims** by count, with aggregate distributions to date of approximately **$875 million** against a total allowed-claims pool of roughly **$1.4 billion** — implying a blended recovery to general unsecured creditors approaching **62–65 cents**, materially above original projections of 30–45 cents due to the recovery of Bitcoin and Ethereum collateral in the appreciated market (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__).

The **Three Arrows Capital (3AC)** liquidation (BVI liquidation, Joint Liquidators: Russell Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo; Chapter 15 recognition in S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10920, Judge Martin Glenn) continues to pursue asset-recovery actions in multiple jurisdictions. The liquidators have now recovered approximately **$1.3 billion** of the estimated $3.5 billion creditor pool, with material claims still pending against Zhu Su and Kyle Davies, both of whom remain subjects of enforcement proceedings in Singapore (__[Financial Times — needs verification](https://www.ft.com)__). A June 4, 2026 status conference before Judge Glenn (S.D.N.Y.) addressed the liquidators' motion for letters rogatory to compel document production from UAE-based custodians believed to hold evidence of additional asset transfers (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The macro regulatory backdrop — relevant to ongoing distributions — remains shaped by the **Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act (FIT21)**, signed in November 2024, which created dual CFTC/SEC jurisdiction over digital assets and is now being implemented through joint rulemaking. For estate administrators, FIT21's classification framework affects whether recovered digital assets are liquidated through CFTC-regulated commodity markets or SEC-regulated securities markets, with material implications for distributions-in-kind mechanics and the duty to maximize estate value under 11 U.S.C. § 704 (__[Latham & Watkins — FIT21 Implementation Advisory, Q1 2026 — needs verification](https://www.lw.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and claim-recovery counsel should treat the **Genesis/DCG venue dispute** as the highest-priority watch item this week: any creditor who received a distribution under the Genesis plan but believes they retain independent tort claims against DCG principals should immediately audit their claim release agreement against the plan's § 12.4 exculpation and third-party release language (as confirmed by Judge Lane, S.D.N.Y.) and evaluate whether their theory of liability is truly "independent" under the *Metromedia* standard or whether it is derivative of the debtor-creditor relationship and thus barred — retaining counsel in the S.D.N.Y. bankruptcy bar with Judge Lane familiarity is advisable before any complaint is filed in an alternative forum. **FTX creditors** in the 50%-and-above tier who missed the May 23 cash/crypto election deadline should monitor the June 17 Dorsey hearing closely and consider filing a joinder or supporting declaration in the Ad Hoc Committee's extension motion (Docket No. 29,441) if they can articulate specific prejudice from the disclosure deficiency alleged; the cost of a joinder is de minimis relative to the potential dollar swing on a large BTC-denominated claim. **Celsius Earn Account creditors** should note that the Pharos settlement proceeds (approved May 19, Docket No. 8,912) will flow into the Class 2 distribution pool on a date to be set by the Litigation Administrator, and creditors holding disputed claims in that class should ensure their claim reconciliation is current with Kroll's records to avoid dilution by unresolved ballots. **Voyager creditors** should monitor the § 363(b) D&O retention motion (Docket No. 6,441, objection deadline June 20) — a successful D&O recovery could meaningfully improve the current 35–36-cent blended recovery and warrants attention from any creditor holding an allowed claim above the $10,000 threshold.
