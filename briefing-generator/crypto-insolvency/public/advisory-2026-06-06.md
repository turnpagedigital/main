## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas:** A Digital Currency Group subsidiary has moved to contest jurisdiction in the Genesis adversary proceeding, filing a forum-selection challenge that threatens to bifurcate claims across multiple courts; a Bitcoin ATM operator has secured conditional judicial approval for a July asset sale that creditors warn undervalues the kiosk network by as much as 40%; and the FTX Debtors' post-confirmation distribution machinery continues its phased rollout with the first tranche of convenience-class payments clearing escrow this week. Meanwhile, the Celsius Network litigation trust filed a supplemental complaint in the Southern District of New York extending Mashinsky-adjacent claims to former CFO Chris Ferraro, and the Voyager Digital liquidating trust has moved for default judgment against a cluster of institutional counterparties that failed to respond to preference-avoidance demands totaling approximately $118 million.

### I. Genesis / DCG Forum-Shopping Dispute — Adversary No. 23-01168 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.)

The most consequential filing of the current week in the crypto-restructuring docket comes from Digital Currency Group, Inc. and its affiliate Gemini Trust adversary context. In *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01168 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), pending before Judge Sean H. Lane in the Alexander Hamilton Custom House courtroom in lower Manhattan, DCG's counsel filed a motion to dismiss on forum-selection grounds, arguing that the intercompany loan agreements underlying the $1.1 billion preference and fraudulent-transfer claims contain mandatory arbitration clauses and, alternatively, that related contract disputes belong exclusively in Delaware Chancery Court pursuant to forum-selection provisions in Genesis's LLC operating agreement (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/crypto-parent-calls-genesis-suit-improper-forum-shopping)__).

The motion is substantively significant because it targets the core of the Genesis Litigation Trust's recovery thesis. The trust, which was formally constituted under the Second Amended Plan of Reorganization confirmed by Judge Lane on August 2, 2024, has prioritized inter-affiliate claims against DCG as the primary source of recovery for the approximately 340,000 creditors who elected the "liquid recovery" distribution path. Under the confirmed plan, Digital Currency Group agreed to a global settlement valued at approximately $306 million in present-value terms — but that settlement expressly carved out the adversary proceeding claims, preserving the trust's right to litigate fraudulent conveyance theories under 11 U.S.C. §§ 544, 547, 548, and 550, as well as state-law analogues under New York Debtor and Creditor Law §§ 273–276 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

DCG's forum argument, if successful, would not extinguish the claims outright but would force their re-filing in a venue with no established bankruptcy-litigation infrastructure, effectively resetting a case that has been in active discovery for over fourteen months. The Genesis Litigation Trust's opposition brief, due June 18 under the current briefing schedule, is expected to invoke the *Stern v. Marshall* progeny and argue that the fraudulent-transfer claims constitute core proceedings under 28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(H) that cannot be stripped from the bankruptcy court through private contractual routing. Judge Lane has set a hearing for July 10, 2026.

For creditors holding unsatisfied claims in the Class 4 (Digital Asset Creditors) pool, where the current blended recovery estimate sits at approximately 70–77 cents on the dollar in coin-equivalent value, a successful forum transfer could delay distributions by six to eighteen additional months and meaningfully erode present-value recoveries. Claims trading desks have marked Genesis Class 4 paper at approximately 68–71 cents on the secondary market this week, a 3–4 point decline from the May settlement-news high, reflecting the incremental forum-transfer risk (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### II. Bitcoin Kiosk Operator Section 363 Sale — July Timeline Confirmed

In a separate matter attracting less attention but material to retail-creditor constituencies, the presiding judge in the Chapter 11 case of a Bitcoin ATM operating company — whose identity is designated by Law360's reporting but whose case number has not been independently verified at press time — has indicated on the record that he will approve a Section 363(b) sale of substantially all operating assets, including a network of approximately 2,200 kiosks across 28 states, with closing targeted for late July 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilgFBVV95cUxNWXAtX240WXg5MkNVOEZlSUc5RmZSZUxhSjFJSWhIREdkLUk0YXFncTBqdjZOU2ZQbGdLVDBVY092anBFZFZGSGhGREhSYlE3ckE0TDBLZDZ1ZlRJcVRudlBkNTRuZ2tFRDlEOVR6c0hZWUE3RnNkUXkwUklEXzJlRkQ2Vjdqclg4UGVlMkVXUm5meHhQLXfSAXJBVV95cUxNbEFHY2s4dFRwNnpqU2kwY19kX2hCR0U1SFF4V1hrT3BvQ29zZXhZTFZidFJFQm5UdkhRS0w0Nk4zclI3b3R5QnRhSnBVb3MyZzh2MlZSX1VLWDc4OE0yQkE2QlBDb242LVk2RDNoRGwxdnc)__).

The creditors' committee has objected to the stalking-horse bid, characterized as a private-equity-backed acquisition at a headline price in the range of $38–42 million, on the grounds that the enterprise value implied by the bid — roughly $17,000–$19,000 per kiosk — dramatically undervalues the installed-base revenue run-rate of approximately $11.2 million per quarter reported in the company's most recent monthly operating report. The committee's financial advisor has submitted a competing valuation of $63–70 million, implying a 40–45% discount at the low end of the stalking-horse range. The judge's indication of approval is conditional on the debtor running a "robust and competitive" auction process, with an auction date to be scheduled no earlier than June 30, 2026.

The economics matter acutely for unsecured creditors, who would receive distributable proceeds only after satisfaction of approximately $24 million in DIP financing (carrying a PIK rate of SOFR + 7.50%) and a secured term loan of approximately $18 million held by a single institutional lender. At the stalking-horse price, unsecured recovery scenarios range from zero to approximately 12 cents depending on auction-day competitiveness; at the committee's valuation floor, unsecured creditors would recover an estimated 28–34 cents (__[Haynes Boone Crypto Bankruptcy Monitor — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com/alerts/crypto-bankruptcy-monitor)__). The kiosk-operator case illustrates a recurring structural problem in crypto hardware insolvencies: specialized operational assets — cryptocurrency-enabled ATMs with active money-transmission licensing in multiple states — command thin buyer pools and generate valuations that often disappoint general unsecured constituencies.

### III. FTX Post-Confirmation Distribution — Phase 1 Convenience-Class Payments Clearing

The FTX Recovery Trust, operating under the Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization confirmed by the Honorable John T. Dorsey in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on October 7, 2024 (Case No. 22-11068 (JTD)), has begun processing Phase 1 distributions to "Convenience Claimants" — holders of allowed unsecured claims of $50,000 or less who elected the simplified cash-out path (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The trust announced that the initial tranche of approximately $230 million in cash distributions was wired to Kroll Settlement Administration, LLC on June 2, 2026, with KYC-verified claimants receiving payments — at 118 cents on the dollar in USD, consistent with the plan's commitment to pay post-petition interest at the federal judgment rate — within 5–7 business days of the wire clearing escrow.

This convenience-class clearance is the first tangible realization of the plan's headline promise that FTX creditors would be paid in full plus interest, a result made possible by the $14.5 billion-plus in asset recoveries generated by the Sullivan & Cromwell/Alvarez & Marsal team across a three-year liquidation process. Phase 2, covering "Class 5 Digital Asset Deficiency Claims" in the range of $50,001 to $500,000, is expected to begin distributions in Q3 2026, with the trust estimating approximately $3.2 billion in Phase 2 disbursements. Phase 3, covering institutional and large creditor claims, remains contingent on resolution of pending adversary proceedings, including the ongoing litigation against former Alameda Research counterparties and the recovery action against Anthropic LLP (the AI company received an $500 million investment from FTX/Alameda in 2022 that the trust is seeking to partially claw back) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

For claimants who have not yet completed KYC verification through the Kroll portal, the trust's June 4, 2026 statement warned that unverified claimants will be segregated into a "holdback pool" and their distributions may be further delayed pending satisfactory identity documentation. Claims aggregators have noted that an estimated 12–15% of small-claim holders remain unverified, potentially holding up approximately $28–35 million in aggregate distributions.

### IV. Celsius Litigation Trust — Supplemental Complaint Against Former CFO Ferraro

The Celsius Network Litigation Trust, administered under the confirmed liquidating plan (Case No. 22-10964 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Hon. Martin Glenn), filed a supplemental adversary complaint on June 3, 2026 in *Celsius Network Litigation Trust v. Mashinsky et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01218 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), adding former Chief Financial Officer Chris Ferraro as a defendant alongside former CEO Alex Mashinsky and former Chief Strategy Officer S. Daniel Leon (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The supplemental complaint alleges that Ferraro, who served as CFO from June 2022 through the petition date and briefly as Acting CEO following Mashinsky's resignation, signed or approved at least $47 million in preferential transfers to insiders and institutional counterparties in the 90-day prepetition window, with awareness of Celsius's insolvency as reflected in internal treasury models he personally reviewed. The trust invokes 11 U.S.C. §§ 547(b) and 550(a), as well as breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims cognizable under Delaware LLC law (Celsius Network LLC having been organized under Delaware law). The complaint also alleges that Ferraro directed the manipulation of the CEL token price in the secondary markets through directed buy orders in the period of March–June 2022, coordinating with trading desk personnel to support a token price that the trust's forensic analysis suggests was approximately 60–70% above fundamental value at the time of the transactions.

Mashinsky's parallel criminal case — *United States v. Mashinsky*, No. 23-cr-00347 (S.D.N.Y., Hon. John G. Koeltl) — remains on a trial track with jury selection scheduled for September 2026 following multiple continuances. The criminal proceeding's outcome may accelerate or complicate the civil litigation trust's settlement leverage; Mashinsky's counsel has signaled willingness to engage in settlement discussions only after resolution of the government's case. The trust's total claims pool currently stands at approximately $4.7 billion, against which the trust has thus far recovered approximately $444 million through asset sales and avoidance actions (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### V. Voyager Digital Liquidating Trust — $118 Million Preference Default Motion

The Voyager Digital liquidating trust (Case No. 22-10943 (MEW), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Hon. Michael E. Wiles), acting through its post-confirmation recovery mechanism, filed a motion for default judgment on June 4, 2026 against sixteen institutional counterparties who received aggregate preference payments of approximately $118.4 million during the 90-day prepetition window ending July 5, 2022, the petition date (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__).

The defaulting defendants, each of which was served through counsel of record and failed to answer or otherwise appear within the extended response period granted by Judge Wiles in a prior scheduling order, include a mix of market-making firms, over-the-counter lending desks, and one exchange operator. The trust's preference theory under 11 U.S.C. § 547(b) turns on Voyager's insolvency during the preference period — a factual predicate that the trust's financial advisor, Berkeley Research Group, has supported through a balance-sheet solvency analysis showing a negative equity gap of approximately $1.1 billion as of the preference window's start date.

Default judgment, if entered, would allow the trust to seek turnover of the full $118.4 million plus pre-judgment interest under 28 U.S.C. § 1961. Realistically, enforcement against offshore or dissolved entities may recover 30–60 cents of the judgment amount, but even partial recovery would supplement a distribution pool that currently projects general unsecured recoveries at approximately 35–45 cents on the dollar against $1.8 billion in allowed claims — a significant improvement from the 25-cent estimate at plan confirmation (__[Haynes Boone Crypto Bankruptcy Monitor — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com/alerts/crypto-bankruptcy-monitor)__).

## Recommended Actions

**For creditors, claims purchasers, and recovery counsel active across the crypto insolvency docket:** In the Genesis matter, holders of Class 4 claims should monitor the July 10 hearing on DCG's forum-transfer motion with acute attention — if Judge Lane grants even a partial stay of discovery pending the forum-selection ruling, the distribution timeline will slip materially, and claims currently trading at 68–71 cents should be stress-tested against a 12–18 month delay scenario. Claims aggregators advising retail creditors should ensure KYC documentation is complete in the FTX Kroll portal before any June 30 system cutover, as the trust has signaled no further cure extensions for the Phase 1 holdback pool. In the Bitcoin kiosk sale, any party with knowledge of potential qualified competing bidders — particularly licensed money-service businesses with multi-state ATM infrastructure — should contact the debtors' investment banker immediately, as the June 30 auction floor is tight and a competitive process is the only mechanism to close the $21–28 million gap between stalking-horse and committee valuations. Counsel pursuing Celsius Litigation Trust claims against Mashinsky-adjacent parties should coordinate with trust counsel before initiating independent state-law actions to avoid claim-splitting complications under *In re Celsius*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01218. For Voyager creditors, the default judgment motion represents a meaningful near-term recovery catalyst; counsel should review whether any of the sixteen named defendants have assets amenable to immediate attachment in U.S. jurisdictions prior to the judgment being entered, given the risk of dissipation in offshore accounts.
