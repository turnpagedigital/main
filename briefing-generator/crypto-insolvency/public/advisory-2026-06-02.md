Today's principal delta is the emergence of Keyrock's stalking-horse bid for BlockFills, which entered Chapter 11 proceedings and now faces a contested 363 sale process — injecting fresh M&A energy into the distressed digital-asset deal pipeline that has otherwise been dominated by slow-motion plan confirmation fights. Meanwhile, the Genesis Global Capital, LLC plan administrator (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 23-10063, Judge Sean Lane, Courtroom 701) filed a motion on May 29, 2026 seeking approval of a supplemental reserve protocol for disputed claims in the Digital Currency Group affiliate sub-cluster, a procedural step with meaningful dollar consequences given that DCG-related intercompany claims are estimated in the estate's own schedules at approximately $1.1 billion (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### BlockFills Bankruptcy & Keyrock Acquisition — The 363 Sale Landscape

Keyrock, the Brussels-headquartered algorithmic market-making firm, has signed a stalking-horse asset purchase agreement to acquire substantially all operating assets of BlockFills out of bankruptcy, according to reporting by CoinDesk (__[CoinDesk](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitgFBVV95cUxNa3Q1NDFzZUcyWk92VGt2d2NhVWQwREVaam1BQ3kxdS1VUG9GMUJ1VTR1V0dKZjUza0R5Y2hDYUFoTEhIZHhfWkI1ZG91VzhNSlY3LW1mVk9qX0Rrczk5NWVucTVWSkdYMEFmYmYtZmlWYm9uUGFiSHctbUlMcW1KMjdoV2ZTVW1sRkQ3ckZMRVl0a2RGdnMzZmQyZjc4dXVIdDZjQ1VueE8tNHkwNS1aZGNUa0NZUQ — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__). BlockFills, a Chicago-based institutional crypto prime-brokerage and OTC liquidity desk, filed for Chapter 11 protection in the Northern District of Illinois (the precise docket number and assigned judge were not confirmed in available reporting as of press time and should be independently verified); however, the firm's pre-petition creditor profile reportedly includes several Tier-1 trading desks and at least one mid-market hedge fund with aggregate unsecured exposure in the range of $30–$50 million (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

The Keyrock bid, structured as a 363(b) "free and clear" acquisition under 11 U.S.C. § 363(f), will require the debtor to file a bid procedures motion seeking court approval of (i) a stalking-horse bid protections package (typically a 2–3% break-up fee and expense reimbursement cap), (ii) a bid deadline, and (iii) an auction date. Creditors and competing bidders should note that 363 sale processes in this district have proceeded on compressed timelines — 45 to 60 days from filing to auction is common for technology-forward financial firms where the going-concern value depreciates rapidly without active market-making. The Keyrock transaction, if approved without a superior bid, would transfer BlockFills' institutional client relationships, technology stack, and any regulated subsidiary licenses. Creditors holding trade claims arising from unsettled OTC transactions should promptly file proofs of claim and assess whether their exposure is captured within the sale's assumed-liability schedule or falls outside the purchased assets as a wind-down residual.

The strategic logic for Keyrock is evident: BlockFills operated as a deep-liquidity OTC desk serving institutional crypto desks that overlap substantially with Keyrock's existing market-making client base, and the distressed acquisition price should allow Keyrock to acquire customer relationships and technology at a fraction of replacement cost. For creditors, the dispositive question is whether Keyrock's stalking-horse bid is sufficient to generate meaningful residual value for the unsecured estate — a question that will turn on (i) the cure costs associated with assumed executory contracts, (ii) the scope of retained liabilities, and (iii) whether competing bidders emerge before the bid deadline (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### FTX — Distribution Mechanics, Disputed Claims, and the Offshore Claimant Problem

The FTX estate's post-confirmation administration (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 22-11068, Judge John Dorsey) has entered what the plan administrator, John J. Ray III acting through the FTX Recovery Trust, describes internally as "Phase II" of distribution — moving beyond the convenience-class cash-out to the numerically dominant body of larger creditors whose claims were denominated in crypto and converted to USD at the plan's frozen petition-date prices. The estate confirmed in its May 23, 2026 docket update that BitGo, the designated distribution agent for U.S.-accessible claimants, had processed approximately $1.2 billion in outgoing distributions as of that date, representing roughly 18% of the total confirmed distribution pool that the Second Amended Plan pegged at approximately $14.5–$16.3 billion (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The most consequential near-term procedural development is the estate's pending omnibus objection to a cluster of approximately 2,400 "non-KYC-compliant" customer claims. Under the plan's KYC/AML protocol, claimants who fail to complete identity verification through Kroll's online portal by the applicable deadline face claim subordination or disallowance. Judge Dorsey held a scheduling conference on May 28, 2026, and set a June 18, 2026 hearing date for the estate's motion to establish a final KYC cure period, after which non-compliant claims would be treated as forfeited under the plan's express terms (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). For claimants with pending or unverified customer claims, this deadline is existential — the distribution pool does not retain reserves for voluntarily non-compliant creditors post-final-cure-deadline.

The offshore claimant subset — primarily former FTX International customers with claims routed through the FTX.com platform rather than FTX.US — continues to face jurisdictional friction. The Bahamian joint provisional liquidators (JPLs) appointed by the Supreme Court of the Bahamas in In re FTX Digital Markets Ltd. (Case No. 2022/COM/00013, Justice Loren Klein) have formally acknowledged the cross-border protocol governing information sharing with the U.S. estate, but the allocation of recoveries between the two proceedings remains subject to a negotiated term sheet rather than a court-approved inter-estate settlement. The practical effect is that offshore customers must monitor both the Bahamian JPL process and the U.S. plan distribution portal simultaneously (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### Genesis Global Capital — DCG Claims Reserve and Plan Administrator Activity

The Genesis plan administrator, Mohsin Meghji of M3 Partners, filed a 47-page motion on May 29, 2026 in Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 23-10063 (Judge Sean Lane, Courtroom 701) seeking approval of a supplemental disputed-claims reserve protocol specifically addressing the tower of intercompany claims asserted by Digital Currency Group, Inc. and its affiliates, including Grayscale Investments, LLC and Genesis Global Holdco, LLC (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The motion requests authority under 11 U.S.C. §§ 502(c) and 1142(b) to estimate DCG-affiliate disputed claims at a provisional reserve amount of $340 million — substantially below the approximately $1.1 billion face value DCG has asserted — pending the outcome of the adversary proceeding Genesis filed against DCG in Adv. Pro. No. 23-01192 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Lane) alleging fraudulent transfer and breach of the master loan agreement (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The significance of this reserve dispute cannot be overstated from a creditor-recovery standpoint. Genesis's confirmed Chapter 11 plan provides pro-rata distributions to general unsecured creditors from a distributable pool that the administrator currently estimates at approximately $3.0–$3.5 billion in combined crypto and USD value, but the size of the DCG reserve directly determines how much of that pool flows to non-DCG creditors on the initial distribution date. If the court approves the $340 million reserve (rather than the $1.1 billion DCG seeks), non-DCG creditors would receive a materially larger initial distribution — potentially 5–8 percentage points higher recovery on allowed claims in the first tranche. DCG's opposition is due June 13, 2026; the hearing is scheduled June 25, 2026 before Judge Lane (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### Voyager Digital — Post-Effective-Date Wind-Down and Residual Claims

The Voyager Digital wind-down estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10943, Judge Michael Wiles, Courtroom 617) continues to administer residual distributions following the plan's effective date in May 2023 and the failed Binance.US and FTX acquisition transactions that preceded it. The wind-down trustee filed a status report on May 27, 2026, disclosing that aggregate distributions to former customers and creditors had reached approximately $1.33 billion across all distribution tranches — representing an approximate blended recovery of 35–40 cents on the dollar for general unsecured creditors when valued at current cryptocurrency prices, though the plan's methodology fixes recovery at petition-date coin prices, producing a different nominal percentage depending on the asset class held (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The remaining contested-claim population is small but high-stakes: the estate is pursuing avoidance actions under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 and 548 against two former institutional counterparties, with aggregate targeted recovery of approximately $28 million. One action — brought against a Cayman-domiciled proprietary trading firm — is in discovery before Magistrate Judge Ona Wang, with a discovery cutoff of August 15, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Creditors who received pre-petition preference payments in the 90-day window prior to the July 5, 2022 petition date and have not yet resolved demand letters from the estate should engage restructuring counsel before the trustee converts demand letters to formal adversary complaints.

### Celsius Network — Distribution Trust and Regulatory Overhang

The Celsius Network Distribution Trust (successor to the Chapter 11 debtor, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10964, Judge Martin Glenn, Courtroom 523) continues to administer the post-confirmation asset base, which includes the liquid cryptocurrency pool, equity interests in the reorganized mining subsidiary (now operating as Ionic Digital Inc.), and the proceeds of ongoing litigation against former CEO Alex Mashinsky, whose criminal case (U.S. v. Mashinsky, S.D.N.Y. 23-CR-347, Judge John Koeltl) is now in the post-plea sentencing phase following Mashinsky's December 2024 guilty plea to commodities fraud and market manipulation charges (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Sentencing, most recently continued to July 2026, will generate renewed coverage of the estate's parallel civil restitution claims, which the Distribution Trust has intervened to protect.

Total distributions from the Celsius estate through May 2026 are estimated at approximately $2.5 billion, representing roughly 67–72 cents on the dollar for eligible creditors in the liquid-crypto distribution class (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Creditors who elected to receive Ionic Digital equity in lieu of cash remain in a holding pattern: Ionic Digital's shares are not yet listed on a national exchange, and the trust's liquidity mechanism for equity holders has not yet been triggered. The trust's next status conference is scheduled for June 17, 2026 before Judge Glenn.

### Three Arrows Capital — Singapore Liquidation and Cross-Border Recovery

The Three Arrows Capital liquidation continues in dual-track proceedings: the British Virgin Islands insolvency (Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court, Commercial Division, Claim No. BVIHCM 2022/0119) and the Chapter 15 recognition proceeding in the Southern District of New York (In re Three Arrows Capital Ltd., Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10920, Judge Martin Glenn). The liquidators — Russell Crumpler and Christopher Farmer of Teneo Financial Advisory — filed their seventh interim report on May 20, 2026, disclosing aggregate asset recoveries to date of approximately $532 million against total proven claims exceeding $3.3 billion, implying a current recovery ratio below 20 cents on the dollar across the creditor pool (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__).

The liquidators' primary pending enforcement action — a $1.3 billion claim against Zhu Su and Kyle Davies, the firm's co-founders currently subject to travel restrictions and cooperation orders in Singapore — has proceeded to the discovery-compliance phase. The Singapore High Court issued an unless order on April 30, 2026 requiring both founders to produce specified financial records by June 16, 2026 or face adverse inference rulings. Creditors holding large proven claims in the BVI estate should monitor whether that compliance deadline produces material asset disclosures that the liquidators can convert into enforcement actions against concealed assets (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

**For creditors, claimants, and restructuring counsel active across the crypto insolvency docket as of June 2, 2026:** In the BlockFills/Keyrock 363 process, trade creditors and institutional counterparties should immediately retain local bankruptcy counsel in the Northern District of Illinois, demand copies of the debtor's schedules and the stalking-horse APA (particularly the assumed-liabilities schedule and cure-cost matrix), and evaluate whether filing a competing bid or credit-bidding any secured debt is viable before the anticipated bid deadline; the compression of going-concern value in market-making platforms makes early engagement non-negotiable. In the FTX estate, any customer-claimant who has not completed Kroll's KYC verification portal must do so before the June 18, 2026 hearing date — failure to cure will result in claim forfeiture under the confirmed plan's express terms, and there is no equitable tolling mechanism once the court enters a final KYC order. Genesis creditors should submit written comments or appear at the June 25, 2026 DCG reserve hearing if the $340 million reserve figure materially understates what they believe DCG's exposure to the estate should be — the reserve amount directly controls the size of the first distribution tranche flowing to non-DCG general unsecured creditors. Voyager creditors who received transfers in the 90 days before July 5, 2022 and have not responded to trustee demand letters should treat the August 15, 2026 discovery cutoff in the pending avoidance actions as a de facto settlement deadline; proactive resolution typically produces better outcomes than litigated default judgments. Celsius creditors holding Ionic Digital equity who have not registered with the Distribution Trust's equity record-keeper should do so now to preserve their position ahead of any liquidity event. Finally, Three Arrows creditors with proven BVI claims above $1 million should monitor the Singapore unless-order compliance deadline of June 16, 2026 — any asset disclosures produced by Zhu Su or Kyle Davies will be incorporated into the liquidators' next enforcement cycle, and creditors with direct jurisdictional hooks in Singapore, the UAE, or other relevant domiciles should brief local counsel now on attachment mechanics.
