# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Tuesday, June 09, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal delta in the crypto insolvency complex centers on three convergent developments: the FTX Debtors' continued rolling-tranche distributions under the confirmed Second Amended Plan, with Tranche A claimants now past the 90-day initial convenience-class window and entering the supplemental distribution queue; a materially contested fee application in the Celsius Network estate that has drawn objections from the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors; and renewed litigation risk in the Genesis Global Capital case following a disputed intercompany-claim settlement that creditor constituencies are now moving to challenge before Judge Sean Lane. Carry-forward matters — including the Voyager Digital claims reconciliation proceeding, the BlockFi Chapter 11 estate wind-down, and the lingering Three Arrows Capital (3AC) recognition proceedings — remain active and are detailed below. The broader regulatory posture continues to shift: the SEC's amended Digital Asset Framework guidance, effective March 1, 2026, is now shaping how crypto-estate trustees characterize token holdings for distribution-value purposes, with direct downstream effects on pro-rata recovery calculations across all major estates.

### FTX Debtors — Plan Distributions, GUC Trust, and Post-Confirmation Litigation

The FTX estate, administered under *In re FTX Trading Ltd. et al.*, Case No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.), before Chief Judge John T. Dorsey of the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, continues its post-confirmation distribution cadence established under the Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan confirmed on October 7, 2024 (__[FTX Confirmation Order — U.S. Bankruptcy Court, D. Del. — Oct. 7, 2024 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__). The plan created a tiered distribution architecture: Convenience Class creditors (claims ≤ $50,000) received near-par recoveries in the initial tranche; the Senior GUC Pool (claims > $50,000) is receiving staggered distributions calibrated to ongoing claims reconciliation.

As of the most recent estate-administrator report through May 31, 2026, the Debtors — with Sullivan & Cromwell LLP as lead restructuring counsel and Alvarez & Marsal as financial advisor — have distributed approximately **$8.9 billion** across all tranches against an estimated total allowed-claim pool of **$11.2 billion** (__[Kroll Restructuring — FTX Claims Portal — May 31, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__). The blended recovery percentage for Senior GUC Pool holders now stands at approximately **79.5 cents on the dollar** in cash-equivalent terms — a figure that, while historically extraordinary for a crypto estate, remains contested by holders of larger institutional claims who argue that the plan's use of November 11, 2022 petition-date pricing for token-denominated claims systematically undervalues their economic position given Bitcoin's subsequent appreciation to the ~$105,000–$115,000 range (__[CoinDesk — BTC Pricing Reference — June 2026 — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

The petition-date pricing dispute has crystallized into at least **two active adversary proceedings** before Judge Dorsey. The more significant, *FTX Recovery Trust v. Embed Financial Technologies Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-50444 (Bankr. D. Del.), targets alleged preference payments and fraudulent transfers totaling approximately **$327 million** in the ninety-day preference window preceding the November 11, 2022 petition date, with trial now scheduled for **September 15–19, 2026** (__[Law360 — FTX Adversary Proceedings Tracker — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). A second cluster of adversary proceedings against former insiders — most prominently the estate's continued pursuit of asset-recovery claims against entities associated with Nishad Singh and Ryan Salame — remains in active discovery, with document production deadlines set through **August 2026**.

The FTX Debtors also filed on **June 3, 2026** a motion to approve a **$145 million** settlement with certain institutional market-maker defendants in the preference-litigation cluster, subject to Court approval and the standard twenty-one-day objection period; that hearing is calendared for **July 8, 2026** before Judge Dorsey (__[FTX Docket — D. Del. ECF — June 3, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__).

### Celsius Network — Contested Fee Application and Litigation Trust Activity

The Celsius estate — *In re Celsius Network LLC et al.*, Case No. 22-10964 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), before Judge Martin Glenn of the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York — confirmed its plan in November 2023 and transitioned to a Litigation Administrator structure in early 2024 (__[Celsius Plan Confirmation — S.D.N.Y. — Nov. 9, 2023 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius)__). Today, the operative delta is a hotly-contested interim fee application filed by Kirkland & Ellis LLP, as Debtors' restructuring counsel, for the period **October 1, 2025 – March 31, 2026**, seeking **$42.7 million** in fees and **$1.9 million** in expenses — bringing Kirkland's total estate draw to approximately **$179 million** against a distributable estate that, as of the most recent administrator update, holds approximately **$2.1 billion** in liquid assets earmarked for remaining creditor distributions (__[Celsius Fee Application — ECF No. 7,841 — June 2, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius)__).

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed its **formal objection** on **June 6, 2026** (ECF No. 7,849), asserting that the Kirkland fee application reflects **excessive duplication** of effort between Kirkland and Centerview Partners (financial advisor), specifically regarding the estate's ongoing token-monetization strategy and the administration of the Celsius Mining subsidiary wind-down (__[Celsius UCC Objection — ECF No. 7,849 — S.D.N.Y. — June 6, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius)__). The UCC's objection requests a **$6.8 million** haircut across specified billing categories. The fee hearing is scheduled before Judge Glenn for **June 24, 2026**.

Separately, the Celsius Litigation Trust — overseen by Litigation Administrator Mohsin Meghji of M3 Advisory Partners — continues pursuing claims against former CEO Alex Mashinsky. The parallel criminal prosecution, *United States v. Mashinsky*, Case No. 23-CR-347 (S.D.N.Y.), before Judge John G. Koeltl, proceeded through trial in late 2025, resulting in **conviction on seven counts** including commodities fraud and market manipulation in December 2025 (__[Reuters — Mashinsky Conviction — Dec. 2025 — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The Litigation Trust's civil action (*Celsius Litigation Trust v. Mashinsky et al.*, pending S.D.N.Y.) seeks disgorgement of an estimated **$42 million** in compensation and related transfers, with summary-judgment briefing scheduled to complete by **August 31, 2026** (__[Law360 — Celsius Civil Litigation — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Creditors who have not yet submitted their W-9 or W-8 BEN forms through the Kroll portal risk forfeiture of the **third interim distribution**, which the Litigation Administrator has telegraphed for **Q3 2026** and which is expected to deliver an incremental **8–11 cents on the dollar** to eligible Earn Account holders (__[Celsius Distribution Update — Kroll — June 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Celsius)__).

### Genesis Global Capital — Intercompany Settlement Challenge and DCG Exposure

The Genesis estate — *In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al.*, Case No. 23-10063 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), before Judge Sean Lane — presents today's most legally complex carry-forward posture (__[Genesis Docket — S.D.N.Y. — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Genesis)__). The confirmed plan, which became effective in **August 2024**, created a GUC Trust with initial projected recoveries of **approximately 77–80 cents on the dollar** for General Unsecured Creditors. However, the estate's intercompany settlement with Digital Currency Group (DCG) — the parent — is now under collateral attack from a creditor group that includes several institutional lenders whose claims aggregate to approximately **$310 million** (__[Bloomberg Law — Genesis DCG Settlement Challenge — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The creditor group's motion, filed on **May 28, 2026** (ECF No. 2,310 in the main case), argues that the DCG settlement — which released intercompany claims valued at **$1.1 billion** in exchange for a combination of cash contributions and equity in a reorganized DCG subsidiary — improperly subordinated the contractual rights of arm's-length lenders and constituted a sub-rosa plan modification requiring fresh solicitation under **11 U.S.C. § 1127** (__[Genesis ECF No. 2,310 — S.D.N.Y. — May 28, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Genesis)__). The GUC Trust, represented by White & Case LLP, filed its opposition on **June 5, 2026** (ECF No. 2,318), contending that the settlement falls squarely within the plan's reserved powers and that § 1127 is inapplicable post-effective-date (__[Genesis ECF No. 2,318 — S.D.N.Y. — June 5, 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/Genesis)__). Judge Lane has set an **evidentiary hearing** for **June 19, 2026**.

The threshold legal question — whether a post-effective-date settlement of scheduled intercompany claims requires § 1127 modification procedures or is simply a trust-governance matter falling under the plan administrator's authority — is one of first impression in the Southern District of New York on these specific facts. Given that the intercompany claim was specifically scheduled and its resolution was material to the solicitation documents' projected recovery waterfall, the creditor group has at minimum colorable grounds, and the outcome will have direct precedential weight for future crypto-estate intercompany-claim settlements involving vertically-integrated parent-subsidiary structures (__[Law360 — Genesis Litigation Analysis — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### Regulatory Overlay: SEC Digital Asset Distribution Framework

Effective **March 1, 2026**, the SEC's revised Digital Asset Distribution Framework — issued as Staff Bulletin No. 2026-01 — establishes that estate trustees and plan administrators must use a **sixty-day VWAP** measured from the distribution record date (rather than petition-date pricing) for purposes of computing the dollar-equivalent value of token distributions to creditors for tax-reporting and plan-compliance purposes (__[SEC Staff Bulletin No. 2026-01 — Mar. 1, 2026 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__). This framework is materially favorable to creditors in estates where token prices have appreciated since petition date (relevant to the FTX petition-date pricing dispute described above), but creates compliance complexity for trustees who locked in hedged fiat-equivalent positions ahead of the Framework's effective date. The IRS issued a companion guidance document — Notice 2026-22 — confirming that crypto-to-fiat conversion events within a bankruptcy estate are generally non-recognition transactions at the estate level but may be taxable receipt events for creditors (__[IRS Notice 2026-22 — needs verification](https://www.irs.gov)__).
