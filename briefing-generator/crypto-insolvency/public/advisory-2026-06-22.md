# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Monday, June 22, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills / Reliz: Sale Approved — Plan Confirmation in Sight, Customer-Property Question Unresolved**

The dominant delta this week is the June 16 sale approval in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) (Bankr. D. Del.), Judge Thomas M. Horan presiding. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge on June 16, 2026 agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group, as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 The buyer is Keyrock SA, a Brussels-based market maker. 
Per the court filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property — confirmed in an official Bankruptcy Court document filed May 26, 2026.
 The sale hearing had been rescheduled at least twice before landing on June 16. 
Reliz was operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with a sale hearing set for June 16, 2026, and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.


The gap between enterprise value and aggregate claims remains stark. 
Court filings showed Reliz reporting assets between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 A $3.25 million § 363 sale price against that liability stack implies nominal general-unsecured recoveries absent additional estate litigation proceeds — the customer-property and claim-waterfall questions therefore drive whatever residual value the estate can marshal for the plan. 
The filing indicated there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
, but the magnitude remains unresolved pending plan confirmation.

**Adversary Proceedings: Artha Investment Partners and the Customer-Property Fight**

The sale does not resolve the estate's pending adversary litigation. 
The jointly administered Reliz entities, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), filed Adversary Proceeding No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners LLC.
 The Veritaglobal docket reflects that the summons response deadline for Adv. Pro. 26-50230 was June 22, 2026 — today. 
The summons established June 22, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. (EST) as the response deadline, with failure to respond deemed consent to entry of default judgment.
 This adversary proceeding is one of multiple actions the estate is running alongside the § 363 sale; recovery from these proceedings will likely determine whether unsecured claimants see any material distribution above the sale proceeds.

The prepetition customer-litigation backdrop remains material context. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026).
 
The plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and the courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 Those TROs were the immediate trigger for the Chapter 11 filing on March 15, 2026, and the underlying customer-property claims — whether BlockFills held customer crypto as estate property under § 541 or as trust/bailment assets outside the estate — have not been adjudicated on the merits. The plan confirmation process will almost certainly force a resolution, or at minimum a settlement posture, on these claims before Judge Horan can confirm.

The Unsecured Creditors' Committee, appointed March 27, 2026 (Docket No. 104), comprises: 
SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
The Committee retained FTI Consulting as its financial advisor, effective April 1, 2026.
 Creditors tracking recoveries should note that the Committee has FTI deployed and actively investigating; the June 19 docket activity (Docket Entry 446 and one additional filing per the tracked-case live data) is the latest signal that estate professionals remain actively engaged with weeks to go before the July 13 confirmation target.

The debtors' financial distress is well-documented in the first-day record. 
The company attributed its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
Compounding these balance sheet pressures were stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 
The June 12 hearing agenda noted that the sale of substantially all assets was going forward at the June 16 hearing, while a protective-order matter was adjourned and sale objections remained from Chicago Blackhawk Hockey Team and Oracle, per Docket 424.
 The Chicago Blackhawks objection — apparently tied to some contractual or IP relationship with the debtor — was sufficiently material to appear on the agenda; whether it was resolved at the June 16 hearing or carried forward will be reflected in the sale order once posted to the Veritaglobal docket.

**FTX Recovery Trust: Fifth Distribution Record Date Passed; July 31 Payment Window Approaching**

The FTX beat has moved from restructuring to distribution administration. 
The FTX Recovery Trust announced that the anticipated record date for the next distribution was June 16, 2026 for holders of allowed FTX claims and interests, with the distribution expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 That record date is now past. 
For transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 Anyone who completed a claim transfer but whose Rule 3001(e) notice period had not elapsed by June 16 is locked out of the July 31 tranche.

The Reserve reduction is also in motion. 
FTX plans to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, with funds to be allocated for the next payout following court approval.
 This is a materially smaller reduction than the prior round's $2.2 billion reduction — a signal that the estate's disputed-claims docket is thinning but not resolved.

The petition-date valuation issue remains the sharpest structural grievance across the crypto-bankruptcy wave. 
The Trust is paying Class 5A holders roughly 96% of allowed amounts through distributions running in Q1–Q2 2026, with a projected total recovery in the range of 118–120%; however, that recovery is paid on the petition-date value of November 11, 2022 — not on what crypto is worth today.
 
The petition date dollar value of Bitcoin was approximately $16,871. A claimant holding 1 BTC on FTX will receive approximately 120% of $16,871 (~$20,245), while that Bitcoin is now worth a multiple of that figure.
 The contrast with the Genesis approach — which provided in-kind, coin-for-coin distributions without capping at petition-date value — continues to draw creditor attention and informs how claims are being priced in the secondary market.

**Genesis / DCG Wind-Down: Litigation Posture**

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC, Case No. 23-10063 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) is in wind-down mode, but the litigation book remains open and material. 
Following the August 2, 2024 effective date, the Wind-Down Debtors' estates are managed by Mark Renzi as Plan Administrator, under the oversight of the Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) and the Litigation Oversight Committee (LOC), which oversee distributions to creditors and prosecution of causes of action retained by the estates.
 
The WDOC and LOC have retained Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP and Selendy Gay PLLC, respectively, as legal advisors.


The live adversary docket is dense. Per the Kroll restructuring site, active adversary proceedings include: *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. 26-03230; *Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital, LLC*, Adv. Pro. 25-01129; *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants*, Adv. Pro. 25-01097; *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants*, Adv. Pro. 25-01028; and *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. 24-01312. 
As part of the initial distributions, BTC creditors received 51% of their in-kind, coin-for-coin value (166% of their petition-date value), and ETH creditors received 66% of their in-kind recoveries
 — but the litigation fund has not been fully monetized. 
As part of Genesis' bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action toward various third parties, including parent company DCG; the fund is comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.
 The LOC's pursuit of DCG and insider defendants is accordingly the primary remaining value driver for Genesis creditors who have not yet received their full in-kind recovery.

The Genesis model stands as the principal alternative precedent to FTX-style dollarization. 
In the wave of "crypto winter" bankruptcy cases, virtually all crypto debtors dollarized claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values. Genesis took a different approach by obtaining court approval of a plan that provided in-kind recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values; because some crypto prices like Bitcoin nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected recoveries for crypto creditors.
 BlockFills and other pending cases will inevitably face the same binary: dollarize or in-kind. Given BlockFills' modest sale proceeds relative to the claims stack, the in-kind question may be academic there — but it remains the central precedential fault line for any future institutional insolvency.

Del.), confirm that your proof of claim was timely filed with the Veritaglobal claims agent and that you have reviewed the now-approved § 363 sale order — any claim that the Keyrock APA assumed as a "transferred liability" versus remained with the estate has direct recovery implications; additionally, the July 13 confirmation target compresses the timeline materially, so creditors with customer-property theories (§ 541 bailment / trust arguments parallel to Celsius's TOS litigation) should evaluate whether to file plan objections well before the confirmation hearing. In **FTX** (22-11068, Bankr. D. Del.), the June 16 record date for the fifth distribution has passed; any claim-transfer that was not processed and reflected on the register prior to that date — with the 21-day notice period elapsed — will miss the July 31 distribution tranche and should confirm eligibility for subsequent tranches. In **Genesis** (23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), LOC-funded adversary proceedings against DCG and insider defendants remain the primary remaining value event; creditors who have received partial in-kind distributions should monitor Adv. Pro. 26-03230 and 24-01312 for any settlement posture or scheduling orders that signal when the litigation fund will yield additional distributions.
