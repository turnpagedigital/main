# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Tuesday, June 30, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — confirmation clock now at 13 days.** The operative delta since yesterday's advisory: the voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline are both tomorrow — July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET — with the confirmation hearing set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Thomas M. Horan. 
The Court entered the disclosure statement order on May 28, 2026 (Docket No. 388), establishing the solicitation framework, objection deadlines, and confirmation schedule.
 
The Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket No. 391) is the operative plan document; the voting deadline expires July 1 at 4:00 p.m. ET, the confirmation objection deadline is the same, and the confirmation hearing is July 13.
 Any creditor who has not submitted a ballot by 4:00 p.m. today Eastern time is effectively foreclosed from the plan vote. This is the most consequential near-term gate in the case.

The plan's structural architecture merits close attention. 
The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization centered on a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 That dual-track structure — a customer-led NewCo plus a liquidating trust to monetize remaining estate assets — is the same template used in the Celsius and Voyager resolutions, and it leaves open the customer-property question in a way that matters for creditor recoveries: the plan's treatment of customer digital asset claims will determine whether those creditors share ratably with general unsecured claimants or receive a different (and typically better) distribution through the trust mechanism.

**The Keyrock § 363 Sale — Approved or Pending?** 
Keyrock SA agreed to a $3.25 million purchase price and, per a court document filed May 26, 2026, was declared the "Successful Bidder" for substantially all of the debtors' assets, certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
A hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026, with parties continuing to collaborate on the administrative process in the interim.
 The June 16 sale-approval hearing predates this advisory by two weeks; as of the last docket sync (June 26, 2026, Docket Entry 467), no entry description is available to confirm whether the § 363 order was entered. The sale approval status is therefore unconfirmed from today's data and should be verified directly against the Veritaglobal docket portal before treating it as closed. What is clear is that 
Keyrock stated that final completion remains subject to final court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in its bid.
 Regulatory sign-off — likely including applicable crypto licensing requirements — is a distinct gating condition that does not automatically follow from the bankruptcy court's order.

The transaction's economics are instructive for the broader wave. 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025 (a 28% year-over-year increase) and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 A $3.25 million gross purchase price against that operating footprint — and against 
$50 million to $100 million in scheduled assets and $100 million to $500 million in scheduled liabilities
 — illustrates how severely distressed-crypto asset sales discount going-concern value when the deposit suspension and customer-fraud allegations have already compromised the client base. 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 With $3.25 million as the gross sale price, general unsecured creditors relying solely on the sale proceeds face recoveries in the low single-digit percentages — making the liquidating trust's success (and the outcome of the adversary proceedings) determinative for most claimants.

**Adversary Proceeding: Artha Investment Partners.** 
The debtors filed an adversary proceeding, Case 26-50230-TMH, in the Bankr. D. Del., against Artha Investment Partners LLC.
 The complaint is not publicly described in detail from available docket data, but its filing context — April 9, 2026, early in the case — suggests a fraudulent transfer or preference claim consistent with the estate's mandate to identify prepetition value extractions. The summons required a response by June 22, 2026; that deadline has now passed, raising the prospect of a default judgment motion if Artha has not answered.

**Customer-Property Question — § 541 Framing.** The central question the BlockFills case shares with every institutional crypto-insolvency that preceded it — Celsius, Voyager, Prime Trust — is whether customer digital assets deposited on the platform constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in bailment/trust for the depositor's benefit. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 Unlike Celsius, which litigated the TOS issue through a lengthy contest before the Honorable Martin Glenn in the S.D.N.Y., BlockFills' institutional-only customer base (
no retail customers — exclusively institutional, high-net-worth, and sophisticated traders
) changes the TOS analysis: institutional master agreements often contain explicit title-transfer language or netting provisions that control how a court characterizes the custody relationship. Counsel for any creditor asserting a trust or bailment claim against the BlockFills estate should review the specific customer agreement in place prior to the petition date before assuming the Celsius analytical framework applies.

The pre-petition liquidity collapse arose from a cascading series of counterparty defaults and failed strategic pivots. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 That the Celsius arbitration contributed to the BlockFills collapse is a notable downstream effect of the 2022 crypto contagion wave: one major insolvency's recovery litigation directly precipitated another. 
Compounding these balance sheet pressures were stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.


**FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Record Date Passed Today; NFT Claims Open Today.** 
The anticipated record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims was set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence July 31, 2026.
 That record date is now two weeks past. Creditors holding transferred claims need to confirm their transfer was fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of June 16; any transfer still in the 21-day objection window as of that date does not qualify for the July 31 round. 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.


Separately, today — June 30, 2026 — is an independent activation date within the FTX plan. 
Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan become eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026.
 NFT claim holders who have not yet completed KYC and onboarding with an approved service provider should treat today as an urgent trigger.

The cumulative distribution picture to date: 
the FTX payout schedule includes four completed distribution rounds, approximately $10 billion paid out in total, and a fifth distribution scheduled for July 31, 2026.
 
The fourth distribution — $2.2 billion on March 31 — lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reached 100%, while Class 7 received a cumulative 120%.
 The Class 5A Dotcom group, where most non-U.S. customer claims sit, 
has been paid approximately 96% of principal so far, with a projected total of approximately 118–120% once the 9% annual consensus rate interest is added.
 The petition-date valuation caveat remains the persistent creditor grievance: 
every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.


The estate continues to pursue clawback recovery in parallel. 
Genesis Digital Assets (the bitcoin mining company, distinct from the Genesis lending entity) is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion; Genesis is pushing to dismiss the suit, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.
 
As part of ongoing claims reconciliation, the estate filed an amended notice seeking court approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion
 — a move that, if approved, would free additional capital for the fifth distribution or subsequent rounds.

**Genesis/DCG Post-Wind-Down Litigation Cluster.** Genesis completed its chapter 11 wind-down and distributed approximately $4 billion in assets. The post-confirmation litigation now occupies the adversary docket. 
A pretrial conference in the Genesis matters was held June 9, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 The active adversary proceedings before the S.D.N.Y. include: 
Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., No. 26-03230; DCG v. Genesis Global Cap., Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129;
 
Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028; and Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc., Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.
 The DCG/Silbert securities class action in the D. Conn. is a parallel track: 
on February 24, 2026, Judge Stefan Underhill denied DCG's motion to dismiss in McGreevy v. Digital Currency Group, No. 23-cv-00082 (D. Conn.), finding that plaintiffs adequately pleaded violations of both the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, and that the Genesis lending program constituted a security under federal law.
 
The ruling also lifted the PSLRA discovery stay, allowing plaintiffs to begin obtaining evidence.
 That discovery activation, coming on top of the adversary-proceeding activity in the bankruptcy court, means DCG is now simultaneously litigating on at least five fronts — and document productions in the district-court action may cross-pollinate the bankruptcy adversary proceedings.

## Recommended Actions

BlockFills creditors must treat today as a hard deadline: ballots and plan objections are due tomorrow, July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and any creditor who misses that window has no standing to contest confirmation at the July 13 hearing before Judge Horan. Creditors whose claims were subject to the customer-property analysis under 11 U.S.C. § 541 should review the specific master trading or custody agreement they held with Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. or its affiliated debtors — the institutional-grade TOS language may support a trust/bailment defense that general unsecured treatment would eliminate. Creditors with claims arising from alleged misappropriation (as in the pre-petition Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y.), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.) actions, both stayed by the automatic stay) should coordinate with bankruptcy counsel on whether their fraud-based claims support an enhanced plan treatment or nondischargeability argument. For FTX claimants: confirm today whether you have Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims and complete onboarding immediately, as June 30 is the distribution-process activation date; for transferred claims, verify that your transfer was fully processed and free of pending objections as of the June 16 record date before relying on July 31 as your expected payment date. In the Genesis/DCG cluster, the PSLRA stay lift in McGreevy v. DCG, No. 23-cv-00082 (D. Conn.) is now active — investors who lent digital assets to Genesis and have not yet joined the class action should assess their standing promptly, as discovery is underway and the posture is more favorable post-denial of the motion to dismiss.
