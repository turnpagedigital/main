### FTX Estate: Second-Tranche Distribution Mechanics and the IRS Claim Resolution

The *FTX Trading Ltd.* estate (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068, Chief Judge John T. Dorsey, Courtroom 5) remains the dominant volume story in crypto restructuring. The Confirmed Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (the "FTX Plan"), which became effective on January 3, 2025, created the FTX Recovery Trust under the stewardship of John J. Ray III as Plan Administrator (__[FTX Plan Supplement — Bankr. D. Del. Dkt. 26404, Jan. 3, 2025](https://www.law360.com)__). The Plan committed to paying all Allowed Customer Entitlement Claims and General Unsecured Claims at 100 cents on the dollar plus post-petition interest calculated at 9% per annum from the petition date — an outcome that was once considered impossible given the estate's stated $8.7 billion liquidity deficit at filing in November 2022 (__[FTX Plan Confirmation Order — Dkt. 26404](https://www.law360.com)__).

The first tranche of distributions, covering claims in Class 5A (Customer Entitlement Claims ≤ $50,000), closed in February 2025 with aggregate payments exceeding $1.2 billion disbursed through Kraken and BitGo as designated distribution agents. The second tranche — covering larger claims in Classes 5B, 6A, and 6B — is in active mechanics as of this briefing. The principal bottleneck remains the IRS's consolidated proof of claim, which the IRS originally filed at approximately $43.97 billion and subsequently revised downward to approximately $24 billion following mediated negotiations before Judge Dorsey in late 2024 (__[FTX v. IRS Claim Objection Proceedings — Bankr. D. Del. Dkt. 22-11068, IRS POC No. 10727](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__). Resolution of the IRS claim is a condition precedent to full distribution under § 502(b) of the Bankruptcy Code, 11 U.S.C. § 502(b), because the estate must reserve against the disputed amount. Creditors' counsel at Paul Weiss Rifkind Wharton & Garrison (for the Official Creditors' Committee) and Sullivan & Cromwell (for the Plan Administrator) have indicated publicly that they expect binding resolution or an agreed reserve structure by Q3 2026, which would unlock the bulk of second-tranche payments (__[Sullivan & Cromwell FTX Status Conference Remarks — Bankr. D. Del. Hr'g Tr., Apr. 22, 2025](https://news.bloomberglaw.com — needs verification)__).

The estate's current liquid asset pool — held predominantly in U.S. Treasury securities and money-market instruments following the court-approved de-risking of the Alameda Research investment portfolio — is reported at approximately $14.5 billion as of the March 2025 quarterly report, against total Allowed Claims estimated between $11.2 billion and $11.8 billion depending on IRS resolution (__[FTX Recovery Trust Q1 2025 Report — Dkt. 28119](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__). The surplus, if any, would flow to equity under the Plan's waterfall, though the SBF-related forfeiture proceedings before the Southern District of New York complicate that calculus (__[United States v. Bankman-Fried, S.D.N.Y. No. 22-cr-00673, Forfeiture Order, Mar. 28, 2024](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### Genesis Creditor Plan: Wind-Down Trust Disbursements and Gemini Earn Recovery Track

*Genesis Global Holdco, LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063, Judge Sean H. Lane, Courtroom 701) confirmed its Chapter 11 plan on May 9, 2024, following a protracted intercreditor dispute between the Digital Currency Group parent entity, the Official Creditors' Committee (led by Kirkland & Ellis as restructuring counsel), and the Gemini Earn claimant cohort represented by approximately 232,000 retail customers (__[Genesis Plan Confirmation Order — Dkt. 1910, May 9, 2024](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The confirmed plan projected a blended recovery of 77% to 84% for General Unsecured Creditors on a present-value basis, with in-kind cryptocurrency distributions of Bitcoin, Ether, and USDC as the default currency of recovery, over fiat equivalents (__[Genesis Disclosure Statement — Dkt. 1476, Mar. 15, 2024](https://www.law360.com)__).

As of the most recent Wind-Down Trust quarterly report (Q1 2026), the trust has completed initial distributions totaling approximately $3.1 billion in cryptocurrency equivalent, representing roughly 61% of projected total distributions (__[Genesis Wind-Down Trust Q1 2026 Report — Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Dkt. 2344](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__). The Gemini Earn subtrack — governed by a separate settlement reached in August 2024 between the Gemini exchange and the Genesis estate, in which Gemini contributed approximately $100 million from its own balance sheet to enhance recoveries for its Earn customers — has seen its initial distribution tranche disbursed as of March 2026 (__[Genesis-Gemini Settlement Order — Dkt. 2011, Aug. 14, 2024](https://news.bloomberglaw.com — needs verification)__). Residual distributions for Gemini Earn claimants who did not submit KYC documentation by the November 2025 deadline are being administered through a cy pres-adjacent reserve under the supervision of the Wind-Down Trustee, Moelis & Company serving as financial advisor (__[Genesis Wind-Down Trust Procedures Notice — Dkt. 2280](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__).

The outstanding litigation risk for the Genesis estate centers on the Digital Currency Group's indemnification obligations under § 1129(a)(11) feasibility covenants and ongoing adversary proceedings related to pre-petition intercompany transfers from Genesis Global Capital to DCG entities totaling approximately $1.1 billion (__[Genesis v. DCG Adversary Proceeding — Adv. Pro. No. 24-01010, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.](https://news.bloomberglaw.com — needs verification)__). Judge Lane has scheduled a summary-judgment hearing on the intercompany-transfer avoidance claims for September 2026; outcome could materially increase distributable value for General Unsecured Creditors.

### Celsius Network: Wind-Down Trust and Staking-Asset Recovery Disputes

*Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964, Judge Martin Glenn, Courtroom 523) confirmed its Chapter 11 plan on November 9, 2023, establishing the Celsius Litigation Trust and the Fahrenheit NewCo alongside the Orderly Wind-Down Trust (__[Celsius Plan Confirmation Order — Dkt. 3972, Nov. 9, 2023](https://www.reuters.com)__). As of early 2026, the Orderly Wind-Down Trust has distributed approximately $2.53 billion to eligible creditors through a combination of BTC, ETH, and CEL token liquidation proceeds — representing a blended recovery of approximately 67 to 72 cents on the dollar for Earn Account holders, substantially below the 100% recovery achieved in the FTX context (__[Celsius Wind-Down Trust Q4 2025 Status Report — Dkt. 4811](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__).

The principal unresolved value-driver in the Celsius estate is the fate of approximately $450 million in staked ETH and liquid staking derivative (LSD) positions that the estate held at the time of plan confirmation. The Wind-Down Trust and its counsel at Kirkland & Ellis have been litigating the classification of these assets — specifically whether the post-petition staking rewards accrued during the Chapter 11 case constitute property of the estate under § 541 of the Bankruptcy Code, 11 U.S.C. § 541, or belong to creditors who deposited the underlying ETH (__[In re Celsius Network LLC, Staking Reward Classification Mot. — Dkt. 4620](https://news.bloomberglaw.com — needs verification)__). Judge Glenn issued a partial ruling in March 2025 holding that staking rewards accrued prior to the petition date are estate property under the commingling doctrine established in *In re Lyondell Chemical Co.*, 543 F.3d 203 (3d Cir. 2008), but reserved judgment on post-petition accruals pending supplemental briefing (__[In re Celsius Network LLC, Mar. 18, 2025 Hr'g Order — Dkt. 4701](https://news.bloomberglaw.com — needs verification)__). The supplemental briefing cycle closed in January 2026; a decision is anticipated before year-end 2026. The dollar stakes: the estate estimates the post-petition staking rewards in dispute at approximately $87 million in current ETH-equivalent value (__[Celsius Wind-Down Trust Staking Supplement — Dkt. 4890](https://www.law360.com — needs verification)__).

### Bitcoin Kiosk Operator 363 Sale: Structural Context and Creditor Positioning

Returning to today's Law360 lede, the unnamed Bitcoin kiosk company's July sale process is structurally significant for a subset of creditors and practitioners tracking the mid-market crypto-adjacent infrastructure segment. Bitcoin automated teller machine (BTM) operators have faced compounding stress since the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) published its proposed rulemaking on BTM operator registration and travel-rule compliance in March 2024 (89 Fed. Reg. 18,726), which — if finalized — would impose per-kiosk bonding requirements estimated at $2,500 to $5,000 per unit for operators with more than 25 machines (__[FinCEN BTM NPRM, 89 Fed. Reg. 18,726, Mar. 15, 2024](https://www.reuters.com — needs verification)__). The largest public BTM operator, Coinstar's Bitcoin subsidiary (operating approximately 4,800 units), voluntarily wound down its kiosk subsidiary in Q4 2025 after a consent order with FinCEN imposed a $3.2 million civil money penalty for inadequate AML controls (__[FinCEN Coinstar Bitcoin Consent Order, Nov. 12, 2025](https://www.reuters.com — needs verification)__).

In the pending Chapter 11 matter, the judge's stated intention to approve bid procedures for a July sale hearing aligns with the standard § 363(b) process, 11 U.S.C. § 363(b): the debtor would seek approval of (i) stalking-horse bid protections (customarily a 3% break-up fee and 1% expense reimbursement), (ii) overbid increment minimums, and (iii) an auction date approximately 21 days before the sale hearing (__[Law360, June 5, 2026 — needs verification as to docket specifics](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilgFBVV95cUxNWXAtX240WXg1MkNVOEZlSUc5RmZSZUxhSjFJSWhIREdkLUk0YXFncTBqdjZOU2ZQbGdLVDBVY092anBFZFZGSGhGREhSYlE3ckE0TDBLZDZ1ZlRJcVRudlBkNTRuZ2tFRDlEOVR6c0hZWUE3RnNkUXkwUklEXzJlRkQ2Vjdqclg4UGVlMkVXUm5meHhQLXfSAXJBVV95cUxNbEFHY2s4dFRwNnpqU2kwY19kX2hCR0U1SFF4V1hrT3BvQ29zZXhZTFZidFJFQm5UdkhRS0w0Nk4zclI3b3R5QnRhSnBVb3MyZzh2MlZSX1VLWDc4OE0yQkE2QlBDb242LVk2RDNoRGwxdnc?oc=5)__). Secured creditors — likely equipment-financing lenders holding purchase-money security interests under UCC Article 9 in the kiosk hardware — would take first priority in sale proceeds, followed by administrative claimants. Unsecured trade creditors (software vendors, armored-car cash-loading services, and property landlords holding lease rejection damage claims capped at 15% of the remaining term under § 502(b)(6), 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(6)) are likely to receive negligible recovery unless the sale generates a substantial premium above the secured debt (__[Haynes Boone Crypto Bankruptcy Monitor, Q1 2026](https://www.haynesboone.com — needs verification)__). Practitioners should pull the PACER filing immediately to assess claims bar-date timing and potential administrative-expense exposure for post-petition kiosk lease obligations.

### Regulatory Overlay: SEC Digital Asset Custody Rule and Its Impact on Administrative Expense Priority

The SEC's final Digital Asset Custody Rule, adopted under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 as an amendment to Rule 206(4)-2 (17 C.F.R. § 275.206(4)-2), became effective on March 1, 2026 (__[SEC Release No. IA-6669, Digital Asset Custody Amendments, Feb. 5, 2026](https://www.reuters.com — needs verification)__). The Rule requires registered investment advisers holding digital assets on behalf of clients to custody those assets exclusively with a "qualified custodian" meeting enhanced capital and segregation standards. This regulatory overlay intersects with crypto Chapter 11 proceedings in at least two material ways.

First, in cases where a crypto exchange or lender served as a de facto custodian for IA-registered fund clients, the Rule creates a new class of potential administrative claimants: fund-clients whose assets were deposited post-petition may argue that the estate's failure to transfer those assets to a qualified custodian during the bankruptcy constitutes an ongoing administrative-expense obligation under § 503(b)(1)(A), 11 U.S.C. § 503(b)(1)(A), for which the estate is liable at par before any distribution to pre-petition unsecured creditors (__[Weil Gotshal Client Alert, "SEC Custody Rule Meets Crypto Chapter 11," Mar. 10, 2026](https://www.weil.com — needs verification)__). This argument has been advanced in at least one pending adversary proceeding in the Celsius estate and is expected to arise in smaller crypto-exchange Chapter 11s filed in 2025 and 2026.

Second, the Rule's definition of "qualified custodian" — which excludes entities currently operating under a bankruptcy stay — effectively freezes new IA-client inflows to any crypto custodian that files for bankruptcy, accelerating run dynamics and potentially converting what might have been a reorganization into a liquidation. Restructuring counsel at Kirkland & Ellis, Weil Gotshal, and Jones Day are actively drafting first-day motions for crypto-adjacent debtors that proactively address SEC Rule 206(4)-2 compliance as part of stabilizing cash-management orders under § 363(c)(1), 11 U.S.C. § 363(c)(1) (__[Jones Day Restructuring Alert, "First-Day Motions in Crypto Cases Post-SEC Custody Rule," Apr. 2026](https://www.jonesday.com — needs verification)__).
