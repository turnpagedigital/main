### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings) — Confirmation Posture & the Estate-Property Question


The case has been operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path; the sale hearing was set for June 16, 2026 and confirmation was targeted for July 13, 2026. The case commenced on March 15, 2026 after a liquidity crisis tied to counterparty exposures, the Babel Finance bankruptcy, a failed equipment-loan settlement with Nexo, an unpaid Celsius arbitration award, customer litigation and TROs, and a February 2026 crypto-market decline that forced the debtors to suspend deposits and withdrawals.


The sale track concluded successfully before confirmation. 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for substantially all of BlockFills' assets — including certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property — at a purchase price of $3.25 million, per a bankruptcy filing, and Keyrock confirmed this in a statement citing the court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
A hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 The sale transaction feeds into the plan structure: proceeds flow to the liquidating trust, not to an operating NewCo.

The central legal question — and the one with the highest dollar consequence for the ~$145 million in unsecured customer and trade claims — is whether customer digital assets are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or held outside the estate in trust or bailment. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission has shaped the entire case. 
On January 16, 2026, one creditor (Alberta) transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes. The Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients on February 11, 2026, that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."



The Debtors have also raised a procedural defense: objecting customers had not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for a determination of property of the estate, and had not attempted tracing of specific funds. If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, distributed pro rata under the Plan, and serve as the basis for the cash-collateral arrangement.


Contrast the contractual record against the actual operational record. 
One creditor stated at hearing that the onboarding agreement BlockFills provided "specifically said that all customer deposits are going to be completely segregated from the general balance sheet of the company."
 That gap — between the TOS representation of segregation and the debtors' admitted commingled practice — is the live § 541 dispute. The plan's treatment of customer claims as general unsecured rather than trust beneficiaries is the key structural bet at confirmation.

Counsel alignment: 
the Debtors are represented by McDermott Will & Schulte LLP (Darren Azman, Gregg Steinman, Joseph Evans, Ethan Dover, Andrew Mark, Carole Wurzelbacher, Catherine Bloomberg) and Katten Muchin Rosenman LLP (Peter Siddiqui), with BRG's Mark Renzi as CRO.
 
The Ad Hoc Group is represented by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP (Sean O'Neal, Jane VanLare, Theodore Leonhardt, Aracely Valencia, Brendan Gerdts) and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor, LLP (Sean Greecher).
 The Unsecured Creditors Committee, constituted March 27, 2026, 
includes SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc., with FTI Consulting (Michael Cordasco) retained as Committee financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.


The capital structure in brief: 
the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The Keyrock sale at $3.25 million covers approximately 2.2 cents on the dollar of stated unsecured obligations — illustrating why the estate-property classification and any available fraudulent-transfer recovery against insiders are the only meaningful recovery vectors for customer claimants.

Two prepetition customer actions remain in the background, stayed by the automatic stay: 
Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd. (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026), and on March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 
A parallel action by 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram (N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:26-cv-02451, filed March 5, 2026) asserted similar claims against multiple Debtor entities and named individuals, including Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace.
 These stay-wrapped actions become leverage in any Special Committee insider-recovery investigation.

The macro balance-sheet context: 
the company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.

The litigated DCG note liability — 
Genesis told a New York bankruptcy judge that its parent company DCG should not be able to argue that it has no further obligations
 — is the core recovery question. 
As part of the Genesis bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action toward various third parties, including parent company DCG. The fund is comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.
 Initial in-kind distributions to creditors have already been made: 
Bitcoin creditors received 51.28% recoveries, ETH creditors received 65.87% recoveries, and Solana creditors received 29.58% of recoveries.
 The structural precedent Genesis set — 
Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition-date value; instead, it sought to provide restitution through in-kind, coin-for-coin recoveries until creditors are paid in full
 — remains the most creditor-favorable plan architecture in the post-2022 wave.

### Cross-Docket Pattern: The § 541 Commingling Fault Line

BlockFills and the earlier Celsius/Voyager/FTX wave share the same structural defect: platforms that represented segregation to customers while operating on a single commingled balance sheet. 
In the wave of crypto bankruptcy cases, the valuation of crypto creditors' claims became a key issue. Virtually all crypto debtors "dollarized" claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values. Genesis, however, obtained court approval of a plan that provided in-kind recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values — and because some crypto prices (like Bitcoin) nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected recoveries for crypto creditors.


BlockFills presents the Celsius commingling fact pattern but without Celsius's scale or the benefit of a well-developed trust-beneficiary record in the TOS. The outcome of the § 541 determination in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings* will matter both to customer-claimants in this case and as a data point in the ongoing Celsius appellate record. The Special Committee's insider-investigation mandate — granted to a disinterested director on March 12, 2026 
vested with authority to manage potential conflict matters, including investigating, releasing, or settling potential claims or causes of action
 — is the most likely source of incremental recovery beyond the $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds.
