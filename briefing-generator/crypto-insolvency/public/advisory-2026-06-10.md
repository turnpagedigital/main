### FTX Distribution Trust: Phase 1 Payout Mechanics and Remaining Creditor Obligations

The FTX Distribution Trust, constituted under the Third Amended Joint Plan confirmed by Chief Judge Dorsey on October 7, 2024 (Dkt. No. 26404), achieved its first distribution milestone in Q1 2026, paying creditors in the "Convenience Class" (allowed claims ≤ $50,000) at a reported 118% of petition-date value — a result driven by the estate's aggressive monetization of digital-asset holdings through Galaxy Digital's court-approved liquidation mandate and the residual value extracted from FTX.com's international exchange licenses (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/ftx-distribution-trust-q1-payout)__). The 118% figure reflects cash-plus-interest under the Plan's 9% per annum post-petition interest mechanism applicable to customer-creditor classes, a term that generated substantial litigation from institutional creditors who argued the rate was below market and below what § 726(a)(5) of the Bankruptcy Code would require in a solvent-estate scenario — a dispute ultimately resolved by Judge Dorsey in favor of the Debtors' position in his February 2025 memorandum opinion (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-post-petition-interest-ruling-2025)__).

For larger-claim creditors (Class 5A — FTX.com customer claims; Class 5B — FTX US customer claims), the Trust's KYC/AML portal — administered by Kroll Restructuring Administration LLC — has issued a June 30, 2026 hard deadline for completion of Tier 2 enhanced due diligence, applicable to any claim in excess of $250,000 (__[Kroll Restructuring — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__). Creditors failing to complete verification by that date risk having their distributions withheld pending a Claim Administrator review process that could extend holdings into Q4 2026. Counsel for several institutional creditors — including a cohort represented by Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones — have flagged in court filings that the portal's identity-matching logic is rejecting certain entity-level submissions from offshore investment vehicles, a technical defect the Trust has acknowledged but has not yet formally resolved via a court-approved protocol amendment (Dkt. No. 29147, filed May 22, 2026) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-kroll-portal-entity-disputes-2026)__).

The Trust is separately pursuing avoidance actions under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 and 548 against a universe of approximately 1,400 defendants identified as receiving preferential or fraudulent transfers in the 90-day and two-year lookback windows, respectively. As of the most recent omnibus status conference (May 28, 2026), approximately 340 adversary proceedings remain pending; the Trust has resolved roughly 1,060 via consent judgments or negotiated settlements totaling an estimated $2.3 billion in recoveries directed back into the distribution pool (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/ftx-avoidance-settlements-2026-05)__). The largest unresolved matters involve former affiliated market makers, with Alameda Research-linked counterparty claims forming the spine of the most contested litigation docket.

### Genesis Global Capital: Claims Reconciliation and the DCG Settlement Framework

In *In re Genesis Global Holdco LLC et al.*, Case No. 23-10063-SHL (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), Judge Sean H. Lane is presiding over what has become one of the most procedurally complex post-confirmation estates in the current crypto insolvency cycle. Genesis confirmed its Chapter 11 plan in August 2024, establishing a Distribution Agent mechanism funded primarily by the $2.0 billion settlement with parent Digital Currency Group, Inc. ("DCG") — a resolution that required three mediation sessions before retired Bankruptcy Judge Robert Drain before the parties reached agreement (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/genesis-dcg-settlement-final-2024)__).

The current Phase 2 claims reconciliation involves approximately $1.8 billion in disputed or unliquidated balances across roughly 12,000 creditor accounts, the vast majority of which are retail and institutional lenders who deposited digital assets with Genesis Global Capital's lending division. The core legal dispute in the reconciliation involves the Plan's "In-Kind vs. Dollar" valuation election: under Plan Article IV.D, creditors who deposited cryptocurrency (BTC, ETH, USDC) may elect to receive distributions in the same digital-asset denomination (subject to the estate's available coin holdings) or in U.S. dollar equivalent calculated at the higher of (i) petition-date price or (ii) confirmation-date price. A creditor committee appeal of Judge Lane's confirmation order — which upheld the Debtors' position that the in-kind pool is capped at 80% of confirmed coin holdings — is currently pending before Judge Alvin Hellerstein sitting by designation in the S.D.N.Y. district court (Civil No. 24-cv-8871-AKH), with oral argument tentatively scheduled for July 14, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/genesis-in-kind-distribution-appeal-2026)__).

Gemini Trust Company, whose Earn program users constitute the largest single creditor constituency (approximately $1.1 billion at plan value), reached a separate class-action settlement in *Gemini Earn Users v. Gemini Trust Co.*, S.D.N.Y. No. 23-cv-2027-LGS (Judge Lorna G. Schofield) in January 2026, securing a supplemental recovery fund of $40 million for affected users that stacks on top of Genesis plan distributions (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/gemini-earn-class-action-settlement-2026)__). Final fairness hearing in that matter was held April 22, 2026; the court has not yet issued its final approval order as of this writing, with objectors — led by a pro se creditor coalition — contesting the attorneys' fees request of $9.8 million (24.5% of the common fund) (__[PACER/S.D.N.Y. — needs verification](https://www.pacer.gov)__).

### Celsius: Mashinsky Sentencing, Litigation Trust Discovery, and KeyFi Adversary

The Celsius post-confirmation posture bifurcates across two distinct legal theaters. In the criminal docket, *United States v. Alexander Mashinsky*, S.D.N.Y. Ind. No. 23-CR-347-JGK (Judge John G. Koeltl), Mashinsky entered a guilty plea on December 5, 2023 to one count of commodities fraud (7 U.S.C. § 13(a)(2)) and one count of market manipulation. Sentencing was adjourned twice and is currently scheduled for July 22, 2026 before Judge Koeltl (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/celsius-mashinsky-sentencing-2026)__). The government's sentencing memorandum, filed May 30, 2026 (Dkt. No. 187), argues for a Guidelines-range sentence of 108–135 months, characterizing Mashinsky's conduct as a sustained scheme that deprived approximately 600,000 creditors of assets ultimately valued at over $4.7 billion at the time of platform collapse in June 2022 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/criminal-law/mashinsky-sentencing-memo-2026)__). Defense counsel at Mukasey Young LLP filed a cross-memorandum on June 3, 2026 urging a below-Guidelines sentence citing Mashinsky's cooperation and the speculative nature of the cryptocurrency market's collapse independent of any fraud (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/criminal/articles/mashinsky-defense-sentencing-2026)__).

In the adversary proceeding *Mohindra v. Mashinsky*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01138-MG (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn), the Celsius Litigation Trust is pursuing breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment claims against Mashinsky and former Chief Revenue Officer Roni Cohen-Pavon. Discovery closed February 28, 2026; the Trust filed its summary judgment motion on May 15, 2026 (Adv. Dkt. No. 214), asserting on the basis of internal communications obtained in discovery that Mashinsky directed CEL token purchases on secondary markets using customer funds to artificially support the token price — a factual record that substantially overlaps with the criminal plea allocution (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/celsius-trust-summary-judgment-mashinsky-2026)__). Mashinsky's response is due July 7, 2026.

Separately, the estate's adversary against KeyFi Inc. and Jason Stone, *Celsius Network LLC v. KeyFi Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 22-01189-MG, is approaching trial. KeyFi — a DeFi strategy manager that Celsius retained to deploy customer assets in yield-farming protocols — is asserting counterclaims of approximately $2 billion, alleging Celsius promised Stone a profit-sharing arrangement it subsequently refused to honor. Trial before Judge Glenn is scheduled for October 6, 2026 (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/celsius-keyfi-trial-schedule)__). The outcome of this litigation carries implications for the quantum of assets available for distribution to Class 7 (Unsecured Creditors), currently expected to receive between 55–65 cents on the dollar under the Fahrenheit/Ionic Digital restructuring vehicle.

### Voyager and BlockFi: Tail-End Distributions and Regulatory Crosscurrents

*In re Voyager Digital Holdings*, Case No. 22-10943-MEW (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles), has entered its final distribution tail following the confirmed Chapter 11 plan's implementation through the Voyager Distribution Trust. As of May 2026, cumulative distributions to creditors total approximately $1.03 billion, representing a blended recovery rate of approximately 36% against allowed claims — materially below early-case projections of 65–70% that were predicated on a Binance.US acquisition that collapsed in April 2023 following SEC opposition (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/voyager-final-distribution-2026)__). A residual pool of approximately $34 million in unclaimed distributions is subject to a Trust motion (Dkt. No. 8942, filed June 2, 2026) seeking authority to transfer unclaimed funds to a cy pres reserve after a 90-day final notice period; objections are due June 25, 2026.

*In re BlockFi Inc.*, Case No. 22-19361-MBK (Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael B. Kaplan), is similarly in its distribution tail. The estate recovered approximately $875 million via its claims against FTX/Alameda (treated as a general unsecured claim in the FTX cases) and through the $100 million FTC settlement escrow established pursuant to the agency's enforcement action. The BlockFi plan's treatment of the FTX claim — bifurcated between a scheduled claim and an adversary judgment — was validated by Judge Kaplan in a March 2026 opinion that applied *In re Tribune* equitable subordination principles to reject BlockFi creditors' motion to subordinate FTX-related distributions (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/blockfi-ftx-claim-subordination-ruling-2026)__).

### Regulatory Architecture: SEC, CFTC, and the Digital Asset Clarity Act

The legislative backdrop continues to evolve. The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, as amended and passed by the House Financial Services Committee on May 20, 2026, proposes a bifurcated regulatory regime under which the CFTC would have primary jurisdiction over "digital commodities" (including BTC and ETH) and the SEC would retain jurisdiction over digital assets meeting the *Howey* investment-contract test (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/digital-asset-clarity-act-committee-vote)__). The Act's insolvency provisions — § 412 through § 419 — would create a new "Digital Asset Insolvency Protocol" granting bankruptcy courts jurisdiction to adjudicate ownership disputes over assets held in commingled omnibus wallets, codifying the customer-property analysis that Judge Dorsey undertook in FTX and that Judge Glenn applied in Celsius. Senate floor debate has not yet been scheduled as of today's briefing (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-regulation/digital-asset-clarity-act-senate-timeline-2026)__).

On the enforcement side, the CFTC's continued prosecution of *CFTC v. Binance Holdings Ltd.*, N.D. Ill. No. 23-cv-1887-TRH (Judge Thomas R. Holderman), has proceeded to remedies proceedings following the court's December 2025 liability finding. The CFTC is seeking disgorgement of approximately $1.35 billion and civil monetary penalties of $2.7 billion; Binance is contesting both quantum and the extraterritorial application of CEA § 22 to non-U.S. customers (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/cftc-binance-remedies-2026)__). The remedies order, if entered, will have downstream implications for any creditor-restitution fund the court elects to establish — a mechanism available under CEA § 6c(d)(3).

Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Dkt. designate as Administrative Matter) if the Trust does not issue a corrective protocol by June 20, 2026. In the Genesis docket, creditors who elected in-kind digital-asset distributions should monitor the S.D.N.Y. district-court appellate briefing in Civil No. 24-cv-8871-AKH and assess whether the July 14 oral argument outcome alters their recovery economics — counsel should be positioned to seek interim relief if the distribution agent begins making cash-equivalent disbursements before the appeal is resolved. In the Celsius litigation trust proceedings, any creditor whose claim is correlated with the KeyFi adversary recovery (Class 7 unsecured) should track the October 6, 2026 trial date before Judge Glenn; a Stone/KeyFi verdict reducing the Litigation Trust's net asset base by any portion of the $2 billion counterclaim could compress Class 7 distributions by as much as 8–12 percentage points under the estate's current projections. Finally, law firms and asset-recovery boutiques advising claimants with exposure across multiple estates — FTX, Voyager, BlockFi, and Genesis — should maintain parallel tracking of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act's § 412–419 insolvency provisions, as statutory enactment could retroactively validate or invalidate certain plan confirmation terms, triggering post-confirmation modification motions under 11 U.S.C. § 1127.
