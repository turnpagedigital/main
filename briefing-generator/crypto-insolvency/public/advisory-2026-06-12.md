### I. BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371) — Sale Hearing Tomorrow; Confirmation Set for July 13

The BlockFills Chapter 11 is the most actively moving tracked case on today's docket. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities operating under the trade name "BlockFills" filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)). The four debtor entities are Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Delaware), Reliz Technologies LLC (Illinois), Reliz LTD (Cayman Islands), and Reliz CI LTD (Cayman Islands). The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. Court filings show Reliz reporting assets between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million.

**The Sale Track — June 16 Hearing.** Per an official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA has been declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors. According to the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property. A hearing to consider approval of the sale is currently scheduled for June 16, 2026. The acquisition will give Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, which includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies. Final completion of the transaction remains subject to final court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.

The $3.25 million transaction price deserves close scrutiny by creditor constituents. The acquisition price tag of $3.25 million represents a stark valuation collapse for an entity that claimed over $60 billion in trading volume just one year prior. For a creditor class holding approximately $145 million in general unsecured claims, the headline cash consideration from the asset sale — before adjustments for assumed liabilities and operating costs — implies a deeply distressed recovery on the sale-side of the dual-track toggle. The pivotal question before Judge Horan on June 16 is whether the sale procedures, including any stalking-horse protections or break-up fee carve-outs, were arms'-length and marketing was sufficiently robust to maximize value under § 363(b) and the applicable good-faith purchaser standard.

**The Plan Track — Disclosure Statement Approved; Confirmation July 13.** In parallel with the sale, the debtors are prosecuting the reorganization track. ### II. Power Block Coin, LLC (Case No. 24-23041) — Liquidation Plan, Celsius Preference Claims, and the Subchapter V Ineligibility Ruling

*In re Power Block Coin, LLC*, Case No. 24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah, presents a starkly different procedural posture: an older, more contentious proceeding now before Hon. Cathleen D. Parker (following the October 2024 Subchapter V eligibility ruling by Hon. Joel T. Marker), with a creditors' committee-proposed liquidation plan and over $192 million in total claims in dispute. On June 20, 2024 (the "Petition Date"), Power Block Coin LLC filed a voluntary petition for relief under Chapter 11 of the United States Bankruptcy Code. The debtor's case was assigned Case No. 24-23041 and is pending in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Utah District (Salt Lake City division).

**The Subchapter V Eligibility Battle and Its Aftermath.** With less than three hours to spare before the Subchapter V debt limit dropped from $7,500,000 to $3,024,725, Power Block Coin filed a Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024, electing to proceed under Subchapter V as an entity that had aggregate noncontingent liquidated secured and unsecured debts of less than $7,500,000 on the petition date. The Celsius litigation administrator objected to Power Block Coin's election under Subchapter V. On October 9, 2024, the bankruptcy court sustained Celsius's objection and converted Power Block Coin's Subchapter V election, triggering the appointment of a full Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors and dramatically expanding the procedural runway of the case.

**Celsius Preference Claims — $133M+ at Stake.** The Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the debtor in connection with the pending bankruptcy case of Celsius Network LLC et al. (Case No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. The Celsius litigation administrators sought to prosecute avoidance actions against Power Block Coin in their bankruptcy court in the Southern District of New York so that the procedures and schedule for the preference litigation, as well as any rulings, are consistent with the approximately 2,500 other avoidance actions the Litigation Administrators have filed. The venue question — Utah or SDNY — is not merely administrative: SDNY's established preference-litigation track provides Celsius estate creditors with economies of scale, consistent judicial interpretations, and rulings binding across thousands of similarly situated transferees.

**Liquidation Plan — Greenberg Traurig, $192M+ in Claims.** The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, LLC filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), and the plan was filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025. The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million. A key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors.

**Insider Transactions and the $17.4M Solara Loan.** The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates. A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah. The committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property. These facts place § 548 fraudulent transfer claims (both actual and constructive fraud) and § 547 preference claims squarely at the center of potential creditor recovery strategy. The Solara loan — if the encumbrances are found to have been placed post-petition or in contemplation of the filing — could support avoidance under § 549 (unauthorized post-petition transfers) or § 547's insider preference window of one year prior to the petition date.

The most recent docket activity in Power Block Coin is Docket Entry No. 519, filed June 9, 2026, per the tracked-case live data, confirming the case remains actively administered as of this briefing date.

### III. Sector Context — FTX Distribution, Celsius Recovery, and the Customer-Property Question

The BlockFills sale hearing occurs against a broader crypto insolvency landscape that has materially shifted creditor expectations. In one notable adjustment earlier in 2026, the FTX Recovery Trust cut its disputed claims reserve by $2.2 billion, directly supporting a fourth payout round. The March 2026 payout of $2.2 billion landed in a market environment influenced by broader economic signals and Bitcoin trading patterns, recalibrating the baseline against which institutional creditors now measure distressed recovery.

On the Celsius front, distributions have been stratified sharply by account type. As of January 2026, the bankruptcy trustee distributed $425 million to custody account holders, representing 100% recovery for users who kept assets in custody wallets rather than earn accounts. The largest creditor class — earn account holders — now expects 35% to 45% recovery. Initial estimates projected 28% to 38%, but successful asset sales pushed the range higher. The estate holds $340 million in Bitcoin and Ethereum that won't sell until Q3 2026 to avoid market impact.

This bifurcated Celsius outcome is directly instructive for BlockFills creditors: the threshold question of whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate or segregated customer property under § 541(b) and applicable trust principles will govern the gulf between a 100%-recovery outcome (custody) and a deeply distressed unsecured creditor recovery. BlockFills' liquidity crisis was triggered by the February 2026 crypto market crash, compounding counterparty losses, a failed mining venture, and lawsuits alleging misappropriation of customer assets. Those "misappropriation" allegations — raised prepetition via two separate TRO actions — are the predicate for any § 541 customer-property tracing argument and should be the first document preservation and forensic analysis target for any creditor committee engagement.

### IV. Avoidance Action Mechanics — § 547 Preference and § 550 Recovery in the Crypto Context

The Power Block Coin preference litigation highlights a recurring operational hazard across every crypto insolvency on this docket. The collapse of major digital-asset platforms in recent years has propelled bankruptcy avoidance actions into the spotlight. Among these, claw-back claims under the Bankruptcy Code — especially § 547 (pre-petition preferential transfers) and § 550 (recovery of value from transferees) — present complex, high-stakes terrain for creditors, trustees, and asset-recovery practitioners. In a typical corporate insolvency, a trustee examines the 90-day look-back period before filing. In the crypto context, however, the complexity is multiplied: token withdrawals, internal wallet transfers, algorithmic stablecoins, and cross-border custody all complicate the inquiry.

For institutional creditors who withdrew assets from BlockFills or Power Block Coin within the 90-day preference window (or one year for insiders), the risk of a preference demand letter is real and compounding. The key defenses — contemporaneous exchange, ordinary course of business, and new value — must be evaluated on a transaction-by-transaction basis, with on-chain records marshaled to establish the factual predicate for each defense.
