# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Wednesday, June 24, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills / Reliz Technology — Confirmation Window Opens; Sale Proceeds; Customer-Property Question Remains Live**


On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities — collectively operating as "BlockFills" — filed Chapter 11 petitions before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 As of today's briefing, the case has crossed two material milestones in the past ten days and is on a compressed runway toward a July confirmation. Both milestones deserve careful attention from creditors and claimants: one closes the sale track; the other opens the plan track.

**Sale Track: The Keyrock Hearing Has Now Passed.** 
Keyrock's bid was recorded in an official Bankruptcy Court filing on May 26, 2026, and a sale approval hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026. Keyrock is a Brussels-based market maker
 that 
raised a Series C round led by SC Ventures, Standard Chartered's venture capital arm, at a $1.1 billion valuation.
 
Under the purchase agreement, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The June 16 hearing date has now passed; the docket reflects two new entries filed June 22 (Dkt. Nos. 448 and 449), but no public description is available for either filing. The most probable reading is that the sale order was entered at or following the June 16 hearing, with the June 22 filings reflecting either the form of order or a closing-related notice — but the specific outcome of that hearing is not yet confirmed from a public primary source, and creditors should pull the docket directly at CourtListener Docket No. 72494188 to verify.


The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that liability stack, a $3.25 million purchase price is a material shortfall. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That concession drives the central creditor-rights question in the case and has not been resolved by any court ruling to date.

**Plan Track: Disclosure Statement Approved; Confirmation Hearing Targeted for July 13, 2026.** 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order Approving Disclosure Statement, Scheduling Hearing on Confirmation of Plan, and Establishing Related Deadlines [Dkt. No. 388].
 The Veritaglobal key-dates page confirms a 
confirmation hearing is scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The plan's architecture features a dual-track toggle: 
the Plan allows the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a Section 363 competitive sale process.
 Now that Keyrock has been designated the successful bidder on the § 363 sale track, the toggle mechanism is operative — the Debtors must elect which path governs distributions before or at confirmation.

**Customer-Property Question: The Core Legal Exposure.** The structural problem this case shares with Celsius, Voyager, and every commingled-custody case since 2022 has not been resolved here. 
On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 
The Debtors also note a procedural deficiency: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 If no such adversary is filed and prosecuted to judgment, the default outcome under 11 U.S.C. § 541 is that commingled assets are property of the estate — distributable pro rata under the plan rather than returned in kind to depositors. That is the Celsius outcome, and the BlockFills capital structure (assets $50–$100 million; liabilities $100–$500 million) makes in-kind restitution arithmetically impossible in any event.

**Adversary Proceeding Against Artha Investment Partners.** 
An adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH)) was filed April 9, 2026, by Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al. against Artha Investment Partners LLC.
 
A summons response was due June 22, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The nature of the Artha claims is not described in available public filings, but the timing — filed against a named investment counterparty approximately three weeks after the petition — is consistent with a preference or fraudulent-transfer theory targeting prepetition transfers. Creditors' counsel should treat this as a potential estate-recovery vehicle that could meaningfully affect distributable value under the plan.

**Pre-Petition Customer Litigation: Dominion and Alberta.** The pre-petition fraud claims that precipitated the Chapter 11 remain instructive background. 
Two customer lawsuits alleged misappropriation of customer funds, commingling, and fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd. (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026).
 
On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte temporary restraining order freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 
1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (N.D. Ill., Case No. 26-cv-02451, filed March 5, 2026) resulted in temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 Both actions are stayed by the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 but the underlying claims are expected to be litigated through the plan claims-reconciliation process.

**BlockFills — Balance Sheet and Liquidity Causation.** For context on where recoveries are likely to land: 
the company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
CoinDesk reported in February that the Chicago-based firm had suffered losses of roughly $75 million and was seeking either a buyer or emergency financing.
 Given those losses against a declared asset base of $50–$100 million, unsecured claimants (including most institutional customers) will need to track whether the Keyrock sale proceeds, plan distributions, and any adversary-proceeding recoveries together produce a meaningful cents-on-the-dollar outcome.

**FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: Record Date Passed; Payments Commence July 31**

The FTX estate moves into its fifth distribution cycle. This is carry-forward context; the development below is materially new for claimants who have not yet completed pre-distribution procedures.


The anticipated record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims and interests has been set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 The June 16 record date has now passed. 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 Any claimant who sold or transferred a claim after June 16 will not receive the fifth distribution on that claim — the transfer buyer must monitor the objection period independently.


Holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan will become eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026. Separately, the trust filed an amended notice seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 Reserve reductions are the mechanical precondition for releasing additional distribution capacity; this reduction is the proximate source of capital for the July 31 round.

On recovery rates: 
the fourth distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reach 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent.
 
Class 5A, the Dotcom group, has been paid about 96% of principal so far, with a projected total of around 118–120% once the same 9% annual interest is added — it is simply paid in stages.
 The benchmark limitation remains: 
every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.


Cumulative distributions stand at approximately $10 billion across four rounds since February 2025. 
Future omnibus hearings in the FTX case are scheduled for July 23 and August 19, 2026, each at 9:30 a.m. ET in Wilmington.

**Genesis/DCG — Wind-Down Litigation Track Active Post-Effective Date**

Genesis completed its chapter 11 restructuring in August 2024 and has since operated through a Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC). 
Genesis emerged from bankruptcy after a hard-fought process since its chapter 11 filing in January 2023, winning a hotly contested confirmation hearing over the objection of DCG, which had argued that all post-petition appreciation in the value of crypto assets should not be distributed to creditors.
 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the plan confirmed by the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on May 21, 2024. This distribution added to the approximately $2.2 billion distribution Genesis made to Gemini Trust Company LLC (Gemini) Earn users between May and June 2024.


Post-effective litigation remains active. The WDOC docket reflects multiple open adversary proceedings against DCG, including: 
Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al. (Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230), and Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants (Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097).
 A pretrial conference in one of these proceedings was held June 9, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET. 
The docket also reflects a hearing on a Motion to Dismiss scheduled for March 25, 2026.


Separately, the private class action track against DCG in federal court continues to move. 
On February 24, 2026, Judge Stefan Underhill denied the defendants' motion to dismiss in McGreevy v. Digital Currency Group (D. Conn. No. 23-cv-00082), allowing the case to move forward; the court found that the plaintiffs adequately pleaded violations of both the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.
 
The ruling also lifted the PSLRA discovery stay, allowing plaintiffs to begin obtaining evidence.
 For Genesis creditors who did not recover in full on an in-kind basis, this litigation represents a potential incremental recovery pathway against DCG and Barry Silbert.

**Cross-Docket Pattern Recognition: The § 541 Commingling Problem**

The central analytical thread across BlockFills, and historically Celsius and Genesis, is the same: when a crypto platform holds client assets in pooled wallets — one balance sheet — customer deposits become property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 unless the customer can demonstrate (a) a resulting trust, (b) a constructive trust, or (c) sufficient earmarking to sustain a bailment theory. None of those theories has prevailed in any completed crypto Ch. 11 to date without a factually unusual TOS provision. The BlockFills debtors' first-day concession that funds were commingled makes the tracing burden under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001(b) substantially heavier. Claimants with sophisticated counsel have an eight-week window — before the July 13 confirmation hearing — to file any adversary proceeding asserting property or trust rights in their specific assets. After confirmation, plan releases and the res judicata effect of the order will significantly narrow that avenue.
