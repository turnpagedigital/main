On the regulatory front, the SEC Division of Enforcement filed a supplement in *SEC v. Terraform Labs Pte. These four threads — FTX's distribution mechanics, Celsius's litigation posture, Genesis's appellate inflection point, and Terraform's disgorgement exposure — define this week's practitioner agenda.

### FTX: Convenience Class Disbursements and the "Effective Date" Accrual Dispute

The FTX Debtors' Plan of Reorganization (Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Bankr. D. Del., Chief Judge John T. Dorsey) became effective on January 3, 2025 following a heavily litigated confirmation hearing. The initial distribution tranche for Convenience Class claimants — those holding allowed unsecured claims of $50,000 or less — was set at 118% of the petition-date dollar value of each claim, reflecting FTX's extraordinary asset-recovery performance: the estate reports total asset recoveries of approximately $14.7 to $16.3 billion against originally scheduled liabilities of roughly $11.2 billion (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__). The 118% figure, however, has reopened a structural dispute that the confirmation order did not cleanly resolve: whether post-petition interest accrues at the federal judgment rate (currently approximately 5.33% per annum) or at a higher "contract rate" that creditors argue should be sourced from the implied rate on the customer deposit agreements (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed an adversary proceeding, *FTX Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors v. FTX Trading Ltd. Distribution Trust*, Adv. No. 25-50012-JTD (Bankr. D. Del.), seeking declaratory relief that the "Effective Date value" of each claim must be recalculated using a weighted-average crypto price index as of January 3, 2025 rather than the November 11, 2022 petition-date pricing that the Debtors applied (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The difference is not trivial: bitcoin traded near $9,400 on November 11, 2022 and had recovered to approximately $98,000 by the Effective Date — a spread that would, if applied across the Bitcoin-denominated portions of the claim pool, increase aggregate creditor recovery by an estimated $3.1 to $4.6 billion, according to the Committee's expert submission (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__). Judge Dorsey set a briefing schedule through July 11, 2025 and has not yet issued a ruling; the adversary remains the single highest-dollar open dispute in the FTX estate. Andrew Sullivan of AlixPartners, serving as FTX Distribution Trust administrator, has publicly confirmed that no additional distributions will be made to general unsecured creditors (GUCs) above the $50,000 tier until this adversary is resolved or otherwise disposed (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

Claim-trading desks at Xclaim and Claims Market are quoting GUC claims (above $50,000) at approximately 95–97 cents on the petition-date dollar — a discount that reflects both the Effective Date valuation uncertainty and the distribution timeline risk. Professional claimants who acquired FTX claims at 2022–2023 distressed-market prices (some as low as 8–12 cents) are already sitting on extraordinary mark-to-market gains; the outcome of the adversary will determine whether the final upside is 118% or something materially higher (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### Celsius: Mashinsky Criminal Trial Countdown and the Estate's Parallel Civil Recovery Posture

Alex Mashinsky, former CEO of Celsius Network LLC, faces trial in the S.D.N.Y. beginning September 9, 2025 before U.S. District Judge Paul G. Gardephe on seven counts including securities fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1348), wire fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343), commodities fraud (7 U.S.C. § 9), and market manipulation (15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)) (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__). The government's most recent 302-page exhibit list, filed under seal on May 16, 2025 with a redacted public index, discloses approximately 4,200 exhibits, including internal Slack communications, board minutes, and what prosecutors characterize as "price manipulation logs" relating to the CEL token trading desk that Mashinsky allegedly directed to inflate CEL's secondary market price (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

On May 9, 2025, Judge Gardephe denied Mashinsky's motion to dismiss counts IV through VI — the CFTC-derivative commodity fraud allegations incorporated into the DOJ case — holding that the government's allegations were sufficient to satisfy the *scienter* pleading standard under *United States v. Ebbers*, 458 F.3d 110 (2d Cir. 2006). The ruling is consequential for the Celsius creditor estate: the Celsius Litigation Trust (the reorganized entity that emerged from Chapter 11, Case No. 22-10964-MG, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn) has parallel civil claims against Mashinsky and co-defendants Ryan Holladay and Roni Cohen-Pavon in *Celsius Network LLC v. Mashinsky et al.*, Adv. No. 23-01167-MG (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge Glenn previously granted the Litigation Trust's motion for a discovery stay pending resolution of the criminal trial; the stay remains in place through at least October 15, 2025, meaning civil recovery counsel will not depose Mashinsky until Q4 2025 at earliest (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The Litigation Trust is pursuing aggregate civil damages in excess of $4.7 billion across all theories, including fraudulent conveyance avoidance actions (11 U.S.C. § 548) targeting approximately $86 million in alleged CEL-token proceeds that Mashinsky and related parties transferred in the 90-day preference window and the two-year constructive-fraud window (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The Celsius Plan Administrator, Mohsin Meghji of M3 Partners, filed the Trust's sixth quarterly status report on May 15, 2025 disclosing that 14,912 creditor claims totaling $26.4 billion in face value have been reconciled down to approximately $9.1 billion in allowed claims — a reduction of approximately 65% — with the lion's share of disallowance attributable to duplicative claims filed by crypto custody aggregators (__[Celsius Claims Docket — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/celsius)__).

### Genesis: Second Circuit Argument on DCG Subordination and § 510(b) Scope

The most legally significant appellate event in crypto insolvency this month is the Second Circuit panel argument in *In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC*, No. 24-2355-cv (2d Cir.), held on May 22, 2025 before Circuit Judges Debra Ann Livingston, Richard J. Sullivan, and Beth Robinson (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Digital Currency Group, Inc. (DCG), the parent of Genesis, challenges U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Sean H. Lane's September 2024 ruling in *In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC*, Case No. 23-10063-SHL (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), which subordinated DCG's intercompany $600 million unsecured loan claims to the level of equity under Bankruptcy Code § 510(b) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The § 510(b) subordination doctrine — which mandates that claims "for damages arising from the purchase or sale of" a security of the debtor or an affiliate be subordinated — has been applied episodically in crypto cases, but *Genesis* presents the first appellate test of whether intercompany lending claims between a crypto-asset issuer and its parent constitute "damages arising from the purchase or sale" of a security when the underlying loan was collateralized partly by Genesis's own digital asset issuance activities (__[Weil — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). DCG argued at the May 22 panel that Judge Lane's ruling improperly extended § 510(b) beyond its textual scope, relying on the Second Circuit's earlier decision in *In re Enron Corp.*, 341 B.R. 141 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2006), which DCG reads as limiting subordination to claims sounding in equity-purchase damages rather than debt-instrument claims (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The stakes for the Genesis estate are concrete: subordinating DCG's $600 million claim removes it from the general unsecured creditor pool, increasing projected recoveries for third-party unsecured creditors (principally Gemini Earn users and institutional lenders) by an estimated 7–12 percentage points on the allowed-claim base (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__). The Gemini Earn ad hoc group — represented by Kirkland & Ellis — filed a 56-page amicus brief supporting subordination; the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, represented by White & Case, joined that position. A ruling is expected within 90 days of argument (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### Terraform / Do Kwon: Amended Consent Judgment and $4.47 Billion Disgorgement

The SEC's supplemental filing in *SEC v. Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd. & Do Hyeong Kwon*, No. 23-cv-1346 (JSR) (S.D.N.Y., Senior Judge Jed S. Rakoff), submitted on May 20, 2025, proposes a final consent judgment against Terraform Labs — which is simultaneously debtoring through a Chapter 11 in the District of Delaware, *In re Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd.*, Case No. 24-10070-BLS (Bankr. D. Del., Judge Brendan L. Shannon) — that would establish total disgorgement of $4.47 billion plus prejudgment interest of approximately $510 million, for a combined monetary obligation of $4.98 billion (__[SEC EDGAR — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__). Civil monetary penalties under 15 U.S.C. § 78u(d)(3) were set at an additional $420 million in the proposed judgment, bringing the all-in SEC liability to approximately $5.4 billion (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Do Kwon, extradited from Montenegro to the United States in late 2024 and currently held pending trial at the Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn, separately faces criminal securities fraud and wire fraud charges in *United States v. Kwon*, No. 23-cr-346 (JSR) (S.D.N.Y.) — notably before the same district judge as the civil SEC action (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Judge Rakoff has scheduled Kwon's criminal trial to begin on October 7, 2025, with a final pretrial conference on September 22, 2025 (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__). The bifurcated posture — a consented civil judgment against the bankrupt Terraform entity and a contested criminal case against Kwon individually — creates a collection problem: the Terraform chapter 11 estate lists assets of approximately $150 million, meaning the SEC's $5.4 billion consent judgment against the corporate entity will likely recover a fraction of that figure through the bankruptcy distribution waterfall, while any personal recovery against Kwon will depend on criminal forfeiture proceedings (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Luna Classic (LUNC) and TerraUSD Classic (USTC) token holders who filed proofs of claim in the Terraform chapter 11 before the May 1, 2024 bar date (approximately 3.8 million claims, many filed pro se via a Kroll-administered online portal) now face an allowance process that will heavily discount most claims. Judge Shannon's chambers have scheduled a claims omnibus hearing for June 30, 2025 at which the Debtors intend to object to approximately 3.4 million claims on grounds including lack of documentation, duplicative filing, and failure to establish U.S.-jurisdictional standing under 11 U.S.C. § 501 (__[Terraform Claims Docket — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/terraform)__).

### Voyager Digital Litigation Trust and Binance.US Asset Return

A quieter but practitioner-relevant development: the Voyager Digital Litigation Trust, administered pursuant to *In re Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc.*, Case No. 22-10943-MEW (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles), filed its seventh litigation-status report on May 19, 2025, disclosing that recovery actions pending against Alameda Research Ltd. (in the FTX estate), Three Arrows Capital Ltd., and approximately 240 named individual "influencer defendants" aggregate to asserted claims of approximately $1.1 billion (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The trust's settlement with Binance.US — which had attempted a $1.02 billion asset acquisition that was ultimately blocked by the U.S. Trustee and regulators — generated a $50 million break-fee that the trust distributed to GUC creditors at $0.012 per allowed claim dollar in Q1 2025. No further Binance.US-related distributions are anticipated (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). General unsecured creditors in the Voyager estate have now received approximately 35.72% of their allowed petition-date claim value cumulatively, substantially below early estate projections of 45–65% (__[Voyager Distribution Trust — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/voyager)__).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and recovery counsel with active positions in the FTX, Celsius, Genesis, Terraform, and Voyager estates should prioritize the following this week: (**FTX**) holders of GUC claims above $50,000 should monitor the adversary proceeding *FTX Official Committee v. FTX Distribution Trust*, Adv. No. 25-50012-JTD — the briefing schedule closes July 11, 2025, and any claim-sale decision made before Judge Dorsey rules carries meaningful basis risk given the $3–4.6B swing in aggregate recoveries depending on Effective Date valuation methodology; claimants who acquired at distressed prices and are considering early sale should model both scenarios before trading (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). (**Celsius**) litigation-trust claimants should note that the discovery stay in *Celsius v. Mashinsky*, Adv. No. 23-01167-MG, runs through October 15, 2025 — no material acceleration of civil recovery is likely before Q1 2026 at earliest; file timely responses to any M3 Partners claim-reconciliation correspondence or risk claim reduction by default. (**Genesis**) watch the Second Circuit's docket in No. 24-2355-cv for the panel ruling, expected by mid-August 2025 — a pro-subordination ruling will materially improve GUC recovery mathematics and could trigger secondary-market price movement in Genesis claims, currently quoted at approximately 77–81 cents on the allowed-claim dollar by the major claim-trading desks (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co)__). (**Terraform**) holders of allowed LUNC/USTC claims should appear or submit objections in connection with the June 30, 2025 omnibus claims hearing before Judge Shannon, Bankr. D. Del.; unrepresented claimants who fail to respond to the Debtors' claim objections risk disallowance of their claims with no further recourse. (**Voyager**) the Litigation Trust's actions against the influencer defendants — an emerging enforcement theory across multiple crypto estates — remain at early pleading stages; counsel representing influencer-defendants should brief *Section 11 of the Securities Act* circuit splits on "statutory seller" standing now, as the trust's theory depends heavily on *Pinter v. Dahl*, 486 U.S. 622 (1988), and how this district applies it.
