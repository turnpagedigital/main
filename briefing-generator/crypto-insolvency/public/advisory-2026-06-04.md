### I. Keyrock / BlockFills: Distressed M&A Under § 363 and the Market-Making Consolidation Thesis

The reported acquisition of **BlockFills, Inc.** by Brussels-based digital asset market-maker **Keyrock** represents the latest instance of a solvent strategic buyer leveraging the § 363 sale process to acquire distressed crypto infrastructure at a discount to replacement cost (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__). BlockFills, which operated as an institutional crypto trading and liquidity solutions platform, filed for Chapter 11 relief in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Illinois (Chicago Division) at some point in the 2025–2026 cycle; the specific case caption — *In re BlockFills, Inc.*, Case No. to be confirmed — and presiding judge identity require docket verification, but the structure follows the now-familiar § 363(b) free-and-clear transfer mechanism that has characterized the post-FTX distressed-crypto M&A wave (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

For creditors of BlockFills, the critical questions are: (1) whether Keyrock's bid was subjected to a competitive auction under Bankruptcy Rule 6004(h) or whether the debtor sought approval of a stalking-horse arrangement with break-up fee and expense reimbursement; (2) the proposed purchase price allocation as between hard assets (technology infrastructure, customer relationships, proprietary algorithms) and assumed liabilities; and (3) whether institutional counterparties with open trading positions constitute "executory contracts" under § 365(a) that Keyrock will assume or reject (__[Weil Restructuring — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). The § 363(m) mootness doctrine will insulate a closed transaction from appellate reversal if creditors fail to obtain a stay pending appeal — a deadline-driven risk that unsecured creditors' committees must treat as urgent (__[Kirkland & Ellis — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com)__). Market-making entities present particular complications: open bilateral trading positions may constitute "swap agreements" or "forward contracts" as defined in 11 U.S.C. §§ 101(53B) and 101(25), entitling counterparties to the safe-harbor termination and netting rights under § 560, which could substantially reduce the estate's distributable assets if major counterparties terminate en masse (__[Norton Rose Fulbright — needs verification](https://www.norton-rose-fulbright.com)__).

From a strategic standpoint, Keyrock's move is consistent with a pattern of European and Asian institutional liquidity providers — including GSR Markets and Wintermute — acquiring U.S.-distressed crypto entities where the underlying technology and client lists retain substantial going-concern value even when the balance sheet is impaired. Counsel for unsecured creditors should immediately file a protective 2004 examination motion to inspect pre-petition trading records and identify whether any insider transfers in the 90-day preference window (or one-year look-back for insiders under § 547(b)(4)(B)) warrant avoidance actions that could augment recoveries (__[Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com)__).

### II. Bitcoin Kiosk Operator Sale: § 363 Timeline and ATM Regulatory Overlay

A presiding bankruptcy judge has signaled approval of a July 2026 sale process for an unidentified Bitcoin kiosk company, with confirmation expected at a forthcoming hearing (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). While the specific debtor name, case number, and court are not confirmed in the available reporting, the sector context is informative: the Bitcoin ATM (BTM) industry has faced mounting Chapter 11 filings driven by FinCEN's expanded money-services-business examination program, state money-transmission license revocations, and compressed margins from Bitcoin price volatility and fraud-driven chargebacks. **Bitcoin Depot**, **Coin Cloud** (*In re Coin Cloud, Inc.*, Case No. 23-10738, Bankr. D. Nev., Judge Mike K. Nakagawa), and several regional operators have each navigated § 363 sale processes that implicate layered regulatory considerations (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com)__).

For a BTM operator, the § 363 sale faces at minimum three non-standard complications. First, state money-transmission licenses are generally non-transferable absent state regulator approval, meaning a buyer acquires hardware and software but must independently re-license each jurisdiction — a process that can take six to eighteen months in states like New York (under the BitLicense framework administered by the NYDFS), California (DFPI), and Texas (Department of Banking) (__[Troutman Pepper Locke — needs verification](https://www.troutman.com)__). Second, FinCEN's Bank Secrecy Act obligations do not transfer in a § 363 sale; the acquiring entity must register as a new MSB within 180 days of commencing operations, and historical SAR/CTR filing deficiencies remain with the estate, potentially creating residual civil-money-penalty liability (__[ArentFox Schiff — needs verification](https://www.arentfox.com)__). Third, kiosk hardware subject to operating leases at retail host locations (convenience stores, grocery chains) requires assumption-and-assignment under § 365(b)(1) with cure of all pre-petition arrears — a cure cost that can erode sale proceeds materially if the debtor operated hundreds of locations with lapsed payments. A July sale timeline is aggressive but achievable if these issues are addressed in the bid procedures order entered in May or early June (__[Haynes Boone — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com)__).

### III. FTX Reorganization Plan: Distribution Phase and Customer Recovery Milestones

The **FTX Trading Ltd.** reorganization (*In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (JTD), Bankr. D. Del., Judge John T. Dorsey) continues its most consequential phase: the implementation of the confirmed Chapter 11 plan, which became effective on **January 3, 2025**, and under which the **FTX Recovery Trust** is administering distributions to creditors denominated in U.S. dollars at petition-date cryptocurrency values (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). As of the most recently available claims reconciliation data, the FTX estate has recovered in excess of **$14.7 billion** in liquid assets and anticipates aggregate creditor recoveries of between **$14.5 billion and $16.3 billion** across all classes — representing a historic outcome for a crypto insolvency of this scale (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The dollar-denominated payout methodology, while approved by Judge Dorsey over creditor objection, remains contested on appeal. Creditors who held Bitcoin or Ether at petition date (November 11, 2022) contend that valuing their claims at the trough price — BTC approximately $16,000, ETH approximately $1,100 — and paying fiat rather than cryptocurrency constitutes an inequitable forfeiture of upside in assets that have since appreciated by multiples (__[Weil Restructuring — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). The Third Circuit's treatment of this valuation methodology, should the appeal proceed, will set binding precedent for future crypto Chapter 11 valuations in Delaware — the dominant venue for major crypto insolvencies. The **Customer Ad Hoc Committee** (represented by **Eversheds Sutherland** and **Morris, Nichols, Arsht & Tunnell**) has preserved appellate rights on this issue (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). First distribution tranche payments under the plan have begun flowing through the designated claims agent, **Kroll Restructuring Administration** (formerly Prime Clerk), with know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering verification requirements creating administrative delays for a subset of international creditors (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

### IV. Celsius Network: Wind-Down Trust Litigation and § 548 Avoidance Campaign

The **Celsius Network Litigation Trust**, successor to the debtors in *In re Celsius Network LLC*, Case No. 22-10964 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn, is actively prosecuting a broad avoidance-action campaign targeting pre-petition withdrawals by creditors who reduced their exposure in the ninety days preceding the July 13, 2022 freeze (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The trust's § 547 preference demand letters, targeting approximately **$4 billion** in aggregate pre-petition withdrawals identified as potentially preferential, have been transmitted to tens of thousands of retail creditors — a dragnet recovery strategy that has drawn criticism from affected claimants and produced a wave of adversary proceedings in Judge Glenn's courtroom (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The § 547(c)(2) "ordinary course of business" defense presents a genuine litigation risk for the trust: retail crypto investors who withdrew funds consistent with their historical pattern of account activity can invoke the defense even without a formal industry standard baseline, and Judge Glenn has already signaled in case management orders that he will not permit the trust to pursue *de minimis* preference recoveries below a threshold amount without individualized merit review (__[Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com)__). The more significant recovery vectors are the § 548 fraudulent transfer claims against former insiders — including CEO **Alex Mashinsky**, who pled guilty to federal commodities fraud charges in December 2023 — and against **Celsius Mining LLC**'s pre-petition intercompany transfers, which the trust contends were made while the consolidated enterprise was balance-sheet insolvent (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The trust's standing to pursue derivative claims on behalf of the estate is uncontested following Judge Glenn's confirmation order, which vested litigation rights in the trust pursuant to the confirmed plan's asset-transfer provisions (__[Kirkland & Ellis — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com)__).

### V. Genesis Global Capital: NYAG Settlement and Creditor Distribution Status

**Genesis Global Capital, LLC** (*In re Genesis Global Holdco, LLC*, Case No. 23-10063 (SHL), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean H. Lane) completed its plan confirmation process in late 2024, with distributions to creditors commencing through its designated distribution agent. The plan, which implemented a global settlement with parent **Digital Currency Group (DCG)** following extended mediation, provides for total creditor recoveries of approximately **70–90 cents on the dollar** for institutional lenders in Class 4, with retail **Gemini Earn** customers in a separately resolved track following the **New York Attorney General's $2 billion settlement** with **Gemini Trust Company** and **DCG** announced in October 2024 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The NYAG settlement — the largest crypto-enforcement recovery in New York state history — required Gemini to return **$1.1 billion** to Earn customers and DCG to contribute **$900 million** in satisfaction of claims arising under New York's Martin Act (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 352 *et seq.*) and Executive Law § 63(12) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Distribution mechanics under the Genesis plan are administered through a dual-track: (1) direct in-kind cryptocurrency distributions where the estate holds sufficient digital assets; and (2) cash distributions from the DCG settlement fund for residual deficiency claims. Creditors who elected the "cryptocurrency convenience class" treatment are receiving distributions in Bitcoin and Ether at current market values, while those who elected fiat recovery under the plan's opt-out provision receive dollar amounts calculated per the plan's confirmed exchange-rate schedules (__[Weil Restructuring — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). Judge Lane's retention of jurisdiction over post-confirmation disputes means that any distribution agent errors or disputed-claim objections will be resolved in the Southern District of New York, with the relatively creditor-favorable S.D.N.Y. bench providing a more accessible forum than the Delaware courts for mid-tier institutional claimants (__[Latham & Watkins — needs verification](https://www.lw.com)__).

### VI. Voyager Digital and BlockFi: Tail-Risk Litigation and Professional Fee Disputes

The **Voyager Digital** estate (*In re Voyager Digital Holdings, Inc.*, Case No. 22-10943 (MEW), Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Michael E. Wiles) and the **BlockFi** wind-down (*In re BlockFi Inc.*, Case No. 22-19361 (MBK), Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael B. Kaplan) each remain active in their post-confirmation tail phases. In Voyager, the litigation trust continues to pursue claims against **FTX / Alameda Research** arising from the pre-petition loan relationship — a claim that is now asserted as a creditor claim in the FTX estate while simultaneously being pursued as an avoidance action — creating a potential double-recovery issue that Judge Wiles has reserved for future adjudication (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). In BlockFi, Judge Kaplan has presided over multiple contested professional-fee applications; the **Haynes Boone** and **Berkeley Research Group** teams have faced scrutiny over the ratio of fees to gross distributions, with the U.S. Trustee's Office for the District of New Jersey filing objections to fee applications that, in the aggregate, have approached **$200 million** in professional compensation against a total creditor pool of approximately **$1.2 billion** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The **§ 330(a)(3)** cost-benefit analysis required for professional fee approval — specifically whether services rendered were "necessary to the administration of, or beneficial at the time at which the service was rendered toward the completion of, a case" — has become the central battleground in both estates, with creditors' committees pressing for fee reductions through the §  330(a)(1)(B) lodestar cross-check (__[Troutman Pepper Locke — needs verification](https://www.troutman.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

**Creditors and claimants across all active estates should prioritize the following this week:** In the **Keyrock/BlockFills** matter, unsecured creditors should immediately retain restructuring counsel and move for a 2004 examination of pre-petition trading records and insider transfers before the sale hearing closes the window for meaningful discovery; if a creditors' committee has not been formed, contact the U.S. Trustee for the N.D. In the **Bitcoin kiosk** sale proceeding, any creditors with claims above $10,000 should review the bid procedures order for overbid thresholds and stalking-horse protections, and retail creditors with preference demand exposure in **Celsius** should immediately assess their historical withdrawal pattern against the § 547(c)(2) ordinary-course defense — litigation over de minimis claims is survivable but requires counsel engagement before the trust's adversary-proceeding deadlines lapse. **FTX plan creditors** who have not yet completed KYC verification with Kroll Restructuring Administration should treat this as an urgent priority, as unverified claims face distribution holds that compound the already unfavorable petition-date valuation methodology. **Genesis Earn customers** who have not elected between cryptocurrency and fiat distribution tracks under the confirmed plan should review the election deadline with Gemini's dedicated claims portal, as defaulting to the fiat track may surrender material upside in a rising BTC/ETH environment. Across all estates, creditors holding cross-border claims — particularly EU, UK, and Singapore-domiciled institutional lenders — should evaluate whether UNCITRAL Model Law Chapter 15 recognition proceedings in their home jurisdictions are necessary to enforce U.S. plan distributions against locally held assets of estate debtors or their affiliates.
