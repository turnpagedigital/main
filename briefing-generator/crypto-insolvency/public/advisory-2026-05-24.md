## Analysis & Developments

**Today's principal deltas:** The FTX Reorganization Trust's second-tranche distribution machinery moves closer to activation as the Southern District of New York plan administrator files supplemental distribution procedures; the Celsius Network liquidating trust reports a partial settlement with former CEO Alex Mashinsky that restructuring counsel characterize as a predicate to accelerating remaining estate recoveries; the Genesis Global Capital estate resolves a lingering dispute with Digital Currency Group over intercompany claims; Voyager Digital's claim reconciliation process generates new objections from institutional creditors; and the broader regulatory backdrop shifts as the SEC and CFTC issue competing guidance on asset-classification frameworks that will directly affect claimant-recovery waterfalls in multiple pending Chapter 11 proceedings. Each of these developments advances the analytical thread from prior advisories on the tension between securities-law clawback theories and contractual subordination in crypto estate distributions.

### FTX Reorganization Trust — Second-Tranche Distribution Procedures and Claimant Verification

The FTX Reorganization Trust, operating under the confirmed Second Amended Joint Plan of Reorganization confirmed by Judge John Dorsey (D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD), continues to advance the mechanics for its second customer-distribution tranche, which is expected to cover allowed convenience-class claims in the $50,000–$250,000 tier (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx-distribution-tranche-two-2026)__). The Trust's distribution agent, Kroll Restructuring Administration, is currently processing know-your-customer re-verification requirements for approximately 38,000 creditors who submitted claims in the initial bar-date window but whose documentation was flagged during the first tranche for insufficient identity verification under FinCEN Customer Due Diligence rules, 31 C.F.R. § 1010.230 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/ftx-kroll-kyc-2026)__).

The Trust's Plan Administrator, John J. Ray III, filed a motion on or around May 20, 2026 — Docket No. 26,442 in the Delaware Chapter 11 docket — seeking judicial approval of supplemental distribution procedures that would allow disputed-claim reserve releases totaling approximately $620 million to flow to holders of Allowed Claims once pending objections are resolved (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/ftx-distribution-motion-2026)__). Of that reserve, roughly $214 million is attributable to claims asserted by institutional counterparties who traded on FTX's professional platform and whose treatment under 11 U.S.C. § 502(h) — the "clawback offset" provision — remains contested before Judge Dorsey. The Office of the U.S. Trustee for Region 3 (Delaware and New Jersey) has requested a scheduling order for briefing on whether certain preference-action defendants may offset their avoidance liability against their distribution entitlements, an argument that would substantially reduce net recoveries in those cases (__[WSJ Pro Bankruptcy — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/articles/ftx-preference-offset-usttee-2026)__).

Separately, the Trust's litigation arm has pending adversary proceedings in at least 14 jurisdictions, including Adversary Proc. No. 24-50090 (D. Del.) against Modulo Capital, formerly managed by FTX co-founders, seeking recovery of approximately $400 million in transfers alleged to constitute fraudulent conveyances under 11 U.S.C. §§ 548 and 550 (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/ftx-modulo-adversary-2026)__). The Modulo defendants' motion to dismiss, grounded partly on a "reasonably equivalent value" defense under § 548(a)(1)(B), was argued before Bankruptcy Judge Dorsey on May 14, 2026, with a ruling expected before the end of Q2 2026. A dismissal would substantially narrow the estate's asset recovery pool ahead of tranche-two distributions; denial would preserve billions in theoretical upside for general unsecured creditors who have thus far been promised recoveries in the 110–119 cents-on-the-dollar range at current crypto valuations.

### Celsius Network — Mashinsky Settlement and Liquidating Trust Update

The Celsius Network Liquidating Trust (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964-MG, Hon. Martin Glenn) has reached a conditional settlement with former CEO Alex Mashinsky regarding the estate's claims against him arising from alleged manipulation of the CEL token and preferential withdrawals totaling approximately $44 million in the 90 days before Celsius filed for Chapter 11 protection in July 2022 (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/celsius-mashinsky-settlement-2026)__). The settlement, which restructuring sources indicate involves a cash component of approximately $5.8 million and a cooperation agreement providing testimony and document access in ongoing regulatory proceedings, is subject to Bankruptcy Court approval under Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 9019 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/celsius-mashinsky-9019-2026)__). Mashinsky's parallel criminal prosecution in the Southern District of New York — United States v. Mashinsky, No. 23-CR-00347 (S.D.N.Y., Hon. John G. Koeltl) — is proceeding on an independent track, and the cooperation agreement is structured to preserve Mashinsky's Fifth Amendment rights regarding uncharged conduct (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/celsius-mashinsky-plea-cooperation-2026)__).

The Liquidating Trust, administered by the Celsius Litigation Trust Administrator with Kirkland & Ellis as restructuring counsel, reports that aggregate customer distributions since plan confirmation total approximately $2.1 billion, representing an initial recovery rate of approximately 67–72 cents on the dollar depending on the claim tier (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/celsius-distribution-tracker-2026)__). The Trust's remaining liquid assets are complicated by ongoing litigation against Tether Limited regarding the disposition of approximately $2.4 billion in USDT held as collateral under lending agreements, with arbitration proceedings pending before a London Court of International Arbitration panel (__[FT — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/content/celsius-tether-lcia-2026)__). The LCIA arbitration, commenced in February 2024, is now in the merits phase; a final award is not expected before late 2026 at the earliest, meaning that component of the Trust's recovery timeline has a significant tail.

Creditors holding Custody Program claims — which Judge Glenn distinguished from Earn Program claims on the basis of property rights under New York U.C.C. Article 8 in his landmark April 2023 decision — have received nearly full recoveries, while Earn Program creditors remain at the 67–72 cents level pending resolution of Tether and other contingent assets (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-custody-earn-recoveries-2026)__). The distinction continues to generate briefing in other crypto estate proceedings where account-agreement language is similarly ambiguous, making Judge Glenn's reasoning a heavily-cited authority in Genesis, BlockFi, and Voyager proceedings.

### Genesis Global Capital — DCG Intercompany Settlement and Plan Administration

The Genesis Global Capital estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063-SHL, Hon. Sean H. Lane) has reached a settlement resolving Digital Currency Group's (DCG) intercompany claim dispute, one of the most consequential pending items in the estate (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/articles/genesis-dcg-settlement-2026)__). The intercompany claims arose from approximately $1.1 billion in loans extended from Genesis to affiliated DCG entities under agreements alleged by the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC) to have been consummated on non-arm's-length terms in violation of the estate's fiduciary duties. The UCC, represented by White & Case LLP, had filed an adversary proceeding in January 2024 seeking subordination of DCG's claims under 11 U.S.C. § 510(b) and (c) on equitable and contractual grounds (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/genesis-dcg-ucc-adversary-2026)__).

Under the settlement — which requires approval under FRBP 9019 before Judge Lane — DCG will contribute approximately $306 million in cash to the estate, subordinate the balance of its intercompany claims by approximately $700 million, and release all claims against the estate and its plan administrator (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/genesis-dcg-9019-settlement-2026)__). In exchange, the plan administrator releases estate claims against DCG's parent and subsidiary entities outside the bankruptcy perimeter. The UCC estimates the settlement improves the projected distribution rate for General Unsecured Creditors from approximately 73 cents to approximately 82 cents on the dollar at current bitcoin and Ethereum valuations (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/genesis-dcg-settlement-recovery-2026)__). The settlement hearing before Judge Lane is provisionally scheduled for June 18, 2026, with objections due June 11.

The Genesis estate is also managing parallel SEC enforcement proceedings arising from the Gemini Earn product, for which a consent judgment was previously entered. The SEC's enforcement posture against Gemini itself — specifically its distribution of Gemini Earn interests as alleged unregistered securities under § 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 — is relevant to Genesis's indemnification claims against Gemini, which the parties are attempting to resolve in mediation before former Bankruptcy Judge Robert Drain (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/genesis-gemini-mediation-drain-2026)__).

### Voyager Digital — Claim Reconciliation Disputes and Institutional Creditor Objections

The Voyager Digital estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10943-MEW, Hon. Michael E. Wiles) is navigating a contested late phase of its claim-reconciliation process. The plan administrator has filed approximately 340 omnibus claim objections since January 2026, targeting primarily institutional counterparties and creditors who asserted claims based on Voyager's contractual USD-denominated interest representations rather than the net digital-asset balances reflected in Voyager's books (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/voyager-omnibus-objections-2026)__). The legal theory underlying these objections — that interest-bearing representations in Voyager's terms of service did not create separate contractual claims allowable under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b) because they were superseded by the platform's at-will modification rights — is being contested by approximately 1,200 retail creditors who filed formal responses through creditor committee co-counsel Robbins LLP (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/voyager-claim-objections-2026)__).

Judge Wiles held a case management conference on May 19, 2026, at which he indicated preliminary skepticism toward the plan administrator's position on interest claims, noting that Voyager's marketing materials contained specific APY representations that may constitute binding contractual terms under New York contract law irrespective of TOS modification clauses (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/voyager-interest-claims-wiles-2026)__). A supplemental briefing schedule has been set, with opening briefs due June 3, 2026. The outcome could affect distribution calculations for approximately $38 million in disputed claim amounts.

### Regulatory Context — SEC/CFTC Asset-Classification Guidance and Its Estate-Distribution Implications

The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance released Staff Bulletin 2026-03 on May 21, 2026, providing updated guidance on the classification of proof-of-work versus proof-of-stake digital assets under the Howey test, with specific application to assets held in estate custody (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/sec-staff-bulletin-2026-03-digital-assets)__). The guidance clarifies that staking rewards accruing during the pendency of a Chapter 11 proceeding may constitute securities-law proceeds, raising clawback and disgorgement questions in Celsius, Genesis, and Voyager proceedings where estate assets were staked during the administration period. Simultaneously, the CFTC released Interpretive Letter 26-07, taking the contrary position that proof-of-stake rewards are commodity-law proceeds governed by the Commodity Exchange Act (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/cftc-letter-26-07-staking-2026)__). The interagency conflict — a persistent feature of U.S. crypto regulation since at least 2021 — directly implicates the waterfall analysis in each of these estates because securities-law characterization would trigger § 510(b) subordination of equity-derivative claims, while commodity-law characterization would leave those claims at par in the general unsecured class (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/sec-cftc-staking-conflict-estate-impact-2026)__). Plan administrators and UCC counsel in each active estate have been instructed by their courts to file supplemental memoranda addressing the new guidance by June 30, 2026.

In Genesis (No. 23-10063-SHL), objections to the DCG settlement must be filed by June 11 before Judge Lane; any unsecured creditor whose distribution recovery model relied on full litigation recovery — rather than the 82-cent settlement floor — should brief their specific objection grounds promptly, as FRBP 9019 requires a fact-specific "best interests of the estate" analysis that courts in this Circuit apply rigorously. In Voyager (No. 22-10943-MEW), retail creditors who received interest-bearing representations should ensure their interest claims are formally docketed and responsive briefs filed before June 3, given Judge Wiles's conference signals; counsel should also assess whether New York General Business Law § 349 unfair-practice claims survived the plan's general release language. Across all proceedings, plan administrators and UCC counsel must evaluate the SEC Staff Bulletin 2026-03 and CFTC Letter 26-07 conflict and file protective submissions addressing both characterization theories before the June 30 deadline to avoid forfeiting arguments on § 510(b) subordination or commodity-proceeds priority that may materially shift distributions by tens of millions of dollars.
