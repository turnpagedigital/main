⛏️ $173M in debt, $1M–$10M in assets. That's the Poolin Technology estate as of July 22.

Singapore-based Poolin Technology and U.S. subsidiaries Lonestar Dream Inc. and Lonestar Taproot LLC filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the District of New Jersey. CRO Michael DuFrayne's declaration puts prepetition obligations at approximately $173.1M — roughly $163.7M of that consists of IOUs issued to Poolin Wallet users after the company halted withdrawals in September 2022.

The math is brutal before costs even enter:

• Thor CALAP LLC's $52M stalking-horse bid covers the Texas mining/HPC sites — 31.8% of IOU obligations, gross
• Cash on hand: approximately $1.2M
• Active operations: none since July 10, 2026

The § 541 question is the same one that defined Celsius and Voyager. Wallet users received IOUs — not segregated custody — so they almost certainly land as general unsecured creditors. Whether auction proceeds from the Lonestar affiliates flow cleanly to satisfy Technology's wallet creditors turns on intercompany claim treatment and substantive consolidation arguments the CRO's first-day declaration begins to address but does not resolve.

The IOU exchange documentation from September 2022 is the single most important recovery document in this case. Its precise language governs whether any trust or bailment argument survives a § 541 motion.

Practical takeaway: If you held a Poolin Wallet balance, verify the judge and docket number on PACER before filing anything — neither has been publicly confirmed as of this morning. Review the IOU terms before estimating any recovery. The 31.8% gross ratio is a ceiling, not a floor.
