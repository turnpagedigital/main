🪙 July 1, 4:00 p.m. ET — that's the hard stop for BlockFills creditors.

Both the plan confirmation objection deadline and the voting deadline close Tuesday. The July 13 confirmation hearing proceeds on whatever record exists at that point.

The math is stark. Keyrock agreed to pay $3.25 million for substantially all assets. The unsecured pool is roughly $145 million. That's approximately 2 cents on the dollar of face exposure — before professional fees and before Celsius's $4.8 million secured claim gets satisfied ahead of unsecured creditors.

The threshold issue driving recovery outcomes hasn't been adjudicated: whether customer digital assets are property of the estate under § 541 or held outside it in trust or bailment. Debtors' counsel conceded at the first-day hearing that customer funds were commingled with company funds and that the Debtors treat all such assets as estate property. The court preserved the issue. It mirrors the posture that produced the Celsius ruling — where Judge Glenn found yield-account holders had transferred property to the estate. BlockFills' terms-of-service question remains open.

Any creditor who believes their assets were segregated, or who objects to plan treatment or the sale price, must file and serve before Tuesday's deadline. That argument does not survive confirmation if it isn't preserved now.
