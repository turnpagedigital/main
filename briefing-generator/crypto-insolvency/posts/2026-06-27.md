$3.25M. That's what a firm that processed $60B in 2025 trading volume sold for in bankruptcy court last week.

Judge Horan approved the Keyrock SA acquisition of BlockFills assets on June 16 (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del.). The Brussels-based market maker assumes substantially all assets, certain liabilities, customer lists, and BlockFills' proprietary IP. The estate reported $50M–$100M in assets against $100M–$500M in liabilities at petition.

The sale closes one track. The harder question opens July 13.

• ~$145M in general unsecured customer and trade claims
• ~$4.8M outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes (first-priority lien asserted)
• $3.25M in sale proceeds — the gap is the story

At confirmation, Judge Horan will have to rule on whether customer digital assets are property of the estate under § 541 or sit outside it under a trust or bailment theory — the same fight Celsius litigated for months in the S.D.N.Y. The answer drives the entire recovery waterfall. The conditional disclosure statement approval already flagged the opt-out release mechanism as a live confirmation objection. Adversary Proceeding 26-50230 against Artha Investment Partners adds an asset-tracing variable to estate value.

Practitioners: the July 13 hearing is the single most consequential date in this beat for the next 30 days.
