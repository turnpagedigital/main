## LinkedIn

⚖️ $3.25M. That's what "substantially all" of BlockFills sold for — against $145M in general unsecured claims.

Delaware approved the § 363 sale to Brussels-based Keyrock SA on June 16. Keyrock takes the operating assets, IP, customer lists, and certain liabilities. What stays behind goes into a liquidating trust. Plan confirmation is set for September 11, 2026.

The sale closes the process question. It does not close the one that matters for creditors.

At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel stated on the record that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds" — and that the debtors' position is that all such funds are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541. That concession is now the baseline. The gap between reported assets ($50M–$100M) and the $3.25M sale price signals that goodwill, technology, and client relationships — the bulk of the asserted value — transferred to Keyrock. What remains distributable to unsecured creditors turns almost entirely on how the commingling issue resolves.

BlockFills had no retail depositor class. Its counterparties are institutional, operating under negotiated master agreements. Those agreements are in the case record. They have not been publicly litigated yet.

• *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.* — Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371 — Confirmation: Sept. 11, 2026

Practical takeaway: Pull the master trading and lending agreements governing your client's position before the confirmation hearing. The plan's classification of customer claims will reflect the debtors' § 541 opening position unless a creditor successfully contests it. Ballot and objection deadlines flow from the May 28 disclosure-statement order (Dkt. 388). Monitor veritaglobal.net/BlockFills.
