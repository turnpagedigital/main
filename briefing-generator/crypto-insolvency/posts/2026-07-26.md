🪙 $52 million against $163.7 million in frozen wallet IOUs. That's the math facing 11,700 Poolin creditors.

Poolin Technology Pte. Ltd. filed liquidating Chapter 11 in D.N.J. (No. 26-18325) on July 22. No reorganization. No emergence. The company halted all mining and hosting on July 10 and retained a skeleton crew solely to secure equipment and move assets to sale.

• Thor CALAP LLC stalking-horse: $37M (Tarbush) + $15M (Pyote) = $52M floor
• Total prepetition liabilities: $173.1M — including $163.7M in unsecured wallet IOUs
• Estate cash on hand: $1.2M
• Gross stalking-horse coverage of IOUs: 31.8%, before administrative claims and liens

The IOU structure is the critical legal point. Poolin Wallet IOUs are express unsecured promissory notes — not bailment instruments, not segregated deposits. There is no § 541 property-of-the-estate fight here. Customers are general unsecured creditors, full stop. The only lever that moves recovery is auction competition. A 335-buyer marketing process included AI and high-performance compute operators, and cheap West Texas power is genuinely scarce — so the floor could move. A prior $49M deal with China Green Agriculture, announced December 2023, never closed. Execution risk is real.

Practical takeaway: IOU holders should monitor bid procedures and auction scheduling now. The stalking-horse is the ceiling absent competitive bids, and there is no contractual path around it.
