## LinkedIn

Aug. 4: A Texas bankruptcy court rejected Linqto customer motions seeking direct turnover of Circle Internet Group shares — $106.6M worth sitting inside the estate. The motions were found legally deficient on their face.

What this means in plain terms: customers who want out of the confirmed plan structure cannot go around it. The February 6 plan — approved by 95% of customers, with VanEck managing the Closed-End Fund and Forge Global as Liquidating Trustee — is the lane. Standalone turnover motions are not.

The ruling reinforces a concern that has been present since the filing: investors in Linqto's SPV structure may never have held title to the underlying shares. If that reclassification holds, the claim is unsecured and cash-denominated — not an in-kind share delivery.

Two things remain live:
• The Forge Global trustee dispute — filed roughly two weeks ago after Forge allegedly moved to withdraw five days before the July 20 trust launch — is still unresolved on the public record.
• The Kahan appeal (Bankr. D. Mass., No. 24-11592) moved forward August 3 with a Statement of Issues and Record Designation, signaling active prosecution.

Practical takeaway: If you hold a Linqto claim, your recovery path runs through the Liquidating Trust or Closed-End Fund election — not the courts. Monitor the Forge dispute; trustee continuity directly affects plan execution.
