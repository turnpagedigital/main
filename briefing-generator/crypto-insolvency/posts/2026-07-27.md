## LinkedIn

🪙 Storj Labs filed Chapter 11 on July 26 in the Northern District of West Virginia (No. 5:26-bk-00512). STORJ token dropped 16% to ~$0.06 on roughly $20M in volume — nearly the entire circulating supply turned over in one session.

The filing narrative: $35M raised through VC, grants, and a 2017 token sale. Parent Inveniam endorses the restructuring. Two 2024 acquisitions — Valdi and PetaGene — are being shed.

The unusual piece is the proposed ownership structure. Management says it intends to develop a framework letting STORJ token holders convert into equity in the reorganized company alongside management and investors. No eligibility criteria, no snapshot date, no lockup terms, no disclosed equity percentage.

Here is what the Bankruptcy Code says: token holders are not creditors unless they hold a contractual claim. Under 11 U.S.C. § 1129(b), any distribution to equity-equivalent holders requires senior class acceptance or a finding that no senior class is impaired. Neither condition can be evaluated yet — no creditor matrix or asset schedule has been filed.

Additional overhang: Binance placed STORJ on a monitoring tag in May 2026, signaling potential delisting risk that would erode whatever value any equity conversion might produce.

Meanwhile, BlockFills (No. 26-10371, D. Del.) logged Dkt. 538 on July 24 — likely the confirmation order — but the entry description is unpopulated in available syncs. Pull it directly from PACER or veritaglobal.net/blockfills before drawing conclusions on plan effectiveness.

Practical takeaway: If you hold a vendor or storage contract with Storj Labs, file a proof of claim at the first opportunity. Do not treat the equity-conversion proposal as a legal entitlement — it is not one yet.
