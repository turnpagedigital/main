## LinkedIn

Four crypto-sector failures in seven days. That's the July 2026 count — and the pattern is structural, not episodic.

The new filing to watch: Storj Labs, Chapter 11, N.D. W.Va., Case No. 5:26-bk-00512, filed July 26. The company had raised ~$35M pre-petition. Parent Inveniam continues to support operations. The legal novelty: the proposed plan would give STORJ token holders equity in the reorganized entity — a structure with no established Chapter 11 precedent. The mechanics (snapshot date, lockup terms, equity percentage) remain undisclosed. STORJ dropped 16% on the announcement. No creditor should treat an equity conversion offer as equivalent to cash recovery without independent valuation and a full § 1129(a)(7) best-interests analysis.

Alongside Storj: Movement Labs filed Subchapter V in Delaware on July 15 (assets $100K–$500K, liabilities up to $10M). Co-founder Rushi Manche holds the largest scheduled claim at $1.6M while retaining a 34.25% equity stake. That insider-creditor dynamic will face scrutiny at the October 13 plan confirmation. Claims bar date: September 14.

On the exchange side: BitMEX closes September 23 (daily volume ~$400K at closure; ~$200M in prior regulatory penalties). BitMart also winding down. Both represent solvency. AscendEX did too — and as of late July, users still have no recovery guarantee.

BlockFills (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371) is the quietest docket this week — no entries in 72 hours as of July 27. Confirmation hearing was July 13. Pull the order from Veritaglobal before assuming anything.

If you hold a balance on any winding-down platform, withdraw now. Represented solvency and confirmed recovery are not the same thing.
