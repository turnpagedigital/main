⚖️ $3 billion to $100K in assets. That's the MVMT Labs petition in one line.

MVMT Labs, Inc. — the Delaware entity behind Movement blockchain — filed Chapter 11 on July 15, 2026 (Case No. 1:26-bk-11113, Bankr. D. Del.), elected Subchapter V, and landed before Judge Thomas M. Horan. That's the same judge presiding over BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371), the $145M general unsecured pool that's been working toward confirmation since March.

Two cases, one courtroom, same core question: are customer crypto assets property of the estate under § 541, or excluded as trust or bailment property?

MVMT Labs snapshot:
• Assets: $100,001–$500,000 | Liabilities: $1M–$10M
• Creditors: 200–999, including Anchorage Digital and state revenue departments
• Co-founder Rushi Manche holds the largest unsecured claim (~$1.6M) while retaining a significant equity stake — the exact posture that triggers § 510(c) equitable subordination analysis
• Bar date: September 14, 2026 | Plan deadline: ~October 13, 2026
• $41.4M raised pre-petition against sub-$500K in current assets — a straightforward § 548 fraudulent transfer question once the Subchapter V trustee is active

Move Industries has stated it is a separate legal entity and not part of the filing. That separateness argument should be stress-tested by any claimant with contractual or token-based exposure to the broader ecosystem.

Practical takeaway: If you have a claim against MVMT Labs or believe you may have rights against Move Industries, file your proof of claim before September 14 and evaluate the corporate separateness question before that window closes.
