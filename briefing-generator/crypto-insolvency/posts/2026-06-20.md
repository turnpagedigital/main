$3.25M against $145M in unsecured exposure — that's the arithmetic BlockFills creditors are staring at after the June 16 §363 sale hearing before Judge Horan in Delaware.

Keyrock SA was declared successful bidder on May 26. The sale hearing ran as scheduled; a sale order has not been confirmed in public records as of this writing, with eight new docket entries (Nos. 442–445 and related) still pending description. Confirmation is targeted July 13.

What that math means: roughly 2.2 cents on the dollar from sale proceeds alone. The real recovery levers from here are claim classification fights, customer-property treatment, and avoidance actions under §§ 548/550 — which must be filed before the two-year petition-date window closes in March 2028.

The unresolved question at confirmation: whether customer crypto held in BlockFills' omnibus accounts is property of the estate under §541 or subject to a constructive trust. Two pre-petition lawsuits assert misappropriation. The §363 sale does not resolve that. The July 13 confirmation hearing will, unless the plan explicitly addresses it first.

Next active docket event: adversary proceeding *Reliz v. Artha Investment Partners* (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230), hearing June 22.

Separately — FTX's June 16 distribution record date has passed. Transferees who missed KYC and onboarding with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer are locked out of the July 31 wave.

Practical takeaway: BlockFills creditors asserting customer-property claims should consider filing constructive-trust declarations before July 13 if the plan does not separately classify those claims above general unsecured trade debt. Consult restructuring counsel.
