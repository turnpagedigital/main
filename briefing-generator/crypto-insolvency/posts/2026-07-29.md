Four crypto companies announced failures or wind-downs in seven days. The one demanding immediate creditor action is Storj Labs.

Storj Labs, Inc. filed Chapter 11 on July 26, 2026 — Case No. 5:26-bk-00512, Bankr. N.D. W.Va. Parent Inveniam supports the reorganization. The company says it will continue operating. Management has floated a framework letting STORJ token holders convert to equity in the reorganized entity.

That proposal does not replace a proof of claim. It should not be treated as one.

• **Storj Labs** (5:26-bk-00512, N.D. W.Va.) — Ch. 11 filed July 26; earliest docket stage; bar date not yet published; plan documents, first-day motions, and creditor matrix not yet public
• **BlockFills / Reliz** (26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan) — Keyrock SA confirmed successful bidder at $3.25M; confirmation hearing was July 13; dkt. entries 539 and 541 filed July 27 with descriptions unpopulated in CourtListener — pull from PACER now
• **Movement Labs** (Subchapter V, Bankr. D. Del., filed July 15) — declared assets $100,001–$500,000 against liabilities up to $10M; recovery prospects limited
• **BitMart** — solvent wind-down stated; on-chain analysts flagged withdrawal issues post-announcement; trading halts August 26

The Storj token-to-equity path sits behind the full § 726 priority waterfall. Unsecured creditors come before equity. Token holders offered equity are, by definition, behind unsecured creditors. The eligibility criteria, snapshot date, lockup terms, and equity percentage are all undisclosed.

Practical takeaway: File a proof of claim in Storj the moment the bar date posts. Do not wait on the equity conversion framework — it may never materialize in a form that benefits token holders, and a missed bar date is unrecoverable.
