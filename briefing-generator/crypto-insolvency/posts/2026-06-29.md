$3.25M. That's what Keyrock SA agreed to pay for substantially all of BlockFills' assets — against $145M in general unsecured obligations and a company that reported $60B+ in 2025 trading volume.

The § 363 sale hearing closed June 16 before Judge Horan (Bankr. D. Del., 26-10371). What he ruled is not confirmed on the public docket as of this morning. Five new docket entries appeared in the 72 hours through June 26, so post-hearing activity is live. The open question that matters most for creditors: does the sale order's "free and clear" language under § 363(f) extinguish customer-trust or bailment claims against the transferred assets, or do those claims survive the transfer? That fault line — the same one that drove three years of litigation in Celsius and Voyager — is unresolved here.

The toggle plan's dual-track structure (NewCo customer reorganization vs. § 363 alternative transaction) appears to have resolved in favor of the sale track, but no confirmed order is on the public record.

Key items to watch:
• Whether any § 363 sale order has issued and how it treats customer-property claims under § 541
• September 11, 2026 deadline — remaining key date per Verita case calendar
• Liquidating trust avoidance actions: the $8.5M Babel impairment, $12M AEXA settlement, and Celsius arbitration obligation are all in scope for §§ 547–548 tracing

If you hold a BlockFills claim and have not filed a hard-copy proof of claim with Verita (222 N. Pacific Coast Highway, Suite 300, El Segundo, CA 90245), do it now. Electronic submissions are explicitly rejected per the docket.
