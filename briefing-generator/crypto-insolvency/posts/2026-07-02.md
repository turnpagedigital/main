$3.25M sale, $145M in unsecured claims. That arithmetic defines the BlockFills case right now.

Keyrock SA was declared successful bidder for substantially all assets of In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan) at that $3.25M price. The sale hearing ran June 16. Nine new docket entries hit the Veritaglobal portal in the 72 hours ending July 1 — no descriptions published yet. Next hard date: September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET), plan confirmation hearing.

The recovery math for unsecured creditors is severe:
• ~$145M in general unsecured customer and trade obligations
• ~$4.8M in Celsius secured promissory notes with first-priority liens asserted
• $3.25M gross sale proceeds

Debtors' counsel confirmed at the first-day hearing that customer funds were commingled with estate funds. That admission effectively forecloses a trust or bailment argument absent a successful adversary proceeding. The adversary proceeding against Artha Investment Partners (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230) is already filed. Fraudulent-transfer and insider-liability claims through the litigation trust are where unsecured recovery comes from, if it comes at all.

The Chapter 11 trustee motion remains unresolved on the public record. If granted, it reshapes the litigation posture materially.

Practical takeaway: Pull the Veritaglobal docket now (Nos. 474–482). Before the plan's claims objection bar date crystallizes, creditors with customer-property arguments need to assess whether to pursue that characterization or file as general unsecured — those are not the same position under the plan.
