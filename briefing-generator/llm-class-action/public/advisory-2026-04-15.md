# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | April 15, 2026

#### Analysis & Developments

The final-approval architecture of *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), has entered its decisive phase following Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín's April 8 order continuing the Rule 23(e)(2) fairness hearing from its previously noticed date to May 14, 2026 at 2:00 p.m. PT in Courtroom 12 of the San Francisco federal courthouse, with Zoom participation reserved for timely objectors who have preserved their right to be heard under Rule 23(e)(5). The continuance comes on the heels of a companion unsealing order — issued in response to a motion by Professor Lea Victoria Bishop of Indiana University's McKinney School of Law — that placed the full objector record, previously filed under seal, on the public docket and sharpened the procedural posture for appellate preservation. With the March 30, 2026 claim-submission deadline now closed and the Settlement Administrator scheduled to compute pro rata per-title distributions on June 11, 2026, the window for substantive challenges to allocation methodology, notice adequacy, and the Rule 23(h) fee petition is compressed into the next four weeks. ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/)) ([Settlement Site — Key Dates](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/dates))

Professor Bishop's unsealed submission articulates the most structurally serious objector theory in the docket and should be read as the template against which the Court will evaluate fairness, adequacy, and reasonableness under Rule 23(e)(2)(A)–(D). Her filing contends that updates presented to the incoming judge omitted material concerns Judge Alsup had previously aired at the preliminary-approval stage, framed as "an affirmative misrepresentation of the record," and argues that the distribution plan allocates roughly half of the net settlement fund to publishers under contractual reversion and royalty-split mechanics, notwithstanding that the operative complaint pleaded authorial harm and statutory-damage exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). Bishop further flags a class-composition defect: foreign works, which need not carry a U.S. registration under § 411(a) as a precondition to suit, are excluded from participation even where Anthropic concededly ingested them through the Library Genesis and PiLiMi corpora, potentially suppressing the class by more than two million titles. The filing closes with the policy argument that per-title compensation at the levels approved here — roughly $3,000 per work after fees, against a statutory ceiling of $150,000 for willful infringement — sets a comparator that will be deployed against plaintiffs in every downstream AI copyright matter, converting Bartz from a settlement into a de facto tariff. ([Authors Alliance Substack](https://authorsalliance.substack.com/p/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update)) ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2026/04/10/anthropic-copyright-settlement-april-update/))

Parallel docket activity reinforces that Bartz's settlement framework is being tested in real time. In the consolidated *In re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, MDL No. 3143 (S.D.N.Y.), Judge Sidney H. Stein in January 2026 affirmed Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang's order compelling production of the full 20 million-log anonymized ChatGPT output sample, rejecting OpenAI's privacy objection on the ground that users "voluntarily submitted their communications" and finding that even non-reproducing outputs bear on the market-substitution prong of the fair-use analysis — a holding that imports the Bartz/Kadrey framework into the output-side discovery context. In *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal.), the publisher plaintiffs moved on March 23 for partial summary judgment, relying on Anthropic's admissions that at least one Claude checkpoint trained on unlicensed lyrics corpora covering hundreds of the publishers' works, with a companion $3 billion action filed in January 2026 expanding the docket to more than 20,000 compositions — the largest non-class-action copyright demand in recent memory. In *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms*, No. 3:23-cv-03417 (N.D. Cal.), plaintiffs' motion to amend to add contributory infringement and upload-based § 106(3) theories remains live, and the *Disney Enterprises, Inc. v. Midjourney, Inc.*, No. 2:25-cv-05275 (C.D. Cal.) docket held an informal discovery conference on April 7 with an April 3 joint status report, signaling that the character-output theory will advance into merits discovery on an accelerated schedule. ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-to-copyright-p.html?id=102lzo9)) ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/infringement-on-a-massive-scale-umg-concord-and-abkco-ask-court-to-rule-against-ai-company-anthropic-ahead-of-trial/)) ([CourtListener — Disney v. Midjourney](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70513159/disney-enterprises-inc-v-midjourney-inc/))

Internationally, the English High Court's November 4, 2025 judgment in *Getty Images (US) Inc. v. Stability AI Ltd.* rejected secondary infringement on the ground that model weights do not "contain or store" the underlying training works within the meaning of CDPA § 27 — a narrower rationale than the U.S. transformative-use rubric but one that reaches a parallel practical outcome on model-training copies — while recognizing limited trade-mark infringement where Stable Diffusion reproduced the Getty watermark in synthetic output. In December 2025 the trial judge granted Getty permission to appeal the dismissed secondary-infringement claim, with Stability's cross-application on the trade-mark finding denied; the Court of Appeal hearing will now sit against the backdrop of a U.S. doctrinal consensus, post-Bartz/Kadrey, that training is presumptively fair use when inputs are lawfully acquired and the output is not market-substituting. Domestically, the U.S. Copyright Office's May 2025 Part 3 report remains the operative administrative gloss: training is not categorically fair use, pirated inputs weigh against the first factor, and the Office declines to recommend compulsory licensing in favor of an evolving private market — a posture that comports with the Bartz settlement's valuation framework and arms objectors' argument that a private-ordering outcome should not undervalue statutory exposure. ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102lvxe/getty-image-loses-copyright-infringement-claim-against-stability-ai-in-uks-first)) ([Copyright Office Part 3 Report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-3-Generative-AI-Training-Report-Pre-Publication-Version.pdf))

#### Recommended Actions

Class members who timely submitted claims through the anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com portal should treat the next four weeks as the operational window for validation: confirm receipt acknowledgments, reconcile group-registration inputs against the Settlement Administrator's per-title match, and preserve correspondence in the event a pre-distribution dispute requires escalation under the approved administration protocol. Rights-holders who hold unregistered or foreign-origin titles allegedly ingested should retain counsel now to evaluate whether to appear at the May 14 hearing — even without objector standing — and to preserve direct claims outside the class release, because a foreign-works carve-out decision at final approval will drive the next 18 months of parallel individual filings. Authors and small-press objectors whose submissions were unsealed should coordinate oral-argument logistics, file any supplemental authority by the Court's local-rule deadline, and prepare Rule 23(e)(5) statements that tie objections to specific adequacy, allocation, or notice defects rather than policy preferences, because appellate preservation under *Devlin v. Scardelletti*, 536 U.S. 1 (2002), requires a clean record. AI developers facing parallel training-data theories — Meta, OpenAI, Stability, Midjourney, and counterparties in the Concord/UMG actions — should benchmark their licensing pipelines against the Bartz per-title comparator, audit DMCA § 1202 CMI preservation across ingestion pipelines given the disproportionate statutory-damages exposure on that theory, and instruct outside counsel to prepare discovery protocols assuming the post-OpenAI MDL log-production standard applies to output-side substitution analysis. Finally, publishers negotiating licensing agreements should memorialize Bartz comparator data and the Copyright Office Part 3 framework in rate sheets to resist the settlement's use as a downward benchmark.
