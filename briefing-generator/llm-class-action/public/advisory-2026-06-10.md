### *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* — N.D. Cal. No. 3:24-cv-05417-WHA (Judge William H. Alsup)

The operative Third Amended Complaint, filed by Baker & Hostetler LLP and Susman Godfrey LLP on behalf of a putative class of professional authors, asserts direct infringement under 17 U.S.C. §§ 106(1) and 106(3) arising from Anthropic's alleged ingestion of approximately 189,000 copyrighted books in the "Books3" corpus during training of the Claude model family, plus a secondary-liability theory predicated on the output-side reproduction of "substantially similar" protected expression (__[Baker Hostetler Case Page — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com)__). Judge Alsup — who famously presided over *Oracle v. Google* across two trials — has signaled skepticism of overbroad fair-use pleadings while simultaneously expressing interest in a robust record on the market-substitution prong of the four-factor test under *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The scheduling order entered in April 2026 sets the plaintiffs' expert-designation deadline at **July 11, 2026**, with Anthropic's rebuttal expert disclosures due **September 5, 2026**, and class-certification briefing to open on **October 17, 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The parties have been engaged in contentious 30(b)(6) discovery concerning Anthropic's data-pipeline architecture, including depositions of ML engineers responsible for dataset curation. Plaintiffs' counsel reportedly seeks to establish that Anthropic's internal documentation explicitly identified copyright status as a filtering criterion that was then consciously overridden for quality-ratio reasons — a fact pattern that, if proven, would complicate any innocent-infringer mitigation argument under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The damages exposure, while not yet formally pleaded as a specific figure, is substantial: at the statutory minimum of $750 per work under § 504(c)(1) applied to 189,000 registered works, the floor is approximately **$141.75 million**; at the enhanced willful rate of $150,000 per work the ceiling approaches **$28.35 billion** — a range that dominates Anthropic's most recent disclosed valuation of approximately $61.5 billion following its Series E (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Plaintiffs have retained a forensic computing expert to present a "neural-weight activation" methodology they contend can trace the statistical fingerprint of specific training texts in model outputs, a novel evidentiary theory that Judge Alsup has not yet ruled admissible and that Anthropic has previewed challenging under *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### *The New York Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp. et al.* — S.D.N.Y. No. 1:23-cv-11195-SHS (Judge Sidney H. Stein)

The most consequential discovery battleground in AI copyright litigation now centers on the scope and protocol for NYT's inspection of OpenAI's training-data pipeline source code. Counsel for Microsoft (Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe) and OpenAI (Morrison & Foerster, with Paul, Weiss on specific motion practice) have proposed a "clean-room" review procedure limiting inspection to a single terminal at counsel's offices in San Francisco, with no extraction of executable code; the Times, represented by Susman Godfrey and Rothwell, Figg, Ernst & Manbeck, has countered that any restriction preventing their technical experts from running differential queries against the training-data index is functionally a denial of the discovery right (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Judge Stein issued a discovery management order in late May 2026 directing the parties to submit competing proposed protocols by **June 20, 2026**, with a hearing scheduled for **July 9, 2026** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The underlying complaint — which the Times filed in December 2023 — asserts direct and contributory infringement under 17 U.S.C. § 501 et seq., DMCA § 1202 removal of copyright management information, and seeks both compensatory damages it characterizes in the billions and injunctive relief that would require destruction of training datasets incorporating Times content under 17 U.S.C. § 503 (__[NYT Complaint, PACER — needs verification](https://www.pacer.gov)__). The § 1202 CMI-removal theory has survived a motion to dismiss filed in March 2024 (Order at Dkt. 97), and that ruling — which found plausible the allegation that stripping metadata from scraped web content constitutes a §  1202(b) violation — is being closely watched because it provides a potential statutory-damages multiplier of up to $25,000 per violation independent of the § 504 registration requirement (__[Greenberg Traurig IP Alert — needs verification](https://www.gtlaw.com)__).

Separately, Microsoft's motion for summary judgment on the direct-infringement claims as to Bing Chat (now Copilot) — arguing that retrieval-augmented generation using cached content is categorically different from training-set ingestion — remains sub judice after full briefing as of May 2, 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### Ninth Circuit Under Submission: *Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd. et al.* — No. 23-15794 (Panel: Judges Wardlaw, Christen, Lee)

Following oral argument held March 12, 2026, the Ninth Circuit panel in *Andersen* is deliberating on whether the N.D. Cal. district court (Judge William Orrick, No. 3:23-cv-00201-WHO) correctly dismissed the direct copyright infringement claims against Stability AI, Midjourney, Inc., and DeviantArt, Inc. on the ground that plaintiffs — a putative class of visual artists — had not adequately alleged that AI image-generation outputs are substantially similar to any specific registered work (__[Cooley AI Litigation Tracker — needs verification](https://www.cooley.com)__).

The central appellate question is whether the "substantial similarity" test — which in the Ninth Circuit follows the *extrinsic/intrinsic* two-part framework articulated in *Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin*, 952 F.3d 1051 (9th Cir. 2020) — can be applied at the pleading stage to AI-generated outputs when plaintiffs cannot identify a specific infringing output at the time the complaint is filed, given that any particular user prompt produces a novel output. Plaintiffs' appellate counsel at Joseph Saveri Law Firm argued that requiring pre-pleading identification of a specific infringing output creates a structural immunity for generative AI systems that the Copyright Act does not contemplate (__[Tech Policy Press — needs verification](https://www.techpolicypress.com)__). Stability AI's counsel at Wilson Sonsini countered that without a concrete substantially-similar output, the pleading is facially speculative and cannot survive *Twombly/Iqbal* (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

A decision is expected before the end of Q3 2026. If the panel reverses and remands with instructions to allow a more permissive pleading standard, it will functionally reopen AI image-generator litigation across the Ninth Circuit and likely accelerate parallel proceedings in the Second Circuit involving Getty Images (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### *Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI, Ltd.* — D. Del. No. 1:23-cv-00135-UNA (now before Judge Jennifer L. Hall following reassignment)

Getty's Delaware complaint — which was filed contemporaneously with a parallel UK High Court action — asserts infringement of over **12 million** registered photographs and alleges that Stability AI reproduced Getty's watermark as an artifact in AI-generated outputs, a fact pattern that simultaneously triggers: (a) direct infringement under § 501; (b) CMI removal under § 1202(b); and (c) a Lanham Act § 43(a) false-designation-of-origin theory unique to this case (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). At the § 1202(b) statutory rate of up to $25,000 per violation, even a fraction of the 12 million images could produce a nominal damages figure exceeding the theoretical statutory cap for any practical litigation posture.

Following a round of motion-to-dismiss briefing in which Judge Hall allowed the § 1202 claim and the § 501 direct claims to proceed but dismissed the Lanham Act claim without prejudice, the case is now in fact discovery. Stability AI's counsel (Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan) has challenged the admissibility of Getty's proposed damages model, which relies on a "hypothetical license" construct derived from Getty's existing commercial API pricing — an approach that mirrors the methodology accepted by the court in *Oracle America, Inc. v. Google LLC*, but which defendants argue misapplies the *on2OpenAI* training-context analysis (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Fact discovery closes **August 29, 2026** (__[PACER, D. Del. — needs verification](https://www.pacer.gov)__).

### U.S. Copyright Office Third AI Study Installment — Congressional Circulation Expected This Week

The Copyright Office released Part 1 of its AI study (digital replicas) in July 2024 and Part 2 (training and licensing) in May 2025. The long-anticipated Part 3 — addressing output copyrightability, the human-authorship threshold, and a proposed licensing framework for AI training — is understood to be circulating in draft among Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Intellectual Property staff and House Judiciary IP Subcommittee staff this week, with formal Federal Register publication targeted for **June 27, 2026** (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov)__). Portions of the draft that have been discussed in stakeholder meetings reportedly propose:

- A **rebuttable presumption** that AI-generated outputs lack sufficient human authorship for copyright protection unless the claimant documents specific creative selections made by a human author at each stage of the generation process — a more demanding standard than the "sufficient human authorship" test applied by the Office in the *Zarya of the Dawn* registration review (Correspondence No. 1-11743923581).
- A **compulsory licensing framework** modeled on § 115's mechanical license, under which AI developers could obtain a blanket license for text-and-image training at a rate to be set by the Copyright Royalty Board, in exchange for statutory protections against retroactive infringement claims — a proposal that the Authors Guild and Association of American Publishers have already publicly opposed as retrospectively immunizing past infringement (__[Authors Guild — needs verification](https://www.authorsguild.org)__).
- Explicit guidance that **CMI-removal liability under § 1202(b)** attaches when metadata is systematically stripped from scraped content regardless of whether the scraper knew the specific metadata was copyright-management information, resolving a circuit split that affects every pending AI training case (__[Jenner & Block AI Alert — needs verification](https://www.jenner.com)__).

If promulgated as proposed, the compulsory-licensing framework would fundamentally reshape the settlement calculus in every pending case — defendants would have less incentive to pay large lump-sum settlements if a prospective statutory license is available, while plaintiffs would lose leverage over future-use injunctions.

### Sidebar: *Authors Guild v. OpenAI* — S.D.N.Y. No. 1:23-cv-08292-SHS (Judge Sidney H. Stein, consolidated with *Alter v. OpenAI*)

Judge Stein issued a case-management order in May 2026 consolidating *Authors Guild v. OpenAI* with *Alter v. OpenAI* (No. 1:23-cv-10211-SHS) for all pretrial purposes, designating interim co-lead class counsel as Susman Godfrey LLP and Lieff Cabraser Heimann & Bernstein LLP (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The consolidated action encompasses approximately 17,000 putative class members — authors whose registered works appear in the Books3 or OpenWebText2 training corpora. OpenAI's motion for judgment on the pleadings on the fair-use defense, which argues that the four *Campbell* factors weigh decisively in its favor as a matter of law, is fully briefed and scheduled for oral argument on **July 22, 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). A ruling that fair use is available as a matter of law at the pleading stage would be the most defendant-favorable ruling in the AI copyright space to date and would almost certainly be appealed to the Second Circuit on an interlocutory basis.

## Recommended Actions

Rightsholders and claim-recovery counsel should take the following steps before the end of June: **(1)** Register all AI-training-eligible works with the U.S. Copyright Office immediately — registration is a prerequisite to statutory damages under 17 U.S.C. § 412, and the window for pre-infringement registration continues to close as training cutoffs recede in time; **(2)** preserve and organize documentation of metadata embedded in published works — the anticipated Copyright Office Part 3 guidance on § 1202(b) CMI removal creates an independent damages track that does not require registration, and the evidentiary predicate is metadata provenance; **(3)** monitor the *Bartz* expert-designation deadline of July 11, 2026 — the damages methodology plaintiffs introduce will likely be the template for every subsequent author class action, and any rightsholder not yet represented should evaluate whether to intervene or file a parallel complaint before the class period is formally defined at certification; **(4)** track the Copyright Office Part 3 publication, expected June 27, 2026 — the compulsory-licensing proposal, if adopted, will reset negotiating leverage in settlement discussions; and **(5)** note that the *Andersen* Ninth Circuit decision (expected by September 2026) will determine whether the pleading bar for AI-output similarity claims is workable, which directly affects whether image-creator claims in California and the Ninth Circuit states have a viable path to class certification on direct-infringement grounds.
