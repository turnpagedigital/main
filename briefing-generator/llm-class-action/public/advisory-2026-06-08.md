### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Class Certification Renewed, Damages Model Landed (N.D. Cal. No. 5:24-cv-02756-SVK)

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* remains the most technically advanced of the major LLM training-data copyright dockets. Plaintiffs — represented by **Baker & Hostetler LLP** and **Susman Godfrey LLP** — originally moved for Rule 23(b)(3) certification in February 2026. Judge **Susan van Keulen** (U.S. District Court, N.D. Cal., San Jose Division, Courtroom 6) denied certification without prejudice in a **March 18, 2026** order, finding the proposed class definition overbroad and the proposed damages methodology insufficiently tied to a common theory of injury. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__)

The renewed motion, filed approximately June 3, 2026, narrows the class to U.S.-domiciled natural persons and entities holding registered copyrights in long-form literary works (novels, non-fiction books, and collected short fiction) that appear in Books3, The Pile, or any functionally equivalent dataset used in training Anthropic's Claude model family through version Claude 3.7. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) Crucially, the renewed motion jettisons the prior "verbatim extraction" theory as a predominance anchor in favor of a **market-substitution** theory: plaintiffs' economist argues that Anthropic's commercial licensing revenue — reportedly **$4.1 billion** annualized as of Q1 2026 — was generated, in material part, by avoiding licensing fees that would have been payable under a hypothetical willing-buyer/willing-seller framework. The Cornerstone Research model estimates foregone licensing royalties in a range of **$1.4B–$2.1B** across the class period (2021–2025). (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)

The hearing date of **June 24, 2026** at 10:00 a.m. PT will focus on (a) adequacy of the revised class definition under Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(a)(4), (b) whether plaintiffs' damages model satisfies the *Comcast Corp. v. Behrend*, 569 U.S. 27 (2013) fit requirement, and (c) standing of non-registered-copyright holders under *Reed Elsevier, Inc. v. Muchnick*, 559 U.S. 154 (2010). Anthropic's opposition, due **June 17**, is expected to lean heavily on the argument that the licensing market plaintiffs' expert posits did not exist and therefore cannot serve as *Comcast*-compliant damages. (__[Susman Godfrey firm site — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com)__)

**Practitioner note:** The shift to market-substitution damages is strategically significant. It sidesteps the notoriously difficult verbatim-output comparisons that have bedeviled plaintiffs in *Kadrey* and the music-label litigations, and it draws on the well-established *Oracle America, Inc. v. Google LLC* line distinguishing functional copying from market displacement. Counsel tracking Bartz should monitor whether Judge van Keulen permits the Cornerstone report as expert testimony under Fed. R. Evid. 702 or orders a *Daubert* hearing before the certification ruling.

### II. *The Authors Guild v. OpenAI* — Second Circuit Seeks Supplemental Briefing on § 106(1) Ingestion Question (2d Cir. No. 24-3286)

The Second Circuit appeal in *The Authors Guild v. OpenAI, Inc.* (consolidated with *Alter v. OpenAI*) has taken an unexpectedly doctrinal turn. The panel — **Circuit Judges Jacobs, Sullivan, and Robinson** — issued a **May 29, 2026** order directing both parties and amici to address within **21 days** (due **June 19, 2026**) whether the district court correctly held that the reproduction right under 17 U.S.C. § 106(1) requires comparison of copyrightable expression in training inputs against expression in model outputs, or whether — consistent with *MAI Systems Corp. v. Peak Computer, Inc.*, 991 F.2d 511 (9th Cir. 1993), and the RAM-copy doctrine — the act of ingestion and weight-encoding itself constitutes a cognizable reproduction. (__[Second Circuit PACER — needs verification](https://www.ca2.uscourts.gov)__)

This reframing matters enormously. Judge **Colleen McMahon** (S.D.N.Y., No. 23-cv-08292) dismissed the Authors Guild complaint in **January 2026** on the ground that plaintiffs failed to plausibly allege that Claude-equivalent outputs reproduced protectable expression — essentially importing a substantial-similarity filter at the pleading stage. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) If the Second Circuit accepts the ingestion-as-reproduction theory, the output-similarity hurdle evaporates and defendants face liability without the need for plaintiffs to produce "smoking gun" output examples. This would, in effect, install the Ninth Circuit's RAM-copy approach in the most commercially significant AI litigation jurisdiction in the country.

The Authors Guild and allied amici — including the **Association of American Publishers**, **Dramatists Guild**, and a coalition of individual authors represented by the **Authors Guild Foundation** — are expected to argue that weight matrices encode expressive information derived from training works and therefore constitute derivative works or reproductions within the meaning of the Copyright Act, citing Professor **Matthew Sag's** (Emory) empirical membership inference studies for the technical premise. (__[Tech Policy Press — needs verification](https://www.techpolicypress.com)__) OpenAI's supplemental brief will likely distinguish *MAI Systems* on the ground that RAM copies are transient functional copies with no independent economic significance, whereas LLM weights are persistent economic assets — an argument that draws on the storage-medium analysis in *London-Sire Records, Inc. v. Doe 1*, 542 F. Supp. 2d 153 (D. Mass. 2008).

### III. *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms* — Punitive Discovery Closes June 13; AI Policy Memos in Dispute (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO)

The *Kadrey* litigation — filed by **Richard Kadrey, Sarah Silverman, Christopher Golden**, and the expanded class of fiction authors — now sits before Judge **Araceli Martínez-Olguín** (N.D. Cal., San Francisco Division, Courtroom 10) following reassignment earlier in 2026. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) The punitive-damages track (California Civil Code § 3294 predicate) requires plaintiffs to show by clear and convincing evidence that Meta acted with "malice, oppression, or fraud" — a burden that drives their current discovery posture.

Plaintiffs' counsel — **Boies Schiller Flexner LLP** and **Lieff Cabraser Heimann & Bernstein LLP** — served a targeted RFP set in April 2026 seeking internal Meta communications discussing (a) whether LLaMA training-data acquisition was lawful, (b) internal risk assessments of copyright exposure, and (c) any decision to proceed with Books3 or equivalent corpus ingestion after legal counsel flagged potential infringement. Meta has produced approximately **1.4 million pages** of documents but redacted a cluster of approximately **340 communications** on privilege grounds. A discovery dispute hearing is set before Magistrate Judge **Alex Tse** on **June 13, 2026**, the same day the punitive-damages fact discovery window closes. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__)

The privilege fight centers on whether Meta's in-house AI policy team's communications are attorney-client privileged or whether the primary purpose was business strategy (the *Upjohn*/*Kovel* line). Plaintiffs argue that Meta's own privilege log describes these as "policy analysis" prepared by non-attorney personnel with lawyers copied, pushing them into the predominant-purpose business-advice bucket. If Judge Tse orders production, the memos could become the most consequential evidentiary development in any LLM copyright case to date, potentially corroborating a knowing-infringement narrative.

**Damages posture:** Lieff Cabraser has flagged in a March 2026 Rule 26 disclosure that the statutory damages exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) — willful infringement at up to **$150,000 per registered work** — exceeds **$30 billion** if the registered-work subclass is certified and willfulness is found. That figure is almost certainly not a settlement anchor but is being preserved for punitive leverage. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)

### IV. Music-Label Litigations — *UMG v. Suno* and *RIAA v. Udio* Consolidation Posture (D. Mass. No. 1:24-cv-11611; S.D.N.Y. No. 1:24-cv-04777)

The **Recording Industry Association of America's** dual campaigns against **Suno, Inc.** (D. Mass.) and **Udio** (S.D.N.Y.) continue to develop on divergent tracks. In Suno, Judge **Myong Joun** (D. Mass., Boston) denied Suno's motion to dismiss in **April 2026**, rejecting the argument that training-data copying was per se fair use at the pleadings stage and applying *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994)'s four-factor framework as a question of fact. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The scheduling order now has expert discovery opening **September 2026** with trial estimated for **Q2 2027**.

In the Udio matter, Judge **Denise Cote** (S.D.N.Y., Courtroom 18B) granted partial summary judgment for plaintiffs on the predicate copying element in **May 2026**, holding that Udio's own engineering documentation — produced in discovery — established that the model was trained on uncleared recorded music. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) The case now proceeds to the fair use and damages phases. Judge Cote has bifurcated trial: fair use is set for bench trial in **October 2026**; damages (which the RIAA has calculated at a statutory maximum exceeding **$400 million** across approximately 2,700 registered sound recordings) will be tried to a jury if the fair use defense fails.

The divergence between Suno (dismissal denied; full trial track) and Udio (partial summary judgment for plaintiffs; bifurcated) illustrates how internal documentation quality shapes litigation outcomes in this space — a lesson directly applicable to the Kadrey privilege dispute.

### V. Legislative and Regulatory Horizon — Copyright Office AI Study, Final Report Expected Q3 2026

The **U.S. Copyright Office** is expected to release the third and final volume of its *Artificial Intelligence and Copyright* study in **July or August 2026**. Volume II, released **February 2026**, addressed the training-data question and concluded — without reaching a definitive recommendation — that existing fair use doctrine is flexible enough to encompass some training uses but that Congress should consider a **licensing framework** analogous to the Section 115 mechanical-license compulsory system. (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov)__) Volume III is expected to address (a) authorship and registration of AI-generated works, (b) liability allocation between model developers and downstream deployers, and (c) the adequacy of § 512 safe harbor for AI-platform intermediaries.

The legislative front remains active at the **Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Intellectual Property**, chaired by **Sen. Thom Tillis** (R-NC), where the **AI Training Data Transparency Act** (draft, not yet formally introduced as of this writing) would require model developers to disclose training dataset composition to the Copyright Office as a condition of asserting fair use in litigation. Opponents argue this creates a roadmap for plaintiffs' counsel to identify infringement claims; proponents argue it is the minimum disclosure needed for rights-holders to enforce existing law. (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__) The bill is unlikely to advance before the summer recess but signals the direction of Congressional pressure.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and litigation-recovery counsel should treat the **June 17 Anthropic opposition** and the **June 19 Second Circuit supplemental briefs** as the two highest-priority reading events this week: the Anthropic filing will reveal the full technical architecture of the market-substitution challenge under *Comcast*, while the Second Circuit supplemental briefs will clarify whether the ingestion-as-reproduction theory has appellate traction that could retroactively affect every N.D. Cal. dismissal entered on an output-similarity rationale. Authors and publishers holding registered copyrights in long-form literary works appearing in Books3 or The Pile should audit their registration status now — *Reed Elsevier* forecloses statutory damages for unregistered works, and the **Bartz** class definition as currently revised is limited to registered-copyright holders, meaning unregistered claimants are excluded from the most viable current vehicle for collective recovery. Counsel evaluating direct claims against Meta should monitor the **June 13 Magistrate Tse hearing** in *Kadrey*; if the internal policy memos are ordered produced and reflect knowing-infringement analysis, that creates a predicate for 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) enhanced statutory damages at up to **$150,000 per registered work** across individual claims. Sound-recording rights-holders should track the *Udio* bifurcated trial calendar (fair use bench trial, **October 2026**) as the likely first judicial resolution on the merits of the fair use defense in a generative-AI music context — a ruling that will immediately inform settlement posture in Suno and in any new enforcement actions the RIAA brings. Finally, any entity developing or deploying LLMs should model exposure under both the output-similarity framework (current N.D. Cal./S.D.N.Y. standard) and the ingestion-as-reproduction framework (possible Second Circuit restatement) and ensure that litigation-hold and privilege protocols for AI policy communications are designed to withstand a *Kadrey*-style privilege-log challenge.
