### Bartz v. Anthropic PBC — No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín)
**Status: Final judgment entered. Administration live. Next fund installment: September 25, 2026.**


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the landmark $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment in the case.
 The 11-day-old judgment is now the controlling posture; the desk's focus shifts entirely to administration, the fee award, and the distribution schedule.

**The order.** 
Judge Martínez-Olguín issued a 23-page order granting final approval of the class action settlement, resolving several outstanding administrative details, and ruling on attorneys' fees.
 
The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," holding that it "was negotiated at arm's length; the relief provided for the Class is adequate; and the proposed Settlement treats Class Members equitably relative to each other," and overruled all 53 objections filed opposing the settlement.


**Objectors.** 
The most substantial objections came from people outside the settlement looking in; the court declined to entertain objections from non-class members, but reminded those individuals that, precisely because the settlement does not cover them, they remain free to bring their own claims.
 That passage is not dicta — it is an express judicial invitation for output-side and post-August 25, 2025 claims, and is the order's most consequential language for rights-holders sitting outside the class.

**Fee award.** 
The fee award for plaintiffs' attorneys received extended discussion; class counsel initially sought $300 million, then trimmed the request to $187.5 million. The court approved approximately $101.5 million.
 That reduction — from a 25% lodestar to roughly 6.8% of the $1.5 billion fund — reflects the court's scrutiny of the multiplier, but the absolute dollar figure remains extraordinary. 
By rough math it works out to approximately $2,953 per hour across the 34,381.6 hours of attorney time the court credited.


**Class composition and claims rate.** 
Under the settlement, Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion plus interest to a class of copyright owners whose works were allegedly torrented from pirated datasets — specifically Library Genesis (LibGen) and Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi) — to train its large language models; the certified class encompasses all beneficial or legal copyright owners of books found in those datasets, as identified on a Works List containing 482,460 works, each possessing an ISBN or ASIN and a U.S. Copyright Office registration within five years of first publication.
 
In aggregate, the decision marks the largest class action copyright settlement in history, with a reported claims rate of 92.77 percent.
 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, and 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 or .03% of the total number of Works.
 (The slight variance between 91.3% and 92.77% reflects differing measurement bases — by claimants versus by works — and is not material.)

**Per-work recovery.** 
The court confirmed that the estimated per-work payment of approximately $3,000 is four times the statutory minimum for willful copyright infringement
 under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2). That framing — four times the § 504(c)(2) floor — is the court's implicit signal on the strength of the infringement posture, and will recur in licensing negotiations across the sector.

**What the release does and does not cover.** 
Under the settlement, class members release only claims relating to Anthropic's past acquisition and copying of their works — the "inputs" side — through August 25, 2025. Claims based on AI outputs are not released, and neither are any claims of any kind about future conduct.
 
Anthropic has also represented that neither dataset, nor any portion of them, was in the training corpus of any of its commercially released large language models.
 That representation is now embedded in the final judgment and creates exposure for any future contradictory disclosure.

**Installment schedule and next milestone.** 
Anthropic agreed to pay the $1.5 billion into the settlement fund distributed in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The third tranche — $375 million on September 25, 2026 — is now 55 days out. 
The Authors Guild looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process; while a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not been announced, it hopes and expects that this will happen quickly.
 Distribution has not commenced as of today; the administrator (JND Legal Administration) carries operational control. Counsel monitoring claims recoveries should confirm JND's timeline through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com.

**Special Master.** 
The court reaffirmed the process for resolution of any disputes: those "that cannot be resolved by mutual agreement with the settlement administrator" will be submitted to the Special Master, and all submissions "should remain confidential and under seal," with Special Master decisions final.
 The sealed-track dispute mechanism insulates the administration from further public docket activity on work-level allocation disputes — watch the Special Master track, not the ECF docket, for claims-recovery intelligence.

**Downstream signal for the AI copyright docket.** The final judgment produces two usable comparators for counsel in active matters. First, the per-work $3,000 recovery — at ~four times the § 504(c)(2) minimum — anchors market-harm arguments under the fourth *Campbell* factor in pending fair-use cross-motions. Second, the court's explicit preservation of output-side and future-conduct claims is a pleading roadmap for cases like *Concord/UMG v. Anthropic* that center on Claude's reproduction of protected content in response to prompts, not merely training-data ingestion.

### Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC — No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee / Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen)
**Status: Active — Second Amended Complaint filed July 22, 2026. Partial summary judgment motion pending.**

The most significant docket activity in this beat over the past two weeks: 
Concord Music Group, Universal Music Publishing Group, and ABKCO Music filed an amended complaint against Anthropic over the alleged copying of copyrighted song lyrics by its Claude chatbot; the publishers filed the Second Amended Complaint on July 22 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California — an expanded version of the same amended complaint they first put forward in May.


**What changed in the Second Amended Complaint.** 
The latest filing builds on earlier claims and incorporates information obtained through the discovery process; at the heart of the dispute is the publishers' claim that Claude was trained on copyrighted lyrics and was designed to provide those lyrics in response to user requests, with internal records showing Anthropic employees and contractors prompted the system with requests for copyrighted lyrics during the model's development.
 
Publishers further allege that Anthropic used tools that removed copyright notices and other identifying information from lyrics during the training process, and argue that the company's systems were intentionally built to reproduce lyrics, describing the capability as "a feature, not a bug."
 That framing maps directly to 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b) (removal or alteration of copyright management information) — which carries statutory damages of up to $25,000 per violation — and to willfulness under § 504(c)(2).

**Scope of the case and the parallel actions.** 
This case updates the litigation first brought in October 2023, covering around 500 songs, and is the first of two separate copyright lawsuits the group has filed against Anthropic.
 
Their second, separate suit, filed in January 2026, covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion over Anthropic's alleged torrenting of lyrics from pirate "shadow" libraries.
 
BMG filed a third suit in March 2026, citing 493 compositions and alleging the AI firm had built its business on unlicensed works.
 The three music-publisher actions against Anthropic collectively represent an exposure stack materially larger than the $1.5 billion *Bartz* settlement.

**Narrowed claims and pending summary judgment.** 
The publishers have narrowed the case to direct infringement and alleged removal of copyright management information, dropping earlier contributory and vicarious infringement claims; they moved for partial summary judgment in March 2026, asking the court
 to rule on liability before trial. That motion remains pending before Judge Lee. The narrowing to direct infringement and § 1202 CMI removal is tactically significant — it eliminates the secondary-liability knowledge disputes that Anthropic used to contest the earlier pleading, and positions the publishers for a cleaner summary judgment record on the output-reproduction theory.

**Discovery posture.** Prior discovery orders in this case established the framework now being tested by the Second Amended Complaint: 
in May 2025, the defendant was compelled to produce a total of 5 million prompt-output pairs, drawn equally from pre-suit and post-suit data and randomly selected.
 The July 22 filing incorporates materials from that production — specifically the internal prompting by Anthropic employees and contractors — which is why the output-as-designed allegation now appears in the complaint with concrete factual support rather than inference.
