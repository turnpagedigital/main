# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | May 13, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Tomorrow, May 14, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. PT, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín will convene the Rule 23(e) fairness hearing for *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), the $1.5 billion class action settlement that remains the largest copyright resolution in United States history. The hearing — available via Zoom to objectors who preserved their Rule 23(e)(5) rights and in person at the San Francisco Federal Courthouse, Courtroom 12 — will address at least five principal objection themes distilled from the unsealed filings ordered accessible in Judge Martínez-Olguín's April 8 docket entry (Dkt. 632): the exclusion of foreign and non-U.S.-registered works from the class definition despite 17 U.S.C. § 411(a)'s registration exemption for foreign authors, a concern that could affect over two million titles; the distribution plan's systematic favoring of publishers over individual authors, with Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's objection (Dkt. 630) estimating that publishers could capture roughly fifty percent or more of the net settlement fund; the allegedly misleading class notice that obscured opt-out value by framing the choice as settlement-or-nothing rather than contextualizing the $3,000 per-title payout against 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)'s $150,000 statutory-damages ceiling; class counsel's alleged concealment of Judge Alsup's December 2025 concerns from the incoming judge; and the group-registration undercounting methodology that collapses dozens of distinct books registered under a single group number into one claimable work (Dkt. 641). ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/))

The settlement's claims data remains remarkable by any class-action standard: 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works have been claimed — a 91.3 percent claim rate that dwarfs the typical ten-percent benchmark — with approximately 120,000 individual authors filing. ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/anthropic-settlement-update-91-percent-of-books-claimed/)) Anthropic's second $300 million installment was funded on April 30, bringing the total in escrow to $600 million of the $1.5 billion total; the Settlement Administrator is scheduled to calculate distributions on June 11, with disbursement beginning later that month. Class counsel's revised fee petition of $187.5 million — reduced from the original $300 million (20%) to 12.5 percent of the gross fund — awaits ruling alongside the fairness determination, with Judge Martínez-Olguín having signaled skepticism toward coordination-counsel fees outside the Susman Godfrey / Lieff Cabraser lead structure. Notable opt-outs include John Carreyrou and other authors who have elected to pursue a separate lawsuit against Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta, xAI, and Perplexity, preserving their individual statutory-damages claims under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/participating-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement/))

The parallel music-publishing front against Anthropic continues to intensify. The RIAA, NMPA, and six additional music industry groups filed an amicus brief on March 30 urging the court to reject Anthropic's fair use defense in the original *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC* action, while UMG, Concord, and ABKCO's January 2026 supersized complaint — alleging infringement of over 20,000 songs and seeking $3 billion in statutory damages — proceeds through early discovery in the Northern District of California. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/riaa-nmpa-and-more-file-amicus-brief-backing-music-publishers-against-anthropic-arguing-ai-companys-unlicensed-copying-is-inexcusable/)) The plaintiffs' motion for partial summary judgment, filed March 23, argues the evidence of Anthropic's infringement is "overwhelming" and seeks a ruling that Anthropic cannot invoke fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 for mass copying from pirate library sites. BMG Rights Management's separate March 2026 action adds 493 copyrighted compositions to Anthropic's docket exposure. Meanwhile, the *In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation* MDL (No. 3143, S.D.N.Y.) continues its intensive discovery phase under Judge Stein, with the March 9 order expanding compelled ChatGPT log production to 78 million plus 10 million additional reservoirs beyond the initial 20 million-log sample, and a March 10 order compelling production of co-founder Greg Brockman's personal journal — the most expansive compelled disclosure in any AI copyright case to date. ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-judge-affirms); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright))

On the appellate horizon, the Third Circuit has set oral argument in *Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence* for June 11 — one month after tomorrow's fairness hearing — making it the first appellate fair-use argument on AI training. The district court's 2025 ruling that ROSS Intelligence's copying of Westlaw headnotes was not fair use as a matter of law generated ten amicus briefs from academics, nonprofits, and technology companies urging reversal, alongside ROSS's own Rule 28(j) letter citing the *ASTM v. UpCodes* fair-use decision. ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/04/14/third-circuit-sets-oral-argument-on-june-11-for-1st-appeal-of-decision-on-fair-use-in-ai-training-case-follows-another-recent-third-circuit-decision-on-fair-use-in-american-society-for-testing-v-upc/)) In the UK, Getty Images has been granted permission to appeal the November 2024 High Court judgment largely dismissing its copyright claims against Stability AI, ensuring that cross-border AI copyright doctrine remains in active development through 2026. ([Chambers and Partners](https://chambers.com/articles/copyright-and-ai-remain-in-focus-for-2026-with-getty-appeal-given-the-green-light)) On the legislative front, the NO FAKES Act and TRAIN Act continue to gather momentum, with Senators Blackburn and Welch convening an April 22 roundtable with over twenty artists, and the Copyright Office's Part 1 report on digital replicas citing an "urgent" need for federal action. ([Blackburn Senate](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/4/ai/blackburn-welch-hold-roundtable-with-over-20-artists-who-support-no-fakes-act-and-train-act-to-protect-creators-from-ai-harms); [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/copyright-office-cites-urgent-need-for-digital-replicas-law-as-legislation-introduced/))

## Recommended Actions

Class members with claims already filed should verify their payment information through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com before the June 11 distribution computation, particularly authors who registered works under group copyright registrations — the undercounting methodology flagged in Dkt. 641 may require supplemental documentation to ensure each distinct title is recognized as a separate claimable work. Rights-holders who opted out to preserve individual statutory-damages claims under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c) should be monitoring tomorrow's fairness hearing for the court's treatment of the per-work valuation framework, as the precedent set by the settlement's $3,000-per-title benchmark will directly inform litigation-value assessments across parallel dockets. Music publishers with compositions in Anthropic's training corpus should coordinate with RIAA and NMPA counsel on the *Concord* summary-judgment briefing and evaluate whether the $3 billion second-lawsuit theory supports joining as intervenors. AI developers facing similar input-side training theories should be conducting outbound-training-data audits against the Bartz framework, benchmarking internal licensing-deal comparators (Disney-OpenAI $1 billion, HarperCollins, Axel Springer), and evaluating DMCA § 1202 CMI-stripping exposure in light of the *Doe v. GitHub* Ninth Circuit interlocutory appeal on copyright-management-information claims.
