# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | April 17, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The Bartz v. Anthropic settlement enters a critical twenty-seven-day countdown to the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín in San Francisco, with the settlement administration apparatus now processing an undisclosed volume of claims submitted by the March 30 deadline while Anthropic's second installment of $300 million comes due on April 30 — a payment that, combined with the initial tranche, will bring funded commitments to roughly $600 million of the $1.5 billion total before the court rules on final approval under Rule 23(e). The Writer Beware blog's April 10 analysis highlights that the settlement administrator has yet to publish final claim counts, a data point that will be central to Judge Martínez-Olguín's assessment of the settlement's adequacy under the Rule 23(e)(2) factors, particularly whether the approximately $3,000 per-title payout represents fair value against the statutory ceiling of $150,000 per work under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2026/04/10/anthropic-copyright-settlement-april-update/)) Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's unsealed objection continues to sharpen the contested issues ahead of the hearing, including the distribution methodology that allegedly favors publishers over individual authors, the group copyright registration gap that may exclude over two million works from adequate per-title compensation, and the class counsel fee petition of $375 million — representing a 25% lodestar — which Bishop challenges on proportionality grounds given what she characterizes as Class Counsel's omission of Judge Alsup's December 2025 concerns about fee-sharing from updates to the incoming judge. ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/))

In the parallel music litigation, the Concord Music, UMG, and ABKCO plaintiffs filed a motion for partial summary judgment on March 23 in the original action (No. 5:24-cv-03811) covering 499 copyrighted musical compositions, asking the court to rule that Anthropic's copying constitutes infringement and to reject the fair use defense outright — a motion bolstered by an amicus brief from the RIAA and NMPA characterizing Anthropic's unlicensed copying as "inexcusable." ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/infringement-on-a-massive-scale-umg-concord-and-abkco-ask-court-to-rule-against-ai-company-anthropic-ahead-of-trial/)) This runs alongside the separate $3.1 billion action filed in January 2026 covering over 20,000 works, where plaintiffs have already shifted their intentional-contributory-infringement claim from Anthropic PBC to CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann in the wake of the Supreme Court's unanimous Cox v. Sony decision narrowing secondary liability. ([IP Watchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/01/29/music-publishers-file-new-piracy-suit-against-anthropic-alleging-mass-torrenting-copyrighted-works/)) The Cox decision's requirement that contributory infringement attach only to services "not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses" is reshaping output-side theories across the docket, including in the OpenAI MDL, where discovery battles intensify as OpenAI seeks access to the New York Times' ChatGPT prompts and expert consultant depositions while the compelled production of 20 million anonymized ChatGPT logs proceeds. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright))

On the legislative and regulatory front, the CLEAR Act (Copyright Labeling and Ethical AI Reporting Act) introduced by Senators Adam Schiff and John Curtis represents the most substantive federal legislative response to AI training transparency, requiring any entity using copyrighted works in AI training datasets to file a detailed notice with the Register of Copyrights — a disclosure regime that, if enacted, would fundamentally alter the compliance landscape for AI developers by creating a public record of training-data composition. ([Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/legislation-watch-for-ai-developers-and-registered-copyright-owners-the-federal-clear-act/)) The White House's March 20 National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence takes an explicitly pro-developer position, expressing the view that training AI models on copyrighted material does not violate copyright law while acknowledging "reasonable arguments to the contrary exist" — a policy signal that may carry weight in pending fair use determinations but lacks the force of law. Internationally, the Court of Justice of the European Union held its first hearing on March 10 in the Like Company v. Google referral, the first occasion on which the CJEU has directly considered generative AI and copyright under EU law, while in the UK the Court of Appeal has formally granted Getty Images permission to appeal the November 2025 dismissal of its secondary-infringement claim, teeing up the first UK appellate decision on whether intangible AI model weights can constitute "infringing copies" under CDPA § 27. ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/15/copyright-owners-v-ai-developers-the-emerging-trends))

## Recommended Actions

Class members who submitted claims by the March 30 deadline should verify their submission confirmations through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com and preserve all correspondence from the settlement administrator, as the final claim count and distribution computation — scheduled for June 11, 2026 — will determine individual payouts under the pro rata formula. Rights-holders who opted out of the Bartz class, including those joining the Carreyrou multi-defendant action, should ensure their separate counsel has preserved all evidence of Anthropic's downloading activity from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror, as the § 504(c) statutory damages theory in that action depends on establishing willful infringement per title. AI developers facing the Concord/UMG summary judgment motion should evaluate their own fair use positions in light of the RIAA/NMPA amicus arguments and the Cox v. Sony contributory-liability narrowing, particularly where output-side infringement theories remain viable. Companies subject to the proposed CLEAR Act should begin internal audits of training-data provenance and copyright status, as the bill's disclosure requirements — if enacted — would require detailed notices to the Copyright Office enumerating every copyrighted work in training datasets, creating potential litigation exposure for any omissions or misrepresentations.
