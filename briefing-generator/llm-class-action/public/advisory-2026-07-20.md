### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Under Submission; Late Opt-Out Dispute Extends Timeline

The May 14, 2026 fairness hearing before 
Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the Northern District of California
 did not yield a ruling from the bench. 
Judge Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission rather than ruling from the bench, and indicated that short orders may follow.
 That ruling has not issued as of this writing — confirmed by the June 2026 Clark Hill update, which noted that 
presiding Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement in the *Bartz v. Anthropic* copyright infringement lawsuit.


The most recent docket activity is a June 30, 2026 filing at Dkt. 676: 
an application by class member Donald S. Passman for leave to submit a late request for exclusion from the class action settlement, filed with supporting declarations from Donald S. Passman and Kenneth D. Freundlich, entered July 1, 2026.
 That filing is now opposed. The docket shows a responsive brief filed by named plaintiffs Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson opposing Passman's late opt-out request. 
Anthropic separately urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs from the proposed settlement, arguing that the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect" and that the remaining three late opt-out requests should also be denied for lack of any record supporting relief.


The underlying settlement terms remain fixed. 
Anthropic will pay $1.5 billion into a settlement fund. Plaintiff lawyers are seeking 25% of the fund — $375 million — in attorneys' fees, with administrative costs handled by JND and service awards of $50,000 per named plaintiff.
 Class counsel at the fairness hearing revised the fee request downward: 
authors urged the court to grant final approval along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million.
 Per-work distribution is now fixed at approximately $3,100 per work based on a 92.77% claim participation rate. 
Class counsel Justin Nelson stated the payout per work will be $3,100; the class had a 92.77% participation rate for works on the works list.
 That participation figure — nearly ten times the typical class action claim rate — was driven by organized outreach: 
440,490 of 482,460 eligible works were claimed, a 91.3% rate (the typical class action claim rate is around 10 percent).


The payment waterfall is already structured in the settlement agreement. 
Anthropic will fund the settlement in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The second installment — $300 million — was conditioned on final approval issuing. 
Per ¶ 2.1(a)(ii) of the settlement agreement, Anthropic pays $300 million within 5 business days after issuance of the Final Approval Order.
 Continued delay in the approval order directly delays cash movement.

For claims recovery counsel: the Passman late opt-out dispute is the live procedural choke point. If Judge Martínez-Olguín grants the Passman application or is perceived as open to late exclusions, it may invite further copycat filings and delay the order further. 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 An objector appeal to the Ninth Circuit remains a separate downstream risk once (if) the order issues.

**Scope caution, which matters for recovery sizing:** 
The scope of this settlement has been widely misunderstood in press coverage — *Bartz* is not really a settlement about AI training.
 
Judge Alsup certified the class only for piracy, not the act of AI training, and the fair use ruling on training applies only with respect to the three named plaintiffs, not the certified class authors and publishers.
 Rights-holders pursuing AI training claims against Anthropic — as opposed to the piracy-of-LibGen/PiLiMi claim — are not released by this settlement and retain independent causes of action. 
For many authors implicated in *Bartz*, the litigation around AI training and book piracy is not ending; it is shifting to other cases.

### II. *In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein) — Sanctions Motion Filed July 9; Discovery Misconduct Allegations Escalate


The MDL was last updated on CourtListener as of July 10, 2026, assigned to Judge Sidney H. Stein with Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang, docketed at 1:25-md-03143 in the Southern District of New York.
 
In April 2025, the U.S. Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation consolidated twelve cases against OpenAI originating in the Southern District of New York and the Northern District of California; the MDL encompasses class actions originally filed in California by authors, New York suits brought by news organizations, DMCA-focused suits, and a case brought by an online video creator.


The July 9, 2026 sanctions motion is the most consequential filing in this docket since Judge Stein's January 5, 2026 discovery order. 
A group of newspapers including the New York Times and New York Daily News asked a federal court in Manhattan to sanction OpenAI in their copyright dispute for allegedly lying to the court about its ability to search its systems for proof that it misused millions of their articles in AI training.
 The specific allegation: 
the newspapers said OpenAI deleted billions of relevant ChatGPT conversations or made them unsearchable; OpenAI previously told the court it did not have tools to search its datasets and output logs for copyrighted material, but an OpenAI employee later testified the company had "performed multiple searches for News Plaintiffs' content."


The deposition that cracked open this issue was court-ordered in April: 
in an April deposition, OpenAI data privacy engineer Vinnie Monaco allegedly revealed that OpenAI had already conducted internal searches of its training corpus; had amassed a database of approximately 78 million de-identified ChatGPT conversations used internally to determine how much it was infringing; and also implemented a "Bloom" filter as part of a set of tools called "Project Giraffe," which detected and kept a record of regurgitation in outputs, shortly after the lawsuit was filed.


The relief sought is targeted and punitive: 
the NYT and the Daily News are asking the judge to discipline OpenAI, prevent it from using the 20 million chat log sample as evidence, claiming it is unreliable; to accept as fact that ChatGPT logs would have shown major regurgitation of plaintiffs' content; to prevent OpenAI from arguing its provided chat logs don't demonstrate substantial regurgitation; and to make OpenAI pay legal fees.
 The logs in question flow directly from Judge Stein's January 5, 2026 order: 
Judge Stein affirmed a magistrate judge's order compelling OpenAI to produce the entire 20 million-log sample, not just cherry-picked conversations implicating plaintiffs' works — a significant discovery victory for the news organizations and authors suing OpenAI.


If granted, the evidentiary sanctions would functionally shift the § 107(4) market-harm analysis. A court finding that accepts, as established fact, that ChatGPT substantially reproduced plaintiffs' journalism removes one of OpenAI's core fair-use arguments. The § 504 statutory damages exposure across the consolidated MDL is material; sanctions rulings of this type typically precede settlement pressure or accelerated trial scheduling.

### III. *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC* ("Concord I"), No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee) — Cross-MSJ Hearing Set October 21, 2026; "Project Giraffe" Parallel Surfaces

The corrected case caption and judge are now confirmed from CourtListener: 
last updated June 22, 2026, assigned to Judge Eumi K. Lee, with Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen for discovery, docketed as *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal.).
 The tracked-case ground truth record (slug: `concord-anthropic`) listed this case as awaiting docket sync; that information is now superseded by the CourtListener confirmation above.

The summary judgment hearing is calendared: 
docket 5:24-cv-03811 before Judge Eumi Lee, with the summary judgment hearing set for October 21, 2026. The questions presented are whether partial summary judgment on copyright infringement should be granted to plaintiffs, or whether summary judgment on fair use and non-infringement should be granted to defendant.


The cross-motions have been fully briefed. 
Music publishers asked the California federal judge to rule that Anthropic infringed their copyrighted song lyrics through its Claude large language model, arguing in a motion for partial summary judgment that fair use does not excuse the AI developer's conduct because it used those lyrics to build a competing commercial product.
 The briefing schedule was previously tight: 
dispositive motions were to be fully briefed by June 8, 2026.


One complication already embedded in the record: a Latham & Watkins associate representing Anthropic acknowledged using Claude.ai to help draft an expert declaration that included an erroneous citation. 
The associate stated she used Anthropic's own Claude.ai tool to help draft an expert's declaration that included an erroneous citation, but argued the error was "an honest citation mistake and not a fabrication of authority."
 That episode produced a prior Magistrate Judge Van Keulen ruling: 
a California federal magistrate judge partially struck an expert report filed by Anthropic that cited a nonexistent study — an error created by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious," but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 The partial striking of expert testimony in a cross-MSJ posture is not trivial; it narrows Anthropic's evidentiary record on the dispositive motions.

A parallel second action, *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC* ("Concord II"), No. 5:26-cv-00880 (N.D. Cal., same judicial assignment), was filed in February 2026 and asserts claims over newly discovered infringement of sheet music and songbooks. 
Major music publishers filed a second, $3 billion suit against Anthropic, alleging newly discovered mass infringement of sheet music and songbooks.
 
Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending final resolution of Concord I, or in the alternative, pending resolution of the parties' motions for summary judgment and any subsequent appeal in Concord I.
 A motion to stay hearing was scheduled before Judge Lee in Courtroom 7, San Jose Division, on May 20, 2026; the outcome of that hearing is not yet confirmed in available sources.

The October 21, 2026 hearing in Concord I is the next hard date on the AI copyright calendar that is expressly about training-data fair use for music — a 17 U.S.C. § 107 determination directly applicable to the § 504 statutory damages exposure across the publisher-plaintiff group.

### IV. Structural Observation: The Shadow-Library Strategy and Cross-Case Precedent Risk

All three active matters share a structural feature worth tracking. 
Law firms like Susman Godfrey have developed what has been called "The Shadow Library Strategy," which attempts to skip the debate over whether AI training is fair use — judges so far seem to think it is — and go straight for the piracy angle. Most new book copyright lawsuits against AI companies are now class actions alleging the defendants downloaded from shadow libraries.
 The Bartz settlement validates the strategy commercially: 
Anthropic settled for $1.5 billion, covering approximately 482,000 works at an implied rate of roughly $3,113 per work.
 That per-work implied rate is now the floor benchmark for licensing and settlement negotiations in parallel matters under 17 U.S.C. § 107(4) market-harm analysis.

The Concord I cross-MSJ, however, will test whether the same theory holds for non-book works — specifically, whether reproducing song lyrics during inference (output) qualifies as fair use independently of how the training corpus was acquired. That question was expressly not resolved in Bartz. A ruling adverse to Anthropic on the output/inference prong in Concord I would materially increase the exposure calculus for every AI developer defending pending and future § 504 claims.
