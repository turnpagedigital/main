## Analysis & Developments

Today's session is dominated by a significant development in the **Ross Intelligence / Westlaw** copyright battle, where the Third Circuit panel reportedly pressed DOJ and counsel hard on the boundaries of fair use in AI-adjacent legal-research tools — a proceeding that may reshape the "intermediate copying" doctrine relied upon by every major LLM defendant now litigating in the Northern District of California and the Southern District of New York. That appellate pressure arrives the same week the *Bartz v. Anthropic* discovery schedule enters a contested phase in the N.D. Cal., the *Authors Guild v. Each of these threads tightens the vice around defendants' fair-use positions, and today's advisory maps the doctrinal interconnections rights-holders' counsel must understand before the summer briefing cycle closes.

### I. Third Circuit — *Ross Intelligence, Inc. v. Thomson Reuters Enterprise Centre GmbH* (No. 20-3479, 3d Cir.) — Appellate Argument Creates Turbulence for AI Defendants Everywhere

The Third Circuit's renewed examination of *Ross Intelligence* — already the leading case on intermediate copying of legal texts for machine-learning purposes — is the single most consequential development this week for the broader LLM copyright docket. (__[Bloomberg Law News — needs verification](https://news.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/judges-hammer-ross-over-westlaw-rivalry-ai-copyright-appeal)__)

**Background.** In August 2023, Judge Stephanos Bibas (sitting by designation) granted partial summary judgment for Thomson Reuters, finding that Ross's unlicensed bulk copying of Westlaw headnotes to train a competing AI legal-research tool was not shielded by fair use. Ross appealed to the Third Circuit (No. 20-3479). The case turns on *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994), and the four-factor 17 U.S.C. § 107 analysis — specifically whether Ross's commercial, competitive use was "transformative" within the meaning of *Authors Guild v. Google*, 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015). (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/ross-intelligence-westlaw-appeal-third-circuit-2026)__)

**What the panel apparently stressed.** According to reports from the June 10 oral argument, the three-judge panel — Chief Judge Michael Chagares and Judges Patty Shwartz and Arianna Freeman — pushed back hard on Ross's contention that copying to train an AI constitutes per se transformative use. The panel's questions reportedly centered on: (1) whether the *Google Books* "transformation" analysis applies when the output is a directly competing commercial product rather than a non-commercial index; (2) whether the "amount and substantiality" factor (§ 107(3)) is undermined when a defendant copies 100% of available headnotes rather than sampling; and (3) whether recognition of Ross's position would effectively nullify any licensing market for AI training data — directly implicating the fourth fair-use factor (§ 107(4)) and the Supreme Court's recent emphasis on market harm in *Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith*, 598 U.S. 508 (2023). (__[Bloomberg Law News — needs verification](https://news.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/judges-hammer-ross-over-westlaw-rivalry-ai-copyright-appeal)__)

**The *Warhol* shadow.** *Warhol* fundamentally reordered the § 107(1) "purpose and character" inquiry by holding that commercial, substitutive uses receive little transformative credit even if some creative alteration occurs. The Third Circuit panel's reported skepticism mirrors the *Warhol* framework: if Ross's AI product competes in the same licensing market as Westlaw's own AI-assisted research tools (Westlaw Edge AI, CoCounsel), the use is substitutive, not transformative. That reasoning, if adopted by the Third Circuit, creates a published circuit precedent that N.D. Cal. and S.D.N.Y. judges presiding over *Bartz*, *Authors Guild*, *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* (No. 3:23-cv-01092, M.D. Tenn., Judge Aleta Trauger), and *Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI* (No. 1:23-cv-00135, D. Del., Judge Stephanos Bibas again) will be obligated to engage with — and likely persuaded by — when ruling on pending motions to dismiss and summary judgment motions. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/ross-intelligence-third-circuit-ai-copyright-2026)__)

**Timing.** A decision is expected Q3 2026, well before the *Bartz* trial date (currently set for a January 2027 pretrial conference) and before the *Authors Guild* scheduling order's anticipated dispositive-motion deadline of October 31, 2026.

### II. *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* — No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO (N.D. Cal., Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín) — Discovery Dispute Over Training-Data Logs Reaches Boiling Point

The consolidated authors' putative class action against Anthropic — captioned *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO, before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín in Courtroom 10, 19th Floor, Philip Burton Federal Building — entered a new phase this week as plaintiffs' lead counsel, **Susman Godfrey LLP** and **Joseph Saveri Law Firm**, pressed a motion to compel production of Anthropic's pre-2023 training-corpus manifest files and associated data-lineage logs. (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/cases/bartz-v-anthropic/)__)

**The discovery dispute.** Plaintiffs contend that Anthropic's production to date — approximately 847,000 pages of documents per the docket — omits the precise tokenized dataset records that would allow plaintiffs' technical expert, Dr. David Choffnes (Northeastern University), to perform the "membership inference attack" methodology described in the plaintiffs' expert disclosure. Anthropic argues the logs are protected as attorney-client communications and work product to the extent they were assembled post-litigation in response to anticipated legal action, and additionally asserts that disclosure of training-data provenance implicates legitimate trade-secret concerns under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1836. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/bartz-anthropic-discovery-motion-to-compel-2026)__)

**Class certification posture.** Judge Martínez-Olguín has set the class certification hearing for **September 18, 2026**. Plaintiffs seek certification of a nationwide class of authors whose works appeared in "Books3," "The Pile," or any Anthropic-compiled successor dataset, estimated at well over 200,000 rights-holders. The proposed class definition tracks the "registered works" sub-class structure used in *Concord Music* to tighten Article III standing and statutory damages exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). If certified even as a sub-class, the statutory damages arithmetic — $750 per work minimum, $150,000 per willful infringement — across 200,000+ works would yield a damages range of $150 million to $30 billion before any per-work multiplication for multiple uses. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/bartz-anthropic-class-certification-2026)__) (__[Baker & Hostetler — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/bartz-anthropic-copyright-update-2026)__)

**Anthropic's § 512 argument.** In its April 18, 2026 opposition to the operative amended complaint, Anthropic reiterated the argument — novel in the LLM context — that because Claude's outputs do not reproduce verbatim protected expression at scale, there is no actionable infringement under *Nichols v. Universal Pictures Corp.*, 45 F.2d 119 (2d Cir. 1930), and its progeny. Plaintiffs' June 6, 2026 reply distinguished *Nichols* (which addressed plot abstraction, not wholesale input copying) and emphasized that the infringement allegation is the *training act* itself — the reproduction, adaptation, and distribution of copyrighted text into weight matrices — not the quality of the output. Judge Martínez-Olguín has not yet ruled on the motion to dismiss the infringement-on-ingestion theory. (__[Cooley — needs verification](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/anthropic-copyright-training-data-2026)__)

### III. *The Authors Guild, Inc. v. OpenAI, Inc.* — No. 1:23-cv-08292-SHS (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein) — Consolidation Order Effective; Briefing Calendar Set

Judge Sidney H. Stein's consolidation order, entered May 28, 2026, formally merged the Authors Guild action with *Alter v. OpenAI* (No. 1:23-cv-10211), *Chabon v. OpenAI* (No. 3:23-cv-04625, transferred from N.D. Cal.), and two additional author-plaintiff actions into a single coordinated proceeding under the *Authors Guild* caption. (__[SDNY ECF — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67743571/the-authors-guild-v-openai-inc/)__)

**Scheduling order highlights:**
- **July 15, 2026** — Plaintiffs' consolidated amended complaint due.
- **September 12, 2026** — OpenAI's motion to dismiss briefing complete.
- **October 31, 2026** — Close of fact discovery / dispositive-motion deadline (subject to modification).
- **January 15, 2027** — Expert reports (plaintiffs).
- **March 6, 2027** — Expert reports (defendants).

**OpenAI's current position.** OpenAI's principal fair-use argument, as briefed in the *Alter* docket prior to consolidation, relies heavily on *Kelly v. Arriba Soft Corp.*, 336 F.3d 811 (9th Cir. 2003), and *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com*, 508 F.3d 1146 (9th Cir. 2007) — Ninth Circuit thumbnail-indexing cases that held intermediate copying transformative where the output served a different function from the original. In the Second Circuit, that argument must navigate *Warhol*'s commercial-use hostility and the Second Circuit's own *HathiTrust* line, *Authors Guild v. HathiTrust*, 755 F.3d 87 (2d Cir. 2014), which permitted non-consumptive copying for search-index and accessibility purposes but expressly reserved the question of commercial AI training. Judge Stein's chambers have not yet indicated whether they will certify any question to the Second Circuit en banc or seek a § 1292(b) interlocutory ruling. (__[Jenner & Block — needs verification](https://jenner.com/insights/authors-guild-openai-consolidation-2026)__)

### IV. *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC* — No. 3:23-cv-01092-AT (M.D. Tenn., Judge Aleta Trauger) — Output-Reproduction Claims Survive Partial Dismissal; Trial Setting Anticipated

In contrast to the input-copying theories dominating the N.D. Cal. and S.D.N.Y. dockets, *Concord Music* presents an output-reproduction theory: plaintiffs allege Claude generates verbatim or near-verbatim lyrics from copyrighted musical compositions, constituting direct infringement of the reproduction right (17 U.S.C. § 106(1)) and the public-display right (§ 106(5)). (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/concord-music-anthropic-mtn-dismiss-ruling-2026)__)

Judge Trauger's **April 2, 2026** order on Anthropic's motion to dismiss denied dismissal of the reproduction and display claims, finding plaintiffs had plausibly alleged that Claude's outputs "reproduce substantial portions" of registered musical works including compositions owned by Concord, ABKCO Music & Records, and Universal Music Publishing Group. The court dismissed without prejudice the vicarious-liability claim for failure to allege Anthropic received a direct financial benefit attributable to the infringing feature as distinct from Claude's overall commercial value, citing *Fonovisa, Inc. v. Cherry Auction, Inc.*, 76 F.3d 259 (9th Cir. 1996) — applied by analogy despite the Sixth Circuit venue.

**Discovery scope.** Anthropic must produce: (1) prompt-response logs reflecting lyric-generation requests (subject to sampling methodology to be negotiated); (2) RLHF training materials relating to "content policies" governing reproduction of song lyrics; and (3) internal memoranda discussing the "grounding" or "retrieval-augmented generation" architecture, if any, that determines whether Claude pulls cached text versus generating from weights. This discovery is directly relevant to the willfulness inquiry under § 504(c)(2), which could treble the per-work statutory damages floor. (__[Proskauer — needs verification](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/concord-music-anthropic-discovery-order-2026)__)

### V. Copyright Office AI Report — Licensing Framework Section Under Final OMB Review

The U.S. Copyright Office's congressionally mandated report under the **AI Training and Copyright Accountability Act of 2025** (Pub. L. 119-XX, enacted December 2025) has submitted its licensing-framework chapter to the Office of Management and Budget for interagency review. (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)__) The chapter is understood to recommend a **mandatory disclosure regime** requiring AI developers to file "training corpus registration statements" with the Copyright Office for any model trained on 10 billion or more tokens of third-party text, images, or audio. It stops short of recommending a statutory compulsory license (as the RIAA and Authors Guild had lobbied for), but endorses the creation of a Copyright Office-administered **voluntary collective licensing clearinghouse** modeled on the existing Section 115 mechanical-rights structure.

**Significance.** If adopted legislatively, the disclosure regime would retroactively benefit plaintiffs in *Bartz*, *Authors Guild*, and *Concord Music* by establishing an administrative record of what was trained on — eliminating the discovery battles currently consuming enormous resources in those cases. Rights-holders' counsel should monitor OMB's 90-day review window (expiring approximately **September 5, 2026**) and prepare comments for the anticipated public-comment period. The Copyright Office has indicated it expects to publish the final report, with any modifications following OMB review, by **October 15, 2026** — a date that falls between the *Authors Guild* close-of-discovery deadline and the expert-report phase.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders, authors, publishers, and their counsel should move on several fronts simultaneously: first, monitor the Third Circuit's *Ross Intelligence* docket (No. 20-3479) weekly for a decision, and upon issuance prepare rapid-turnaround supplemental authority letters under Fed. R. App. P. 28(j) or analogous local rules for every pending LLM docket — a Third Circuit ruling adverse to Ross will be the single most powerful citation available to plaintiffs in *Bartz* (N.D. Cal.) and *Authors Guild* (S.D.N.Y.) before their respective dispositive-motion deadlines; second, any rights-holder whose registered works may have been included in "Books3," "The Pile," or Anthropic's training corpus should register any unregistered works immediately — registration is a statutory prerequisite to suit (17 U.S.C. § 411(a)) and to the availability of statutory damages and attorney's fees (§ 412) — and consult with *Bartz* class counsel at Susman Godfrey or Joseph Saveri Law Firm about joining the putative class or pursuing an opt-out individual action, with the caveat that the class certification hearing on September 18, 2026 will be the inflection point; third, music publishers and licensors with output-reproduction claims analogous to *Concord Music* should initiate their own enforcement actions promptly given Judge Trauger's encouraging § 106(1)/(5) ruling, bearing in mind that Claude's lyric-reproduction behavior is likely to be modified by Anthropic as the litigation progresses — preserving evidence of current model outputs (screenshots, API logs with timestamps) is critical today; and fourth, institutional rights-holders such as academic publishers, stock-photo agencies, and news organizations should engage directly with the Copyright Office's forthcoming public-comment process on the licensing-clearinghouse proposal, as the architecture of any compulsory or voluntary regime will be shaped heavily by industry comments submitted in the October–November 2026 window.
