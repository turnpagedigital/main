On the *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* track, the cross-MSJ posture in the first-filed action (Concord I, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK, Judge Eumi K. Lee, N.D. Cal. San Jose) is the live decisional pressure point for the broader lyric-reproduction theory. 
As of late March 2026, both the publishers' motion for partial summary judgment and Anthropic's cross-motion for summary judgment on fair use were fully briefed before Judge Lee

A ruling there — if it comes before fall — will be the first federal court decision directly adjudicating whether AI training on licensed lyrics is fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107, a factual record distinct from *Bartz* (which settled before the output-reproduction theory was adjudicated) and from *Kadrey v. Meta* (where the court disposed of the case on transformativeness grounds without reaching lyric output).

### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Post-Fairness-Hearing Status and Distribution Mechanics


The fairness hearing took place on May 14, 2026, before Judge Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California.
 The hearing was consequential for several reasons beyond the headline figure.

**Participation rate.** 
Class counsel reported that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% participation rate, vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in the Motion for Final Approval of Settlement filed March 19, 2026, eleven days before the March 30 claims deadline.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson confirmed to the court that the per-work payout will be approximately $3,100
 — near the originally projected $3,000 per work, since the higher-than-expected participation rate absorbed the headroom created by the earlier low-claims projection.

**Fee request.** 
Plaintiff lawyers seek 25% of the fund, or $375 million in attorneys' fees; the named plaintiffs receive $50,000 service awards each ($150,000 total for three class representatives); and settlement administration by JND Legal Administration will consume additional millions.
 
Judge Martínez-Olguín questioned class counsel Justin Nelson and Rachel Geman at the hearing specifically about the service awards, the cost reserve, and the fee multiplier
 — suggesting the court is not rubber-stamping the fee request and may trim one or more line items in the final order.

**Post-hearing briefing.** 
Judge Martínez-Olguín's post-hearing order required Anthropic to file, by May 21, 2026, a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored; Plaintiffs were simultaneously required to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026. The court declined to require or accept anything further from objectors.
 Whether those filings satisfied the court's concerns is unknown; no further docket entries post-May 21 were captured in the June 4 sync.

**What the settlement does and does not resolve.** Rights-holder counsel tracking this matter for licensing or damages-recovery purposes should note the settlement's structural limitation: 
*Bartz* is not a settlement about AI training. In June 2025, Judge William Alsup ruled that Anthropic's use of books to train its LLMs was fair use; the settlement does not disturb that ruling.
 
What the settlement resolves is Anthropic's potential liability for downloading and storing files from LibGen and Pirate Library Mirror. If Judge Martínez-Olguín grants final approval, Anthropic will be required to destroy the pirated copies and pay $1.5 billion into the settlement fund.
 The fair-use question on AI training — which has far greater forward-looking commercial significance — remains open in cases where no settlement has intervened.

**Payment schedule.** 
Per the Plan of Allocation and Distribution, per-work and per-claimant calculations were scheduled for June 11, 2026, with summer 2026 as the earliest payment window if objectors exist but no appeals are filed.
 
The second scheduled Anthropic payment of $450 million (Schedule Payment 1) falls due September 25, 2026, twelve months after the preliminary approval order; a further $450 million (Schedule Payment 2) is due September 25, 2027.
 The installment structure means class members will receive staggered distributions rather than a single lump sum — a point material to claims-investor valuation models.

**Objections and appeal risk.** 
After a 75-minute hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín declined for now to approve the settlement; seven objectors each received two minutes to present their objections.
 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved
 — meaning even a single objector pursuing a Ninth Circuit appeal could delay initial distribution by twelve to eighteen months.

**Human authorship parallel.** As a doctrinal backdrop: 
on March 2, 2026, the Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, upholding the Copyright Office's refusal to register AI-generated artwork and confirming that material must have human authorship to be copyrightable.
 That ruling does not affect the *Bartz* class's claims — all works at issue were human-authored — but it forecloses a class of counterclaims that AI defendants had floated in early discovery about the copyrightability of AI-assisted derivative outputs.

### II. *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* — Concord I (5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK) and Concord II (5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK) — Cross-MSJ Pending; Stay Motion Resolved

The Concord litigation now runs on two parallel dockets before the same judge in San Jose, both of which carry material fair-use risk for music publishers.

**Concord I — Cross-MSJ on the core lyric-reproduction claim.** 
The Concord I docket (CourtListener last updated June 10, 2026) is assigned to Judge Eumi K. Lee, referred to Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen.
 
Fact and expert discovery are complete, and the case is approaching summary judgment practice that will determine threshold legal issues governing the claims.
 
On March 30, 2026, the RIAA, NMPA, A2IM, SoundExchange, SONA, BMAC, the Music Artists Coalition, and the Artist Rights Alliance jointly filed an amicus brief urging the court to reject Anthropic's fair-use defense.
 
A second amicus brief, filed the same day by the Association of American Publishers, News/Media Alliance, International Association of Scientific, Technical & Medical Publishers, and the Authors Guild, made parallel arguments from the book, news, and academic publishing sectors.


The central § 107 issue in Concord I is bifurcated in a way that makes it doctrinally distinct from *Bartz*: 
the threshold legal questions include whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — and those questions will determine the viability of most if not all of the publishers' claims in Concord II and could have preclusive effect in the second case.
 The output-reproduction theory — Claude generating copyrighted lyrics verbatim when prompted — is the factual predicate that most distinguishes Concord I from *Bartz*; Judge Alsup's June 2025 training-data fair-use ruling never reached output reproduction because the *Bartz* plaintiffs did not allege output infringement.

**Concord II — Stay motion before Judge Lee.** 
Anthropic moved to stay Concord II (No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK) pending final resolution of Concord I — or at minimum pending resolution of the parties' cross-motions for summary judgment in Concord I — with a hearing scheduled for May 20, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in Courtroom 7, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, San Jose Division, 280 South 1st Street.
 
Concord II, filed in January 2026, covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion in statutory damages under 17 U.S.C. § 504
 — a damages exposure that dwarfs Concord I's 499-work complaint and could represent the largest music-copyright exposure in AI litigation history if statutory damages are awarded at the $150,000-per-work maximum. No ruling on the stay motion has been confirmed in available public sources; the outcome of that May 20 hearing should be treated as unverified pending a PACER pull.


Judge Lee had already denied Anthropic's second motion to dismiss in Concord I
, keeping the direct infringement and output-reproduction claims alive. That ruling, combined with the industry amicus filings, places Anthropic in the position of litigating fair use on the merits before a judge who has shown no inclination to clear the case on threshold procedural grounds.

### III. Parallel Docket Landscape — *In re OpenAI* MDL, *Kadrey v. Meta*, and the Fair-Use Divergence

**In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation (S.D.N.Y., No. 1:24-md-03143).** 
In April 2025, the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation consolidated twelve cases against OpenAI originating in the Southern District of New York and Northern District of California. The MDL encompasses class actions by authors, New York suits by news organizations, DMCA-focused suits, and a case by an online video creator — assigned to the Southern District of New York on the ground that the cases involve common questions of fact regarding defendants' use of copyrighted works to train LLMs.
 
Plaintiffs allege that defendants infringed their copyrights by downloading and reproducing works to train OpenAI's LLMs and to create infringing outputs through ChatGPT.
 No summary judgment ruling has been confirmed in available sources as of this run; the MDL remains in the discovery and briefing phase.

**Kadrey v. Meta — Piracy-seeding claims survive.** 
Two days after the *Bartz* order, on June 25, 2025, Judge Vince Chhabria of the Northern District of California granted summary judgment for Meta on the AI-training fair-use question, finding that the use of plaintiffs' books for training Meta's Llama LLM was "highly transformative."
 However, 
claims related to alleged reproduction of pirated works during "seeding" in the torrenting process remain active in the Northern District of California
 — preserving a live damages theory against Meta on the same piracy-as-acquisition theory that drove the *Bartz* settlement.

**The Alsup-Chhabria divergence.** The structural tension between the two June 2025 rulings is not merely academic. 
In *Kadrey*, the court did not follow Judge Alsup's lead in *Bartz*, and instead treated the downloaded copies as a necessary and integral part of Meta's overall effort to train its AI models — which the court ultimately concluded was fair use.
 
To the extent that *Bartz* is the pattern for future litigation, the settlement leaves AI companies in an odd position: Judge Alsup's ruling says training on legally purchased books is fair use — a green light — but the piracy-acquisition route extracted a $1.5 billion settlement from Anthropic before trial.
 Judge Chhabria's more permissive treatment of the acquisition method, if adopted by other courts, would substantially reduce the leverage that music and book publishers currently hold in cases where training data provenance is contested.

### IV. UK and International — Getty v. Stability AI (UK High Court)


On November 4, 2025, the UK High Court issued its judgment in *Getty Images (US) Inc & ors v. Stability AI Limited* [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch)
 — a ruling that practitioners have described as a loss for Getty on copyright infringement grounds, though the full appellate trajectory remains to be confirmed. The UK case matters to the U.S. docket system because its reasoning on training-data fair dealing under the UK CDPA and the scope of the TDM exception will inform how the EU AI Act's Article 4 text-and-data mining carve-out develops in practice. Any EU jurisdiction that narrows the Article 4 TDM exception will create a licensed-market infrastructure that directly feeds § 107(4) market-harm analysis in U.S. courts — the factor that most distinguished the *Bartz* piracy track from the legal-acquisition track where Anthropic won on summary judgment.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claims investors tracking the *Bartz* settlement should immediately confirm whether a final-approval order has been filed in No. 3:24-cv-05417 since the June 4, 2026 docket sync — the absence of a written order means the June 11 distribution-calculation milestone and any summer disbursement are functionally suspended; pull the PACER docket for entries 675 onward and specifically look for the final approval order and any ruling on the late-opt-out question Anthropic was directed to brief by May 21. For clients with exposure in the music-publisher track, the Concord I cross-MSJ before Judge Lee (No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK) is the next dispositive event on the AI-copyright calendar and warrants monitoring at the PACER level, not just press coverage; the outcome on the output-reproduction theory will determine whether the $3+ billion Concord II damages claim proceeds on a full or truncated theory. For clients weighing AI-training licensing terms, the Alsup-Chhabria divergence on piracy-acquisition fair use is the live pricing risk: any AI counterparty that sourced training data from shadow libraries without a settlement runs an unresolved *Bartz*-pattern liability, and the *Kadrey* ruling does not fully extinguish that exposure given the surviving seeding claims. Finally, track the *In re OpenAI* MDL (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:24-md-03143) for any summary judgment briefing schedule that may generate the next federal ruling on training-data fair use — as of available sources, the MDL remains pre-decisional on the merits, and any ruling there will carry outsized precedential weight given the consolidation scope.
