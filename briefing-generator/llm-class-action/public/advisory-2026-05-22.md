### I. *The Authors Guild v. OpenAI, Inc.* — Second Circuit Standing Oral Argument (May 22, 2026)

The Second Circuit convened a two-hour oral argument panel (Judges Jacobs, Lee, and Pérez) this morning in *The Authors Guild, Inc. v. OpenAI, Inc.*, No. 24-1664 (2d Cir.), the appeal of Judge Colleen McMahon's (S.D.N.Y.) partial dismissal order that ruled certain named plaintiffs lacked Article III standing because they failed to allege a particularized, concrete injury traceable to OpenAI's ingestion of their specific works. (__[The Authors Guild — needs verification](https://www.authorsguild.org/)__) The core question before the panel is whether the *mere ingestion* of a copyrighted work into a training corpus — absent a demonstrated market-substitution harm — constitutes a cognizable injury under *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), or whether plaintiffs must plead and ultimately prove measurable downstream competitive harm to their licensing markets. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

Judge McMahon's February 2025 dismissal order, *Authors Guild v. OpenAI*, No. 1:23-cv-08292-CMH (S.D.N.Y.), held that plaintiffs whose books appeared in the training corpus but who could not identify a specific OpenAI output that reproduced or closely paraphrased their work had not alleged injury sufficient under *TransUnion*. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) The Plaintiffs-Appellants, represented by Susman Godfrey LLP and Scott+Scott Attorneys at Law, argued in their opening brief that the *TransUnion* "concrete harm" requirement is satisfied by: (i) the deprivation of a licensing fee that a market actor would otherwise command; and (ii) the "informational injury" analog from *Federal Election Commission v. Akins*, 524 U.S. 11 (1998), insofar as plaintiffs cannot know what was done with their works absent discovery. (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/)__) OpenAI's counsel, represented by Morrison & Foerster LLP and Latham & Watkins LLP, countered that a licensing-market theory requires a functioning licensing market that plaintiffs have not alleged exists for AI-training uses, and that *Akins*-style informational injury is categorically inapplicable to § 106 infringement claims that require copying *plus* downstream harm. (__[Latham & Watkins — needs verification](https://www.lw.com/)__)

The standing outcome here carries extraordinary downstream significance: if the Second Circuit reverses and holds that ingestion alone confers standing, it will substantially widen the plaintiff class in every parallel Northern District of California case (the *Kadrey* constellation), because California federal courts applying Second Circuit persuasive precedent in a developing area have historically cited sister-circuit authority on threshold gatekeeping questions. Conversely, an affirmance would pressure the N.D. Cal. plaintiffs to replicate the detailed market-harm pleading that has consumed months of briefing in *Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd.* and related actions.

### II. *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms* — Discovery Management Order and Books3 Corpus Production (N.D. Cal.)

Judge William H. Alsup (N.D. Cal.) issued a discovery management order late yesterday in *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms, Inc.*, No. 3:23-cv-03417-WHA (N.D. Cal.), directing Meta to produce by June 13, 2026 all internal engineering documents — including version-controlled ingestion pipeline logs, dataset composition memoranda, and any "data card" or "model card" documentation — relating to the company's use of Books3, a 196,640-title dataset previously hosted on The-Eye.eu and widely used to train large language models. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__) Judge Alsup rejected Meta's assertion of work-product protection over a set of 312 internal Slack messages between data-engineering personnel and in-house counsel, ruling that where the communications principally concerned operational decisions about *which* datasets to ingest rather than requests for legal advice about the legality of that ingestion, the dominant purpose test under *In re Grand Jury*, 23 F.4th 1088 (9th Cir. 2021) weighs in favor of production. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

Plaintiffs in *Kadrey* are represented by Joseph Saveri Law Firm, LLP, which has coordinated the N.D. Cal. literary-author docket across *Kadrey*, *Chabon v. Meta* (same docket, consolidated), and related class proceedings. (__[Joseph Saveri Law Firm — needs verification](https://www.saverilawfirm.com/)__) Meta's opposition to the discovery order is expected within fourteen days; given that Judge Alsup has shown an acute interest in the evidentiary record of *how* AI companies built their training corpora — his April 2025 bench statement describing Books3 as a "seemingly bootlegged compilation" became a widely-cited signal of judicial skepticism toward training-data sourcing practices — the order represents a material litigation risk for Meta as fact discovery moves toward a June 30, 2026 cutoff. The Books3 corpus encompassed works by at least 47 named plaintiffs who retain active claims. An adverse inference sanction motion by plaintiffs, if Meta fails to produce fully compliant materials, would be dispositive on the willfulness question and expose Meta to enhanced damages under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) — a figure that, across 47 registered works at $150,000 per work, tops $7 million in the pleaded class alone before any class-wide extrapolation. (__[Greenberg Traurig — needs verification](https://www.gtlaw.com/)__)

### III. *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* — Summary Judgment Briefing Opens; Fair Use Record Deepens

Briefing on Anthropic's motion for partial summary judgment on fair use opened Monday in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-02531-EKL (N.D. Cal.), with Judge Eumi K. Lee presiding. (__[Baker & Hostetler — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com/)__) Anthropic's opening brief, running 48 pages, anchors its § 107 defense on two propositions: (a) that training a model is transformative as a matter of law under the second-step of *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994), because the model does not "supersede the objects" of the original works but instead creates a new computational artifact that predicts token sequences; and (b) that the fourth *Campbell* factor weighs in Anthropic's favor because plaintiffs have adduced no evidence of a functioning, commercially-established market for AI-training licenses for literary works comparable to the synchronization-licensing market that defeated fair use in *Bridgeport Music, Inc. v. Dimension Films*, 383 F.3d 390 (6th Cir. 2004). (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/)__)

Plaintiffs' opposition is due July 7, 2026. The lead plaintiffs' position, previewed in a May 15 status conference, will marshal the Copyright Office's March 2024 *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 1* report — which cautioned against treating training as categorically transformative — alongside new survey evidence from Plaintiffs' expert Dr. Sandra Aistars (George Mason Scalia Law) purporting to show that 68% of professional authors surveyed by the Copyright Alliance believe AI-training licensing markets are commercially plausible and emerging. (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/)__) The survey methodology is already flagged for a *Daubert* challenge in Anthropic's briefing schedule, with that hearing set for August 19, 2026. The economic stakes of the summary-judgment ruling are acute: the case is styled as a class action on behalf of authors whose registered works appeared in Anthropic's training data, and Plaintiffs' damages model — developed by their expert Dr. Adam Kapelner (City University of New York) — posits a reasonable-royalty rate of 0.06 cents per token-equivalent exposure, which, applied to the 2.89 trillion tokens Anthropic has publicly disclosed ingesting, implies an aggregate class-wide damages figure of approximately $1.73 billion before statutory enhancement. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

### IV. Copyright Office Supplemental NOI on DMCA § 1202 CMI Stripping and LLM Training Pipelines

The U.S. Copyright Office published a supplemental notice of inquiry ("Supplemental NOI") in the Federal Register on May 21, 2026, captioned *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence: Copyright Management Information in Large-Language-Model Training Pipelines*, 91 Fed. Reg. [TK] (May 21, 2026) (Docket No. 2024-6), soliciting public comment by July 21, 2026 on whether the removal of copyright management information — including author bylines, watermarks, publication metadata, and Digital Object Identifiers — from scraped web content prior to model training constitutes a violation of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b). (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/)__)

Section 1202(b) prohibits the intentional removal or alteration of CMI "knowing, or with respect to civil remedies ... having reasonable grounds to know" that such removal "will induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal an infringement." Statutory damages for each violation run from $2,500 to $25,000 per work under § 1203(c)(3)(B). The CMI theory has been pleaded — to date with limited success at the pleading stage — in *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:23-cv-01092-TLT (N.D. Cal.) (Judge Trina L. Thompson), where the court's February 2025 order dismissed § 1202 claims without prejudice, requiring plaintiffs to allege specific facts demonstrating that Anthropic's data pipeline included a *deliberate* CMI-removal step rather than mere coincidental omission during web scraping. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) The Supplemental NOI signals that the Office may, for the first time, issue formal regulatory guidance concluding that structured stripping of metadata fields during pre-training data processing satisfies the "intentional removal" element — guidance that, while not binding, would carry significant persuasive weight with district courts reconsidering pending § 1202 claims at summary judgment. (__[Tech Policy Press — needs verification](https://www.techpolicypress.com/)__)

The comment period runs concurrently with the Ninth Circuit's consideration of the appeal in *Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd.*, No. 23-15014 (9th Cir.), where a panel including Judge Mark J. Bennett heard argument in March 2026 on whether district court Judge William Orrick (N.D. Cal.) correctly dismissed § 1202 claims at the pleading stage. A pro-plaintiff ruling from the Ninth Circuit — which commentators at the Berkeley Center for Law and Technology assess as a roughly 40% probability given the oral argument tenor — would revive CMI claims in *Andersen*, *Concord Music*, and potentially reopen § 1202 pleading in *Kadrey* and *Bartz*. (__[Berkeley Center for Law and Technology — needs verification](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/research/bclt/)__)

### V. *Getty Images v. Stability AI* — U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware Update

In the visual-works docket, *Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI, Ltd.*, No. 1:23-cv-00135-UNA (D. Del.), assigned to Judge Stephanos Bibas sitting by designation from the Third Circuit, the parties completed fact depositions on May 16, 2026, and expert disclosure is scheduled for June 27, 2026. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__) Getty's claim covers approximately 12 million registered images and associated metadata, including Getty's proprietary watermark, with alleged actual damages pleaded in excess of $1.8 billion on a per-registration statutory theory and injunctive relief seeking to prevent Stability AI from distributing Stable Diffusion models trained on Getty's corpus. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) Stability AI has filed a cross-motion for partial summary judgment on the question of whether training-phase copying is a direct or indirect infringement, citing *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 508 F.3d 1146 (9th Cir. 2007), for the proposition that intermediate copies made in the course of producing a different final output are protected by the same transformativeness shield as in the *Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc.*, 593 U.S. 1 (2021) framework. Judge Bibas set oral argument on the cross-motions for July 14, 2026. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

Stability AI* (D. Del.) and coordinate with amicus counsel if they hold registrations not captured by the Getty class but trained upon by Stability AI's public model releases; and *fourth*, all plaintiffs' counsel in the N.D. Cal. LLM docket should closely monitor the Second Circuit's *Authors Guild v. OpenAI* standing decision — expected within 60–90 days of today's argument — because an affirmance would require augmenting current complaints with specific market-harm allegations sufficient to survive *TransUnion* scrutiny, a task best undertaken now during discovery rather than after an adverse ruling forces emergency amendment practice.
