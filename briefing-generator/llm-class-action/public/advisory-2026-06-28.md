### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Still Pending; Order Expected Imminently

The matter remains under submission before 
Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the Northern District of California, who held the final approval hearing on May 14, 2026
 — rescheduled from the originally noticed April 23 date — and 
took the matter under submission rather than ruling from the bench, indicating that short orders may follow.
 As of June 25, 2026, the last docket update on CourtListener confirmed two new entries since June 23 but no final approval order in the public record. (__[CourtListener Docket 69058235](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69058235/bartz-v-anthropic-pbc/)__)

The settlement fund mechanics bear repeating for claims-investment purposes. 
Anthropic will pay $1.5 billion into a settlement fund.
 
Anthropic will fund the settlement in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The first installment has already been paid. The second installment of $300 million was due April 30, 2026, within five business days of a final approval order — an assumption the settlement timeline document keyed to an April 23 issuance date. (__[IPG/Settlement Timeline PDF](https://www.independentpublishersguild.com/Common/Uploaded%20files/IPG%20document%20library/IPG%20email%20documents/Anthropic%20settlement_Timeline.pdf)__) Whether and when the second installment has actually been paid is unverified on the current record; the order has not yet appeared publicly. The third installment of $450 million comes due September 25, 2026 — twelve months after preliminary approval — and the fourth $450 million installment follows September 25, 2027. (__[IPG/Settlement Timeline PDF](https://www.independentpublishersguild.com/Common/Uploaded%20files/IPG%20document%20library/IPG%20email%20documents/Anthropic%20settlement_Timeline.pdf)__)

The post-hearing supplemental briefing window closed May 21. 
Following the hearing, the court ordered Anthropic to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing late opt-outs, and ordered plaintiffs to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the April 30 proposed order.
 Seven objectors were heard at the May 14 hearing. (__[Chat GPT Is Eating the World, May 15, 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/05/15/final-approval-of-class-settlement-hearing-in-bartz-v-anthropic-recap/)__)

On participation rates: 
class counsel filed papers advising the court that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% rate.
 
The high claim rate means forecasts of a larger payout premised on the March 19 report of 54% will not materialize; actual payout will likely remain closer to the original $3,000 per-work figure.
 Class counsel Justin Nelson confirmed at the May 14 hearing that 
the payout per work in the class will be $3,100.


For claimants and rights-holder counsel tracking the downstream timeline: 
distribution calculations were projected for June 11, 2026, with summer 2026 the earliest payment date if objectors appear but no appeals are filed.
 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 Any 9th Circuit objector appeal would materially delay distribution and should be monitored closely.

One structural point that has been understated in press coverage: 
the scope of this settlement has been widely misunderstood — *Bartz* is not really a settlement about AI training.
 
Judge William Alsup ruled on summary judgment in June 2025 that using books without permission to train AI was fair use if they were acquired legally, but denied Anthropic's request for summary judgment related to piracy — finding that the piracy was not fair use.
 The class is consequently narrower than widely reported: 
the court certified a class of all rightsholders of books Anthropic acquired from LibGen and PiLiMi, provided the books were registered with the U.S. Copyright Office in a timely manner and have ISBN or ASIN numbers.
 The § 107 fair-use question on AI training is therefore not resolved by *Bartz* and travels forward to every other docket in this system.

Fee exposure is substantial. 
Plaintiff counsel are seeking 25% of the fund — $375 million — with service awards of $50,000 per named plaintiff ($150,000 total for three class representatives) and Special Master fees for resolving disputes between class members.
 Judge Martínez-Olguín questioned class counsel Justin Nelson and Rachel Geman on the fee multiplier at the May 14 hearing. (__[Chat GPT Is Eating the World, May 15, 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/05/15/final-approval-of-class-settlement-hearing-in-bartz-v-anthropic-recap/)__)

### II. *Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC* (Concord I), No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal.) — Cross-MSJ Fully Briefed; Ruling Awaited

This is the most consequential live liability adjudication on the music-publisher track. 
CourtListener last updated the Concord I docket on June 22, 2026; the case is assigned to Judge Eumi K. Lee, with Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen referred.
 (__[CourtListener Docket 68889092](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68889092/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)__)

Dispositive motions briefing closed June 8, 2026. (__[Anthropic Motion to Stay Concord II, at Dkt. 587, Mar. 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Motion-to-Stay-Concord-II-1_59.pdf)__) 
Universal Music Publishing Group, Concord Music Group, and ABKCO asked the court to rule that Anthropic infringed their copyrights and to reject the AI company's fair use defense.
 
The motion for partial summary judgment was filed on March 23, 2026 in the Northern District of California and concerns the alleged infringement of 499 copyrighted musical works.
 
It argues that Anthropic's own admissions and evidence gathered during the case leave no material facts in dispute on core issues, and is accompanied by a 47-page statement setting out 218 "undisputed facts" supported by deposition testimony, internal Anthropic documents, and the company's own admissions.


Publishers' § 107 argument rests on all four factors. On factor one (purpose and character), 
publishers argue Anthropic's use was purely commercial — noting the company is "a for-profit technology company valued at $380 billion or more" with a revenue run rate approaching $14 billion — that it copied lyrics in full, and that its output directly competes with licensed lyrics services such as LyricFind and Musixmatch.
 On factor four (market harm), 
the filing states that when Claude outputs lyrics in response to user requests, "Anthropic provides the same service to its users as Publishers' licensees, without Publishers' authorization or any restrictions on that use," and cites data showing AI-generated songs flooding streaming platforms, with a Deezer study estimating over 60,000 AI-generated tracks being submitted daily by January 2026, accounting for 3% of total streams.
 On the underlying infringement predicate, 
the filing states Anthropic admits "at least one Claude model was trained on a dataset containing the lyrics to at least one hundred (100) of Publishers' Works" and that Anthropic "has never sought nor obtained a license from Publishers to use the Works."



Anthropic has asked the court to find that its use of copyrighted materials to train Claude is "transformative" fair use, comparing Claude's learning to how humans learn from reading and internalizing themes of various works.
 The court also has the benefit of a partially stricken Anthropic expert report: 
a California federal magistrate judge partially stricken an expert report filed by Anthropic that cited a nonexistent study — an error created by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious," but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 
A Latham & Watkins associate representing Anthropic acknowledged using Claude.ai to help draft the expert's declaration that included the erroneous citation, characterizing it as "an honest citation mistake and not a fabrication of authority."
 That procedural episode has a substantive overlay: it surfaced during discovery briefing before Judge Lee, the same judge now ruling on the cross-MSJs. (__[Law360 Case Page](https://www.law360.com/cases/66c67e7efda84001199de4bb/articles)__)

A second, parallel action — *Concord II*, No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK — was filed January 28, 2026 before the same judge. 
Major music publishers filed a second, $3 billion suit against Anthropic, asserting newly discovered mass infringement of sheet music and songbooks.
 
Anthropic moved to stay *Concord II* pending final resolution of *Concord I*, or alternatively pending resolution of the parties' motions for summary judgment and any subsequent appeal in *Concord I*.
 The stay motion hearing was set for May 20, 2026 in Courtroom 7 of the San Jose Division; its outcome is not yet reflected in the publicly available RECAP docket. (__[CourtListener Docket 72199828](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72199828/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)__) 
The threshold legal issues in *Concord I* — including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — will determine the viability of most if not all of publishers' claims in *Concord II* and could have preclusive effect.

### III. *In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein) — Discovery Continues; Docket Updated June 26


The OpenAI MDL docket was last updated June 26, 2026 and is assigned to Judge Sidney H. Stein with Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang referred, in the Southern District of New York.
 (__[CourtListener Docket 69879510](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69879510/in-re-openai-inc-copyright-infringement-litigation/)__) No new public summary of the June 26 filing has been retrieved in this run; the content of those entries is unverified beyond the docket timestamp.

The structural state of play: 
on January 5, 2026, Judge Stein affirmed a magistrate judge's order compelling OpenAI to produce the entire 20 million-log sample of anonymized ChatGPT conversations, rather than the cherry-picked conversations implicating plaintiffs' works that OpenAI sought to hand over.
 
The ruling represents a significant discovery victory for the news organizations and authors suing OpenAI.
 That production — the entirety of a 20-million-conversation sample — is now in plaintiffs' possession. The downstream use of that production in expert analysis and briefing is the most material outstanding variable for this docket heading into summer 2026.

The MDL consolidates cases originally filed in both the Northern District of California and the Southern District of New York. 
Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1407, the JPML transferred all listed actions to the Southern District of New York, assigning them to Judge Stein for coordinated or consolidated pretrial proceedings.
 
Judge Stein and Magistrate Judge Wang had already been assigned to six of the lawsuits brought by the New York Times, Daily News, Authors Guild, journalists, and authors, including suits from Sarah Silverman and Paul Tremblay.


The December 15, 2025 ruling in the related *Ziff Davis v. OpenAI* matter (No. 1:25-cv-04315-SHS-OTW) is instructive on scope management. 
Judge Stein granted OpenAI's motion to dismiss with respect to claims 4 and 5, and granted OpenAI's motion to stay as to models not already in the MDL — specifically the o1, o1 mini, o1-pro, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.5, o3, o3-mini, o4-mini, and GPT-5 models.
 That scoping decision matters because it defines the outer boundary of liability exposure in the current MDL cycle — newer, more capable models are not yet in scope. 
Magistrate Judge Wang's May 13, 2025 preservation order required OpenAI to retain all ChatGPT conversation logs, affecting over 400 million users worldwide.
 (__[Nelson Mullins client alert](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/corporate-governance-insights/all/from-copyright-case-to-ai-law-data-crisis-how-the-new-york-times-v-openai-reshapes-companies-data-governance-and-ediscovery-strategy)__)

### IV. Cross-Docket Fair-Use Architecture: What the Pending Rulings Will Actually Decide

The three active decisional postures — *Bartz* final approval, *Concord I* cross-MSJ, and the *OpenAI* MDL discovery buildout — are converging on a single question the Ninth Circuit has never answered for generative AI: does training a commercial LLM on copyrighted text, standing alone, satisfy § 107(1)'s transformativeness inquiry and § 107(4)'s market-harm bar?

Judge Alsup's June 2025 *Bartz* ruling addressed a narrower question — legally-acquired books used for AI training — and found transformative fair use for that specific posture. 
In June 2025, Judge Alsup split the question, ruling that Anthropic's use of legally acquired books for training was "quintessentially transformative" and protected as fair use, but simultaneously held that downloading and keeping pirated copies was not fair use.
 That ruling binds only the three named plaintiffs on the training question, not the class. (__[Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/)__)

*Concord I* presents the output-reproduction dimension that *Bartz* did not resolve: when Claude produces verbatim lyrics in response to user prompts — not just ingesting them for training — the § 107(1) transformativeness argument weakens substantially, and the § 107(4) market-harm argument against licensed services like LyricFind and Musixmatch sharpens. 
Five technology industry groups urged Judge Lee to find that training on copyrighted music falls under the umbrella of fair use.
 That amicus posture signals the industry's awareness that a ruling against Anthropic on the output-reproduction theory would destabilize every Claude competitor offering similar capabilities.


Law firms including Susman Godfrey have developed what has been called a "Shadow Library Strategy" — attempting to bypass the AI training fair-use debate entirely and proceed directly on piracy. Most new book copyright lawsuits against AI companies now allege defendants downloaded from shadow libraries.
 The *Bartz* settlement's 92.77% participation rate and ~$3,100 per-work recovery demonstrates that the piracy theory is viable settlement leverage even absent a merits ruling. (__[Chat GPT Is Eating the World, May 15, 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/05/15/final-approval-of-class-settlement-hearing-in-bartz-v-anthropic-recap/)__)

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and counsel with exposure to this docket system should take four immediate steps. First, verify claim status for any works in the *Bartz* class — the 91.3% participation rate was exceptional, but the 8.7% that did not file (approximately 42,000 works) are now bound by the settlement and will receive no payment; the only remaining remedy for non-filers is a late-claim petition to the administrator before the June 11 distribution calculation window closes or has closed. (__[Authors Guild Settlement Update](https://authorsguild.org/news/anthropic-settlement-update-91-percent-of-books-claimed/)__) Second, monitor the *Bartz* docket at CourtListener 69058235 for the final approval order — the $300 million second installment trigger and the distribution timeline both key off that issuance date; any 9th Circuit objector appeal filed within 30 days of final approval will push first-wave payments from summer 2026 into an indefinite appellate window. Third, treat the *Concord I* ruling from Judge Lee as a primary-source marker for the § 107 output-reproduction theory: a ruling for publishers on the lyrics-output claim under factors one and four would be the first federal precedent finding that commercially-deployed LLM output can defeat fair use on market-harm grounds, directly relevant to claims against OpenAI, Google, and every other major model provider. Fourth, in the *OpenAI* MDL, the 20-million-log production ordered January 5, 2026 by Judge Stein is likely now complete or near-complete; counsel with claims in that docket should press for expert access to those logs promptly, as their analysis will drive any theory of actual damages under 17 U.S.C. § 504(b) or statutory damages elections under § 504(c).
