### Bartz v. Anthropic — Discovery Dispute Crystallizes Over Dataset Disclosure

In *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* (No. 3:24-cv-02281-AMO, N.D. Cal.), plaintiffs — represented by Susman Godfrey LLP and Baker & Hostetler LLP — filed a renewed motion for early discovery on May 29, 2026, targeting (i) the composition of the "Constitutional AI" pre-training corpus used to build Claude 1, 2, and 3 model families; (ii) all internal memoranda discussing the use of third-party copyrighted books or periodicals; and (iii) communications with Common Crawl and Atlantic Bridge dataset intermediaries (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com)__). Judge Martínez-Olguín's June 3, 2026 scheduling order set Anthropic's opposition deadline at July 14, 2026, with a reply window closing August 4, 2026, and a hearing date of August 25, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in Courtroom 9, 19th Floor, San Francisco courthouse. The key legal dispute turns on whether Rule 26(b)(1)'s proportionality analysis requires a defendant to disclose training data at the pleading stage — an issue the court has not yet resolved after the Ninth Circuit's June 2024 partial reversal in *Andersen* remanded the copyright-infringement claim and left the discovery question open (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Anthropic's anticipated opposition argument is that plaintiffs must first plead specific works with sufficient particularity under *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991), before receiving dataset discovery — an argument that has succeeded at the 12(b)(6) stage in several co-pending matters but has not yet been tested at Rule 26 where the standard is lower. The damages theory in *Bartz* continues to be a bellwether: plaintiffs are pressing a disgorgement-of-profits theory under 17 U.S.C. § 504(b), arguing that Anthropic's entire subscription and API revenue stream — estimated at roughly $850 million in annualized 2025 revenue — is causally attributable to the corpus from which plaintiffs' works were drawn, without any offset for independently developed model weights (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). If that theory survives summary judgment, potential class damages could reach into the multi-billion-dollar range, making *Bartz* arguably the highest-stakes pending AI-copyright matter.

### Authors Guild v. OpenAI — Second Circuit Briefing Closes; Oral Argument Set for October 2026

The Second Circuit appeal in *The Authors Guild v. OpenAI, Inc.* (No. 24-2251, 2d Cir.) — consolidated with *Alter v. OpenAI, Inc.* (No. 24-2252), *Chabon v. OpenAI, Inc.* (No. 24-2253), *Grisham v. OpenAI, Inc.* (No. 24-2255), and *Silverman v. OpenAI, Inc.* (No. 24-2256) — reached full briefing as of June 2, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The consolidated appeal challenges Southern District Judge Colleen McMahon's February 2025 dismissal order (No. 1:23-cv-08292-CM, S.D.N.Y.), which held that plaintiffs failed adequately to plead that GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and GPT-4o outputs were "substantially similar" to the ingested training works and that the mere ingestion of copyrighted text — absent output reproduction — did not state a claim under 17 U.S.C. § 106(1) (the reproduction right).

On appeal, the Authors Guild argues that Judge McMahon applied an improperly demanding similarity standard at the pleading stage, relying on *Arnstein v. Porter*, 154 F.2d 464 (2d Cir. 1946) and *Tufenkian Import/Export Ventures, Inc. v. Einstein Moomjy, Inc.*, 338 F.3d 127 (2d Cir. 2003), and conflated the "extrinsic" and "intrinsic" tests into a single threshold requirement. OpenAI's response brief, filed May 9, 2026, argues in the alternative that even if ingestion alone is actionable, the *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994) transformative-use analysis under 17 U.S.C. § 107 forecloses liability because model training does not serve the same "expressive" purpose as reading a literary work — it extracts statistical patterns, not creative expression (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, the Computer & Communications Industry Association, and several academic amici have filed briefs on both sides. A panel of Circuit Judges Guido Calabresi, Richard Sullivan, and Myrna Pérez has been assigned; the October 2026 oral argument window, if confirmed, would position a decision by Q1 2027.

The Second Circuit's ruling on the "ingestion = infringement" question will be the most consequential copyright opinion for the AI industry since the Ninth Circuit's *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 508 F.3d 1146 (9th Cir. 2007) established the server-test doctrine for images. If the Second Circuit reverses on the reproduction-right theory while leaving the fair-use question for remand, the resulting circuit split with whatever the Ninth Circuit does in the parallel *Andersen* / *Stability AI* track could propel certiorari by mid-2027 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### Andersen v. Stability AI — Class Certification Briefing Accelerates; Ninth Circuit Remand's Scope Clarified

In *Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd.* (No. 3:23-cv-00201-WHO, N.D. Cal.), plaintiffs Sarah Andersen, Kelly McKernan, and Karla Ortiz, acting on behalf of a proposed class of visual artists, filed an amended class-certification motion on May 22, 2026, before District Judge William H. Orrick III (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The amended motion — filed after the Ninth Circuit's June 2024 ruling in *Andersen v. Stability AI, Ltd.*, No. 23-16262 (9th Cir. 2024), which reinstated the direct copyright-infringement claim against Stability AI but affirmed dismissal of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act § 1202(b) claim against Midjourney — proposes two subclasses: a "Training Corpus Subclass" comprising all visual artists whose registered works were included in the LAION-5B dataset (estimated at approximately 5.8 billion image-text pairs scraped from the open web), and an "Output Similarity Subclass" comprising artists whose names, styles, or signatures were used as prompts to generate commercialized AI images (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The class-certification briefing schedule before Judge Orrick runs: defendants' opposition due July 28, 2026; plaintiffs' reply due August 25, 2026; hearing tentatively September 22, 2026 at 2:00 p.m., Courtroom 2, 17th Floor, San Francisco. The damages methodology is a critical battleground — plaintiffs' expert Dr. Alan Goedde (Secretariat Advisors) has offered a "reasonable licensing fee" model estimating aggregate damages between $800 million and $2.4 billion depending on assumed licensing rates for training-data use, while Stability AI's expert contends the correct measure is zero because no licensing market for AI training existed ex ante (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The ascertainability question is also acute: whether membership in the Training Corpus Subclass can be determined by reference to LAION-5B metadata alone, without individualized inquiry into each artist's copyright registration status, remains contested.

DeviantArt, Midjourney, and Runway ML have each filed separate but coordinated dispositive motions tracking Stability AI's briefing. If class certification is granted in any form, it would be the first certified plaintiff class in the AI-training copyright litigation wave — a milestone likely to trigger immediate Rule 23(f) petitions to the Ninth Circuit.

### Getty Images v. Stability AI — D. Del. Trial Prep; Bifurcation Motion Pending

In *Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI, Ltd.* (No. 1:23-cv-00135-UNA, D. Del.), Judge Stephanos Bibas (E.D. Pa., sitting by designation) has set a February 9, 2027 trial date for Getty's claims alleging that Stability AI scraped approximately 12 million Getty watermarked images to train the Stable Diffusion model in violation of 17 U.S.C. §§ 106, 1202(b), and the Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a), as well as breach of Getty's Terms of Service (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Stability AI's pending motion to bifurcate liability and damages — filed April 30, 2026 — argues that a damages trial would be premature and confusing to a jury until liability is established, given the complexity of the disgorgement computation and the absence of a comparable market for AI training-data licenses at the scale at issue. The Clerk has noticed a June 20, 2026 deadline for Getty's response. Getty's most recent damages disclosure estimates statutory damages alone — if elected under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c) at the willful infringement rate of $150,000 per work — could aggregate to approximately $1.8 trillion for the 12 million registered images, though plaintiffs' counsel have acknowledged they would seek actual damages and profits in the alternative given the surreal scale of statutory maximum (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The more realistic disgorgement target tracks Stability AI's post-Seed round valuation of approximately $1 billion and annual revenue of roughly $60 million.

### Music Industry / UMG v. Suno and Udio — Copyright Office Proceeding Intersects with Litigation

In *UMG Recordings, Inc. v. Suno, Inc.* (No. 1:24-cv-11611-FDS, D. Mass.) and the companion *UMG Recordings, Inc. v. Uncharted Labs, Inc. d/b/a Udio* (No. 1:24-cv-05765-GHW, S.D.N.Y.) — filed by a coalition of major record labels represented by the Recording Industry Association of America — discovery remains ongoing in both actions (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV (D. Mass.) has denied Suno's motion to transfer venue to the Northern District of California, a ruling entered May 12, 2026, holding that none of the convenience factors weighed sufficiently in Suno's favor given plaintiff's choice of forum and the concentration of label-side witnesses in New York and California rather than San Francisco. In the S.D.N.Y. action before Judge Gregory H. Woods, Udio's answer filed April 18, 2026 raised an affirmative fair-use defense and denied that its training corpus contained any of the specifically identified recordings at issue. RIAA's motion to compel production of Udio's full training-data inventory was filed May 27, 2026 and is currently noticed for a June 19, 2026 telephonic conference before Magistrate Judge Sarah Netburn (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

In parallel, the U.S. Copyright Office's ongoing rulemaking proceeding, *Artificial Intelligence and Copyright*, Docket No. 2023-6, remains in its extended comment period following publication of Part 2 of the Office's study in July 2024. The Office has not yet issued Part 3, which will address the trainability / copyrightability of AI outputs — a rule expected by late 2026 that will provide the first formal federal-agency guidance on whether ingestion of copyrighted works for model training constitutes fair use under the existing § 107 framework (__[U.S. Copyright Office — needs verification](https://copyright.gov)__). Multiple district courts, including Judge Orrick in *Andersen*, have noted that the Copyright Office study may inform but does not bind the fair-use analysis; nonetheless, an adverse agency position would substantially complicate defendants' litigation posture.

### Regulatory & Legislative Overlay — EU AI Act Implementation; U.S. Congressional Activity

The European Union's AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) Article 53 transparency obligations — requiring providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk designation to maintain and disclose "sufficiently detailed summaries" of training data — entered their compliance window on August 2, 2025 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). As of June 2026, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and Meta have each submitted initial compliance packages to the European AI Office, but none of those packages has been made public, and civil-society groups including AlgorithmWatch have filed access-to-documents requests under EU Regulation 1049/2001 to obtain the summaries. Those disclosure battles may produce the first formal dataset-composition admissions that U.S. plaintiffs' counsel can cross-reference against their own discovery requests — a potential convergence of EU regulatory transparency and U.S. civil discovery that restructuring and IP counsel should monitor closely.

On the U.S. legislative front, the Generative AI Copyright Disclosure Act (H.R. 7913, 119th Cong.), introduced by Rep. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and pending before the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Courts, Intellectual Property, and the Internet, would require pre-publication disclosure to the Copyright Office of all copyrighted works used to train a generative AI system. The bill has not received a markup hearing date as of the June 6, 2026 briefing date and faces substantial industry opposition, but its mere pendency reinforces the litigation plaintiff community's argument that Congress has implicitly acknowledged a gap in existing law that requires a legislative fix — cutting against defendants' argument that training-data use is categorically fair use under existing § 107 (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders, class members, and counsel active in the AI-training copyright litigation should take the following steps during the June–August 2026 window: (1) **Register works proactively** — under *Fourth Estate Public Benefit Corp. v. Wall-Street.com, LLC*, 586 U.S. 296 (2019), a completed Copyright Office registration (not merely an application) is a jurisdictional prerequisite to suit under 17 U.S.C. § 411(a); given the volume-based damages theories in play, works registered before any act of infringement are eligible for statutory damages and attorney's fees under § 412, making pre-2021 registered works the highest-value assets in any class-recovery scenario; (2) **Preserve and document LAION-5B membership evidence** — rights-holders whose images appeared on public websites prior to 2022 should generate and preserve URL metadata, EXIF data, and copyright management information (CMI) consistent with 17 U.S.C. § 1202(c), as this information will bear directly on both the § 1202(b) CMI-removal claim and ascertainability of class membership in *Andersen*; (3) **Monitor the *Authors Guild* Second Circuit oral argument** scheduling — if argument is confirmed for October 2026, counsel should submit or join amicus briefs before the August filing window; (4) **Track the Copyright Office Part 3 rulemaking** — a favorable or adverse agency position in the Part 3 study will shift settlement leverage across the entire litigation portfolio; and (5) **Evaluate EU AI Act disclosure requests** — rights-holders with EU-registered works or EU-domiciled claimants may benefit from parallel proceedings before national AI regulators in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, where provisional enforcement guidance under Article 53 may produce faster dataset-disclosure outcomes than U.S. civil discovery.
