# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | April 19, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The Bartz v. Anthropic settlement enters its final-approach phase with twenty-five days remaining until the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín, while Anthropic's second installment of $300 million comes due on April 30 — just eleven days from now — bringing total funded commitments to roughly $600 million of the $1.5 billion settlement fund before the court rules on final approval under Rule 23(e). The Kluwer Copyright Blog this week published a comprehensive analysis of the settlement's structural mechanics, emphasizing that the approximately $3,000 per-title payout against the $150,000 per-work statutory ceiling under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c) represents a significant discount that class counsel must justify under the Rule 23(e)(2) adequacy factors — particularly in light of Judge Alsup's June 2025 ruling that training on pirated copies from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror was not fair use, which created the strongest possible leverage for the class. ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/)) Fieldfisher's parallel analysis characterizes the settlement as a potential precedent-setter for the entire AI industry, noting that other AI companies that scraped copyrighted content from the open web without authorization face analogous statutory-damages exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c), and that the Bartz settlement's per-title valuation will inevitably be cited as a benchmark in future negotiations and class-action mediations. ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/anthropic-s-1-5-billion-copyright-settlement-with)) Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's unsealed objection remains the most substantive challenge on the docket, targeting the publisher-vs-author distribution methodology, the group copyright registration gap that may exclude over two million works from adequate per-title compensation, and the class counsel fee petition of $375 million — a 25% lodestar that Bishop challenges on proportionality grounds under Rule 23(h). ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/))

The most consequential industry development this week is Disney's three-year deal with OpenAI authorizing the Sora video-generation model to produce short, user-prompted videos featuring over 200 characters from Disney, Lucasfilm, and Marvel franchises, accompanied by a $1 billion Disney investment in OpenAI — the first major entertainment studio to establish a comprehensive AI-developer licensing framework of this scale. ([AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/generative-ai/ai-lawsuits-in-2026-settlements-licensing-deals-litigation)) This deal has immediate implications for the Disney v. Midjourney consolidated litigation, where Disney and Warner Bros. allege that Midjourney's unlicensed generation of their characters constitutes infringement, because it establishes a market-rate pricing comparator that undercuts Midjourney's fair-use defense under the fourth factor — market harm — by demonstrating that a willing-licensor/willing-licensee market for AI character generation now exists and is commercially significant. Norton Rose Fulbright's comprehensive 2026 case tracker confirms that courts are expected to deliver the next wave of fair-use rulings beginning in summer 2026, with the In re OpenAI MDL, UMG v. Suno (Judge Saylor), and Concord v. Anthropic all on track for dispositive motions, while the March 9, 2026 order granting plaintiffs' motion to compel additional ChatGPT logs beyond the original 20-million-log sample signals that the discovery phase is producing the kind of granular output evidence that will test the "transformative use" framework at scale. ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026))

On the legislative front, the CLEAR Act introduced by Senators Schiff and Curtis continues to draw endorsements from across the creative industries, with the RIAA, SAG-AFTRA, Authors Guild, ASCAP, BMI, NMPA, WGA, and the Recording Academy all backing the bill's requirement that AI developers file detailed notices with the Register of Copyrights enumerating every copyrighted work in their training datasets — a retroactive disclosure mandate that, if enacted, would create a public database of training-data composition and expose any omissions or misrepresentations to civil penalties. ([Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/legislation-watch-for-ai-developers-and-registered-copyright-owners-the-federal-clear-act/)) Internationally, the CJEU's March 10 hearing in Like Company v. Google — the first occasion on which the Court of Justice has directly considered generative AI and copyright under EU law — remains under advisement, while the UK Court of Appeal's grant of permission to Getty Images to appeal the November 2025 secondary-infringement dismissal will produce the first UK appellate ruling on whether intangible AI model weights constitute "infringing copies" under CDPA § 27. ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/15/copyright-owners-v-ai-developers-the-emerging-trends)) The Supreme Court's March 2, 2026 denial of certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter, reaffirming the human-authorship requirement for copyright registration, closes one avenue of AI-authorship doctrine but leaves open the critical question of how much human contribution suffices for AI-assisted works — a question that will become increasingly urgent as AI-generated outputs proliferate across creative industries. ([Norton Rose Fulbright — Six Key Rulings](https://www.insidetechlaw.com/blog/2026/03/ai-copyright-six-key-rulings))

## Recommended Actions

Class members who submitted Bartz claims by the March 30 deadline should verify their submission confirmations through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com and preserve all correspondence from the settlement administrator, as the final claim count — which the administrator has not yet published — will be central to Judge Martínez-Olguín's Rule 23(e)(2) adequacy analysis at the May 14 hearing. Rights-holders who opted out of the class, including those joining the Carreyrou multi-defendant action, should ensure their separate counsel has preserved all evidence of Anthropic's downloading activity from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror, as the § 504(c) statutory damages theory in that action depends on establishing willful infringement per title. AI developers with character-generation capabilities should study the Disney-OpenAI deal structure as a licensing template and pricing benchmark, given that the existence of a willing-licensor market substantially weakens the fourth fair-use factor in pending character-output cases. Companies subject to the proposed CLEAR Act should begin internal audits of training-data provenance and copyright status now, as the bill's retroactive disclosure requirements — if enacted — would require notices to the Copyright Office with potential civil penalties for omissions, and early compliance preparation may reduce litigation exposure. Class members and practitioners who wish to attend the May 14 fairness hearing remotely should monitor the docket for Zoom access instructions, which will be posted per Judge Martínez-Olguín's April 8 order.
