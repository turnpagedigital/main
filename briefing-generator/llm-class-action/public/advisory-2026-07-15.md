### Bartz v. Anthropic — Docket Entry 679: Anthropic Opposes Objectors, Final Approval Still Pending

The dominant near-term event on this beat remains *Bartz, et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), before Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín. The tracked-case ground truth confirms a new filing as of July 13: 
authors urging final approval of Anthropic's $1.5 billion settlement — the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history — also pressed for an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 That July 13 filing (Docket Entry 679) is Anthropic's opposition to the objectors — the last substantive brief the court ordered. Judge Martínez-Olguín held the Rule 23(e) fairness hearing on May 14, 2026, 
did not rule from the bench, and indicated she would take the matter under submission and expected to issue short orders soon after.
 She has not yet done so as of this writing. 
As of mid-2026 the court has not yet granted final approval, but observers expect it soon; payments are contingent on that approval and the resolution of any appeals.


The settlement mechanics bear restating for claimants tracking recovery timing. 
Class Counsel reported a claims rate of 92.77% and 350 valid opt-outs covering 1,802 works; as of the March 19 motion for final approval, the Settlement Administrator had received more than 99,000 claims representing 273,331 works — 90,476 from authors, 2,379 from publishers, and the remainder from literary estates, trusts, and loan-out companies.
 
The high rate of claims means that forecasts about a bigger payout based on the March 19 report of 54% claims rate will not materialize, and the actual payout will likely end up closer to the original $3,000 per work figure.
 The $1.5 billion fund is being paid in installments: 
the $1.5 billion minimum settlement is being funded by Anthropic in multiple installments; the first $300 million has already been deposited in an interest-bearing escrow account.


On fee optics: the reduction from $300 million to $187.5 million — from 20% to 12.5% of the common fund — followed skeptical bench questioning. 
Judge Martínez-Olguín's questioning at the fairness hearing focused heavily on the fee request and on expense accounting; most of the bench's questions concerned attorneys' fees and the structure of the cost reserve, not the merits of the objections.
 The cost reserve drew particular scrutiny: 
the Judge pressed counsel on why expenses were being handled through a reserve rather than reimbursement, and signaled skepticism about money being administered outside the court's oversight.
 
The settlement drew 53 formal objections, roughly half of which came from people trying to add themselves to the class rather than challenge the deal.
 Entry 679 — Anthropic's opposition — works through those objections and the late opt-out question the court raised; Judge Martínez-Olguín's order on both will set the distribution trigger.

One structural note for claims investors: 
Judge Alsup's class certification order included in the class "all beneficial or legal copyright owners of the exclusive right to reproduce copies of any book in the versions of LibGen or PiLiMi downloaded by Anthropic." Under copyright law, when an author grants a publisher an exclusive license to publish the book in exchange for royalties, the publisher is the legal owner of publication rights and the author is the "beneficial" owner of that right.
 That bifurcation is the key due diligence variable for any secondary-market position in author-side claims: the publisher may hold the legal copyright, and a $3,000 per-work payout may require a royalty allocation dispute to resolve who captures what share.

**What's immediately watchable:** Entry 679 is the final substantive brief. Final approval order — which will also resolve the fee award and any remaining late opt-out questions — is the next docket event. No further briefing has been authorized; the court decides on submission. *((__[CourtListener Docket 69058235](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69058235/bartz-v-anthropic-pbc/)__))*

### Concord Music Group v. Anthropic — Two Active Dockets, Cross-MSJ Fully Briefed in Concord I

The Anthropic music-publisher track now runs on two parallel dockets before the same judge, and the structure matters for anyone sizing the residual exposure Anthropic carries beyond Bartz.

**Concord I — *Concord Music Group, Inc., et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal.):** 
Last updated June 22, 2026 on CourtListener; assigned to Hon. Eumi K. Lee, referred to Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen.
 This is the first-filed action, originally filed in M.D. Tenn. in October 2023 by Concord Music Group, Universal Music Corp., Songs of Universal, ABKCO Music, and related UMG publishing subsidiaries, before transfer to N.D. Cal. 
The motion for partial summary judgment, filed March 23, 2026, is the latest step in a case first brought in October 2023 over the alleged infringement of 499 copyrighted musical works.
 Cross-motions for summary judgment were fully briefed by June 8, 2026, per Anthropic's own representation in the Concord II stay papers. *((__[Anthropic Motion to Stay, Concord II](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Motion-to-Stay-Concord-Music-II-1_59.pdf)__))* Judge Lee has the motions.

The publishers' theory is direct and hard to blunt on the output side. 
The publishers filed for partial summary judgment arguing that Anthropic's use of their lyrics as training data was not fair use; "Anthropic trained Claude using Publishers' lyrics precisely so the model could respond to queries for those lyrics, including by serving up unauthorized copies and derivatives of Publishers' lyrics on demand, and Claude has repeatedly been put to that very use."
 On market harm — the fourth § 107 factor — 
the filing states that when Claude outputs lyrics in response to user requests, "Anthropic provides the same service to its users as Publishers' licensees, without Publishers' authorization or any restrictions on that use," and cites data showing AI-generated songs are increasingly flooding streaming platforms, with a Deezer study estimating over 60,000 AI-generated tracks were being submitted to the platform daily by January 2026, accounting for 3% of total streams.



The filing states that Anthropic admits "at least one Claude model was trained on a dataset containing the lyrics to at least one hundred (100) of Publishers' Works" and "has never sought nor obtained a license from Publishers to use the Works."
 On the § 107 commerciality factor, 
the publishers note that Anthropic is a "for-profit technology company valued at $380 billion or more" with a revenue run rate approaching $14 billion.


**Concord II — *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal.):** 
Assigned to Hon. Eumi K. Lee, date filed January 28, 2026.
 
This second, larger lawsuit covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion in statutory damages.
 Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending resolution of Concord I. 
Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending final resolution of Concord I, or in the alternative, pending resolution of the parties' Concord I summary judgment motions and any subsequent appeal.
 The stay hearing was set for May 20, 2026 in San Jose Courtroom 7 before Judge Lee; its outcome has not been confirmed in available public sources.

The strategic read: Concord I's cross-MSJ ruling — expected in the back half of 2026 — will set the § 107 framework for both the training-data theory and the output-reproduction theory as applied to lyric works. If Judge Lee grants the publishers' partial-summary-judgment motion on the output side (a targeted ruling that Claude directly reproduced lyrics on demand), that ruling is an out-of-circuit data point that directly pressures the Bartz objectors' argument that $3,000 per work understates residual liability. It also functions as § 107(4) market-harm evidence in the OpenAI MDL. *((__[CourtListener Docket 68889092](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68889092/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)__))*, *((__[CourtListener Docket 72199828](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72199828/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)__))*

### In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation, MDL No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.) — Richner Newspaper Coalition Joined, Case Placed on Hold

The most operationally significant MDL development this week: the Richner newspaper coalition case has been absorbed. 
A case against OpenAI Inc. and Microsoft Corp. for allegedly scraping the copyrighted works of some 400 small newspapers to train AI programs will remain on hold pending resolution of motions filed in a broader case against tech companies; the news outlets, led by Richner Communications, sued the tech giants in June, accusing them of conducting the AI scraping without authorization and compensation, and the lawsuit has since been consolidated into the MDL in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The joint stipulation and order were filed Monday, July 14, 2026. *((__[Bloomberg Law, July 14, 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/newspapers-openai-microsoft-case-on-hold-after-joining-mdl)__))* 

The underlying complaint, filed June 24, 2026, is aggressive on DMCA CMI removal — a theory the S.D.N.Y. has not yet resolved in the MDL context. 
The publishers claim OpenAI and Microsoft "systematically and secretly crawled" hundreds of news websites — including content behind paywalls — and copied articles onto their own servers without authorization; all copyright management information associated with those works was then stripped, including ownership-establishing information like author credits, publication names, terms of use information, and copyright notices, which the publishers assert was "an instrumental part of defendants' ingestion pipeline" that helped sever the link between copied content and its lawful copyright owners.
 
The publishers say they've spent billions of dollars to protect the work — including by shielding it behind paywalls — and are seeking statutory damages and injunctive relief for copyright infringement and violations of the DMCA.


The MDL itself, 
assigned to Hon. Sidney H. Stein with referral to Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang, S.D.N.Y., case No. 1:25-md-03143,
 continues to expand. 
This is now the 26th suit against OpenAI and the 11th against Microsoft in the ongoing MDL.
 The consolidated case posture as of July 10, 2026 reflects active discovery practice. 
In January 2026, U.S. District Judge Sidney Stein affirmed a magistrate judge's order compelling OpenAI to produce the entire 20 million-log sample of anonymized ChatGPT logs, not just the cherry-picked conversations implicating plaintiffs' works that OpenAI wanted to hand over.
 The privilege dispute over LibGen dataset deletion remains the MDL's sharpest discovery front: 
OpenAI waived privilege over the reasons it deleted the LibGen material.
 
OpenAI labeled these datasets "Books1" and "Books2" in public, but internally they were known by names reflecting their pirated origins: LibGen1 and LibGen2; OpenAI went on to use many different versions of LibGen materials to train GPT-3 and 3.5 models, including the model which powered ChatGPT at launch.


For rights-holder counsel tracking the MDL as a comparator to Bartz: the Richner stay means the 400-newspaper coalition will not receive independent discovery until the MDL's core § 107 and § 1202 questions are resolved. That sequencing favors OpenAI in the near term but eliminates a path to an early, isolated settlement that might set a discount-to-Bartz benchmark for news-publisher claims.

### Bartz Fair-Use Framework: What Judge Alsup's Ruling Does and Does Not Bind

This is worth restating precisely because both the Concord publishers and the Richner newspapers are now litigating in a post-Alsup world. 
In June 2025, Judge Alsup ruled on summary judgment that using books without permission to train AI was fair use if they were acquired legally, but denied summary judgment on piracy — finding that piracy was not fair use.
 
Judge Alsup certified the class only for piracy, not for the act of AI training, and as such the fair use ruling on training applies only with respect to the three named plaintiffs, not the certified class authors and publishers.


The functional consequence: Alsup's ruling on lawful-acquisition-plus-training is persuasive authority — not binding — in Concord I before Judge Lee, and is not before Judge Stein in the MDL. Judge Lee will conduct her own § 107 analysis. The publishers' Concord I partial-MSJ brief directly attacks the transformativeness finding as inapplicable to lyrics, where verbatim output reproduction is the plaintiffs' lead theory. If Lee diverges from Alsup, the circuit will eventually face competing N.D. Cal. rulings on identical statutory text. That conflict would almost certainly generate a Ninth Circuit appeal, extending the Concord I timeline well beyond any 2026 trial date. Claims investors should model that tail scenario. *((__[Norton Rose Fulbright, Bartz Case Summary](https://www.insidetechlaw.com/blog/2025/09/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-reached-after-landmark-summary-judgment-and-class-certification)__))*, *((__[Copyright Alliance, Alsup Fair Use Order, June 23, 2025](https://copyrightalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Bartz-v.-Anthropic-Order.pdf)__))*

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claims-position investors should prioritize three immediate tasks. First, on *Bartz*: docket-watch for Judge Martínez-Olguín's final approval order — the controlling document for distribution timing — and pay particular attention to how she resolves the fee award (the revised $187.5 million ask vs. the court's evident skepticism about the cost reserve) and the late opt-out question addressed in Docket Entry 679; any fee reduction directly enlarges the net fund available for per-work distribution. Second, on *Concord I* (No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL): review Judge Lee's ruling on cross-MSJs when it issues — it will be the first substantive federal ruling on whether training-plus-verbatim-output loses fair use protection for lyric works, and if plaintiffs prevail on the output theory, that ruling materially strengthens the § 107(4) market-harm argument in every parallel AI-copyright matter including the OpenAI MDL. Third, on the MDL (No. 1:25-md-03143, S.D.N.Y.): track Magistrate Wang's follow-on orders on the LibGen privilege waiver; document-production from that corpus — Anthropic internally called its datasets LibGen1 and LibGen2 while publicly labeling them Books1 and Books2 — could surface evidence directly usable in the Concord I fair-use trial and in any eventual trial in the MDL author track. Any client holding a secondary-market position in Bartz author-side claims should confirm the author/publisher split in the specific works at issue before the final approval order issues, given the legal-vs.-beneficial-owner allocation risk that the settlement does not fully resolve on its face.
