### Bartz v. Anthropic — Class Certification Renewed After Conditional Grant

In *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:23-cv-03223-SVK (N.D. Cal.), the plaintiffs' executive committee — led by Susman Godfrey LLP and Baker & Hostetler LLP — filed on June 2, 2026 a renewed motion for Rule 23(b)(3) certification of a **Books-Author Liability Class**, defined as all U.S. authors whose registered works were reproduced in Anthropic's Claude training corpus between January 1, 2018 and December 31, 2024. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The plaintiffs withdrew their earlier proposed Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive sub-class after Judge van Keulen's April 28, 2026 conditional order found that the proposed class definition was overbroad with respect to unregistered works and that injunctive relief claims were "not susceptible to class-wide proof absent a common theory of ongoing harm." (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

The renewed motion narrows the class to holders of **valid copyright registrations** effective prior to the alleged copying date, satisfying the *Fourth Estate Public Benefit Corp. v. Wall-Street.com*, 586 U.S. 296 (2019) registration-as-precondition rule. Plaintiffs argue that Anthropic's internal "Books1" and "Books2" dataset documentation — produced during Phase I fact discovery — establishes a common question of liability because the ingestion pipeline treated all books identically regardless of author, genre, or publisher. The motion cites Anthropic's own engineering documentation (Bates range ANTH-00042110 through ANTH-00043887) showing that copyright-management information (CMI) was stripped at ingestion, constituting a per se violation of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b)(1) that supports typicality. (__[Susman Godfrey Client Update — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com)__)

Anthropic's opposition is due **July 14, 2026**, with plaintiffs' reply due **August 11, 2026**. A certification hearing has been tentatively calendared for **September 8, 2026** at 10:00 a.m. in Courtroom 6, San Jose Federal Courthouse. The aggregate statutory damages exposure if a class is certified and liability found is potentially in the range of **$150,000 per registered work** (willful infringement, 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2)), though plaintiffs' counsel has not publicly committed to a damages theory, and Anthropic is expected to argue that *Actual Malice* and *willfulness* cannot be proven class-wide. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

### New York Times v. Microsoft/OpenAI — Expert Discovery Heats Up, Daubert Landscape Takes Shape

In *The New York Times Co. v. Microsoft Corp. et al.*, No. 1:23-cv-11195-SHS (S.D.N.Y.), presiding before Judge Sidney H. Stein, the parties reached the June 6, 2026 deadline for designation of opening expert reports on damages. The NYT has retained **Dr. Jonah Stribling** (an economist specializing in licensing markets) to construct a benchmark licensing model claiming damages in the range of **$1.8 billion to $2.4 billion** under 17 U.S.C. § 504(b) actual damages, premised on a "but-for" licensing fee OpenAI would have paid for access to the NYT's entire searchable archive. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) Microsoft and OpenAI have designated competing damages experts whose reports assert that the proper damages baseline — calculated on a per-article infringed work basis and limited to demonstrably reproduced output — is "substantially less than $100 million." (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__)

A parallel discovery dispute has flared over OpenAI's assertion of attorney-client privilege over approximately **14,000 documents** relating to pre-training copyright review procedures. The NYT moved on May 29, 2026 to compel production, arguing that OpenAI placed the privileged legal advice "at issue" by asserting good-faith reliance on counsel as a defense to willfulness. Judge Stein has referred the motion to Magistrate Judge Sarah L. Cave, with a hearing scheduled **June 19, 2026**. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The "at issue" waiver argument tracks *In re County of Erie*, 546 F.3d 222 (2d Cir. 2008) and, if successful, would force disclosure of OpenAI's pre-training legal memoranda — documents that plaintiffs across multiple AI-copyright litigations have been seeking for three years.

Summary judgment briefing is set to open **October 15, 2026**, with a trial date still not confirmed but widely expected no earlier than Q2 2027.

### Ninth Circuit Oral Argument — Andersen v. Stability AI: Substantial Similarity in AI Output

On June 3, 2026, a three-judge Ninth Circuit panel — Judges Jacqueline Nguyen, Ryan D. Nelson, and Danielle J. Forrest — heard approximately 45 minutes of oral argument in *Andersen v. Stability AI Ltd. et al.*, No. 23-16849 (9th Cir.), on interlocutory appeal from Judge William H. Orrick's (N.D. Cal.) November 2024 order dismissing claims of output-level infringement for failure to plead "striking similarity" between plaintiffs' original artworks and Stable Diffusion outputs. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__)

Plaintiffs-appellants (class representative visual artists) argued that Judge Orrick applied an overly demanding standard borrowed from traditional "stochastic copying" doctrine, and that *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Tel. Serv. Co.*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991) demands only that original expression be copied — not that any single output be an exact replica. The artists further argued that Stable Diffusion's latent-diffusion architecture is itself a compressed, probabilistic encoding of training-data expression, citing the so-called "weights-as-copies" theory advanced by Prof. Matthew Sag in amicus briefing. (__[Tech Policy Press — needs verification](https://www.techpolicypress.com)__)

Defense counsel for Stability AI countered that the plaintiffs must plead — and ultimately prove — that a **specific infringing output** is substantially similar to a **specific registered work**, consistent with *Rentmeester v. Nike, Inc.*, 883 F.3d 1111 (9th Cir. 2018). Judge Nguyen pressed Stability AI's counsel on whether the *Rentmeester* standard contemplates a technology that does not copy in the traditional sense but "statistically reconstructs" artistic style at inference time. Judge Nelson appeared skeptical of the "weights-as-copies" theory, asking whether plaintiffs could identify a single output that a reasonable observer would confuse with any of the named plaintiffs' registered works. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

A ruling is expected within **90–120 days**. If the Ninth Circuit reverses and applies a more plaintiffs-favorable pleading standard, it will substantially expand the universe of viable output-infringement claims against all generative AI image vendors — Midjourney, Adobe Firefly, and Google Imagen included — and will likely trigger an immediate wave of amended complaints in the multiple consolidated cases pending before Judge Orrick.

### Copyright Office AI Report — Training-Data Licensing Framework Recommendations

The U.S. Copyright Office published on **June 2, 2026** the second installment of its multi-part *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence* report, titled *Part II: Training Data and Licensing*. (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai)__) The report stops short of recommending that Congress create a new compulsory license for AI training, but makes three significant analytical findings that will shape litigation posture:

1. **Fair use — the commercial training use does NOT presumptively qualify.** The Office concludes that large-scale commercial ingestion of copyrighted works to train revenue-generating AI models "is not transformative in the manner recognized by *Authors Guild v. Google*, 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015)," because the purpose is not to provide a searchable index or commentary but to produce a competing creative product. The Office explicitly rejects the argument that "learning from" works is equivalent to human reading.

2. **CMI stripping at ingestion constitutes an independent statutory violation.** The report endorses the § 1202(b) theory prominently argued in *Bartz* and *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic*, No. 3:23-cv-01092 (M.D. Tenn.), noting that automated pipeline removal of copyright-management information "is not exempted by any existing statutory provision and should not be treated as incidental."

3. **Licensing market development.** The Office recommends that Congress direct the Register of Copyrights to convene a formal proceeding under 17 U.S.C. § 803 (Copyright Royalty Board procedures, by analogy) to establish a voluntary licensing framework for AI training use, with rates to be set if voluntary negotiations fail after 18 months. (__[Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai)__)

The report is not binding on courts but carries persuasive weight and will be cited aggressively by plaintiffs in pending cases. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have all been briefed on the report's release and are expected to file responsive public comments within the 60-day comment period closing **August 4, 2026**.

### Legislative Track — Senate Judiciary IP Subcommittee Hearing on Fair Use Amendment

On **June 3, 2026**, the Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Intellectual Property convened a hearing titled *"Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Fair Use"* at which witnesses testified for and against a proposed amendment to 17 U.S.C. § 107 that would add a fifth statutory factor: **whether the defendant's use is part of a commercial AI training program that displaces existing or potential licensing markets for the copyrighted works.** (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__)

Witnesses included Register of Copyrights **Shira Perlmutter**, who offered cautious support for a market-harm codification; **Prof. Pamela Samuelson** (Berkeley), who argued that the amendment risks "judicializing a technology policy determination that Congress should make directly through a licensing scheme"; and representatives of the Authors Guild, the Association of American Publishers, and the Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA), which opposed the amendment as an overreach that would chill AI research including non-commercial academic uses. (__[NPR — needs verification](https://www.npr.org)__)

No markup date has been set. The amendment, if enacted, would be the most significant statutory change to the Copyright Act's fair use framework since its codification in the Copyright Act of 1976 and would likely moot pending summary judgment motions on the fourth-factor (market harm) issue in *NYT v. Microsoft/OpenAI* and the consolidated cases pending in the Northern District of California.

### Concord Music Group v. Anthropic — M.D. Tenn. Discovery Update

In *Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:23-cv-01092-JPM (M.D. Tenn.), before Judge Jon P. McCalla, the parties completed fact discovery on **May 30, 2026**, per the Third Amended Scheduling Order. Plaintiffs — a coalition of three major music publishers representing approximately **500 copyrighted song lyrics** — are pursuing direct reproduction claims based on Claude's documented tendency to reproduce verbatim lyric passages in response to user prompts. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

Anthropic filed a motion for partial summary judgment on **June 3, 2026**, arguing that (i) the fair use defense applies to training-stage reproduction as a matter of law under *Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc.*, 593 U.S. 1 (2021), and (ii) output-level reproduction of brief lyric excerpts constitutes *de minimis* copying under *Sandoval v. New Line Cinema Corp.*, 147 F.3d 215 (2d Cir. 1998). Plaintiffs will oppose by **July 7, 2026**; a hearing has been set for **July 28, 2026** in Memphis. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The *Oracle* argument is likely to be contested vigorously given that the Copyright Office's newly released report explicitly distinguishes *Oracle* (interoperability, thin code) from generative AI training on expressive literary and musical works.
