## Analysis & Developments

The last 24 hours produced no new filings in either *Bartz* or *Concord*, but the distribution clock in *Bartz* is now running — and in *Concord*, the music-publisher front continues to expand, with the July 22 Second Amended Complaint marking the most substantive pleading update since summary-judgment briefing opened.

### Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Distribution Limbo Begins


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment.
 The operative order — a 23-page ruling — is now the governing instrument, and the case has shifted from a litigation posture to a settlement-administration posture. No further substantive docket activity has been recorded since Entry 681 (July 21, 2026), consistent with the tracked-case ground truth. 
Activity since the May 14 fairness hearing has been minimal, with outstanding matters including objections, late opt-out requests, and attorneys' fees still to be resolved.


**Fee Award Structure.** 
Class counsel, who revised their fee request down from $300 million to $187.5 million ahead of final approval, argued the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 
The court approved reimbursement of expenses totaling $2,635,197.46 and a cost reserve of $18,220,000 in anticipated expenses, and reduced service awards from the requested $50,000 to $15,000 for each of the three Settlement Class Representatives (totaling $45,000). The court also withheld 10 percent of Class Counsel's fee award pending a post-distribution accounting per this District's Procedural Guidance for Class Action Settlements.


**Objections and Opt-Outs.** 
Judge Martínez-Olguín overruled all 53 objections filed with the court on various grounds, finding the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate" and "negotiated at arm's length."
 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately .03% of the total number of covered works.
 
The court disallowed opt-outs submitted after the February 9, 2026 deadline, with limited exceptions.


**Release Scope — What It Does and Does Not Cover.** This matters to any rights-holder counsel evaluating residual exposure. 
Under the settlement, class members released all claims broadly related to past AI *inputs* — including Anthropic's pirating and copying of works through the LibGen and PiLiMi datasets — but expressly do *not* release claims about past AI *outputs*, nor claims of any kind about future conduct on or after August 25, 2025.
 That output/future-conduct carve-out is the primary hook for any follow-on litigation by class members who stayed in.

**Distribution Timeline.** 
The Authors Guild notes it looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process, though a specific date for payouts has not been announced.
 
Commentary tracking the docket suggests distributions are unlikely before the end of summer, and potentially much later.
 The 10% fee holdback and the post-distribution accounting requirement are the most likely procedural checkpoints before final disbursement closes. Watch for the Special Master process: 
disputes that cannot be resolved by mutual agreement with the settlement administrator are to be submitted to the Special Master, with all submissions remaining confidential and under seal, and Special Master decisions deemed final.


**Structural Read-Across.** The Bartz settlement does not create binding precedent on the fair-use question — 
Anthropic denied any wrongdoing, and the infringement question was never adjudicated, meaning this settlement does not bind future courts that may find such training use non-infringing.
 What it does generate is a data point on statutory damages exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504 that every defendant in the parallel AI-copyright docket will need to address in settlement valuations. 
Even if AI training ultimately proves legal, defending a class action can cost millions just to reach trial, and with statutory damages in play, the downside risk is catastrophic.

### Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC, No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee) — Second Amended Complaint Filed July 22

The most consequential pleading event of the past week is the music publishers' Second Amended Complaint, filed ten days ago and not yet addressed by the court.


Concord Music Group, Universal Music Publishing Group, and ABKCO Music filed a Second Amended Complaint against Anthropic on July 22, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California — an expanded version of the First Amended Complaint they had put forward in May.
 
The filing was made pursuant to ECF No. 725 by Concord Music Group, Universal Music Corp., Songs of Universal Inc., ABKCO Music Inc., Polygram Publishing Inc., Universal Music – Z Tunes LLC, Capitol CMG Inc., and Universal Music – MGB NA LLC.


**What's New in the SAC.** 
The latest filing builds on earlier claims and incorporates information obtained through the discovery process, with the publishers alleging that Claude was trained on copyrighted lyrics and designed to provide those lyrics in response to user requests. According to the amended complaint, internal records show Anthropic employees and contractors prompted the system with requests for copyrighted lyrics during the model's development.
 
The publishers further allege that Anthropic used tools that removed copyright notices and other identifying information from lyrics during training, and that the company's systems were intentionally built to reproduce lyrics — framing the capability as "a feature, not a bug."
 The copyright management information removal theory is a 17 U.S.C. § 1202 claim in addition to the direct infringement claims under § 501.

**Narrowed Claims.** 
The publishers have narrowed the case to direct infringement and alleged removal of copyright management information, dropping earlier contributory and vicarious infringement claims.
 That narrowing sharpens the § 107 fair-use analysis: a direct-infringement posture puts the publishers squarely on the four-factor test, with particular emphasis on factor four (market harm) given Claude's commercial use.

**Scope: Case I vs. Case II.** 
The amended complaint covers roughly 500 songs; a separate lawsuit filed earlier this year involves more than 20,000 songs and seeks damages exceeding $3 billion.
 That second action — 
filed January 28, 2026, and explicitly "distinct and separate" from Concord I — names Anthropic and two individuals, Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann, and centers on the alleged downloading of millions of books via BitTorrent from LibGen and PiLiMi, including songbooks and sheet-music/lyrics books covering at least 714 publisher-controlled musical works.
 
In that second action (No. 5:26-cv-00880), Judge Lee's April 24 order set Anthropic's motion to dismiss or answer deadline as August 3, 2026, with responses due August 31 and replies due September 14.
 That means Anthropic's response to the *Concord II* complaint is due **tomorrow**, August 3 — the next immediate milestone on this desk.

**BMG Parallel.** 
BMG filed a third suit in March 2026, citing 493 compositions and alleging Anthropic had built its business on unlicensed works.
 The music-publisher front is now three separate actions at different procedural stages, all before Judge Lee.

**Partial Summary Judgment.** 
The publishers moved for partial summary judgment, asking the court to rule that Anthropic infringed their copyrighted song lyrics through Claude, arguing fair use does not excuse the conduct because Anthropic used the lyrics to build a competing commercial product.
 
Anthropic, for its part, has argued that its use of copyrighted materials to train Claude is "transformative" fair use, comparing Claude's learning to how humans learn by reading and internalizing works.
 The partial summary judgment motion is pending — no ruling has issued. That ruling, when it comes, will be the most consequential fair-use decision in the music-AI space, and it will frame the § 107(1) transformativeness analysis for every downstream AI-training copyright case.
