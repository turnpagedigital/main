### Bartz v. Anthropic PBC — Post-Judgment: Distribution Phase Opens

*Bartz et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417-AMO (N.D. Cal.), Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín — 
final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement was entered on July 20, 2026, concluding what is the largest copyright class action settlement in U.S. history.



Document 680, signed by Judge Martínez-Olguín on July 20, 2026, is captioned "Order Granting Final Approval of Class Action Settlement; Granting in Part Motion for Attorney's Fees, Reimbursement of Expenses, and Plaintiff Service Awards; Judgment."


**The delta from today's perspective**: The docket shows a single filing after the judgment — Dkt. 681, a Report entered July 21, 2026. No further filings have appeared as of this morning. The case has crossed from litigation into claims administration. That transition is where rights-holder counsel should focus.

**Fee award.** 
Class counsel had initially sought $300 million in fees, then trimmed the request to $187.5 million. The court approved approximately $101.5 million.
 
The court applied a lodestar multiplier of 3.75 on class counsel's estimated lodestar of $27,082,963, finding that figure reasonable under Ninth Circuit precedent.
 
The court further withheld 10% of even that amount until class counsel files a post-distribution accounting.
 The effective fee rate is approximately 6.8% of the fund — less than half the 12.5% counsel requested and well below the 25% cap the settlement agreement permitted.

**Claims rate and per-work recovery.** 
Claims were filed for at least 91.3% of eligible works — 440,490 works claimed out of 482,460 eligible works — a figure the court characterized as "overwhelmingly favorable."
 
The reduced fee award means the per-work payout is likely to exceed $3,100, above the originally estimated ~$3,000.
 
The court confirmed that the estimated per-work payment of approximately $3,000 is four times the statutory minimum for willful copyright infringement.
 For context on § 107(4) market-harm framing: 
the court noted the per-work amount is an order of magnitude greater than the maximum proposed for books in the *Google Books* settlement, which was rejected in 2011 in part because it released future claims — unlike the Anthropic settlement, which preserves those claims.


**Objections and opt-outs.** 
Judge Martínez-Olguín overruled all 53 objections filed opposing the settlement.
 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate and 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately 0.03% of the total works on the list.
 
The court declined to entertain objections from non-class members but reminded those individuals that, because the settlement does not cover them, they remain free to bring their own claims.


**Non-monetary relief.** 
The settlement requires Anthropic to destroy all original files of works torrented or downloaded from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror, along with any copies originating from them, subject to legal preservation obligations — relief the court expressly recognized as a benefit to class members. Anthropic has also represented that neither dataset, nor any portion thereof, was in the training corpus of any of its commercially released large language models.


**Service awards.** 
The court trimmed the requested service awards for the three named plaintiffs — Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson — from $50,000 to $15,000 each.


**What's next.** No distribution commencement date has been publicly announced. 
The Authors Guild has indicated it looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process but notes that a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not been announced.
 The standard objector window for a Ninth Circuit appeal runs 30 days from entry of judgment — that window closes August 19, 2026. Given the 350 opt-outs and the court's on-the-record reminder that non-class members retain independent claims, watch for new filings by opted-out rights holders and by publisher entities asserting contract-allocation disputes over the author/publisher split embedded in the plan of distribution. Any dispute "that cannot be resolved by mutual agreement with the settlement administrator" goes to the Special Master under seal. 
The court reaffirmed that all Special Master decisions are final.

### Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC — Amended Complaint Filed; Anthropic Supplements Summary Judgment Record; CMC Set September 2

*Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL (N.D. Cal., San Jose), Hon. Eumi K. Lee, referred to Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen.

This case is the music-publisher parallel to the author class actions, covering lyrics reproduction and training-data infringement. Two filings in the last ten days advance the record materially.

**Amended Complaint — July 22 (Dkt. filed 7/22/2026).** 
Concord Music Group, Universal Music Corp., Songs of Universal, ABKCO Music, Polygram Publishing, Universal Music – Z Tunes, Capitol CMG, and Universal Music – MGB NA filed an Amended Complaint pursuant to ECF 725 on July 22, 2026.
 The amended pleading attaches two new exhibits plus a redline of the First Amended Complaint — a filing posture consistent with either newly discovered infringement or a court-authorized expansion of the works list. Law360 has separately reported that 
the publishers filed a second, $3 billion suit against Anthropic, citing newly discovered mass infringement of sheet music and songbooks.
 Whether that second action has been consolidated into the amended pleading or filed as a separate docket is not yet confirmed from the CourtListener entries reviewed this morning.

**Anthropic's Supplemental Summary Judgment Filing — July 21 (Dkt. filed 7/21/2026).** 
Anthropic filed a Notice of Supplement to its Response to Fact No. 149 of Plaintiffs' Moving Separate Statement in Support of Motion for Partial Summary Judgment on July 21, 2026.
 A supplement this narrow — keyed to a single numbered fact in the separate statement — typically signals that a specific evidentiary gap was flagged at oral argument or in a court order, and that Anthropic is shoring up its factual record on that point before the court rules. The subject of Fact No. 149 is not visible from the public docket entry but falls within the broader cross-motions on whether Anthropic's use of copyrighted song lyrics to train Claude constitutes fair use. 
The publishers have argued in their partial summary judgment motion that fair use does not excuse Anthropic's conduct because it used the lyrics to build a competing commercial product.


**Upcoming schedule.** 
A Case Management Conference has been set for September 2, 2026 at 3:00 PM before Judge Eumi K. Lee via Zoom.
 
The summary judgment hearing in this matter is set for October 21, 2026 before Judge Lee, with the opinion date TBA.
 The cross-motions present the court with the threshold question of whether partial summary judgment on copyright infringement should be granted to the publishers, or whether Anthropic's fair-use and non-infringement arguments defeat the motion.

**Claude hallucination in Anthropic's expert record.** As noted in earlier coverage: 
a California federal magistrate judge partially struck an expert report filed by Anthropic that cited a nonexistent study — an error created by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious" but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 
The publishers have continued to press the issue, accusing Anthropic of downplaying the seriousness of errors in the filing and arguing that Anthropic's counsel violated the judge's standing order.
 The supplemental filing on Fact No. 149 lands in this context; the court will weigh Anthropic's evidentiary credibility as it evaluates the competing summary judgment papers.

**July 29 filing.** The tracked-case docket reflects one additional filing on July 29, 2026 — described as an "Order on" (entry truncated in the live sync). The document text is not yet indexed in publicly available sources reviewed this morning.
