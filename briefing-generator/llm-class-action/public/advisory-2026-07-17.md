## Analysis & Developments

### I. Bartz v. Anthropic (3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Still Pending; Late-Opt-Out Dispute Reaches the Docket

The controlling delta this week is Docket Entry 679, filed July 13, 2026 in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal., 
Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín
): Anthropic's opposition to honoring late opt-outs — the last remaining post-hearing submission the court expressly requested before issuing a final approval order. That brief closes out the briefing calendar Judge Martínez-Olguín set in her order of May 14, 2026, 
which directed Anthropic to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored, and directed plaintiffs to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026
 (Dkt. 646-1). With both submissions now on file, the record is closed; the court may issue its final approval order at any time.

The settlement's terms are not in dispute. 
*Bartz v. Anthropic* settled for $1.5 billion — the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history.
 
Anthropic will fund the settlement fund in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The participation metrics are equally settled. 
Class counsel filed papers apprising the court that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3 percent rate.
 
Class Counsel Justin Nelson reported at the fairness hearing that the payout per work in the class will be approximately $3,100.
 That figure reflects a normalization from earlier projections: 
the high rate of claims means that the forecasts about a bigger payout based on the March 19 report of 54 percent will not happen, and the actual payout will likely end up being closer to the original $3,000 per work figure.


On fees: 
class counsel urged the court to grant final approval along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 The May 14 hearing itself was low-drama. 
After a 75-minute hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement — but given the tone of the hearing, most observers expect the judge will give final approval relatively quickly.
 
Her questions focused on attorneys' fees and the structure of the cost reserve, rather than the merits.


The late opt-out issue is the final substantive obstacle. 
The deadline to exclude oneself from the settlement passed on February 9, 2026.
 Any late opt-out request would require a showing of excusable neglect or extraordinary circumstances; Anthropic's Dkt. 679 presumably argues neither standard is met. The practical stakes are material: class members who successfully opt out after the deadline would preserve independent litigation rights, potentially fragmenting the settlement class and complicating distribution. 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 Rights-holders who filed claims should not expect disbursements before the Ninth Circuit's objector-appeal window closes — a contingency that, if triggered, could push actual payments well past the originally projected late-2026 window.

The statutory substrate bears repeating for claim-investment purposes. Judge Alsup's June 2025 ruling — on the record that preceded settlement — held that 
using books without permission to train AI was fair use if they were acquired legally, but he denied Anthropic's request for summary judgment related to piracy, finding that the piracy was not fair use.
 The class is therefore defined by the piracy channel: 
a class of all rightsholders of books Anthropic acquired from LibGen and PiLiMi, provided the books were registered with the U.S. Copyright Office.
 That registration prerequisite (17 U.S.C. § 411) is the standing gate for every individual claim; it is also the reason the claims-to-eligible-works ratio could approach 92.77% — the settlement class was deliberately narrow.

### II. In re OpenAI Copyright MDL (1:25-md-03143-SHS-OTW, S.D.N.Y.) — Richner/Newspaper Action Stayed; MDL Pipeline Expanding

The week's second concrete development: 
a case against OpenAI Inc. and Microsoft Corp. for allegedly scraping the copyrighted works of some 400 small newspapers to train AI programs will remain on hold pending resolution of motions filed in a broader case against tech companies, per Bloomberg Law, July 14, 2026.
 
The news outlets, led by Richner Communications, sued the tech giants in June, accusing them of conducting AI scraping without authorization and compensation; the lawsuit has since been consolidated into multidistrict litigation involving numerous similar cases, according to a joint stipulation and order filed Monday in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York.



The MDL is assigned to Hon. Sidney H. Stein, with discovery matters referred to Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang, captioned *In Re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.).
 The Richner action is now the 26th suit against OpenAI folded into or stayed in favor of the MDL. 
This lawsuit is the 26th suit against OpenAI and the 11th against Microsoft in the ongoing MDL Litigation.


The underlying complaint's theory tracks the broader MDL playbook. 
The complaint alleges the defendants "systematically and secretly crawled" publisher websites, copied articles without permission or compensation, and used that content to train ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot; publishers say they spent billions protecting their content through paywalls, only to have those investments rendered worthless by AI systems that reproduced articles in response to user queries.
 Counsel is Platkin LLP. 
The lawsuit alleges the companies stole the newspapers' copyrighted news articles and used that content to build and train commercial AI products; Platkin LLP was founded by former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin and a team of litigators from the attorney general's office.


The stay posture is standard MDL mechanics — the Richner action will be governed by whatever fair-use framework Judge Stein establishes on summary judgment in the consolidated proceeding. The strategic significance is in the § 107(4) market-harm analysis: publishers who can demonstrate that AI-reproduced outputs displaced subscription and licensing revenue have a substantially stronger fourth-factor argument than book authors, whose primary market harm is more attenuated. That market-harm evidence, if developed in discovery, could also function as comparator pricing data in any eventual licensing negotiation — directly relevant to how the AI training-data licensing market is priced under 17 U.S.C. § 107(4).

On the MDL's internal discovery posture: the November 2025 order (Dkt. 701) established that 
the Consolidated Class Action Complaint adequately states a prima facie claim of copyright infringement based on ChatGPT's outputs — OpenAI's motion to dismiss that claim was denied.
 The privilege dispute over deleted training datasets (Books1/Books2) remains live. 
Class Plaintiffs sought a ruling on whether OpenAI must produce discovery and communications related to the reasons for its deletion of the Books1 and Books2 datasets; it is undisputed that in 2018, an OpenAI employee downloaded pirated copies of books from Library Genesis ("LibGen"), a "notorious shadow library which has been repeatedly ordered shut down by this court due to piracy."
 
When class plaintiffs sought discovery about the reasons for the deletion, OpenAI asserted attorney-client privilege; OpenAI's position on whether those reasons are privileged has shifted several times; class plaintiffs contend privilege was waived by selective voluntary disclosure or through the crime-fraud exception.
 That motion was granted in part. The Books1/Books2 deletion issue is the most consequential live discovery dispute in the MDL because its resolution will determine whether OpenAI's willfulness is on the table for § 504(c)(2) enhanced statutory damages purposes.

### III. Concord Music Group v. Anthropic — Cross-MSJ Still Pending; Docket Sync Awaited

The music-publisher track (*Concord Music Group, Inc., et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, M.D. Tenn. → N.D. Cal.) remains in its cross-motion-for-summary-judgment posture. As noted in prior advisories, the last confirmed public filing is Anthropic's cross-motion for summary judgment on fair use, filed April 22, 2026. No docket sync is yet wired for this matter; the case-ground-truth block continues to carry an `awaiting_sync` flag. This will not be updated until a CourtListener docket ID and token are confirmed.

The substantive stakes remain distinct from Bartz. The music-publisher complaint targets lyrics reproduction — a use case where § 107(3) (amount and substantiality of the portion used) bears more weight than in the books context, because choruses and verse hooks represent the "heart" of a copyrighted work under *Harper & Row*, 471 U.S. 539 (1985). Anthropic's cross-MSJ presumably argues transformativeness under factor one and disputes market substitution under factor four; the publishers' theory likely centers on both the reproduction of recognizable lyric fragments and the downstream licensing market for AI-generated music products. A ruling on the cross-MSJs will be the first federal decision squarely addressing AI/lyrics reproduction — with direct precedential weight for the separate Concord/UMG track and for any future music-publisher settlement valuations.

### IV. Parallel Docket — Key Posture Markers

**NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft (1:23-cv-11195-SHS-OTW, S.D.N.Y.):** Consolidated into the MDL for pretrial proceedings before Judge Stein. 
The OpenAI defendants' objection to Magistrate Judge Wang's May 13, 2025 Order was denied by Judge Sidney H. Stein on June 26, 2025.
 The NYT action remains the anchor of the MDL's news-media track; the Richner stay order issued this week is expressly conditioned on resolution of pending motions in cases including NYT. The § 107(4) market-harm record being developed in NYT discovery — including anonymized ChatGPT output logs ordered produced in December 2025 — 
will determine whether AI systems reproduced protected articles
 and will feed directly into Richner's eventual merits analysis.

**Kadrey v. Meta Platforms (No. 23-cv-03417, N.D. Cal.):** Active on the public docket per the case-ground-truth block. The MDL briefing reference in Dkt. 325 (July 14, 2025) cites Kadrey for the proposition that output-based derivative-work claims require specific content allegations — 
in *Kadrey*, plaintiffs alleged that every output of the LLaMA language models is an infringing derivative work; the court dismissed the claim, finding that the complaint offered no allegation of the contents of any output, let alone one that could be understood as recasting, transforming, or adapting the plaintiffs' books.
 That ruling has become the baseline pleading standard for output-based infringement claims across the MDL — including in the authors' consolidated complaint, which survived dismissal precisely because it did plead specific output examples.

**Ziff Davis MDL Stay (1:25-cv-04315-SHS-OTW):** Judge Stein's December 15, 2025 order granted OpenAI's motion to stay discovery as to newer models. 
OpenAI's motion to stay is granted as to the models not already in this multidistrict litigation, namely the o1, o1 mini, o1-pro, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.5, o3, o3-mini, o4-mini, and GPT-5 models.
 This carve-out has a practical consequence for rights-holders: infringement claims tied to outputs of those models are not currently subject to active discovery, meaning any Ziff Davis-style claim based on a GPT-5 output sits outside the current MDL fact-development track.
