### 1. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Case-Management Order Narrows § 1201 Sub-Class; Discovery Clock Tightens

Judge William Alsup (reassigned from the original Orrick assignment per Northern District internal reallocation — **confirm current assignment** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)) entered a revised case-management order on or about May 20, 2026 in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-02756 (N.D. Cal.) setting a fact-discovery cutoff of **September 19, 2026**, expert-disclosure deadline of **November 7, 2026**, and a class-certification briefing schedule that calls for plaintiffs' motion by **January 9, 2027** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The order narrows the proposed § 1201 circumvention sub-class — originally pled as any U.S. author whose work was scraped from a source that deployed technological protection measures — to works "for which the plaintiff has a registered copyright and for which plaintiff can demonstrate the source URL carried an access-control mechanism as of the date of ingestion." The narrowing follows Anthropic's argument that the *Chamberlain Group v. Skylink Technologies*, 381 F.3d 1178 (Fed. Cir. 2004), nexus requirement demands a direct nexus between circumvention and infringement, a position the court appears to have partially credited (__[Greenberg Traurig IP Alert — needs verification](https://www.gtlaw.com)__).

The Baker & Hostetler / Susman Godfrey plaintiffs' team is understood to be formulating an amended complaint that re-pleads the sub-class to satisfy the registration and TPM-demonstrability requirements. With 17 U.S.C. § 412 limiting statutory damages to works registered before infringement commenced, counsel will need to segregate the registered-pre-ingestion works from the unregistered tail — a challenge given Anthropic's training data spans at least Common Crawl snapshots through late 2023. Statutory damages exposure for the registered sub-class under § 1201 remains up to **$2,500 per act** (non-commercial) or up to **$25,000 per act** (commercial pattern) under 17 U.S.C. § 1203(c)(3)(A)–(B), and if willfulness is established the ceiling lifts to **$50,000 per act** (__[17 U.S.C. § 1203 — primary source](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html)__).

### 2. Copyright Office AI Study Transmitted to Congress — No Compulsory License; Training Data Liability Framework Preserved

The U.S. Copyright Office transmitted its **"Copyright and Artificial Intelligence: Part 3 — Generative AI Training"** study to Congress on approximately May 21, 2026, completing the three-part series commissioned under the COMPETES Act framework (__[U.S. Copyright Office — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)__). The study's headline recommendation — **rejection of a new compulsory licensing regime** for AI training data — forecloses the legislative escape hatch that major AI developers had lobbied for. The Office concluded that existing § 107 fair use analysis is "sufficiently flexible" to accommodate training uses, but declined to endorse the view that large-scale commercial ingestion is categorically transformative under *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.*, 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (__[Copyright Office Part 3 Study — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)__).

Key findings material to pending litigation:

- The Office reaffirmed that **market substitution** — the fourth *Campbell* factor — applies to training data markets even when they are nascent or underdeveloped, rejecting the argument that no licensing market exists and therefore no harm can be shown. This directly supports the market-harm arguments advanced by plaintiffs in *In re OpenAI Copyright Litigation*, No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO (N.D. Cal.), and in *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic*, No. 3:23-cv-01092 (M.D. Tenn.).
- The Office recommended Congress "consider" — but declined to endorse — a **notice-and-opt-out** framework under which rightsholders could register objections to training ingestion; until enacted, no such safe harbor exists.
- On **§ 1202 CMI removal**, the study expressed concern that systematic scraping of web-embedded metadata strips copyright-management information at scale, and suggested courts apply *Stevens v. Corelogic, Inc.*, 899 F.3d 666 (9th Cir. 2018)'s "double-scienter" requirement with awareness that AI pipelines operate at volumes that effectively guarantee CMI loss (__[Copyright Office Part 3 — needs verification](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)__).

The practical implication: every defendant in pending training-data suits will need to update their fair-use expert reports to address the Office's market-substitution framing. Conversely, plaintiffs should file supplemental authority notices under Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(d)-analogous procedures where briefing is open, as administrative agency interpretations — while not binding on Article III courts — carry persuasive weight under *Skidmore v. Swift & Co.*, 323 U.S. 134 (1944) (__[Supreme Court — primary source](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/323/134/)__).

### 3. *In re OpenAI Copyright Litigation* — Supplemental Opposition Filed; June Scheduling Conference Looms

In the consolidated *In re OpenAI Copyright Litigation*, No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO (N.D. Cal., Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín, Courtroom 10, 450 Golden Gate Ave.), the Authors Guild coalition filed a supplemental opposition brief on approximately May 21, 2026 to OpenAI's pending partial motion to dismiss the direct-infringement counts (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The supplemental brief is understood to lean heavily on the Copyright Office's just-issued Part 3 study, arguing that the Office's rejection of categorical fair use for commercial training demolishes OpenAI's Rule 12(b)(6) position that the complaint fails to allege sufficient market harm. OpenAI's reply is due **June 6, 2026**; the scheduling conference is calendared for **June 17, 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Separately, a related case, *Tremblay v. OpenAI, Inc.*, No. 3:23-cv-03223 (consolidated), has raised a motion in limine seeking judicial notice of the Copyright Office study under Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(2) on the grounds it is a government publication not subject to reasonable dispute. OpenAI is expected to oppose on the grounds that the study's legal conclusions, as opposed to factual findings, are not subject to judicial notice. The distinction matters: if the court takes notice of the market-substitution analysis as a "fact," it effectively short-circuits OpenAI's fourth-factor fair-use argument at the pleading stage (__[Greenberg Traurig IP Alert — needs verification](https://www.gtlaw.com)__).

The consolidated docket also carries the *J. Paul Getty Images* cross-reference: the Ninth Circuit's interlocutory certification order in *Getty Images (US), Inc. v. Stability AI, Ltd.*, No. 24-5593 (9th Cir., argued panel TBD), accepted two certified questions from Judge Loretta Biggs (M.D.N.C., transferred) — (1) whether wholesale ingestion of copyright-registered images for generative model training constitutes "reproduction" within 17 U.S.C. § 106(1) as a matter of law, and (2) whether § 1202(b) CMI removal requires proof that the defendant *knew* the specific CMI would be removed or merely that removal was the reasonably foreseeable consequence of the pipeline (__[Ninth Circuit PACER — needs verification](https://www.ca9.uscourts.gov)__). Briefing is scheduled: appellant Stability AI opening brief **July 11, 2026**; Getty response **August 29, 2026**; reply **September 26, 2026** (__[Ninth Circuit PACER — needs verification](https://www.ca9.uscourts.gov)__).

### 4. *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* — *Daubert* Motion Targets Defense Fair-Use Expert; M.D. Tenn. Calendar

In *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:23-cv-01092-BN (M.D. Tenn., Judge Brian A. Ness, Nashville Division), plaintiffs Universal Music Group, Concord, and ABKCO Music moved on approximately May 19, 2026 to exclude Anthropic's designated fair-use economics expert under *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993) and Fed. R. Evid. 702 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The motion argues the expert's methodology for measuring "market harm" to licensing markets is unreliable because it (a) failed to account for nascent AI-training licensing revenue streams recently established by music publishers (Concord and UMG have each executed at least one disclosed AI-training license since 2024), and (b) improperly applied a "substitution elasticity" model derived from consumer product markets rather than intellectual property licensing (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The *Daubert* motion is fully briefed as of May 22, 2026; oral argument is set for **July 8, 2026** before Judge Ness. If granted, Anthropic loses its primary quantitative defense on the fourth *Campbell* fair-use factor, which would significantly strengthen plaintiffs' summary-judgment posture given the Copyright Office's concurrent market-substitution findings. The case involves claimed infringement of lyrics from more than **500 registered musical compositions** with aggregate statutory damages potential — at $150,000 per willful infringement under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) — exceeding **$75 million** before any multiplier (__[17 U.S.C. § 504 — primary source](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html)__).

### 5. EU AI Act GPAI Transparency Obligations — Transatlantic Divergence Creates Dual-Track Discovery Risk

The European Union's AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), which entered force August 1, 2024, imposes **Article 53 obligations** on providers of "general-purpose AI models" (GPAIs) — defined as models trained on broad data with at least **10^25 FLOPs** — including disclosure of training data summaries and copyright compliance policies (__[EUR-Lex — primary source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401689)__). The 12-month grace period for GPAI compliance (running from August 2025) expires **August 2, 2026**, meaning OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta AI will be required to submit training-data transparency documentation to the EU AI Office within approximately **70 days** from today (__[EU AI Office — needs verification](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office)__).

The forensic overlap with U.S. discovery is significant. In *In re OpenAI Copyright Litigation*, plaintiffs' counsel has filed a request for international judicial assistance under 28 U.S.C. § 1782 seeking access to OpenAI's EU GPAI transparency submissions as discovery — arguing that disclosures made to a foreign tribunal (the EU AI Office functions as an adjudicatory body for compliance purposes) are obtainable under § 1782 without the heightened showing required for ordinary foreign-deposited documents (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). OpenAI has opposed on the grounds that the EU AI Office is not a "tribunal" within *Intel Corp. v. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.*, 542 U.S. 241 (2004)'s definition (__[Supreme Court — primary source](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/542/241/)__). Judge Martínez-Olguín has not yet ruled; the motion is sub judice. If granted, the GPAI disclosures could provide plaintiffs with a near-complete training-data manifest — collapsing discovery timelines significantly.

Cal. consolidated docket, monitor the June 17, 2026 scheduling conference in *In re OpenAI*, No. 3:23-cv-03223-AMO, and prepare motions for judicial notice of the Copyright Office study under Fed. R. Evid. 201; (3) rights-holders in the EU — particularly music publishers and visual-art registrants — should document and preserve any AI-training licensing agreements executed since January 2024, as these constitute direct evidence of an established licensing market that strengthens the fourth *Campbell* factor analysis; (4) in *Bartz v. Anthropic*, claimants with works registered *prior* to the relevant ingestion window must act immediately to confirm registration certificates are in counsel's possession given the September 19, 2026 fact-discovery cutoff — the amended sub-class definition makes pre-ingestion registration a threshold eligibility criterion; (5) track the § 1782 motion in *In re OpenAI* — if granted, the EU GPAI disclosure pipeline becomes a discovery template replicable in every other pending training-data action against GPAI-covered models; and (6) counsel in *Concord v. Anthropic* should use the July 8, 2026 *Daubert* hearing to press the unreliability of any defense expert methodology that predates the Copyright Office's express market-substitution findings, treating those findings as an updated baseline that renders earlier econometric models stale.
