## LinkedIn

78 million. That's how many de-identified ChatGPT conversations OpenAI allegedly assembled internally — before the NYT lawsuit was even filed — to evaluate its own infringement exposure. The public found out only because a court-ordered deposition of OpenAI data privacy engineer Vinnie Monaco produced it.

On July 9, the NYT-led publisher coalition filed a sanctions motion in No. 1:23-cv-11195-SHS (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein / Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang). The core allegations: OpenAI deleted billions of chat logs after a preservation order took effect, concealed for two years that it could search its training corpus, and produced a 20-million-log sample so heavily redacted the court called it unusable. On top of that, "Project Giraffe" — a Bloom-filter toolset that tracked output regurgitation — allegedly existed and was withheld.

The remedy sought is surgical. Plaintiffs want an adverse-inference instruction under Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e) — a finding, deemed established without trial, that the destroyed logs would have shown systematic reproduction of plaintiffs' journalism. If granted, the litigation pivots entirely to fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107, with § 504(c) statutory damages up to $150,000 per registered work for willful infringement becoming a live front. The Times has spent $28M in litigation costs to date, including $4.2M in Q1 2026 alone.

Takeaway: If you hold registered works and are tracking the MDL (1:25-md-03143), watch for Judge Wang's response deadline order — a spoliation ruling here resets the factual baseline for all 16 consolidated cases.
