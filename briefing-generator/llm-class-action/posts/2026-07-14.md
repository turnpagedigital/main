⚖️ Five late opt-outs are the only thing standing between a $1.5B settlement and the distribution clock.

Judge Martínez-Olguín has *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal. 3:24-cv-05417) under submission after a fairness hearing that went smoothly on every substantive metric: 91.3% claims rate, 440,490 works covered, objections described as "minuscule." The deal is structurally sound. What's holding up final approval is a discrete procedural question — whether five untimely opt-out requests satisfy *Pioneer*'s excusable-neglect standard.

The most watched of the five: the author of *Como agua para chocolate*, who argues she never received Spanish-language notice, didn't learn of the deadline until three weeks after it passed, and suffered a stroke during the relevant period. She also contests Class Counsel's "co-owner consent" requirement, calling it inconsistent with the Long Form Notice.

The ruling matters beyond these five claimants. A court that denies all five on excusable-neglect grounds narrows Ninth Circuit appeal risk. A court that credits even one defective-notice argument opens a wider door for post-approval challenges — and delays the September 25, 2026 Anthropic installment.

Separately: the $75M non-class counsel fee request remains disputed, and the default 50/50 author-publisher split will govern any claim without documentation — relevant to anyone facing a publisher asserting a higher share (see: Elsevier's reported 88/12 proposal on one author's work).
