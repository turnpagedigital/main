$1.5 billion. Final judgment entered July 20, 2026 — *Bartz v. Anthropic*, N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín.

That closes the district court chapter. Here's where things stand:

• Claims: 440,490 works, 92.77% participation rate, ~$3,100 per-work recovery
• Fees: Court approved ~$101.5 million — down sharply from counsel's initial $300M ask
• Injunctive relief: Anthropic must destroy all files torrented from LibGen and PiLiMi, plus any downstream copies — no precedent for this at scale in AI copyright
• Appeal window: 30-day Ninth Circuit clock runs from July 20; 53 overruled objectors are the universe of potential appellants
• Distribution: No date announced; post-distribution disputes go to a Special Master, confidentially, with no public appeal window

The merits of infringement were never adjudicated. Anthropic denied wrongdoing throughout. What the settlement does establish: $3,100 per work is now the market's pricing floor on Anthropic training-data claims.

Next inflection point: October 21, 2026 — *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic*, N.D. Cal., Judge Lee. Cross-motions for summary judgment on whether AI training on song lyrics, and AI output reproduction of lyrics, survive the § 107 four-factor test. Unlike *Bartz*, that court is going to the merits.

Practical takeaway for *Bartz* claimants: verify your claim is accurately recorded in the administrator's system now. Once the Special Master process opens, all disputes are confidential and final — there is no public appellate window to correct errors.
