## LinkedIn

⚖️ $1.5B settled. Fair use: still unanswered.

Judge Martínez-Olguín entered final judgment in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal. 3:24-cv-05417) on July 20. Largest copyright class action settlement in history. Class counsel received ~$101.5M in fees, down from the $187.5M request. Claimants recover roughly $3,100 per work across ~440,000 filed claims.

What the settlement does not do: adjudicate infringement. Anthropic's deputy GC told NPR after final approval that "training AI on books is fair use." That position is uncontested on the record in *Bartz* — and it carries to every parallel case.

The more active docket is now *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal. 5:24-cv-03811, Judge Lee). Music publishers filed a discovery-informed amended complaint July 22. The new theory: three-stage direct infringement — data ingestion, fine-tuning, and output reproduction. Internal records allegedly show Anthropic employees prompted Claude for copyrighted lyrics during development. That willfulness framing, if it holds, opens the § 504(c)(2) door to $150,000 per work in statutory damages.

Also watch: the newspaper coalition's sanctions motion in the OpenAI MDL (S.D.N.Y. 1:25-md-03143), alleging evidence deletion and misrepresentation. Judge Stein has not yet ruled.

Practical note for rights-holders: the $3,100-per-work figure is now a documented market-clearing price for pirated corpus access. Works with clean chain of title and documented commercial harm support a higher licensing-value argument in any downstream claim.
