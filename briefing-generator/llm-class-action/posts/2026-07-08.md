91.3% of eligible works were claimed in *Bartz v. Anthropic* — and that number is now doing real work in the market.

Judge Martínez-Olguín took the $1.5B settlement under submission after the May 14 fairness hearing and has not yet issued a final approval order. As of the June 2026 reporting cycle, final approval remains pending. Until it issues — and any Ninth Circuit appeal resolves — no disbursements go out.

What the record now shows:
• 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed (91.3% participation rate)
• Per-work payout confirmed at approximately $3,100 (class counsel Justin Nelson, May 14 hearing)
• Attorneys' fees revised down to $187.5M (12.5% of fund) from the original $375M ask
• Anthropic's $450M Schedule Payment 1 pegged to September 25, 2026 — contingent on no pending appeal
• Late opt-out question unresolved; answer will appear in the final approval order

One structural point worth repeating: *Bartz* is not a settlement about AI training. Judge Alsup's June 2025 fair-use ruling on book training stands untouched. This settlement covers liability for downloading files from LibGen and PiLiMi — piracy, not training.

The $3,113 implied per-work figure is already being used as a pricing floor in indemnification modeling. For music-publisher plaintiffs in *Concord I*, statutory damages for willful infringement run to $150,000 per work — a ceiling that dwarfs it.

Practical takeaway: Confirm your works were claimed before the March 30, 2026 deadline. The pool is 91% subscribed. Late claims are almost certainly barred.
