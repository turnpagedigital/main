## LinkedIn

📋 $1.5B on hold. The Bartz v. Anthropic settlement — the largest copyright class action against an AI company — still lacks final approval as of July 19, 2026.

Judge Martínez-Olguín (N.D. Cal.) declined to approve at the May 14 fairness hearing. The last confirmed docket entry is DE 679, filed ~July 13, opposing class member Donald Passman's late opt-out request. The record is still open.

Key figures from the fairness hearing:
• 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed — 91.3% participation rate
• Per-work payout: ~$3,100 (a function of that high claim rate, not a fixed term)
• Attorneys' fees revised down from $300M to $187.5M; non-class counsel's $75M request separately contested

On timing: Anthropic's second installment of $450M is due September 25, 2026. A second distribution wave is expected around October 9, 2026 — but only after final approval clears, and only after any appeals resolve. No meaningful distribution before Q4 2026 at the earliest.

Scope note that matters: Bartz is a piracy (shadow-library) case, not an AI training case. Judge Alsup's fair-use ruling on training covers only the three named plaintiffs. The settlement releases past conduct and requires destruction of pirated copies. It creates no forward licensing scheme and binds no other AI developer.

Practical takeaway: If you filed a claim, confirm your claim was logged with the administrator and monitor the court docket — final approval is the trigger for any payout, and that ruling has not come.
