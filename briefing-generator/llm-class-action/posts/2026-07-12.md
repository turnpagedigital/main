🗂 A deposition broke NYT v. OpenAI wide open last week.

On July 9, the plaintiff news organizations — NYT, Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and a dozen affiliates — filed a sanctions motion in S.D.N.Y. accusing OpenAI of hiding evidence for two years. The core allegation: OpenAI told the court it lacked tools to search its training corpus and that producing chat logs would be technically burdensome. A court-ordered re-deposition of OpenAI engineer Vinnie Monaco in April told a different story.

What Monaco allegedly revealed:
• OpenAI had already run internal searches of its training data for copyrighted journalism
• Before the NYT suit was filed, OpenAI had assembled a 78-million-conversation database it used to measure its own infringement exposure
• A post-filing tool called Project Giraffe — including a "Bloom" filter — detected and logged output regurgitation

The relief sought is surgical. Plaintiffs want the court to bar OpenAI from using its 20-million-log sample as evidence, to accept as established fact that logs would have shown substantial regurgitation of plaintiffs' content, and to instruct jurors that OpenAI deleted billions of conversations after a preservation order took effect — a spoliation inference that tells jurors to presume the destroyed evidence hurt OpenAI.

OpenAI called the allegations "blatantly false" and framed the motion as a user-privacy issue. That framing doesn't address the core claim: that OpenAI misrepresented its own search capabilities to the court.

The § 107(4) stakes extend well beyond this docket. An adverse ruling here — particularly the evidentiary-preclusion request — would migrate directly into the In re OpenAI Copyright MDL, No. 3143 (S.D.N.Y.), and every parallel publisher proceeding.

Practical note: counsel with positions in both the NYT case and the MDL should begin coordinated docket monitoring now. The opposition deadline and any hearing date are the next critical entries to track.
