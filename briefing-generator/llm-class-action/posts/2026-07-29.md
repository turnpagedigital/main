⚖️ $1.5B. Final judgment entered July 20 in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín). Largest copyright class action settlement in U.S. history.

The numbers that matter now:

• 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed — 91.3% participation rate
• ~$3,100 per work across the class
• $101.5M in attorneys' fees approved (down from $187.5M requested)
• 350 valid opt-outs covering 1,802 works — 0.03% of covered works
• Appeal window closes ~August 19; distributions begin one business day after

One watch item: Anthropic is paying into the fund over time, so payouts won't arrive in a single disbursement.

The court's approval order also carries a specific reminder — non-class-member objectors are free to bring independent claims. Rights-holders outside the LibGen/PiLiMi corpus perimeter retain full § 504 statutory-damages exposure against Anthropic.

The parallel action that now owns the field: *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., No. 5:24-cv-03811). Second Amended Complaint filed July 22. Cross-MSJ hearing set October 21 before Judge Lee. The publishers' theory — that Claude reproduces lyrics on demand, substituting for licensed services — is a different and commercially closer infringement claim than anything *Bartz* resolved. Anthropic's confidential IPO filing in June 2026 makes that October 21 date an implicit negotiating deadline.

Practical takeaway: If your works were registered but fell outside the LibGen/PiLiMi dataset, you are not bound by *Bartz* and your claims remain live. Track the August 19 appeal window if you are a class member awaiting distribution.
