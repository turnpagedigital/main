## LinkedIn

$1.5B out the door — final approval granted July 20 in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín). Largest copyright class action settlement on record.

A few numbers worth tracking:

• 92.77% claims rate — extraordinary against a ~10% class-action baseline
• 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed
• ~$3,000 per work expected payout, down from the $4,876 projected at the earlier 54% claim rate
• Attorneys' fees cut from $187.5M requested to ~$101.5M approved — court applied a 3.75x lodestar multiplier, rejecting the 6.92x ask

Two things the order does NOT do: it does not make Anthropic's fair-use-on-training argument binding precedent, and it does not start the clock on distributions. Anthropic's public statement frames the *Bartz* training ruling as "the law today" — that characterization is worth reading carefully. Judge Alsup's fair-use ruling covered only the three named plaintiffs, not the certified class.

The 30-day appellate window runs to approximately August 19. Any timely Ninth Circuit appeal freezes distributions. Earliest realistic payout: late fall 2026, with a 2027 contingency worth modeling.

Meanwhile, *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal.) goes to cross-summary-judgment on October 21 — the first live merits ruling on fair use since *Bartz* settled that question away.

Practical takeaway: If you hold a *Bartz* claim position, confirm now whether any objector preserved appellate rights below and plan your cash-flow timeline around late 2026 at the earliest.
