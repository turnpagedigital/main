## LinkedIn

A sanctions motion filed July 9 in *NYT v. OpenAI* (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:23-cv-11195) may be the most consequential discovery development in the AI-copyright docket to date.

The factual core: OpenAI data privacy engineer Vinnie Monaco testified in an April deposition that the company ran searches of its training corpus for plaintiffs' content — including before any news organization had filed suit. That directly contradicts OpenAI's litigation-long position that searching its corpus was technically infeasible and burdensome.

Two additional disclosures compound the problem:

• Monaco's deposition revealed OpenAI had assembled a database of ~78 million de-identified ChatGPT conversations for internal infringement assessment — pre-lawsuit.
• "Project Giraffe," including a "Bloom" filter tool designed to detect and log content regurgitation in ChatGPT outputs, was allegedly implemented shortly after the lawsuit was filed.

Plaintiffs are requesting four categories of relief: bar OpenAI from using its 20-million-log sample as evidence; deem as established fact that logs would show substantial regurgitation; prevent OpenAI from contesting that inference; and award attorneys' fees. The deletion allegation — that OpenAI deleted billions of relevant conversations or made them unsearchable after suit was filed — sits on top of all of that.

Statutory damages for willful infringement run to $150,000 per work. The NYT has already spent $28M+ litigating this.

Practical takeaway: Press-publisher and MDL counsel should treat the Monaco transcript and Project Giraffe disclosures as primary willfulness evidence now — before Judge Stein rules.
