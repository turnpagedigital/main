## LinkedIn

$1.5 billion. That's where *Bartz v. Anthropic* stands as of July 20, when Judge Martínez-Olguín signed the final approval order — the largest copyright class action settlement on record.

Key terms from the 23-page ruling:

• Fee award: $187.5 million (down from the requested $300M), with 10% withheld pending post-distribution accounting
• Expenses approved: $2,635,197.46 reimbursed; $18.2M cost reserve set
• Service awards: $15,000 per class rep (court cut the requested $50K), $45,000 total
• Objections: all 53 overruled; 350 valid opt-outs covering 1,802 works (~0.03% of covered works)
• Claims rate: 91.3%

The release covers past AI *inputs* — training on LibGen and PiLiMi datasets. It expressly carves out claims about AI *outputs* and any conduct on or after August 25, 2025. That carve-out is the live hook for follow-on litigation.

On the music side: the *Concord* publishers filed a Second Amended Complaint on July 22 adding 17 U.S.C. § 1202 CMI-removal claims and alleging internal records show Anthropic employees prompted Claude for copyrighted lyrics during development. And *Concord II* hits a hard deadline tomorrow — Anthropic's motion to dismiss or answer is due August 3.

Distributions in *Bartz* are unlikely before year-end. Watch the Special Master process for disputed claims; those decisions are final and sealed.

Practical note for class members: the output/future-conduct carve-out means staying in the settlement did not close all doors. Evaluate what claims, if any, survived the release before assuming full resolution.
