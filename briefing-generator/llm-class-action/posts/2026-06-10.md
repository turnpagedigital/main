## LinkedIn

Tomorrow is the date authors have been waiting on: in Bartz v. Anthropic, the settlement administrator's per-work distribution calculation is due June 11, with payments to follow.

The numbers are historic:

• $1.5 billion fund — the largest copyright recovery in U.S. history
• ~500,000 works, averaging ~$3,000 each
• 91.3% claim rate (440,490 of 482,460 eligible works)

What makes Bartz different from Meta's fair-use win in Kadrey is one word: provenance. Anthropic trained on books pulled from pirate libraries. That gave plaintiffs a clean liability theory and a $1.5B result. Meta trained on a record the court found too thin on market harm — and won, narrowly, on the evidence.

The lesson for rights-holders is now concrete:
1. Register your works. Registration is the jurisdictional key to statutory damages.
2. Provenance matters. Pirate-sourced training is the strongest claim.
3. Build the market-harm record. That's the gap that decided Kadrey.

And in NYT v. OpenAI, the court just affirmed that OpenAI must hand over 20 million ChatGPT logs — turning output-similarity from theory into evidence.
