## LinkedIn

$1.5B. That's the final judgment entered July 20 in *Bartz v. Anthropic* — the largest copyright class action settlement in U.S. history. Judge Martínez-Olguín approved it. Distribution cannot start until the objector-appeal window closes August 19.

Key numbers from the order:
• 91.3% claims rate across ~480,000 works — roughly one in ten eligible works still unclaimed
• 350 opt-outs covering 1,802 works — 0.03% of the class
• Fee award: $101.6M (3.75x lodestar multiplier), not the $187.5M counsel sought
• Service awards: $15,000 per representative, down from $50,000 requested
• Non-monetary term: Anthropic must destroy all training-data copies sourced from shadow libraries

That destruction obligation is already being cited by music publishers in *Concord I* (No. 5:24-cv-03811, N.D. Cal.), where a cross-MSJ hearing on whether AI training on unlicensed lyrics constitutes fair use is set for October 21 before Judge Lee. If she applies the same piracy-source exception Alsup used in *Bartz*, publishers could secure infringement as a matter of law.

Meanwhile in S.D.N.Y., a July 9 sanctions motion accuses OpenAI of concealing a search capability it had built before litigation and continuing to delete chatbot logs after a preservation order. Magistrate Judge Wang has the motion; any adverse ruling could mean adverse-inference instructions or evidence preclusion across the MDL.

Practical note for claimants: August 19 is the gate. Confirm your filing status at the settlement administrator portal before that date — the fund is non-reversionary, but unclaimed shares redistribute, not revert to Anthropic.
