$1.5B and no final order yet. That's where *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) sits as of June 21, 2026.

Judge Martínez-Olguín held a 75-minute fairness hearing May 14. She didn't approve. She didn't refuse. The case is under submission. CourtListener last synced the docket June 12 — no final approval order on the public record.

The participation numbers are striking: 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a 92.77% rate — producing a per-work payout of approximately $3,100. That figure is now the only hard benchmark in the AI training copyright space, settling at roughly 2.1% of theoretical maximum statutory exposure.

What this settlement is actually about matters. Judge Alsup ruled in June 2025 that training on lawfully acquired books is fair use. *Bartz* doesn't touch that. The $1.5B resolves Anthropic's potential liability for downloading and storing files from two shadow libraries — LibGen and PiLiMi — not for training itself.

The near-term risk is delay, not denial. Seven objectors appeared at the hearing. One Ninth Circuit appeal could push first distributions past summer 2026. The four-installment funding schedule runs regardless — the September 25, 2026 tranche is the next date to watch.

Practical takeaway: If you filed a claim, the distribution clock doesn't start until final approval issues and any appeal clears. Monitor CourtListener docket No. 69058235 for the order.
