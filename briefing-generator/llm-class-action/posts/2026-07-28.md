## LinkedIn

$1.5B. Final judgment entered July 20 in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal.). Judge Martínez-Olguín approved the settlement, overruled all 53 objections, and awarded class counsel ~$101.5M in fees — down from the $300M initially sought. A 10% holdback (~$10.15M) stays in reserve until post-distribution accounting is filed.

Per-work recovery: ~$3,100 across 440,490 claimed works. Claims rate hit 92.77% at the May 14 fairness hearing. 350 opt-outs covering 1,802 works.

Distribution is not yet scheduled. The critical variable: any of the 53 overruled objectors has until ~August 19, 2026 to file a 9th Circuit notice of appeal. An appeal would toll the fund. Monitor CourtListener docket 69058235.

What *Bartz* does not resolve: Judge Alsup's underlying ruling held that training on *lawfully acquired* books was fair use — "quintessentially transformative." The settlement covers the pirated-copy category only. Anthropic exits without an infringement finding on training itself. That gap is exactly what the music publishers (*Concord/UMG/ABKCO v. Anthropic*, cross-MSJ pending) and the *In re OpenAI* MDL (S.D.N.Y.) are pressing. Meanwhile, the Third Circuit heard argument June 11 in *Thomson Reuters v. Ross*, No. 25-2153 — the first federal appellate proceeding on AI training fair use. Decision pending.

Practical takeaway for claimants: confirm your claim was filed for all qualifying works before fund allocation closes. The 7.23% unclaimed tranche may not stay open long.
