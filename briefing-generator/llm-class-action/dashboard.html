II. Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee) — Second Amended Complaint Filed; MSJ Hearing Set October 21

This matter is now the most active AI-copyright litigation in the country, and the July 22 filing materially advances both the scope of the case and its settlement-comparator value.

Concord Music Group, Universal Music Publishing Group, and ABKCO Music filed a Second Amended Complaint on July 22, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California — an expanded version of the amended complaint first filed in May, updating the case originally brought in October 2023, which covers approximately 500 songs.

The publishers have since narrowed the case to direct infringement and alleged removal of copyright management information, dropping earlier contributory and vicarious infringement claims.

The docket is dense. Per CourtListener, Dkt. No. 5:24-cv-03811 (last accessed July 28, 2026): Anthropic filed a notice supplementing its response to Fact No. 149 in Plaintiffs' MSJ separate statement on July 21; a Case Management Conference is set for September 2, 2026 at 3:00 PM before Judge Eumi K. Lee.

The cross-MSJ hearing is scheduled for October 21, 2026 before Judge Lee.

The briefing posture heading into that October 21 argument is fully joined. The publishers moved for partial summary judgment in March 2026, asking the court to reject Anthropic's fair use defense ahead of trial; Anthropic is contesting the case and has argued that training Claude on copyrighted material amounts to fair use.

Anthropic has asked the court to find its use "transformative" fair use, comparing Claude's learning to how humans learn from reading and internalizing themes of various works.

The publishers' § 107(4) market-harm submission is direct. On market harm, the publishers' filing states that when Claude outputs lyrics in response to user requests, "Anthropic provides the same service to its users as Publishers' licensees, without Publishers' authorization or any restrictions on that use," and cites a Deezer study estimating over 60,000 AI-generated tracks were being submitted to that platform daily by January 2026, accounting for 3% of total streams. On the training-data admission: Anthropic "admits at least one Claude model was trained on a dataset containing the lyrics to at least one hundred (100) of Publishers' Works" and "has never sought nor obtained a license from Publishers to use the Works."

The amicus coalition is notably broad. The RIAA, NMPA, A2IM, SoundExchange, SONA, BMAC, the Music Artists Coalition, and the Artist Rights Alliance filed an amicus brief on March 30 urging the court to reject Anthropic's fair use defense.

A second amicus brief, filed by the Association of American Publishers, News/Media Alliance, the International Association of Scientific, Technical & Medical Publishers, and the Authors Guild, makes similar arguments from the perspective of book, news, and academic publishing industries.

Concord II exposure. This case is separate from, and substantially larger than, the first. The publishers' second, larger lawsuit against Anthropic, filed in January 2026, covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion in statutory damages. Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending resolution of Concord I's summary judgment; Concord I had completed fact and expert discovery and was approaching summary judgment on threshold legal issues — including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — that will determine the viability of most if not all of the publishers' claims in Concord II and could have preclusive effect.

**Why this matters for the Bartz settlement as a pricing comparator.** The $1.5 billion Bartz figure resolves pirated-book training at approximately $3,100 per work. Concord I involves lyrics reproduction in Claude's outputs — a distinct, and arguably more commercially proximate, infringement theory. The publishers' § 107(4) submission and CMI claims sit outside the book-training fair-use framework that shaped Bartz. If Judge Lee rules for the publishers on partial MSJ — finding infringement and rejecting fair use — the Concord II $3 billion statutory-damages ask becomes a live litigation floor, not a negotiating posture. Anthropic confidentially filed for an IPO in June 2026, days after a $65 billion funding round valued the company at $965 billion — lifting it above rival OpenAI for the first time — and its run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion in May. That valuation profile reframes the settlement math in any music-side negotiation.

III. In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein / Mag. Judge Ona T. Wang) — Discovery Active; NYT Sanctions Motion Filed

The MDL is assigned to Judge Sidney H. Stein and referred to Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang, pending in the Southern District of New York as No. 1:25-md-03143.

The consolidated action combines 16 copyright lawsuits.

The January 2026 discovery order remains the operative benchmark for the docket. In January 2026, Judge Stein affirmed Magistrate Wang's order compelling OpenAI to produce 20 million de-identified ChatGPT logs — a landmark discovery ruling. The relevance theory is § 107(4) market harm: Judge Wang found that even output logs without reproductions of plaintiffs' works are discoverable because they bear on OpenAI's fair use defense, and logs showing what ChatGPT produces across a broad range of queries could reveal patterns relevant to whether ChatGPT's outputs compete with or substitute for copyrighted works.

The NYT front has escalated. In July 2026, The New York Times filed a new motion requesting sanctions against OpenAI.

The filing requests that the judge impose legal sanctions, which could result in financial penalties or procedural rulings favoring plaintiffs; the publishers argue that OpenAI has failed to fully produce documents and information requested. Discovery sanctions in copyright cases — adverse inference instructions under the § 107 fair-use framework — could be materially dispositive: an adverse-inference instruction on training-data comprehensiveness would undercut OpenAI's § 107(1) transformativeness argument at summary judgment.

The circuit split that will ultimately resolve the doctrinal question is now in motion. Three U.S. judges have ruled on AI training and fair use — Alsup (Bartz), Chhabria (Kadrey), and Bibas (Thomson Reuters v. Ross) — with Alsup and Chhabria finding for AI companies on training, and Bibas finding against; the Third Circuit's first appellate ruling, argued June 11, 2026, is pending. The Third Circuit outcome in Thomson Reuters v. Ross will be the first appellate word on AI training fair use in any U.S. circuit, and its reasoning under § 107 factors one and four will directly inform Judge Lee's MSJ analysis in Concord I and Judge Stein's summary judgment posture in the OpenAI MDL.

IV. Systemic Observations: Settlement-Pricing, Licensing Comparators, and the Post-Bartz Litigation Landscape

Three structural points are now cleanly in view.

**First, the Bartz final judgment sets a floor, not a ceiling.** The settlement resolved liability for pirated-corpus training at roughly $3,100 per registered work. That figure reflects the Alsup/Chhabria fair-use rulings limiting liability to the piracy nexus. Cases premised on output reproduction — Concord I and II, NYT v. OpenAI, Authors Guild v. OpenAI — assert a different and commercially more proximate theory: not merely that copyrighted works were ingested during training, but that the model reproduces and distributes substantial portions on demand. The per-work value in a successful output-reproduction case would not be bounded by the $3,100 Bartz reference.

Second, the 91.3% claims rate is a data point for damages calibration. 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a participation rate far above the typical class-action average. Rights-holders who opted out (350 valid opt-outs, 1,802 works, 0.03% of the class) retain independent § 504 statutory-damages exposure against Anthropic ranging from $750 to $150,000 per work, subject to willfulness findings. The court's reminder that non-class-member objectors are free to bring their own claims creates a separate layer of tail exposure.

Third, Anthropic's IPO posture creates near-term settlement pressure in Concord. Anthropic confidentially filed for an IPO in June 2026. Unresolved material litigation is a S-1 disclosure obligation. A company filing to go public with a $3 billion-plus statutory-damages case unresolved and a cross-MSJ hearing scheduled for October 21 has structural incentives to negotiate before the offering window opens. Rights-holder counsel should treat the October 21 MSJ hearing date as an implicit negotiating deadline.

Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claims investors operating on this beat should take four concrete steps now. On Bartz distribution: monitor the Ninth Circuit docket from today through August 19 — the effective date trips one business day after the appeal window closes, and any Ninth Circuit notice of appeal restarts the clock on distributions entirely, likely pushing payouts to late 2026 or beyond; the 10% fee holdback provision means class counsel has a financial incentive to facilitate a clean effective date with no appeal. On Concord I and II: the October 21 MSJ hearing before Judge Lee in No. 5:24-cv-03811 is the next decisive inflection point — the Second Amended Complaint filed July 22 refreshes the pleading scope, and any counsel advising music-publishing rights-holders on licensing posture should treat Judge Lee's ruling as the pricing signal for unlicensed AI-training exposure; the § 107(4) market-harm record (the Deezer 60,000-daily-tracks data, the competing-licensee theory) is substantially stronger than the record Anthropic faced in Bartz. On the OpenAI MDL: the NYT sanctions motion in No. 1:25-md-03143 should be tracked for Judge Stein's response — adverse-inference instructions in discovery-sanction orders can survive into jury instructions at trial and are among the most litigation-determinative outcomes short of a merits ruling. On the Third Circuit: the Thomson Reuters v. Ross appeal (argued June 11, 2026) is the only pending appellate ruling on AI training and fair use in any U.S. circuit; a Third Circuit ruling for Ross would deepen the circuit split and accelerate pressure for Supreme Court review, materially expanding the valuation of claims portfolios across every active matter on this docket.
