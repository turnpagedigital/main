Concord Music Group v. Anthropic PBC — No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee / Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen)

Status: Active — Second Amended Complaint filed July 22, 2026. Partial summary judgment motion pending.

The most significant docket activity in this beat over the past two weeks: Concord Music Group, Universal Music Publishing Group, and ABKCO Music filed an amended complaint against Anthropic over the alleged copying of copyrighted song lyrics by its Claude chatbot; the publishers filed the Second Amended Complaint on July 22 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California — an expanded version of the same amended complaint they first put forward in May.

What changed in the Second Amended Complaint. The latest filing builds on earlier claims and incorporates information obtained through the discovery process; at the heart of the dispute is the publishers' claim that Claude was trained on copyrighted lyrics and was designed to provide those lyrics in response to user requests, with internal records showing Anthropic employees and contractors prompted the system with requests for copyrighted lyrics during the model's development.

Publishers further allege that Anthropic used tools that removed copyright notices and other identifying information from lyrics during the training process, and argue that the company's systems were intentionally built to reproduce lyrics, describing the capability as "a feature, not a bug." That framing maps directly to 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b) (removal or alteration of copyright management information) — which carries statutory damages of up to $25,000 per violation — and to willfulness under § 504(c)(2).

Scope of the case and the parallel actions. This case updates the litigation first brought in October 2023, covering around 500 songs, and is the first of two separate copyright lawsuits the group has filed against Anthropic.

Their second, separate suit, filed in January 2026, covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion over Anthropic's alleged torrenting of lyrics from pirate "shadow" libraries.

BMG filed a third suit in March 2026, citing 493 compositions and alleging the AI firm had built its business on unlicensed works. The three music-publisher actions against Anthropic collectively represent an exposure stack materially larger than the $1.5 billion Bartz settlement.

Narrowed claims and pending summary judgment. The publishers have narrowed the case to direct infringement and alleged removal of copyright management information, dropping earlier contributory and vicarious infringement claims; they moved for partial summary judgment in March 2026, asking the court to rule on liability before trial. That motion remains pending before Judge Lee. The narrowing to direct infringement and § 1202 CMI removal is tactically significant — it eliminates the secondary-liability knowledge disputes that Anthropic used to contest the earlier pleading, and positions the publishers for a cleaner summary judgment record on the output-reproduction theory.

Discovery posture. Prior discovery orders in this case established the framework now being tested by the Second Amended Complaint: in May 2025, the defendant was compelled to produce a total of 5 million prompt-output pairs, drawn equally from pre-suit and post-suit data and randomly selected. The July 22 filing incorporates materials from that production — specifically the internal prompting by Anthropic employees and contractors — which is why the output-as-designed allegation now appears in the complaint with concrete factual support rather than inference.
