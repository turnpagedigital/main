Concord v. Anthropic — Two Active Dockets; Second Action Advances on Motion-to-Dismiss Track

The Concord/Universal music-publisher litigation against Anthropic now runs on two parallel N.D. Cal. dockets before Hon. Eumi K. Lee, with Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen referred for discovery.

Concord I (Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK, N.D. Cal.) — the original action filed October 18, 2023. Plaintiffs, a group of music publishers led by Concord and Universal Music Corp., brought claims for direct and secondary copyright infringement and CMI removal, alleging Anthropic infringed their exclusive rights to musical lyrics both in training Claude and in distributing lyrics through Claude's outputs.

The court ruled in Anthropic's favor in two consecutive decisions: first denying the preliminary injunction as overbroad and failing to establish irreparable harm, then granting Anthropic's motion to dismiss the contributory and vicarious infringement claims.

Prior to those rulings, the parties entered a joint stipulation by which Anthropic agreed to maintain already-implemented guardrails as to its current AI models and products, with the court noting the stipulation resolved the injunction motion to the extent it concerned Claude's output. The surviving claims — direct training-input infringement under § 106(1) and (2) of Title 17 — remain pending on cross-motions for summary judgment. The docket was last updated June 22, 2026.

Concord II (Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC, No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK, N.D. Cal., filed January 28, 2026) — a separate complaint filed by Oppenheim & Zebrak LLP and Coblentz Patch Duffy & Bass. Publishers allege Anthropic concealed during Concord I discovery that it had separately torrented millions of copyrighted books — including works containing musical compositions — from pirate library websites, and that publishers did not learn of this torrenting until July 2025 when Judge Alsup's ruling in Bartz publicly revealed it.

Anthropic opposed amendment of Concord I on the grounds that adding torrenting claims would "fundamentally transform" that case, prompting publishers to file this separate action to address the piracy conduct.

Anthropic filed a motion to stay Concord II, with a hearing set for May 20, 2026, before Judge Lee in San Jose, Courtroom 7, 4th Floor. The docket was last updated May 31, 2026; no ruling on the stay motion has been verified as of this advisory.

The strategic interplay between Concord II and the Bartz settlement is direct: Bartz resolved the piracy-against-book-publishers class exclusively through the LibGen/PiLiMi book channel. Publishers in Concord II allege Anthropic copied their 499 works as training input and in output and bring a separate action addressing 20,517 works identified in Exhibit B. If Judge Lee denies the stay, Concord II discovery into Anthropic's training pipeline will produce documentary evidence — torrent logs, model card documentation, data procurement records — directly relevant to the § 107 fair-use factor-four market-harm analysis: specifically, whether an emerging market for AI-training licenses for musical compositions existed at the time of copying, a predicate that distinguishes Concord's exposure from the Alsup ruling in Bartz.

In re OpenAI Copyright MDL — Discovery Volume Deepens; Model-Scope Stay Remains

In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation, No. 1:25-md-03143-SHS-OTW (S.D.N.Y., Hon. Sidney H. Stein; referred discovery, Hon. Ona T. Wang) — the consolidated 16-case MDL — continues its document-production phase. The docket was last updated July 2, 2026, and is assigned to Judge Sidney Stein with discovery referred to Judge Ona T. Wang.

The most consequential discovery ruling to date: on January 5, 2026, Judge Stein affirmed a magistrate judge's order compelling OpenAI to produce the entire 20 million-log anonymized ChatGPT sample — not the cherry-picked conversations OpenAI proposed — marking a significant discovery victory for the news organizations and authors suing OpenAI. That log corpus is now in the hands of plaintiffs' counsel and will drive both (a) § 504(c) statutory-damages quantification — each discrete act of verbatim reproduction documented in a log is a separate infringement at $750–$150,000 per work under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(1)–(2) — and (b) factor-four market-harm evidence on whether ChatGPT substitutes for licensed access to plaintiffs' works.

Magistrate Judge Wang's May 13, 2025, preservation order requiring OpenAI to retain all ChatGPT conversation logs — affecting over 400 million users worldwide — was affirmed by the presiding judge, representing the first time a court has mandated mass preservation of AI-generated content at such scale.

On the pleadings front, the court granted OpenAI's motion to dismiss First Amended Complaint claims 4 and 5, granted in part and denied in part claim 8, and denied dismissal of claims 3, 6, and 7; OpenAI's motion to stay was granted as to models not already in the MDL — specifically the o1, o1-mini, o1-pro, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.5, o3, o3-mini, o4-mini, and GPT-5 models. The model-scope stay means plaintiffs cannot yet pursue claims tied to OpenAI's most commercially significant current products, creating a split record: the MDL proceeds on the GPT-4 generation while the most capable (and most likely infringing) models remain stayed. Claims investors should track any motion to lift the stay as that event will substantially expand the damages exposure universe.

System-Level Fair-Use Framework: What Alsup's Training Ruling Does and Does Not Settle

The Bartz training-data ruling — that AI training on legally acquired books is "quintessentially transformative" and fair use under § 107(1) — is a district court decision binding only Anthropic's conduct in that case and only with respect to the three named plaintiffs (not the certified class). Judge Alsup certified the class only for piracy, not the act of AI training, so the fair-use ruling on training applies only with respect to the three named plaintiffs, not the certified class authors and publishers.

Anthropic denied any wrongdoing in agreeing to the settlement, and the infringement question has not been adjudicated; the settlement does not create binding precedent that future courts must find such use non-infringing.

This doctrinal fragility matters across the docket system. In the OpenAI MDL before Judge Stein — a different circuit, different factual record, different data sources — the Alsup training-fair-use ruling carries persuasive weight at best. The Second Circuit's § 107 factor-one analysis has historically applied a narrower transformativeness standard than the Ninth Circuit (Authors Guild v. Google, 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015), analyzed database search indexing, not generative model training). Plaintiffs in the OpenAI MDL have every incentive to distinguish Alsup on the ground that training on pirated library copies — the precise conduct he found non-fair-use — rather than legally acquired copies is the operative fact in OpenAI's case as well, once the 20 million ChatGPT logs and training data manifests are analyzed. There are dozens of other AI copyright suits at varying stages, and several appear to be adopting the strategy that proved effective in Bartz: rather than challenging AI training as such, they focus on the underlying piracy and on infringing copies.

Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claims investors tracking this docket should take three immediate steps. First, monitor the N.D. Cal. docket in Bartz, No. 3:24-cv-05417, daily for the written final approval order from Judge Martínez-Olguín, which remains outstanding as of today; the moment it issues, the 30-day window for filing a notice of appeal under Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(1)(B) begins, and any appeal by a timely objector will delay the September 25, 2026 installment distribution, so clients who filed claims should be positioned to monitor for appeals and intervene if necessary to defend the settlement. Second, for music-publisher rightholders, the status of Anthropic's stay motion in Concord II, No. 5:26-cv-00880, before Judge Lee is unresolved; a grant of stay would subordinate the piracy-discovery track to the Bartz settlement dynamics, while a denial opens a parallel discovery channel into Anthropic's torrent conduct with direct § 107(4) market-harm implications — rights-holders with works in the 20,517-title Exhibit B corpus should audit their registration status under 17 U.S.C. § 411 now, before any scheduling order issues. Third, for investors in OpenAI-related claims, the model-scope stay in MDL No. 1:25-md-03143 artificially caps the current damages universe at legacy GPT-4 generation models; the event to watch is any motion to lift that stay, which would substantially expand per-work infringement counts across the 20-million-log corpus and recalibrate damages exposure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c).
