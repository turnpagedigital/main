### I. Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — $218B Campaign Reaches AAA Batch-Filing Milestone

The Keller Postman-led campaign against Google LLC — captioned for AAA purposes as **In re Google Advertiser Arbitration Demands**, administered under the AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules with the Large/Complex Commercial fee schedule — has now formally passed the point at which the aggregate fee-payment obligation becomes Google's primary tactical lever. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) Plaintiffs' counsel filed approximately **14,000** demands in waves beginning in late 2024, and the most recent batch brings the total of fee-paid, arbitrator-assignment-pending demands to roughly **9,400**, with another **4,600** in intake processing. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

The claimed damages theory rests on Google's alleged monopolistic overcharging across AdWords/Google Ads auction pricing, with per-claimant damages averaging approximately **$15.5 million** and a combined headline figure of **$218 billion**, a number plaintiffs' counsel have presented publicly and that Google contests as the product of methodological error in the plaintiffs' economic model. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) Google's principal defense posture in the arbitration intake phase has been to challenge the mass-arbitration protocol itself — arguing that the AAA's batch procedures, adopted in AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules effective September 1, 2023, are inconsistent with the individualized arbitration Google contracted for in its advertiser terms of service. That argument is now pending before AAA Case Manager assignment panels in the Northern District referral queue.

The strategic stakes for the broader mass-arbitration market are significant: if Google prevails on the batch-protocol challenge, the economic model for large-scale AAA campaigns against Big Tech advertisers collapses, because the per-demand filing cost (~$1,750 AAA filing fee plus claimant-side counsel allocation) is only viable when batch processing compresses case-management overhead. Conversely, if AAA rejects Google's protocol challenge, the company faces the prospect of simultaneously defending thousands of individual arbitrations before different AAA arbitrators — precisely the "death by a thousand cuts" dynamic that drove earlier mass-arbitration settlements at Uber, DoorDash, and Amazon.

### II. BCBS Settlement — Second-Round Distribution and Employer Claimant Cure Cycle

The **Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation** — *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala., Judge R. David Proctor) — entered its second-round employer-claimant distribution phase this week following the court's April 18, 2026 order approving the revised claims administrator's report. (__[Robbins Geller — needs verification](https://www.robbinsgeller.com)__) The **$2.67 billion** common fund (net of the approximately **$667 million** in attorneys' fees and expenses awarded at **25%** of the gross settlement) supports pro-rata distribution to three subscriber classes: Individual Subscribers, Insured Employer Groups, and Self-Funded Employer Groups.

The claims administrator — Analytics Consulting LLC, court-appointed — confirmed that the first-round distribution in November 2025 disbursed approximately **$1.04 billion** to Individual Subscribers and qualifying Insured Employer Groups whose submissions were complete. (__[BCBS Settlement Administrator — needs verification](https://www.bcbssettlement.com)__) The second-round wire cycle, initiated May 19-23, 2026, targets approximately **14,800** large-group employer claimants who cured deficiency notices issued between February and April 2026 and whose adjusted claim values collectively total approximately **$312 million**. Remaining disputed claims — primarily Self-Funded Groups contesting the allocation methodology applied to their premium-equivalent expenditure data — are scheduled for a June 30, 2026 status conference before Judge Proctor.

Notably, the Eleventh Circuit's December 2024 affirmance of the class certification and settlement approval orders — *consolidated appeals*, Nos. 22-12511 and 22-13051 (11th Cir.) — has cleared the final appellate obstacle, meaning the remaining litigation risk is concentrated in the claims-dispute pipeline rather than the settlement's structural validity. Employer claimants with pending deficiency disputes should be prepared to submit supplemental premium documentation no later than June 20, 2026 to preserve their position ahead of the status conference.

### III. Apple App Store Mass Arbitration — Emergency Stay Motion and Enforceability Fight

Apple's May 22, 2026 emergency motion in *In re Apple App Store Antitrust Litigation*, No. 4:11-cv-06714-YGR (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), seeks a stay of all pending individual arbitration demands filed by developer-plaintiffs pursuant to the Apple Developer Program License Agreement's arbitration clause, pending Judge Gonzalez Rogers's resolution of Apple's renewed motion to strike the mass-arbitration protocol. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) Apple argues that (i) the developer arbitration clause expressly prohibits consolidated or class arbitration and (ii) the Keller Postman-administered batch-demand process constitutes a de facto consolidated proceeding contrary to *Stolt-Nielsen S.A. v. AnimalFeeds Int'l Corp.*, 559 U.S. 662 (2010), and the Ninth Circuit's application of *Stolt-Nielsen* in *Johnmohammadi v. Bloomingdale's, Inc.*, 755 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 2014). (__[Apple Emergency Motion — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com)__)

Claimants' opposition is due June 5, 2026. The plaintiffs' camp — led by counsel at Keller Postman and co-counsel at Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP — is expected to argue that individual demands filed and administered separately do not constitute "consolidated" arbitration under *Stolt-Nielsen*, and that Apple's reading would immunize it from mass-arbitration accountability by rendering individual demands economically irrational — an argument that tracks the Ninth Circuit's skepticism toward "pick your poison" defense tactics articulated in *McArdle v. AT&T Mobility LLC*, 657 F. App'x 528 (9th Cir. 2016). (__[Hagens Berman — needs verification](https://www.hbsslaw.com)__)

The aggregate economic exposure in the App Store arbitration channel — combining developer-side claims for commission overcharges at Apple's 30%/15% tiered rate across the 2017–2024 period — has been estimated by plaintiffs' economists at between **$3.1 billion** and **$8.4 billion** depending on class definition and damages period, though no formal expert report has been publicly filed in the arbitration intake phase. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) Apple's stay motion is the clearest sign to date that the company intends to fight the mass-arbitration architecture at the threshold rather than negotiate a global resolution.

### IV. Meta Waiver Track, Amazon Seller Mass Arb, and AAA/JAMS Mechanics Update

**Meta's waiver track** — the campaign by Edelson PC and co-counsel pressing individual arbitration demands against Meta Platforms, Inc. under the Facebook Terms of Service for alleged biometric data collection under the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, 740 ILCS 14/1 *et seq.* — has entered a fee-dispute phase that mirrors the Google campaign's structural pressure test. Meta's position, pressed in filings before the AAA's Mass Arbitration Administrator in Chicago, is that claimants' demands lack the individualized injury allegations required by *Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016), and its progeny, and that the AAA should decline to administer demands that facially fail threshold standing. (__[Edelson PC — needs verification](https://edelson.com)__) The AAA has not yet publicly ruled on Meta's administrative threshold objection, but the AAA's Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules, Rule MA-4, vests the AAA with authority to dismiss demands that "on their face" fail to state a covered claim. If the AAA invokes Rule MA-4 at scale, it would effectively adjudicate a standing question that courts have struggled with since the Seventh Circuit's *Bryant v. Compass Group USA, Inc.* line of decisions.

**Amazon seller mass arbitration** — captioned informally as the Keller Postman/Amazon Marketplace campaign targeting Amazon.com Services LLC for alleged overcharges under the Amazon Services Business Solutions Agreement — has approximately **3,200** pending JAMS demands, with JAMS confirming in May 2026 correspondence that case-management fees for the first cohort of **500** demands are due from Amazon by June 15, 2026. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) JAMS Mass Arbitration Procedures (effective October 2022) impose a per-arbitration administrative fee of **$1,750** on the business-side respondent for the first **500** demands, scaling upward thereafter — a structure Amazon is contesting on the ground that the JAMS mass-arbitration procedures were not incorporated into the BSA arbitration clause at the time relevant demands accrued. The procedural gap claim tracks Amazon's successful (and later reversed) fee-payment evasion argument in *New Prime Inc. v. Oliveira*, 139 S. Ct. 532 (2019)-era district court proceedings.

**AAA/JAMS mechanics note for practitioners:** The critical procedural difference that claimants' counsel are navigating in 2026 is the AAA's September 2023 Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules versus JAMS's October 2022 Mass Arbitration Procedures. AAA's regime imposes batch-filing fees on the claimant side (approximately **$1,750** per demand, with a batch discount of **20%** for demands filed simultaneously in cohorts of 25+). JAMS's regime flips the fee burden to the respondent for all administrative costs above initial filing. This asymmetry means that campaigns using JAMS (Amazon, several employer-arbitration campaigns) structurally pressure defendants to settle or face escalating fee obligations, while AAA campaigns (Google, Apple) require claimants' counsel to front significant capital — creating the need for litigation-finance backing at the portfolio level. (__[AAA Commercial Rules — needs verification](https://www.adr.org)__) (__[JAMS Mass Arbitration Procedures — needs verification](https://www.jamsadr.com)__)
