### I. Roundup / Glyphosate: Forum-Selection Dispute Signals Fresh Appellate Risk for Bayer

The glyphosate multi-district litigation, *In re: Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2741 (N.D. Cal., Hon. Vince Chhabria presiding), entered a new procedural chapter this week when Reuters reported that a "funny thing is happening on the way to forum selection" — language pointing to disputes over where newly-filed state-court cases will be consolidated or tried as federal-court bellwether capacity approaches exhaustion (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitwFBVV95cUxQRkgxWGtzaDlQN2VwcGZvOGdSVWpMWWZkakdvY3haXzhhOGNSa3J3cEFhcDZWaGJWZ1dXbFFNUlk1X3V5RHVQNVFnQkMyMTRBSm0wYUNsaHcxQVYtY1JtNUdWZ0wtbTlvNDRzdjhhT0FDUS1jUVQ3bTBfeXhxbjQycDF4RzZHVE9WRFJJWTA4VzhWMXFxSEd1MVdYbUN6ZDU2a3l0cG1LVm15SnFxS2V2LUhMZTFxNTg)__).

The practical mechanics: Judge Chhabria certified a class for purposes of certain notice claims in 2022 but has otherwise kept the MDL on a bellwether track. With Bayer having already paid approximately **$10.9 billion** across roughly 100,000 settlements in 2020–2022, approximately **65,000** claims remained unresolved as of Q4 2025 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxQT0ZUNWlvZ1JfYVlDOFZSdDFDeVhnOEVobkVxc0ttTk9IZ0x5R1RXVHN0RTBsYVJ5VUFDUVN4a2FNVTBMcG04TExURHlfamFwdmljU1lWX3RkQVBwbWduM0pSYzNudGltNXVCTGJ0cU5jcFF0VmlyUXZnT0V4WnM1SFZ0am1Rb1JKR1k1MlVDWlJBVlpuN3E1enBxaEExa3dVVjZzcElYQ2RJQmFlZHZJ)__). The forum-selection dispute appears to implicate whether Bayer can successfully remove state-court cases to federal MDL posture under 28 U.S.C. § 1407 — or whether state-court plaintiffs can block removal and preserve jury instructions that decline to apply federal preemption under *Bayer AG v. Hardeman* precedent as interpreted by the Ninth Circuit.

Bayer's public statement that it has "no plans to restructure" is a deliberate signal to capital markets that the company is not contemplating a Texas Two-Step or divisive-merger strategy of the type deployed by LTL Management LLC (J&J's talc subsidiary) — which the Third Circuit ultimately rejected in *LTL Management LLC v. Those Parties Listed on Appendix A*, No. 23-1108 (3d Cir. 2024) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxQT0ZUNWlvZ1JfYVlDOFZSdDFDeVhnOEVobkVxc0ttTk9IZ0x5R1RXVHN0RTBsYVJ5VUFDUVN4a2FNVTBMcG04TExURHlfamFwdmljU1lWX3RkQVBwbWduM0pSYzNudGltNXVCTGJ0cU5jcFF0VmlyUXZnT0V4WnM1SFZ0am1Rb1JKR1k1MlVDWlJBVlpuN3E1enBxaEExa3dVVjZzcElYQ2RJQmFlZHZJ)__). For plaintiffs' counsel, Bayer's anti-restructuring posture is double-edged: it removes bankruptcy-channeling-injunction risk but also forecloses the structured-settlement trust mechanism that historically has allowed mass-tort defendants to achieve global finality. Plaintiffs tracking the forum-selection dispute should watch for any motions to remand filed in the JCML tag-along docket and monitor whether Judge Chhabria issues updated trial-scheduling orders for the 2026–2027 bellwether tranche.

### III. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass-Arbitration Campaign: $218B Exposure Threshold

The mass-arbitration campaign targeting Google's digital advertising practices — *coordinated demands filed by Keller Postman LLC on behalf of approximately 25,000+ Google advertiser claimants* — continues to represent the single largest aggregate-demand mass-arbitration campaign in U.S. legal history, with total claimed damages exceeding **$218 billion** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). The campaign exploits the same bilateral-arbitration provisions that Google inserted into its advertiser Terms of Service (ToS) to avoid Rule 23 class exposure — a classic boomerang dynamic where defendants' own arbitration clauses become the weapon of mass individual demand.

The procedural posture as of June 2026: Google has challenged the administrability of the campaign under AAA's Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules (effective August 1, 2023), arguing that the volume of demands requires a bellwether-selection protocol rather than simultaneous individual proceedings. Keller Postman has contested any bellwether procedure that would delay or reduce per-claimant recoveries, relying on *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022) and *Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736 (2023) for the proposition that arbitration agreements must be enforced as written without court-imposed procedural modifications. The central legal question — whether a court may impose bellwether sequencing over claimants' objection when the bilateral arbitration clause is silent on mass-demand procedures — is likely headed to the Ninth Circuit if district court intervention is sought (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

**Practical implication for claimants:** Google advertisers who believe they were overcharged through auction manipulation, AdX self-dealing, or Display & Video 360 tying arrangements should verify that their individual demand has been filed and accepted by AAA. The statute of limitations under California's UCL (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200) runs four years from the date of the deceptive act; federal Sherman Act § 4 (15 U.S.C. § 15) claims carry a four-year limitations period with tolling available under the discovery rule recognized in *Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp.*, 521 U.S. 179 (1997).

### IV. Apple App Store Mass Arbitration & Meta Waiver Track

**Apple App Store** (*Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR (N.D. Cal., Hon. Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers); parallel developer mass-arb demands administered by AAA): The mass-arbitration track filed by app developers challenging Apple's 30% commission under the App Store developer agreement is distinct from the Epic litigation but strategically linked. Following the Ninth Circuit's partial affirmance in *Epic Games v. Apple*, 67 F.4th 946 (9th Cir. 2023), and the Supreme Court's denial of certiorari, Apple modified its anti-steering restrictions — but plaintiffs' counsel contend that the modifications are inadequate and that historical overcharge claims survive. Developer claimants are in active arbitration at AAA; the bellwether process designated approximately **50 test cases** for initial merits hearings in Q1–Q2 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

**Meta "waiver track":** Meta Platforms previously attempted to assert class-action waivers and mandatory arbitration clauses against privacy plaintiffs in *In re: Facebook, Inc. Consumer Privacy User Profile Litigation*, MDL No. 2843 (N.D. Cal., Hon. Vince Chhabria). The district court's rulings on arbitrability — and Meta's subsequent consent to Rule 23 class treatment for certain subclasses — created the so-called "waiver track" where plaintiffs who opted out of individual arbitration demands are proceeding in the class action. The **$725 million** Cambridge Analytica settlement (*In re: Facebook, Inc. Consumer Privacy User Profile Litigation*, MDL No. 2843) achieved final approval in October 2023; claims distribution has been ongoing through the settlement administrator. Claimants who were active Facebook users between January 1, 2007 and December 22, 2022 and filed timely claims should monitor distribution status at the settlement administrator portal (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

### V. Securities Mega-Cycle and JAMS Mechanics

The securities class action pipeline remains elevated. The **BCBS / Blue Cross** securities track (*Public Employees' Retirement System of Mississippi v. Anthem, Inc.*, No. 1:18-cv-06209 (S.D.N.Y., Hon. Lewis J. Liman)) — distinct from the antitrust MDL — settled for **$26.5 million** in 2023; lead plaintiff Arkansas Teacher Retirement System continues to monitor related derivative claims (__[Robbins Geller — needs verification](https://robbinsgeller.com)__).

On **JAMS mechanics**: JAMS updated its Mass Arbitration Procedures in November 2023 to introduce a phased-filing fee structure — a direct response to the financial pressure that mass-arb campaigns impose on defendants who must pay JAMS commencement fees on a per-demand basis. Under the revised JAMS rules, defendants may petition for a fee-allocation hearing if aggregate commencement fees exceed $250,000 in any 180-day period. Plaintiffs' mass-arb counsel at Keller Postman, Labaton Keller, and similar firms are navigating whether JAMS's revised fee rules conflict with the *American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant*, 570 U.S. 228 (2013) holding that arbitration agreements must be enforced even when individual arbitration is economically impractical — because the argument runs in reverse: defendants cannot use fee-shifting to make individual arbitration impractical for plaintiffs. The Ninth Circuit has not squarely ruled on this post-JAMS-revision question (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).
