### Bartz v. Anthropic — Final Approval Delayed; Late Opt-Out Briefing Closed


Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic*.
 The fairness hearing went forward on May 14, 2026. 
The hearing took place before Judge Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California.
 
Judge Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission rather than ruling from the bench, and indicated short orders may follow.


The principal post-hearing dispute involves five untimely opt-out requests. 
In a supplemental filing ordered after the fairness hearing, Anthropic urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs from the proposed $1.5 billion settlement, arguing that the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect" and that the remaining three late opt-out requests should also be denied for lack of any record supporting relief.
 
Final approval remains pending.


The tracked-case ground truth (docket ID 69058235, CourtListener) confirms the last filing date as July 1, 2026, with zero new entries in the prior 72 hours, consistent with the matter being fully under submission.

On the claims economics: 
class counsel filed papers apprising the court that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a remarkable 91.3 percent rate (the typical class action claim rate is around 10 percent). The final claims rate vastly surpasses the 54 percent rate reported in counsel's Motion for Final Approval of Settlement filed on March 19, 2026, eleven days before the claims filing deadline of March 30, 2026.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson stated the payout per work in the class will be $3,100.


The fee structure warrants attention for rights-holders modeling net recovery. 
Plaintiff lawyers are seeking 25% of the fund, or $375 million — a staggering sum that illustrates the business model of class action litigation — with administrative costs (administered by JND) expected to consume several million dollars and service awards of $50,000 per named plaintiff totaling $150,000 for the three class representatives.
 
Anthropic will be required to destroy the pirated copies and to pay the $1.5 billion into the settlement fund, from which administrative costs, attorneys' fees, and per-work payments will be distributed.


One structural note that matters downstream: 
Judge Alsup certified the class only for piracy, not the act of AI training, and as such the fair use ruling on training applies only with respect to the three named plaintiffs, not the certified class authors and publishers.
 That means final approval here extinguishes piracy claims but leaves AI-training fair use questions entirely open for the next wave of cases. 
For many authors implicated in *Bartz*, the litigation around AI training and book piracy is not ending; it is shifting to other cases — including consolidated actions against OpenAI and Microsoft, and active putative class actions against Meta.

### Visa/Mastercard Interchange — Revised Settlement Receives Preliminary Approval; Fairness Process Restarts


On June 9, 2026, U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan granted preliminary approval to the revised Visa and Mastercard settlement. This is a meaningful update because prior versions of the settlement have been rejected. The revised proposal, announced in November 2025, is now moving further through the court process.



Judge Cogan wrote: "The Court's responsibility is not to determine whether the settlement is ideal by class members' varying standards, but whether it is fair, reasonable, and adequate," finding that the agreement had met the threshold for approval.
 
The ruling follows an April 27 hearing at which Cogan heard objections from several large merchants, including an attorney for Walmart, that do not support the settlement.


Practitioners need to distinguish carefully between the two Visa/Mastercard tracks currently live. 
An older Visa/Mastercard settlement included a cash fund for merchants that accepted Visa and Mastercard between January 2004 and January 2019, with a $5.5 billion class action claim — but the deadline to file has already passed. The newer lawsuit making headlines in June 2026 is an entirely different settlement. The $38 billion figure being used here is not a fund for cash payouts, but rather represents cumulative merchant savings on interchange rates over a five-year period.


On the older cash-distribution track: 
on May 26, 2026, class counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds. The second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026, with claims included for payment limited to the population detailed in class counsel's motion.

### BCBS Antitrust — $2.67B Distribution in Mid-Wave; Tier-3 Employer Payments Not Yet Begun


*In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, Northern District of Alabama, is one of the largest healthcare antitrust settlements in U.S. history: $2.67 billion approved in October 2020, with final court approval in August 2022 and distributions actively ramping up in 2026.



A $2.67 billion Blue Cross Blue Shield antitrust settlement began paying out on May 11, 2026. On April 23, 2026, subscriber plaintiffs filed a motion in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Alabama asking the court to approve a distribution plan for nearly 7.8 million claimants.



About $1.9 billion will be distributed to roughly six million claimants, with individual payouts varying based on coverage history and premiums paid.
 
Claimants with a calculated distribution of $200 or more will receive 90% in the initial round with 10% held in reserve for a second distribution; claimants will receive funds by check, PayPal, Venmo, or electronic debit card.


The tiered-distribution sequence has material implications for claimants still waiting. 
Tier-2 (employee) notification emails started June 1 for the first batch — approximately 15% of validated Tier-2 claims — with payments arriving four to eight weeks after notification.
 
Estimated total Tier-1 wave completion is by end of July 2026.
 Tier-3 employer payments have not yet begun, per the settlement administrator's public timeline. 
Subclass appeals, batch-processing delays, and IRS-reporting holds all continue to shift actual payment dates.

### Google Mass Arbitration — Keller Postman Clears Intake Stage; Google Updates TOS to Install Batch-Arbitration Framework

This is the most structurally significant development on the mass-arbitration track. 
In April 2026, Keller Postman's Ashley Keller began filing mass arbitration claims against Google/Alphabet on behalf of advertisers, seeking damages tied to two federal court rulings in 2024 that found Google illegally monopolized online search and advertising technology. Potential claims are estimated at $218 billion or more — the largest mass arbitration campaign ever attempted by dollar value.


The liability predicate is bifurcated. 
The Google mass arbitration campaign is directly downstream of two landmark federal antitrust rulings in 2024. First, a federal judge in Washington, D.C., found that Google had illegally monopolized online search — the first major antitrust ruling against a tech giant in decades.
 
On April 17, 2025, a second ruling followed in the ad-tech proceedings; the remedies phase concluded closing arguments on November 21, 2025, with Judge Brinkema confirming she is actively drafting her remedies opinion.
 A remedies ruling from Judge Brinkema — likely to address whether Google must divest AdX — remains the single most consequential pending event for the advertiser-side damages quantum.


The $218 billion figure is based on calculations from an economist retained by the firm, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly.
 
Potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade.



Due to contractual arbitration provisions implemented by Google, these claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation. Keller Postman is coordinating thousands of individual claims on behalf of businesses nationwide, allowing each claimant to pursue recovery based on its own advertising spend.


The most actionable development this week: Google updated its Ads Terms of Service effective July 1, 2026. 
The updated terms take effect on July 1, 2026. Batch arbitration is a process for handling 25 or more similar claims together to make dispute resolution more efficient; the new terms allow for batch arbitration of identical or nearly identical claims, replacing the previous framework that handled each claim individually.
 
The new terms include a 30-day window to opt out via a web form. If you opt out, you retain the right to bring disputes in court. If you miss the window, you are bound by the arbitration clause.
 The 30-day opt-out window opened July 1, 2026 and closes July 31, 2026. Advertisers who have already filed pre-July-1 arbitration demands under the prior TOS are unaffected — Google's adoption of batch procedures for new claims is, if anything, operationally favorable to Keller Postman's intake pipeline.


Ashley Keller has noted that similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between the filing of claims and resolution.
 Google's stated posture: 
"These claims are unfounded," said Google spokesperson Christa Muldoon. In a recent corporate filing, the company said: "Given the nature of these matters, we cannot estimate a possible loss."

Third, for BCBS (MDL 2406, N.D. Ala.): Tier-2 employee claimants who received notification emails after June 1 should expect payment four to eight weeks out; Tier-3 employer claimants should prepare documentation now, as that tranche has not yet received payment instructions from JND, and the 10% reserve hold on Tier-1 claims ≥ $200 anticipates a second distribution round later in 2026.
