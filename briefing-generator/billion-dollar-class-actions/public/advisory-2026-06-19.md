# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Friday, June 19, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas are the Roundup remand, the NextEra $150M preliminary-approval filing, and the Pfizer Depo-Provera MDL settlement-in-principle.** Each moves a different lever: Roundup's remand is a jurisdictional win for Bayer that clears the path to a July final-approval hearing but leaves the federal objector track alive; NextEra's filing formally opens the PSLRA claims-rate clock in the Southern District of Florida's largest-ever securities settlement; and Pfizer's MDL settlement triggers a bellwether-trial stay that reshapes the Depo-Provera claims landscape through at least year-end 2026. Carry-forward items — Bartz v. Anthropic settlement posture, Keller Postman Google mass arbitration, BCBS and Purdue distribution cycles — receive updated analysis below.

### I. Roundup — Bayer AG | $7.25 Billion | Missouri State Court

The week's lead development: 
U.S. District Judge Henry Edward Autrey in St. Louis sent Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion Roundup settlement back to Missouri state court on June 18, overruling objections from plaintiffs who had argued the state court lacked authority to bind a nationwide class — finding that only Bayer, as defendant, held the right to remove the case to federal court.


The jurisdictional history matters for claim-recovery posture. 
Objecting plaintiffs had transferred the class action to federal court on May 22, disrupting Bayer's fast-track schedule; Missouri state court Judge Timothy Boyer had granted preliminary approval in March and had been set to consider final approval in early July.
 Judge Autrey's remand restores that timetable.


The ruling is likely to bolster Bayer's efforts to win approval by restoring the case to the state court where the deal is being fast-tracked and avoiding the risk that it would be reviewed by a different federal judge who has already criticized the deal's terms.
 That other judge is U.S. District Judge Vince Chhabria in the Northern District of California, who oversees all federal Roundup cases and has called the settlement's terms "mind boggling." 
Objecting plaintiffs had sought to have the case sent to Judge Chhabria in San Francisco.


Claimant-facing mechanics: 
the $7.25 billion deal, announced February 17, 2026, is structured for payout over a 21-year period and is aimed at resolving the vast majority of Bayer's remaining U.S. Roundup litigation.
 
The initial approval triggered a settlement provision requiring Bayer to pay $500 million into a fund within 10 days to cover notice and administration costs, and opens a window for class members to object or opt out — with Bayer retaining the right to walk away if too many plaintiffs decline to participate.
 
Plaintiffs faced a June 4 deadline to decide whether to opt out of the class-action settlement.
 That opt-out window has now closed.


Bayer faces approximately 65,000 lawsuits alleging Roundup caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma and other cancers.
 
Objecting plaintiffs have appealed the remand ruling as Bayer presses for final approval.
 The appellate exposure is real: an Eight Circuit reversal of the remand — or a Eighth Circuit merits ruling that a Missouri state court cannot certify a nationwide class under due process and personal-jurisdiction principles — could unwind the fast track entirely. Practitioners watching this case should track whether objectors pursue an emergency stay in the Eighth Circuit before the July final-approval hearing.

**Claim-recovery lens:** The 21-year payment structure discounts present value materially. Claimants weighing opt-out versus stay-in should model the NPV of the structured payout against their individual damages exposure and the risk of continued federal litigation before Judge Chhabria, whose hostility to the deal's terms suggests he would not have granted final approval had the case remained in his court.

### II. NextEra Energy / Florida Power & Light — $150 Million Securities Settlement | S.D. Fla. | Judge Aileen M. Cannon

The second major delta this week: 
NextEra Energy Inc. agreed on June 16 to pay $150 million to end proposed class claims that the major utility misrepresented to investors its involvement in a Florida election interference scandal — the largest settlement of a securities class action in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida since the PSLRA was enacted in 1995, according to the lead plaintiffs' preliminary-approval filing.


Case posture: the matter is *Jastram v. NextEra Energy, Inc.*, No. 9:23-cv-80833, before U.S. District Judge Aileen M. Cannon in the S.D. Fla. 
Plaintiffs brought claims under Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and SEC Rule 10b-5, alleging that Florida Power & Light, through political consulting firm Matrix LLC, used corporate funds to provide funding to "ghost candidates" in Florida state elections, extend a job offer to a Jacksonville city councilor, surveil a reporter critical of FPL, and gain control over a news website.
 
The lawsuit was once on life support: in 2024, Judge Cannon dismissed it, finding that the lead investors had failed to demonstrate that any of the company's statements during the maelstrom of news coverage about those controversies was sufficiently misleading.
 The Eleventh Circuit reversed, reinstating the case.


Under the proposed settlement, defendants — including NextEra, its subsidiary Florida Power & Light, and former top executives from both companies — would pay $150 million into a fund to be recovered by investors who purchased or acquired NextEra securities between December 2, 2021, and February 1, 2023.
 
There are approximately 200,000 investors potentially eligible; the lead plaintiffs are the City of Hollywood Police Officers Retirement System and the Pembroke Pines Firefighters & Police Officers Pension Fund.
 
Insurance will cover the payout, the company said.


Complicating factor: 
the settlement was filed in federal court about a month after NextEra and Dominion Energy announced plans to merge in a $67 billion deal.
 
The proposed settlement comes at a critical time for NextEra as it moves to acquire Dominion Energy, a blockbuster merger that would make the Florida-based company the world's largest regulated power company.
 Virginia utility regulators reviewing the proposed Dominion merger will have the political-scandal record squarely before them.

**Claim-recovery lens:** With ~200,000 eligible class members and $150 million in the fund, average per-claimant recovery before fees will be modest — roughly $750 gross before any tiering by shares purchased and loss magnitude. PSLRA-standard claims rates for mid-size securities settlements typically run 20–40%, meaning per-filing recoveries could be meaningfully higher if filing participation tracks the lower end of that range. Watch for the court-approved plan of allocation and fee petition at the preliminary-approval stage.

### III. Pfizer Depo-Provera MDL — Settlement-in-Principle | N.D. Fla. | Judge M. Casey Rodgers | MDL No. 3140

The Law360 report that Pfizer has agreed to a deal to end the Depo-Provera MDL is corroborated by secondary reporting, though no specific aggregate dollar figure has been publicly confirmed as of this writing. The operative facts, verified:


Pfizer has agreed to resolve a substantial portion of federal lawsuits alleging that Depo-Provera caused women to develop meningioma brain tumors; the judge overseeing the Depo-Provera MDL has since vacated upcoming bellwether trial deadlines and postponed a key hearing on expert evidence while the parties work to finalize settlement details.
 
Eligible victims with claims pending in the MDL may have the opportunity to resolve their cases through the proposed settlement program, though the agreement would not affect Depo-Provera lawsuits filed in state courts.


The MDL is formally captioned *In re: Depo-Provera (Depot Medroxyprogesterone Acetate) Products Liability Litigation*, 
MDL No. 3140, before Judge M. Casey Rodgers in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Florida.
 
As of June 2026, over 5,500 Depo-Provera lawsuits have been filed in federal court.


The key threshold litigation issue that shaped settlement leverage: Pfizer's preemption defense argued that FDA had effectively blocked any additional labeling warning about meningioma risk, which if successful would have extinguished the failure-to-warn claims entirely. 
Judge Rodgers issued Case Management Order 11 on March 11; in CMO 11, she noted that Pfizer's preemption motion remained under review.
 The decision to settle before that ruling issued suggests Pfizer was not confident in its preemption position — a meaningful data point for plaintiffs still in state court, where the same preemption defense will be raised.

The bellwether-trial stay is the most consequential near-term procedural development. 
The parties had chosen December 7, 2026, as the start date for the first test trial.
 Vacating that date eliminates the primary near-term pressure point for both sides. State-court plaintiffs — whose cases are unaffected by any MDL global deal — should expect Pfizer to deploy the preemption argument aggressively in those venues going forward, now that the federal MDL pressure has been relieved.

**Claim-recovery lens:** No aggregate fund figure is public, so per-claim recovery projections are premature. The case profile — surgical treatment, long-term disability, clear dose-response data — suggests tiered grid structures similar to the 3M Combat Arms or Camp Lejeune frameworks. Claimants with documented meningioma requiring craniotomy are positioned at the top of any allocation grid. Monitor for the formal settlement-program term sheet, which will govern opt-in deadlines, claims-submission requirements, and the carve-out for state-court filers.

### IV. Bartz v. Anthropic PBC — Final Approval Pending | N.D. Cal. | Judge Martínez-Olguín | No. 3:24-cv-05417

No new docket activity in the 72-hour window — the last filing was docket entry 674 (June 4, 2026, Order to Withdraw as Attorney). The case remains pending before Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (San Francisco). Settlement status: final approval pending. CourtListener docket ID 69058235.

The structural issue carrying forward from prior advisories: the Bartz settlement is proceeding in parallel with several other author-class LLM copyright cases that have not yet reached settlement, including the consolidated proceedings before Judge William Orrick. Final approval in Bartz will establish the first precedential benchmark for what Anthropic's liability exposure looks like on a per-work or per-class-member basis — a data point that defendants in the broader LLM copyright litigation cycle are watching carefully, and that plaintiffs' counsel in non-settling cases will use to calibrate their damages theories. The fee petition, once filed, will also be scrutinized: the percentage-of-fund benchmark in creative-work copyright settlements lacks the depth of precedent that securities or antitrust settlements provide, so the lodestar cross-check will carry unusual weight here.

### V. Mass Arbitration — Keller Postman / Google Advertiser | ~$218 Billion | AAA Mass Track

No new filings or rulings confirmed in this 72-hour window. The Keller Postman Google advertiser mass arbitration campaign — asserting Sherman Act §§ 1–2 and Clayton Act § 4 treble-damages claims for alleged monopolization of the digital advertising stack — remains the single largest mass-arbitration campaign by claimed damages quantum. The AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules govern batching, fee-allocation, and the sequencing of bellwether arbitrations. The strategic question that has not yet been resolved publicly: whether Google will attempt to pivot toward a global settlement or continue litigating individual batches, which at $218 billion in claimed treble damages creates escalating financial exposure with each unfavorable bellwether award. Monitor for any joint status report or mediator appointment, which would signal the pivot.
