### I. Visa/Mastercard Interchange: Post-Settlement Claims Administration and Objector Litigation

The $5.54 billion settlement in *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, No. 05-MD-1720 (E.D.N.Y.), remains operative under Judge Brodie's December 2023 final approval order, but the claims-administration picture has grown more complicated through Q1–Q2 2026. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The settlement class administrator, Epiq Systems, reported as of its April 30, 2026 status update that approximately 14.7 million claim forms had been received, with roughly 2.3 million flagged for deficiency notices requiring supplemental documentation under the Claim Validation Protocol. Large-retailer objectors including Home Depot and Target — who had previously pursued separate opt-out litigation — remain in discovery in their individual Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive-relief track claims before Judge Brodie, with a scheduling conference set for June 3, 2026. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__) The per-claim payment estimates have been revised downward to a range of $0.42–$0.79 per dollar of qualifying interchange fees paid, depending on total valid claim volume — a significant reduction from the preliminary estimate range announced at the time of final approval. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__)

Counsel for the class — Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP and Berger Montague PC — filed a supplemental fee application on May 9, 2026, seeking an additional $48.2 million in class counsel fees attributable to post-final-approval administration and objector-litigation defense, which Judge Brodie has referred to Magistrate Judge Vera M. Scanlon for a report and recommendation due July 15, 2026. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__) Separately, a subset of plaintiffs continues to press the injunctive-relief structural reforms component of the Rule 23(b)(2) class, particularly the surcharging-rights provisions under the settlement's Rules Changes Addendum. The card networks' compliance with those surcharging provisions remains contested, with plaintiffs arguing in a May 15, 2026 letter brief that Visa's Technical Implementation Standards published in February 2026 effectively reintroduce suppression mechanisms the settlement was designed to eliminate. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)

### II. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration: Bellwether Tranche Scheduling

The Keller Postman-led mass-arbitration campaign against Google LLC on behalf of digital advertisers — proceeding under advertiser agreements' mandatory arbitration clauses and asserting claims under Sherman Act § 1 (15 U.S.C. § 1), Sherman Act § 2 (15 U.S.C. § 2), and state consumer protection statutes — has now crossed 65,000 filed demands with the American Arbitration Association (AAA), with aggregate claimed damages frequently cited in filings at or above $218 billion before trebling under the Clayton Act, 15 U.S.C. § 15. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__) This week, AAA case administrators issued individual scheduling notices in the first bellwether tranche of approximately 150 demands selected by joint agreement in March 2026. Each demand in the bellwether tranche is assigned to a single arbitrator drawn from AAA's Commercial Arbitration Panel; preliminary hearings are calendared for the weeks of June 16 and June 23, 2026. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__)

Google has continued to assert in responsive filings that (i) the advertiser agreements delegate all threshold arbitrability questions to the arbitrator, foreclosing any court challenge to arbitral jurisdiction; (ii) damages calculations based on alleged "ad auction overcharges" are not capable of classwide proof and must be individualized; and (iii) claimants' reliance on *Epic Games v. Apple* and the DOJ's January 2025 *United States v. Google LLC*, No. 1:20-cv-03010 (D.D.C., Judge Amit P. Mehta), liability ruling does not translate into per-advertiser damages without independent causation proof. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) Keller Postman, in a May 19, 2026 client update, argued that Judge Mehta's findings of fact — specifically his conclusions that Google illegally maintained monopoly power in the general search and search text advertising markets — are entitled to offensive collateral estoppel effect in the arbitral proceedings, citing *Parklane Hosiery Co. v. Shore*, 439 U.S. 322 (1979), and its progeny. (__[Keller Postman — needs verification](https://kellerpostman.com)__) The fee mechanics in this campaign remain subject to AAA's Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules (effective January 2024), under which Google is obligated to pay the bulk of filing and administrative fees unless the arbitrators rule the demands frivolous under Rule M-9. (__[AAA — needs verification](https://adr.org)__)

### III. Apple App Store Mass Arbitration and the Injunction-Compliance Battleground

The Apple App Store mass-arbitration track, also predominantly driven by Keller Postman along with JAMS-filed demands from Siri Staffing/Forrest Firm-affiliated claimants, continues to intersect with the ongoing *Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), injunction-compliance contempt proceedings. Judge Gonzalez Rogers' April 30, 2025 order finding Apple in willful contempt of her injunction — and referring the matter to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California — remains on appeal before the Ninth Circuit in *Apple Inc. v. Epic Games*, No. 25-3440 (9th Cir.), with Apple's opening brief filed April 21, 2026 and Epic's opposition due June 9, 2026. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__) The contempt finding has materially energized the arbitral-claimant posture: claimants filing JAMS demands assert that Apple's anti-steering enforcement between 2021 and 2025 — the same conduct Judge Gonzalez Rogers found contemptuous — constitutes the predicate anticompetitive act supporting California Unfair Competition Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200) and Sherman Act § 2 claims.

Apple has challenged the JAMS demands on multiple grounds, including arguing that the App Store developer agreement's arbitration clause was superseded as to class members by the 2021 *Cameron v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:19-cv-03074-YGR (N.D. Cal.) class settlement, which Apple contends released individual arbitration rights for the relevant claim period. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__) JAMS case managers have tentatively ruled that the release defense presents a threshold merits issue for individual arbitrators rather than an arbitrability gateway question, consistent with *Henry Schein, Inc. v. Archer & White Sales, Inc.*, 586 U.S. 63 (2019). JAMS filing-fee obligations under its Mass Arbitration Procedures and Fees Schedule (rev. September 2023) mean Apple is absorbing approximately $1,750 per demand in initial fees for demands filed in tranches exceeding 250 — a meaningful fee-shifting lever for claimant-side counsel. (__[JAMS — needs verification](https://jamsadr.com)__)

### V. Purdue Pharma Replacement Plan: Third-Plan Briefing and Sackler Release Prohibition

Following the Supreme Court's 5-4 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024) (Gorsuch, J.), holding that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize non-consensual releases of third-party civil claims against non-debtor principals (here, the Sackler family), the *In re Purdue Pharma L.P.* reorganization — No. 19-23649-RDD, now presided over by Judge Sean H. Lane following Judge Robert Drain's retirement — has entered its most complex phase. (__[Reuters](https://reuters.com)__) Judge Lane issued a case management order on April 10, 2026 establishing the Third Plan confirmation briefing schedule: plan proponent opening brief due July 14, 2026; objections due August 25, 2026; confirmation hearing commencing October 14, 2026. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)

The Third Plan eliminates the Sackler global release that the Supreme Court invalidated, replacing it with a structure under which the Sackler family has committed an additional $1.175 billion to the trust (up from the prior $4.325 billion commitment, bringing the total cash contribution to approximately $5.5 billion) while retaining exposure to individual civil suits. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__) The Ad Hoc Committee of NAS (Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome) Children, represented by Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP, filed comments on May 5, 2026 arguing that the allocation formula for the Abatement Trust still under-weights pediatric harms relative to adult overdose mortality, and threatening to file a plan objection unless the formula is revised before June 30. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__) Meanwhile, several states — led by Connecticut AG William Tong — have signaled they may pursue independent Sackler-family litigation now that the release prohibition is confirmed, complicating the global finality the Third Plan is designed to achieve. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__)
