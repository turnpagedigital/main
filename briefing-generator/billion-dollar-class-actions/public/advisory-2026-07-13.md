### I. Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal. No. 3:24-cv-05417) — Sub Judice; Payment Clock Running


The fairness hearing in *Bartz, et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417, took place May 14, 2026, before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California, addressing approval of the $1.5 billion class-action settlement.
 
After a 75-minute session in San Francisco, Judge Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to enter a final approval order — but given the tone of the hearing, most observers expect approval to issue relatively quickly.


The post-hearing record is what matters most to claimants right now. 
By May 21, 2026, Anthropic was permitted to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing whether late opt-outs should be honored, and plaintiffs were required to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026 (Dkt. 646-1).
 Docket entry 678 (filed July 10, 2026) is the most recent filing, but no public description was returned in the data pull — its significance is unverified pending CourtListener sync. 
At the hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín heard from seven objectors.


On the claims economics: 
class counsel filed papers showing 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% participation rate — vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in counsel's Motion for Final Approval filed March 19, 2026, indicating a large volume of last-minute claims.
 
Class Counsel Justin Nelson placed the per-work payout at approximately $3,100.
 
Plaintiff counsel are seeking 25% of the $1.5 billion fund — $375 million in attorneys' fees — alongside a cost reserve, $50,000 service awards per named plaintiff (totaling $150,000), and Special Master fees.


The payment installment structure is the near-term watch item for claimants: 
Anthropic funds the settlement in four tranches — October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 
Payouts to class members will only issue after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 That sequencing means the September 25, 2026 installment ($450 million) will land in the escrow account irrespective of whether the final approval order has been entered — but no distributions flow to claimants until the order is in place and any Ninth Circuit appeal window closes. Appellate exposure remains the dominant risk: seven objectors appeared at the fairness hearing, and any one can file a notice of appeal from a final approval order within 30 days of entry, triggering a Ninth Circuit track that, absent a motion to expedite, would likely extend distribution into 2027 or beyond.

**Advisory note:** Rightsholders who filed claims are fully positioned. The relevant action now is monitoring the CourtListener docket for the final approval order and watching whether any objector files a notice of appeal within 30 days of that order.

### II. Visa / Mastercard Interchange — Injunctive Track: Preliminary Approval Entered; Final Approval 12–18 Months Out

The injunctive-relief track of *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.), crossed a significant threshold last month. 
U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan granted preliminary approval to a revised $38 billion settlement between Visa, Mastercard, and merchants, clearing a major hurdle in a nearly two-decade antitrust dispute over swipe fees.
 
Cogan wrote that the agreement "provides more extensive relief" than a prior settlement the court rejected in June 2024.
 
The pact covers approximately 12 million merchants.


The practical scope: 
the settlement focuses solely on injunctive/equitable relief, requiring Visa and Mastercard to make broad changes to network rules over the next five years — merchants gain greater flexibility over which cards they accept, including high-cost premium or commercial cards, and may surcharge credit cards up to 3%, regardless of whether they also accept or surcharge American Express; rate reductions and caps benefit all merchants whether they pay posted or negotiated rates.
 
The $38 billion figure represents cumulative merchant savings on interchange rates over a five-year period, not a cash settlement fund.


Objector exposure is real. 
The April 27 hearing before Cogan drew objections from several large merchants, including counsel for Walmart, who do not support the settlement.
 
NACS has stated it will appeal to the Second Circuit if Cogan grants final approval.
 
Analysts predict the settlement may not be fully finalized until 2029.


Separately on the damages track: 
in April 2026, a putative class action was filed on behalf of U.S. merchants seeking damages related to interchange fees associated with Mastercard and Visa credit card transactions since January 2019, and named plaintiffs concurrently filed a motion for summary judgment before the MDL 1720 court seeking a declaration that the release from the Damages Class Settlement Agreement — which by its terms prospectively releases damages claims through August 2028 — does not bar their claims.
 
Briefing on that motion has not yet been scheduled.
 That release-scope dispute is the sleeper issue on the damages side: if the court holds the August 2028 release is enforceable, the new putative class is barred for the relevant period; if not, a second damages class action opens.

And on the first-wave damages distribution: 
on May 26, 2026, class counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds from the $5.54 billion damages settlement; the court approved that motion on June 15, 2026, with the second distribution anticipated in September 2026.

### III. BCBS MDL 2406 (N.D. Ala. No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP) — Distribution in Progress; Tier-2 Notification Underway

The $2.67 billion subscriber antitrust settlement in *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, N.D. Ala. Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, is now in active distribution — the longest-running item in this portfolio. 
A motion for distribution of settlement funds was filed April 23, 2026 and approved by the court April 24, 2026, with payments directed to issue on or around May 11, 2026.
 
Approximately $1.9 billion is being distributed to roughly six million claimants, with individual payouts varying based on coverage history and premiums paid.
 
Payouts average $333; only those who filed by the November 2021 deadline are eligible.


Distribution cadence as of this advisory: 
the administrator confirmed a second batch of prepaid debit cards was mailed the week of May 19, with paper checks for non-debit elections beginning to ship May 25.
 
Tier-2 (Employee) notification emails started June 1 for the first batch, covering approximately 15% of validated Tier-2 claims.
 
Estimated Tier-1 wave completion is by end of July 2026.
 That places the close of the Tier-1 distribution window this week. Claimants who have not received payment or a "Paid" status in the Epiq portal should verify contact information with JND Legal Administration at info@BCBSsettlement.com immediately — the window is closing.

The structural backdrop: 
the settlement was granted final approval August 9, 2022; appeals were filed shortly after; and on June 24, 2024, the Supreme Court denied the appeals, confirming the settlement agreement as final.
 Thirteen years from case filing to check-in-hand.

### IV. Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Claims Filed; Treble Damages Theory Live

The largest mass-arbitration campaign by nominal claimed value is past the intake stage. 
Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding the company engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.
 
The $218 billion demand figure is based on calculations from an economist the firm has retained, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly — the two separate rulings establishing Google's liability.


The liability predicate — critical for assessing claim strength — rests on prior adjudications, not a pending trial. 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) have ruled in favor of the U.S. Department of Justice, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade.
 
Potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law (Clayton Act § 4).



Because these proceedings take place in private arbitration, they have remained largely outside the public record.
 That opacity cuts both ways: claimants get confidentiality and avoid being the "face" of anti-Google litigation, but it also means no docket trail for monitoring settlement pressure or bellwether outcomes. 
In addition to representing private claimants in arbitration, Keller Postman represents seven state attorneys general in a parallel case.
 The AG parallel track is the external pressure point — state AG enforcement histories (cf. the TurboTax $141M FTC resolution, the Amazon Alexa campaign that prompted removal of mandatory arbitration entirely) suggest Keller Postman's strategy is to build volume pressure that forces a global resolution rather than arbitrating 10,000 claims to award individually.


The firm cautions that deadlines vary by state, and that earlier action increases the probability of a successful claim being filed within applicable statutes of limitations.
 State-specific limitations periods under the Clayton Act (four years, 15 U.S.C. § 15b) and applicable state antitrust laws mean that advertisers who spent on Google search or display ads from August 2016 onward are in the eligible window now, but that window narrows with each passing month for pre-2022 spend.

### V. Visa/Mastercard New Putative Class & Broader Carry-Forward Context

Two other MDL-adjacent developments warrant brief carry-forward notation:

**New Potayto-Potahto action (SDNY):** 
on April 21, 2026, Potayto-Potahto, LLC and two other merchants filed a class action complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York against Visa Inc., Visa U.S.A., Visa International, Mastercard Incorporated, and Mastercard International Incorporated, asserting violations of federal antitrust laws consistent with MDL 1720 allegations, on behalf of merchants accepting Visa and/or Mastercard credit cards since January 25, 2019, seeking damages from that date.
 This action runs directly into the release-scope issue noted above; its trajectory depends entirely on whether the MDL 1720 court finds the August 2028 prospective release bars it.

**European Visa merchant action (UK High Court):** 
on April 20, 2026, a group of merchants from across Europe filed a claim in the UK High Court against several Visa entities, alleging that interchange fees on European transactions are an unlawful restriction of competition and seeking damages for the period from January 1, 2019 to present.
 Not directly actionable for U.S. claimants, but relevant to the overall litigation pressure picture for Visa's global interchange posture.
