### 1. Second Circuit Partially Revives Oral Phenylephrine MDL "Maximum Strength" Claims — *Yousefzadeh v. J&J Consumer Inc.*, No. 24-3296-cv (2d Cir. July 30, 2026)


The Second Circuit on Thursday revived claims in the MDL alleging makers of oral decongestants misled consumers by labeling their products as "maximum strength," while finding the bulk of the remaining claims were preempted by federal regulation.


The panel — Judges Raggi, Chin, and Pérez — drew a precise line. 
The court largely affirmed the district court's dismissal, finding that the majority of consumer plaintiffs' state-law claims were preempted by the FDCA because the manufacturers were following FDA labeling requirements — but allowed state-law claims to move forward as to the manufacturers' use of "maximum strength" or "max strength," finding that the FDA had not required or blessed those terms, and thus no preemptive shield attached.
 That carve-out is the live engine: the FDA never mandated the "maximum strength" descriptor, so plaintiffs can still argue in state court that the label is independently misleading independent of the underlying efficacy debate.


The court also remanded for further consideration claims related to non-monograph, brand-name drugs, because for those drugs the manufacturers may have been able to make labeling changes under the FDA's "changes-being-effected" regulation.
 That remand creates a second, narrower track — defendants whose products sit outside the OTC monograph system face renewed Daubert and liability exposure on a changes-being-effected theory.


As to the RICO claims, the court held that plaintiffs lacked a cause of action under RICO as indirect purchasers.
 That disposes of the treble-damages upside that plaintiffs' counsel had preserved on appeal.

Named defendants in the surviving claims include 
CVS Health Corp., Target Corp., The Procter & Gamble Co., and Walmart Inc., with the underlying MDL before Judge Brian M. Cogan.
 The MDL — *In re: Oral Phenylephrine Marketing and Sales Practices Litigation*, MDL No. 3089 — 
centers on allegations that the "maximum strength" labeling on defendants' oral phenylephrine products is false and misleading because phenylephrine is not as strong as other OTC decongestants such as pseudoephedrine-containing products, as demonstrated by studies finding pseudoephedrine superior.


**Recovery-posture read:** The surviving "maximum strength" claims are narrower than the original class theory but are real. Defendants have no FDCA shield on those specific label words. The non-monograph remand adds a second exposure pocket. RICO dismissal removes indirect-purchaser trebling but does not eliminate state-consumer-protection multipliers in California, New York, or Illinois — all of which have UDAP statutes that can approximate or exceed that effect. Expect defendants to move quickly on remand to argue that state unfair-competition claims are separately preempted; watch for a renewed motion-to-dismiss schedule before Judge Cogan.

### 2. Depo-Provera MDL Gets New Pilot Plaintiffs Post-Global Settlement — *In re Depo-Provera (Medroxyprogesterone Acetate) Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 3140, N.D. Fla., Judge M. Casey Rodgers


The Florida federal judge overseeing the Depo-Provera MDL on Wednesday approved three new pilot plaintiffs for the cases remaining after a global settlement that the judge called an "excellent outcome."


The context: 
on June 15, 2026, Judge Rodgers announced in a court order that Pfizer and plaintiffs' lead counsel had reached a global agreement to resolve eligible claims in the MDL, though the terms had not yet been made public as both sides worked to finalize the details.
 
Following the announcement, Judge Rodgers vacated deadlines in the first bellwether case, which had been scheduled for trial in December 2026.


Wednesday's pilot-plaintiff order confirms that a residual litigation track is running alongside the settlement program. 
As of July 2026, more than 5,800 lawsuits have been filed in the MDL.
 
The global settlement does not include cases filed in state court, most of which are pending in New York and Delaware.
 The pilot-plaintiff designation for the residual federal cases is a standard MDL triage mechanism — Judge Rodgers is preserving a live bellwether runway for claims that either do not qualify under the settlement program's eligibility criteria or whose claimants elect to remain in litigation. 
The deal has not been finalized, and no amounts have been made public.


**Recovery-posture read:** The "excellent outcome" language from Judge Rodgers on the record is a signal of judicial approval that will make any opt-out or objection campaign harder to sustain. The absence of public terms means claims-rate analysis is impossible today; the settlement structure's eligibility criteria — once disclosed — will be the key determinant of residual litigation volume and per-claim value in the non-settled track. New York and Delaware state cases are entirely outside the program and retain full trial exposure.

### 3. J&J Talc MDL — $5.5 Billion Proposal Status (Background; No New Court Filing Today)

Announced July 27 and covered yesterday. For completeness: 
J&J said on July 27 it would pay an estimated $5.5 billion to resolve tens of thousands of lawsuits alleging its baby powder and other talc products cause ovarian cancer; the settlement covers about 76,000 claims, including ones consolidated in federal court in New Jersey and related state-court cases, representing nearly all remaining talc claims.
 
MDL-2738 is before Judge Michael A. Shipp in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey.
 
The deal must be accepted by 95% of the ovarian cancer claimants in state or federal court before it becomes final.
 
Unlike the proposed bankruptcy settlements, the agreement applies only to existing claims and does not address future lawsuits.
 
Chris Seeger, representing approximately 2,500 clients, said J&J could ultimately pay $7 billion or more, as the settlement assigns specific values to qualifying ovarian cancer claims but does not cap J&J's total payout.


No new docket entries have posted in MDL-2738 since the July 27 announcement. The 95% participation threshold is the critical near-term watch: if lead plaintiffs' firms hold the requisite block of claims and formally sign on, the deal moves to court-supervised documentation; if sub-threshold holdouts emerge, expect side negotiations or a fee-allocation dispute among plaintiffs' counsel to surface first. Next milestone to track: formal participation-rate disclosure and any preliminary court-approval scheduling order from Judge Shipp.
