# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Wednesday, July 29, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's Deltas:** Two major settlements crossed final-approval and distribution milestones in the past ten days. The most consequential is *Bartz v. Anthropic*, where Judge Martínez-Olguín issued her final-approval order on July 20, closing the largest copyright class action in U.S. history and cutting fees to roughly one-third of what class counsel originally sought — a fee-compression outcome worth studying in every pending AI training case. On the mass-tort front, Johnson & Johnson announced a $5.5 billion uncapped settlement of the talc MDL in late July 2026, ending the bankruptcy-pivot strategy after three failed Chapter 11 attempts and replacing it with a 95%-threshold claims-made deal in Judge Shipp's court. Meanwhile, the BCBS MDL distribution — long stalled by objector and opt-out campaigns — began rolling payments on May 11 to nearly 7.8 million claimants. Each of these provides a live denominator-math case study for recovery-oriented holders.

### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Entered; Fee Award Compressed 73% from Original Request


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the landmark $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment in the case.
 The order carries immediate strategic weight beyond the four corners of this docket: it is the first fully resolved mega-settlement arising from AI training-data copyright claims, and the fee-compression arithmetic it produced will be cited in every subsequent AI copyright proceeding.

On fees: 
class counsel initially sought $300 million, then trimmed the request to $187.5 million; the court approved approximately $101.5 million.
 That figure works out to 
roughly $2,953 per hour across the 34,381.6 hours of attorney time the court credited.
 The lodestar cross-check appears to have functioned precisely as designed — constraining a percentage-of-fund request that, at its peak, would have consumed 25% of the fund. 
$122 million of the settlement money will go toward attorneys and litigation costs in total.


On the class itself: 
the agreement requires Anthropic to pay $1.5 billion to the authors and publishers of nearly half a million books it downloaded from notorious pirate sites, and also to destroy all original torrented files and any copies originating from them — a result the AAP describes as the largest class action copyright settlement in history, with a reported claims rate of 92.77 percent.
 
Class counsel separately reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802, or 0.03%, of the total number of works.
 Either figure — 91.3% or 92.77% — represents an extraordinary claims-participation rate by any measure; the standard "low claims rate = higher per-claim recovery" dynamic is effectively inverted here. 
Bartz is "the largest copyright class action settlement in U.S. history," with payments of approximately $3,100 per author of more than 480,000 works, for claims filed on 440,490 works downloaded from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror by Anthropic.


On objectors: 
Judge Martínez-Olguín overruled all 53 objections filed with the court.
 
The court declined to entertain objections from non-class members, but took care to remind those individuals that, precisely because the settlement does not cover them, they remain free to bring their own claims.
 That carve-out is significant: 
the *Chicken Soup for the Soul* and Carreyrou suits are separate, individual actions, and the court's approval order may prompt others to follow.


Distribution timing: 
the Authors Guild looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process, though a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not yet been announced.
 No appellate stay has been reported as of the date of this advisory. The 30-day appeal window runs from the July 20 final judgment date; that window closes August 19, 2026. Recovery counsel should note this date.

### II. *In re: Johnson & Johnson Talc Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2738 (D.N.J.) — $5.5 Billion Uncapped Settlement; 95% Threshold; First Payment 2027


The Plaintiffs' Negotiation Committee in the Johnson & Johnson talcum powder multidistrict litigation announced an uncapped settlement expected to provide at least $5.5 billion to approximately 70,000 plaintiffs in MDL No. 2738
 and related state court proceedings. 
As of July 2026, there are 68,435 lawsuits in the talcum powder MDL, which is being tried in the U.S. District Court for New Jersey, presided over by District Judge Michael A. Shipp.


This resolution ends a decade-long series of failed bankruptcy maneuvers. 
The settlement comes after several previous failed attempts by J&J to settle the litigation via bankruptcy; J&J subsidiary Red River Talc LLC filed for Chapter 11 in September 2024 in what was its third attempt to use bankruptcy to address talc-related claims, as courts had previously dismissed both earlier attempts.
 
A federal judge in Houston dismissed Red River Talc's bankruptcy in March 2025, ending that settlement attempt
 and forcing J&J back into the civil MDL track.

The structural terms of the settlement carry specific conditionality: 
J&J has agreed to pay up to $5.5 billion to resolve remaining U.S. talc-ovarian cancer lawsuits, but the deal requires at least 95% of remaining claimants to participate before it takes effect, with a first payment of up to $3 billion scheduled for 2027 and no further payments before 2028.
 The 95% threshold is J&J's leverage mechanism — it ensures near-complete resolution before J&J releases any funds, in contrast to the trust-based, percentage-of-vote mechanics of a Chapter 11 plan.

The causation picture contributed to J&J's improved negotiating posture: 
a pivotal MDL court ruling on causation shifted the balance — finding that plaintiffs were unable to prove specific causation — and J&J is betting that most plaintiffs will take the deal rather than fight on weakened legal ground.
 
A significant development in July 2026 was the federal MDL court questioning whether plaintiffs could present admissible expert evidence showing that talc use caused each claimant's ovarian cancer, ordering plaintiffs to explain why the remaining claims should not be dismissed for lack of specific causation evidence; the ruling did not immediately dismiss cases, but created additional pressure as the parties negotiated.



No figures have been released yet on what individual payouts could look like for people who have filed talc lawsuits.
 Independent estimates range from $100,000 to $1 million per case depending on disease type and severity, but those ranges are unverified by the settlement administrator at this stage. 
If approved, the settlement could bring payouts to families as soon as 2027.
 The deal is not yet filed for court approval; claimants and their counsel should watch the MDL 2738 docket for a formal settlement agreement submission and Judge Shipp's scheduling of a fairness hearing.

### III. *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala.) — $2.67 Billion Distribution Now Live; ~7.8 Million Claimants

The BCBS subscriber settlement is now in active distribution. 
The initial distribution of payments to Damages Class Members with valid claims began on May 11, 2026, arising from the antitrust class action *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, N.D. Ala. Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, which was reached on behalf of individuals and companies that purchased or received health insurance provided or administered by a Blue Cross Blue Shield company.



Under the oversight of U.S. District Judge R. David Proctor in the Northern District of Alabama, the court cleared the final hurdles for distribution.
 
The $2.67 billion antitrust class action settlement sees approximately 6 million claimants sharing roughly $1.9 billion after fees, for an average payout of approximately $333.
 
On April 23, 2026, subscriber plaintiffs filed a motion in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Alabama asking the court to approve a distribution plan for nearly 7.8 million claimants.


The path to distribution was extended by objector campaigns. 
A judge granted final approval in August 2022, but the settlement was delayed for years by appeals through the Eleventh Circuit and the U.S. Supreme Court.
 
The Supreme Court-level objection was filed by David Behenna, who argued that the $667 million in legal fees and costs was too high, contending the fee should be no more than $194 million based on a lodestar calculation.
 
Attorneys for Home Depot also filed a challenge at the Supreme Court level, arguing that the settlement terms are too sweeping and will harm the ability of future private parties to sue BCBS over alleged violations of competition law.
 Those challenges have now been resolved; distribution is underway.

For practitioners tracking distribution mechanics: 
claimants who selected electronic payment methods such as Venmo, PayPal, or electronic debit card were among the first to receive payments; claimants who originally chose mailed checks may be able to switch to electronic debit card through a button in their claim determination notice.

### IV. Keller Postman v. Google (Mass Arbitration) — $218B+ Campaign; Filing Wave Underway; Judge Brinkema Remedies Opinion Pending

The Keller Postman Google advertiser mass arbitration campaign — the largest by claimed dollar value ever mounted — is now in active filing phase. 
Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings; the firm is targeting companies of all sizes that spent money on Google search or display advertising on or after August 2016.



Google faces billions in potential damage claims as part of mass arbitration tied to its online search and advertising technology businesses, which courts have ruled were illegal monopolies; advertisers are banding together to seek payouts through mass arbitration proceedings, because many companies that displayed ads purchased through Google — including USA Today Co. and Advance Publications Inc. — have sued for damages since the 2024 rulings, but advertiser contracts require mandatory arbitration over legal disputes.



Keller estimates potential claims for online search and display ads could reach $218 billion or more, based on calculations from an economist his firm has hired; similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between the filing of claims and resolution.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases have estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs, creating potential for substantial recovery under federal antitrust law.


The structural mechanics matter for assessing this campaign's leverage. 
Arbitration clauses in Google's advertiser contracts prevent damages via class action; instead, advertisers can join together in mass arbitration, which is usually handled by a single law firm and gives them more leverage in settlement negotiations.
 The historical analog is instructive: 
Ashley Keller is the architect of prior mass arbitration campaigns including the Amazon Alexa campaign that prompted Amazon to remove its mandatory arbitration clause entirely after 75,000 demands, and the TurboTax campaign that resolved in a confidential settlement alongside a $141 million FTC fund.


The remedies phase in the underlying antitrust case provides the critical near-term catalyst. 
Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling is expected in 2026; Keller Postman is actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines. Closing arguments concluded in the remedies phase on November 21, 2025, with DOJ arguing for divestiture of Google's ad exchange (AdX) and Google proposing behavioral remedies only.
 Judge Brinkema's opinion — once issued — will either sharpen or complicate the damages quantum underpinning the $218 billion estimate, particularly on ad tech-side claims vs. search-side claims. Watch for that ruling as the critical trigger for Keller Postman's next acceleration.

### V. Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL — Injunctive Relief Settlement; $500M Escrow Posted


On November 10, 2025, Visa entered into a superseding and amended settlement agreement to resolve the interchange MDL injunctive relief class claims, subject to court approval; on December 23, 2025, Visa deposited $500 million into its litigation escrow account, which was previously established under the company's U.S. retrospective responsibility plan to insulate the company and Class A common stockholders from financial liability.
 The injunctive relief class — distinct from the damages class that received an earlier $5.54 billion payout — remains the live contested track. Court approval of the superseding settlement agreement has not been reported as confirmed as of this advisory.

## Recommended Actions

Claims-holders and recovery counsel should move on three fronts in the next 30 days. In *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417), track the August 19, 2026 appellate deadline following Judge Martínez-Olguín's July 20 final judgment; any notice of appeal filed before that date would expose the distribution to stay risk, and rights-holders who have not yet confirmed their claim status with the Anthropic Copyright Settlement Administrator (877-206-2310 / info@AnthropicCopyrightSettlement.com) should do so now, as distribution timing post-final-approval has not yet been formally announced. In the J&J Talc MDL (MDL 2738, D.N.J., Judge Shipp), counsel representing the approximately 68,435 pending claimants should evaluate individual case facts against the 95% threshold mechanic — that structure means late holdouts have effective veto power over the entire deal, and claimants with strong specific-causation records may warrant individualized analysis before tendering consent; watch the MDL 2738 docket for a formal plan document filing. In the Keller Postman/Google mass arbitration, any U.S. business that spent on Google Search or display ads after August 2016 should assess claim eligibility before state-law limitations deadlines close the window; the 12-to-24-month timeline to resolution means filing now — not after Judge Brinkema's remedies opinion — preserves optionality.
