Recovery counsel advising claimants should note that the supplemental claims administration portal (administered by **JND Legal Administration**) closes for new proof-of-coverage submissions on **July 21, 2026** — a hard deadline with no extension authority under the settlement agreement (__[JND Legal Administration — needs verification](https://jndla.com)__).

### Visa / Mastercard Interchange Fee MDL — Fee Remand Briefing (E.D.N.Y.)

In **In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation** (MDL No. 1720, E.D.N.Y.), the Second Circuit's November 2025 ruling vacating the **$5.54 billion** Rule 23(b)(3) class settlement on class notice grounds sent the case back to Judge Margo K. Defense counsel at **Wilmer Cutler Pickering Hale and Dorr LLP** has previewed an argument that the supplemental notice costs — estimated at **$28–35 million** — should be paid from the existing escrow rather than from Visa's and Mastercard's operating budgets, a question that will require further briefing. Separately, the Rule 23(b)(1) injunctive relief class remains in place; the network rule changes limiting surcharging remain operative regardless of the (b)(3) fee dispute (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

### Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — ~$218 Billion Exposure (9th Cir. / AAA)

The **Keller Postman** campaign against Google LLC on behalf of digital advertisers alleging anticompetitive manipulation of real-time bidding auctions remains the largest mass-arbitration proceeding in dollar terms, with lead counsel publicizing aggregate claimed damages of approximately **$218 billion** across more than **90,000 individual demands** filed with the **American Arbitration Association (AAA)** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Google's threshold challenge — that the AAA's mass-arbitration supplementary rules are procedurally unconscionable as applied to its advertiser terms of service — was rejected by the Northern District of California (No. 5:24-cv-02451-EKL, Judge Eumi K. Lee) in an April 14, 2026 order compelling arbitration and denying Google's motion to enjoin AAA administration (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Google noticed its appeal to the **Ninth Circuit** (No. 26-1847) on May 5, 2026, arguing that the district court applied an incorrect standard under **FAA § 4** for compelling arbitration under a mass-proceedings protocol not contemplated by the original arbitration clause. Briefing is as follows: Google's opening brief is due **July 14, 2026**; Keller Postman's response is due **September 8, 2026**. In parallel, the AAA has begun administering bellwether selections under its **2024 Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules (Rule M-1 through M-17)**; Google has sought a stay of AAA proceedings pending appeal, which the AAA has declined to grant as an administrative matter. The practical result is that arbitrator appointments are proceeding — slowly — for an initial cohort of **500 bellwether demands**, with initial evidentiary hearing dates being set for Q1 2027. Fee exposure under Google's advertiser terms (AAA administrative fees on a per-case basis, before cost-shifting) could reach **$45–60 million** based on current demand count, a dynamic that historically accelerates settlement posture (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

### Apple App Store Mass Arbitration — Ninth Circuit Enforceability Dispute

The **Keller Postman / Apple App Store** mass-arbitration track (consolidated demands against Apple Inc. alleging monopolistic App Store commissions under **Sherman Act § 2** and **California Cartwright Act**) has generated a parallel Ninth Circuit dispute. Following the N.D. Cal. ruling in *In re Apple App Store Developer Antitrust Litigation* (No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR, Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), which enforced Apple's arbitration clause but expressed skepticism about mass-joinder batching, **Apple** filed a mandamus petition (No. 26-70245, 9th Cir.) arguing that Judge Gonzalez Rogers erred in permitting simultaneous bellwether selection from among **32,000+ demands** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The Ninth Circuit denied the mandamus petition on **May 22, 2026**, holding that management of mass arbitration batching falls within a district court's supervisory power under **FAA § 4** without reaching the merits of Apple's cost-shifting argument. That ruling is now being cited by Keller Postman as persuasive authority in the Google appeal. Apple's substantive arbitration track continues before **JAMS** arbitrators; the parties have agreed to a **12-demand pilot cohort** with initial hearings scheduled in **August 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### Meta Platforms — Arbitration Waiver Track (N.D. Cal.)

In *Hodge v. Meta Platforms, Inc.* (No. 3:23-cv-04883-JST, N.D. Cal., Judge Jon S. Tigar), approximately **28,000 plaintiffs** who opted out of Meta's arbitration clause via the **JAMS waiver track** (following Meta's 2022 terms-of-service revision) are proceeding as a consolidated pre-class action. Judge Tigar certified a **limited issues class** on May 6, 2026 under Rule 23(c)(4) with respect to whether Meta's algorithmic amplification of harmful content constitutes an unfair business practice under **California Business & Professions Code § 17200** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). The certified issues class — which does not include damages — is significant because it allows common proof on the liability gateway question without requiring individualized damages showings at the class stage. Meta has already petitioned the Ninth Circuit for **Rule 23(f)** review (No. 26-80068); briefing on the petition is fully submitted and a ruling is expected before **June 30, 2026**. If the Ninth Circuit denies 23(f) review, the issues class proceeds to summary-judgment briefing on a schedule that has the liability question resolved by **April 2027** at the earliest. The plaintiffs' steering committee — led by **Motley Rice LLC** and **Social Media Victims Law Center** — has separately filed a companion action in the **MDL coordination motion** before the JPML, seeking transfer of related suits to the N.D. Cal. docket (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__).

### Bartz v. Anthropic — AI Copyright Class Action (N.D. Cal.)

In **Bartz v. Anthropic PBC** (No. 3:24-cv-05417-WHA, N.D. Cal., Judge William H. Alsup), the parties have now completed initial fact discovery on the threshold question of what text corpora were used to train **Claude 1.0 through Claude 3.5** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Judge Alsup's April 30, 2026 case management order set the following upcoming schedule: (i) **expert designation deadline: June 23, 2026**; (ii) **expert discovery close: September 5, 2026**; (iii) **class certification motion due: October 15, 2026**. Plaintiffs — represented by **Susman Godfrey LLP** and **Joseph Saveri Law Firm** — are expected to move for certification of a **17 U.S.C. § 106** reproduction class and a separate **§ 1202 (CMI removal)** class; the two proposed classes correspond to the two principal copyright theories in the operative complaint. Anthropic's answer, filed April 4, 2026, raised a **fair use** affirmative defense under **17 U.S.C. § 107** and argued that training use constitutes a transformative purpose as a matter of law — an argument Anthropic plans to press at the summary-judgment stage. The damages theory advanced by plaintiffs projects **statutory damages of $750 per infringed work** under **17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(1)** across an estimated corpus of **400,000+ works**, implying theoretical maximum exposure exceeding **$300 million** before any multiplication for willful infringement (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). Separately, the parallel **Concord Music Group v. Anthropic** copyright case (No. 3:23-cv-01092-BHL, M.D. Tenn., Judge Brian H. Lauck) is tracking similar discovery timelines; a coordination order between the two courts has not been sought but informal case-management coordination continues between counsel.

### Amazon Seller Mass Arbitration — JAMS Administration (W.D. Wash.)

The **Amazon third-party seller mass-arbitration campaign** — driven by approximately **9,000 demands** filed with **JAMS** by claimants represented by **Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP**, alleging that Amazon's fulfillment and buy-box algorithms violate **Sherman Act § 1** and state consumer protection statutes — is proceeding on a parallel track to the advertiser campaigns (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Amazon's threshold motion to compel consolidation in arbitration (seeking a single consolidated proceeding rather than 9,000 individual cases) was denied by JAMS Case Manager ruling on **April 28, 2026**, applying the **2024 JAMS Mass Arbitration Procedures (Rule 16)**. Amazon then filed in the Western District of Washington (No. 2:26-cv-00731-LK, Judge Lauren King) to vacate the JAMS procedural ruling under **FAA § 10(a)(4)** (arbitrator exceeding authority), but Judge King dismissed the petition on **May 30, 2026**, holding that mid-proceeding procedural rulings by JAMS case managers are not "awards" subject to § 10 vacatur and that Amazon's challenge was premature (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The ruling has immediate practical importance: it prevents defendants from using § 10 petitions as a delaying mechanism at the case-management stage of mass arbitrations, a tactic that had been deployed with some success in prior proceedings. Amazon has 30 days to appeal (deadline: **June 30, 2026**).
