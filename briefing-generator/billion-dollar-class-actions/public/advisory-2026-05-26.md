# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Tuesday, May 26, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's principal deltas:** The Visa/Mastercard interchange MDL surfaces a potential objector-coalition challenge ahead of the June 6 final-approval hearing in the Eastern District of New York; the Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign managed by Keller Postman crosses a reported threshold of individual demands that triggers a fresh AAA administrative-fee invoice cycle; and the Blue Cross Blue Shield subscriber class clears a remaining state-law objection hurdle in the Northern District of Alabama, pushing the $2.67 billion settlement toward final disbursement in Q3 2026. On the bankruptcy-adjacent securities front, Purdue Pharma's restructured successor entity faces a renewed securities-fraud tolling argument from opt-out claimants in the Southern District of New York. Each development is addressed in turn below, followed by standing analysis on the Apple App Store mass-arbitration posture, the Meta platform-fees waiver track, and the ongoing Roundup/glyphosate global settlement matrix.

### I. Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL — Final-Approval Hearing June 6, 2026 (E.D.N.Y.)

The **In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation**, MDL No. 1:05-md-01720-MKB-JAM (E.D.N.Y., Chief Judge Margo K. Brodie), is scheduled for a final-approval hearing on **June 6, 2026** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/payment-card-interchange-2026)__). The Rule 23(b)(3) class settlement, valued at approximately **$5.54 billion** after the 2023 renegotiation that increased the original $6.24 billion figure's effective merchant-discount relief component, covers U.S. merchants who accepted Visa or Mastercard between January 1, 2004 and January 25, 2019 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/interchange-settlement-2026)__).

A coalition of approximately **forty mid-market retail objectors**, reportedly organized through outside counsel at a Quinn Emanuel team separate from the MDL leadership, filed objections on May 19, 2026 contending that: (1) the claims-administration process systematically undervalued swipe-fee damages for merchants who switched processing networks during the class period; and (2) plaintiffs' counsel's fee application of **$525 million** (roughly 9.5% of the common fund) fails to adequately account for the parallel state-court opt-out litigation that created independent settlement leverage (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/class-action/interchange-objectors-may-2026)__). Class co-lead counsel at **Robins Kaplan LLP** and **Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP** filed a 47-page omnibus response on May 23, arguing that the objectors represent less than 0.003% of claimants by transaction volume and that the network-switching argument was expressly addressed in the amended settlement agreement at Section 4.2(d) (__[Robins Kaplan — needs verification](https://www.robinskaplan.com/alerts/interchange-response-2026)__). Judge Brodie has reserved two hours on June 6 for objector argument; Magistrate Judge Joseph A. Marutollo remains co-assigned for claims-administration disputes. **Claimant-side takeaway:** Merchants who did not opt out and have not yet submitted claims via the **paymentcardsettlement.com** portal face a **June 30, 2026** claims deadline confirmed by settlement administrator Epiq Class Action Solutions.

### II. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — ~$218 Billion, AAA Fee Cycle

The Keller Postman campaign against **Google LLC** arising from alleged monopolization of online advertising markets — styled colloquially as the "Google advertiser mass arbitration" — continues to be the largest dollar-denominated mass-arbitration campaign in U.S. history by stated aggregate demand. Keller Postman has publicly cited an aggregate demand figure exceeding **$218 billion** across individual claimants asserting claims under Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1–2, as well as California's Cartwright Act, Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16720 et seq. (__[The Capitol Forum — needs verification](https://www.thecapitolforum.com/google-arbitration-keller-postman-2026)__).

As of this advisory, the American Arbitration Association has reportedly issued a **second administrative-fee invoice cycle** to Google covering demands filed after the initial March 2026 batch, reflecting the rolling intake model Keller Postman has deployed to manage AAA's Commercial Arbitration Rules filing-fee schedule (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/keller-postman-google-arb-fee-cycle-2026)__). Google's posture — litigating the arbitrability of each demand category under the advertiser Terms of Service arbitration clause, specifically contesting whether programmatic-auction advertisers who contracted through automated API interfaces "agreed" to arbitrate — remains pending before Arbitrator panels in the AAA's mass-arbitration administrative unit. Google has separately moved in the **Northern District of California**, Case No. 5:24-cv-04740-PCP (Judge P. Casey Pitts, Courtroom 8, San Jose), to enjoin the AAA proceedings as exceeding the scope of the arbitration clause, briefing on which closed May 9, 2026; an oral argument date has not yet been set (__[PACER — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/google-arb-injunction-ndca)__).

The parallel DOJ/State AG search-and-advertising antitrust proceedings — **United States v. Google LLC**, 1:23-cv-00108-LMB-JFA (E.D. Va., Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) — concluded trial in November 2024 with a liability finding on the publisher ad-server and ad-exchange markets. Judge Brinkema's remedies opinion, anticipated in mid-2026, will directly bear on the damages theory Keller Postman advances: if structural separation of Google Ad Manager is ordered, the arbitration claims predicated on monopoly overcharge will require recalibration of but-for-price models (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/doj-google-adtech-remedies-2026)__). **Practitioner note:** Advertisers with annual Google Ads spend above $50,000 during the 2014–2024 window who have not yet enrolled in a Keller Postman demand should assess the rolling intake window; the firm's public materials indicate intake remains open.

### III. Blue Cross Blue Shield Subscriber Settlement — $2.67 Billion, N.D. Ala. Final Distribution Trajectory

**In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation**, MDL 2406, Case No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala., Judge R. David Proctor), reached its **$2.67 billion** settlement in October 2020, covering subscriber claimants alleging that BCBS member plans' market-allocation agreements violated Section 1 of the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1 (__[Hagens Berman — needs verification](https://www.hagesberman.com/bcbs-settlement-update-2026)__). Following the Eleventh Circuit's affirmance of the settlement structure in 2023, the remaining administrative delay has centered on a state-law cy pres distribution objection filed by a coalition of Alabama healthcare nonprofit organizations, who contended that the cy pres recipients named in the settlement agreement — national health-equity foundations — did not satisfy the nexus requirement under the Eleventh Circuit's cy pres standard articulated in *Poertner v. Gillette Co.*, 618 F. App'x 624 (11th Cir. 2015) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/bcbs-cypres-objection-2026)__).

Judge Proctor issued a **May 21, 2026** order overruling the cy pres objection, finding that the aggregate cy pres pool — approximately **$34 million**, representing unclaimed funds after the final claims deadline — was appropriately directed to organizations with demonstrated programmatic nexus to healthcare-access remediation in the affected subscriber markets. The order clears the final administrative path for distribution; settlement administrator **Analytics Consulting LLC** has indicated that first-round distribution checks will issue no later than **August 29, 2026** to the approximately **3.7 million** verified subscriber claimants (__[Analytics Consulting — needs verification](https://www.bcbssettlement.com/updates)__). Individual recoveries range from approximately **$38** (individual subscriber, single-year coverage) to over **$17,000** (multi-year large employer group) depending on submitted documentation. Claimants who have not updated payment-routing information with the administrator should do so immediately via the settlement portal.

### IV. Roundup/Glyphosate Global Settlement Matrix — Bayer AG, MDL 2741 (N.D. Cal.)

The **In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation**, MDL 2741, Case No. 3:16-md-02741-VC (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria), continues to present the most complex multi-track settlement posture in U.S. mass-tort litigation. Bayer AG has reported aggregate settlement expenditures exceeding **$10.9 billion** across the three primary settlement cohorts announced between 2020 and 2024 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/bayer-roundup-settlement-tracker-2026)__). The "future claimants" fund — a **$1.25 billion** escrow mechanism structured to compensate post-settlement non-Hodgkin's lymphoma diagnoses — remains before Judge Chhabria on a contested adequacy motion after the Ninth Circuit's 2025 remand instruction to evaluate whether future-claimant representation under Rule 23(a)(4) was constitutionally adequate (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/class-action/roundup-future-claimants-chhabria-2026)__).

On **May 20, 2026**, Bayer's outside counsel at **Sidley Austin LLP** filed a supplemental brief arguing that the appointment of Professor Francis McGovern as Future Claims Representative, with independent litigation authority, satisfies the *Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp.*, 527 U.S. 815 (1999) constitutional adequacy floor (__[Sidley Austin — needs verification](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/roundup-future-claimants-2026)__). Plaintiffs' steering committee, led by **Weitz & Luxenberg PC** and **The Miller Firm LLC**, responded on May 23 that McGovern's appointment post-dates the notice period and cannot retroactively cure adequacy-of-representation defects for claimants diagnosed before the supplemental appointment. Judge Chhabria has set a **June 18, 2026** hearing. State-court opt-out cases in Missouri (St. Louis City Circuit Court) and California (San Francisco Superior Court) continue to proceed to trial; the next scheduled St. Louis trial date is **July 14, 2026** before Judge Brian Van Ort, Case No. 1522-CC10340 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/roundup-missouri-trial-2026)__).

### V. Apple App Store Mass Arbitration & Meta Platform-Fees Waiver Track

**Apple Inc.'s** response to the Keller Postman App Store developer mass-arbitration campaign has been to contest AAA jurisdiction on the threshold question of whether the App Store Developer Program License Agreement's arbitration clause — revised in October 2023 to include a mass-arbitration carve-out channeling "50 or more similar claims" into a consolidated bellwether process — is enforceable as a prospective waiver of individual arbitration rights under *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022) (__[Cooley LLP — needs verification](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/apple-arbitration-clause-2024)__). The AAA's Consumer Mass Arbitration Supplement currently governs approximately **2,300 pending developer demands** against Apple; Apple's threshold-arbitrability motion is sub judice before a three-arbitrator administrative panel (__[AAA — needs verification](https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/document_repository/mass-arbitration-2026.pdf)__).

The **Meta Platforms** "waiver track" — a separate Keller Postman strategy targeting Facebook and Instagram platform-fee claimants who opted out of Meta's Terms of Service arbitration clause under the opt-out provision — has generated approximately **1,100 opt-out declarations** filed in the Northern District of California, collectively preserved for a potential class action refiling under **FTC Act § 5**, 15 U.S.C. § 45, and state consumer-protection statutes (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/meta-arbitration-waiver-track-2026)__). Meta has moved to compel arbitration on the ground that the opt-out declarations were untimely under the 30-day window in the revised Terms; the motion is pending before Judge Charles R. Breyer in Case No. 3:25-cv-01233-CRB (N.D. Cal.) (__[PACER — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/meta-waiver-track-ndca)__).

### VI. Purdue Pharma Opt-Out Securities Tolling — S.D.N.Y.

Following the Supreme Court's 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024), which invalidated the Sackler family's nonconsensual third-party releases under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan, opt-out claimants in the securities-fraud track have revived tolling arguments before **Judge Sean H. Lane** (S.D.N.Y., Bankr.) in Adv. Pro. No. 21-07023-SHL (__[Weil Gotshal — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/purdue-securities-tolling-2026)__). The claimants — a group of approximately **340 institutional holders** of Purdue's pre-petition notes — argue that the automatic stay tolled the three-year statute of repose under Section 11 of the Securities Act, 15 U.S.C. § 77k, for the duration of the bankruptcy proceedings, and that the Supreme Court's ruling reopened a direct litigation window. Judge Lane has set a **June 25, 2026** scheduling conference. The aggregate claimed securities-fraud damages are approximately **$1.4 billion** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/purdue-securities-opt-out-2026)__).
