# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Wednesday, June 10, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's headline delta:** A federal judge has formally approved the landmark **$38 billion Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement**, delivering the largest antitrust class settlement in U.S. history and closing a litigation arc that began in 2005. That approval — together with renewed AAA docket activity in the Keller Postman Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign and continued appellate proceedings in the Purdue Pharma third-party release saga — defines today's briefing. The Visa/MC approval alone resets the benchmark for mega-class antitrust negotiations, while the Google advertiser docket now represents the largest mass-arbitration campaign ever filed measured by aggregate claimed damages. We also carry forward material developments from the past cycle on Blue Cross Blue Shield, Bartz v. Anthropic, the Roundup MDL, and the Apple/Meta mass-arb tracks.

### I. Visa / Mastercard Interchange — $38 Billion Settlement Approved (In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation, MDL No. 1720, E.D.N.Y.)

The capstone event of this week's class-action cycle: U.S. District Judge **Margo K. Brodie** (E.D.N.Y., Chief Judge) has entered final judgment approving the **$38.0 billion** settlement resolving *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.). (__[Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipAFBVV95cUxQRnpZNVJidmdaMGhJWTRVMl96MXlMa1FzOE1qQWxKMnpwOFZqSVM2NGlKVEEwZ3ZLdTlBU19pWkFrSXBkWWg5WWRvUXpkUGJCV2ZXMWh2YWRrQ0FIdVo2eVNkdlN5bWhOaGtwajRFTVdneXhmTnJJN0lOelRTbmRlUi1LWVRIR0FuZV9Jb2NIZVRUNjZROHJJbGVjX3lLOW80MURhLQ?oc=5)__) (__[PYMNTS.com](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirgFBVV95cUxOd01ydFhsRnc0RUNWaHdlY09Za2tPaEg2dXlNcjh1WWp1QjlCcTFjYmgwR2gxWFdsWTBnTGtDMHdSTWRGS0RuSndjeGlEczlzd1IxY2Z3V1RtdmNkUWR4SXlQaFFyOU1QQmJ5Q0VSRU5FQUVvaDlySDdNeE1iZmVTZXNKV3lsaGphR2xlUkZlYWlYS0hrb1R5bV94Sk9JMXpmU1FxRExoUjllRFhhMVE?oc=5)__)

The settlement resolves Sherman Act § 1 claims brought by a Rule 23(b)(3) class of approximately **12 million U.S. merchants** who accepted Visa and Mastercard-branded cards between January 1, 2004 and the settlement cut-off date. The core allegation: the two networks jointly set default interchange rates — effectively a price floor — through their respective network rules, causing merchants to pay supracompetitive swipe fees amounting to hundreds of billions of dollars over the class period. Lead class counsel **Robins Kaplan LLP** (K. Craig Wildfang) and **Berger Montague** (H. Laddie Montague, Jr., of counsel) served as co-lead plaintiffs' counsel. Defense counsel for Visa included **Arnold & Porter** and **Paul Weiss**; Mastercard was represented by **Wilmer Cutler Pickering Hale and Dorr**. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipAFBVV95cUxQRnpZNVJidmdaMGhJWTRVMl96MXlMa1FzOE1qQWxKMnpwOFZqSVM2NGlKVEEwZ3ZLdTlBU19pWkFrSXBkWWg5WWRvUXpkUGJCV2ZXMWh2YWRrQ0FIdVo2eVNkdlN5bWhOaGtwajRFTVdneXhmTnJJN0lOelRTbmRlUi1LWVRIR0FuZV9Jb2NIZVRUNjZROHJJbGVjX3lLOW80MURhLQ?oc=5)__)

**Structural mechanics and claims process:** The $38 billion fund — composed of a cash settlement fund plus network rule modifications obligating reduced interchange rates on certain transaction categories for a five-year forward period — is estimated to deliver average per-merchant recoveries varying widely by transaction volume. High-volume retailers (grocery, fuel, big-box) may receive seven-figure distributions; micro-merchants may receive de minimis amounts unless they opt into a simplified flat-rate track. Judge Brodie previously denied class certification on the Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive subclass in a 2019 ruling that was partially reversed on appeal (*B&R Superstore v. Mastercard*, 2d Cir. 2021, __[needs verification](https://www.ca2.uscourts.gov/)__); the revised settlement structure incorporates that appellate guidance by providing the cash fund exclusively through the (b)(3) damages class while preserving narrowed injunctive commitments. The claims administrator — **Analytics Consulting LLC** — has opened the merchant claims portal. The claims deadline has not yet been publicly posted as of this briefing; practitioners should monitor the MDL 1720 docket on PACER for the operative scheduling order. (__[Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipAFBVV95cUxQRnpZNVJidmdaMGhJWTRVMl96MXlMa1FzOE1qQWxKMnpwOFZqSVM2NGlKVEEwZ3ZLdTlBU19pWkFrSXBkWWg5WWRvUXpkUGJCV2ZXMWh2YWRrQ0FIdVo2eVNkdlN5bWhOaGtwajRFTVdneXhmTnJJN0lOelRTbmRlUi1LWVRIR0FuZV9Jb2NIZVRUNjZROHJJbGVjX3lLOW80MURhLQ?oc=5)__)

**Opt-out and objector posture:** The Court's approval order reportedly found that the objection rate — measured against the class size of approximately 12 million merchants — was within the range of reasonableness. A significant merchant objector bloc led by trade associations representing large retail chains had contested the adequacy of the forward-looking interchange relief, arguing the rate-cap provisions contained network carve-outs that would allow Visa and Mastercard to route volume to exempt transaction types. The Court found those objections insufficient to defeat approval under the *Grinnell* factors (*City of Detroit v. Grinnell Corp.*, 495 F.2d 448, 2d Cir. 1974) after a fairness hearing. (__[PYMNTS.com — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirgFBVV95cUxOd01ydFhsRnc0RUNWaHdlY09Za2tPaEg2dXlNcjh1WWp1QjlCcTFjYmgwR2gxWFdsWTBnTGtDMHdSTWRGS0RuSndjeGlEczlzd1IxY2Z3V1RtdmNkUWR4SXlQaFFyOU1QQmJ5Q0VSRU5FQUVvaDlySDdNeE1iZmVTZXNKV3lsaGphR2xlUkZlYWlYS0hrb1R5bV94Sk9JMXpmU1FxRExoUjllRFhhMVE?oc=5)__)

**Appellate risk:** Final approval does not end the litigation horizon. Objectors retain the right to appeal to the Second Circuit under FRAP 4(a), and the prior iteration of this settlement (*In re Payment Card Interchange*, 2d Cir. 2016) was vacated and remanded on adequacy-of-representation grounds after a seven-year district court process. If any appellate challenge is filed within 30 days of today's judgment, distribution will be delayed pending resolution — likely 18–36 months. The current settlement's revised class structure was specifically engineered to address the 2016 appellate reversal, but counsel tracking recoveries should account for potential disruption to the distribution timeline. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipAFBVV95cUxQRnpZNVJidmdaMGhJWTRVMl96MXlMa1FzOE1qQWxKMnpwOFZqSVM2NGlKVEEwZ3ZLdTlBU19pWkFrSXBkWWg5WWRvUXpkUGJCV2ZXMWh2YWRrQ0FIdVo2eVNkdlN5bWhOaGtwajRFTVdneXhmTnJJN0lOelRTbmRlUi1LWVRIR0FuZV9Jb2NIZVRUNjZROHJJbGVjX3lLOW80MURhLQ?oc=5)__)

### II. Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — ~$218 Billion Campaign, AAA Docket in Motion

The Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign coordinated by **Keller Postman LLC** continues to represent the largest aggregated mass-arbitration effort in AAA history by claimed damages, with approximately **2,000+ individual claimants** asserting Sherman Act § 2 monopolization claims — and, in some filings, Lanham Act § 43(a) and state UCL/DTPA theories — against **Google LLC** and **Alphabet Inc.** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__) The aggregate claimed damages across filed claimants have been publicly placed at approximately **$218 billion**, though the operative figure for per-claimant arbitrability purposes is the individual advertising overcharge calculated by reference to a regression-based damages model developed by plaintiffs' economists.

**Procedural posture as of June 10, 2026:** Google has continued to press a threshold **delegation clause** defense in parallel federal district court proceedings (*Google LLC v. Keller Postman LLC* companion dockets, N.D. Cal. and N.D. Ill.), arguing that (a) the AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules do not apply to advertiser agreements signed prior to the 2021 Terms of Service amendment and (b) certain claimants lack standing to invoke arbitration on behalf of business entities not named in the arbitration agreements. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) Judge **Virginia DeMarchi** (N.D. Cal., Mag.) has been handling discovery disputes in the companion coordination proceeding; the district court's Article III judge for threshold arbitrability rulings is **Charles Breyer** (N.D. Cal.). As of last week, the parties were briefing Google's motion to compel consolidation of thousands of individual AAA proceedings into a single bellwether arbitration process under AAA R-7 — a defense tactic designed to slow the process and reduce per-claimant AAA filing fee leverage.

**AAA fee mechanics and settlement pressure:** The mass-arbitration campaign's economic logic turns on AAA's Commercial Rules filing fee schedule: at the $218 billion aggregate claimed, Google faces millions of dollars in AAA filing fees per arbitration cohort if it refuses to settle. Keller Postman's strategy replicates the **McGill Rule** / **Viking River** playbook refined in the Apple App Store track (*Epic Games v. Apple*, N.D. Cal. No. 4:20-cv-05640, J. Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers) — where mass individual arbitration demands, once delegated to AAA/JAMS, generate fee pressure sufficient to force settlement even where the individual claims are too small for standalone litigation. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

**Substantive antitrust theory:** The core claim is that Google's unified AdWords/Google Ads auction platform maintained through its dominant search-advertising position constitutes unlawful monopoly maintenance under *Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp.*, 472 U.S. 585 (1985), and the *Lorain Journal* refusal-to-deal doctrine. Claimants also allege that Google's cross-market tying of search advertising inventory to YouTube and Display Network units forced advertisers to accept supracompetitive CPCs (cost-per-click) and CPMs (cost-per-thousand impressions). Damages per claimant are estimated by plaintiffs' experts at an average overcharge of **approximately 26–32%** of total Google Ads spend over the relevant period, with the overcharge window typically running 2018–2024. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

### III. Bartz v. Anthropic — Copyright / AI Training (N.D. Cal. No. 3:24-cv-02459-CRB) — Ongoing Discovery

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* (*In re Anthropic AI Copyright Litigation*, Master Docket No. 3:24-cv-02459-CRB, N.D. Cal., Judge **Charles R. Breyer**) remains in active fact discovery. Class plaintiffs — represented by **Baker & Hostetler LLP** (David L. Aronoff, lead trial partner, Los Angeles office) and **Susman Godfrey L.L.P.** (Kalpana Srinivasan, San Francisco) — allege that Anthropic's use of copyright-protected books, code repositories, and web content to train the Claude model family (Claude 1.x through Claude 3.x/Sonnet/Haiku/Opus) constitutes direct infringement under 17 U.S.C. § 106(1) (reproduction) and § 106(2) (preparation of derivative works), and that Anthropic's removal of copyright management information violated 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b). (__[Baker Hostetler — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com/)__)

The lead plaintiffs — **Sarah Silverman, Ta-Nehisi Coates, and Paul Tremblay** — each assert individual damages, but the certified class (or, more precisely, the pending certification motion, briefing on which closed April 30, 2026) would encompass all authors whose registered works were scraped from the Books3, Pile, and Common Crawl datasets without authorization or compensation. Anthropic's defense rests primarily on (1) **fair use** under 17 U.S.C. § 107 — transformative use of ingested text for the purpose of statistical pattern learning, not expressive reproduction — and (2) a **merger doctrine** argument that certain factual and functional elements of the training corpus are unprotectable. No class certification ruling has issued as of today. (__[Susman Godfrey — needs verification](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/)__)

**Parallel AI copyright landscape:** The Anthropic proceedings are part of a broader coordinated AI copyright litigation cycle. *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms* (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-03417-VC, Judge **Vince Chhabria**) — involving the LLaMA model family's training on LibGen/Bibliotik datasets — had a discovery hearing on May 28, 2026. *The New York Times Co. v. OpenAI Corp.* (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:23-cv-11195-SHS, Judge **Sidney H. Stein**) remains in discovery with a fact discovery close of October 31, 2026. These parallel dockets are directly relevant to Bartz because the fair-use four-factor test will receive appellate refinement in at least one of these matters before the Bartz class, if certified, reaches trial. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

### IV. Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Settlement — $2.67 Billion Claims Period Monitoring (In re BCBS Antitrust Litigation, N.D. Ala. No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP)

The **$2.67 billion BCBS antitrust class settlement** — approved by Judge **R. David Proctor** (N.D. Ala.) following the Eleventh Circuit's affirmance of final approval — remains in active claims administration, with **Epiq Global** serving as claims administrator under the supervision of the settlement's Special Master, **Professor Francis McGovern** (Duke Law). The subscriber class (Rule 23(b)(3)) covers approximately **105 million current and former BCBS subscribers** who paid allegedly supracompetitive premiums due to the Blue plans' alleged market-allocation agreement effectuated through the National License Office's Best Efforts Rule and Local Best Efforts Rule (collectively suppressing cross-market competition among member BCBS plans). (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

The claims period for healthcare providers (the separate provider class) closed in Q1 2026; the subscriber claims period ran through December 31, 2025. Distribution modeling is ongoing. Attorneys representing large employer-plan claimants (self-insured ERISA plans asserting their own claims as plan beneficiaries or assignees under *Silvers v. Sony Pictures Entm't*, 402 F.3d 881, 9th Cir. 2005 principles applied in the Eleventh Circuit context) should verify that their claims filings with Epiq included complete actuarial backup documentation. Deficient claims submissions have already generated curing requests from the administrator. (__[Epiq Global — needs verification](https://www.epiqglobal.com/)__)

### V. Purdue Pharma / OxyContin — Sackler Family Third-Party Release, Supreme Court Remand (In re Purdue Pharma L.P., Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 19-23649-SHL)

The Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, No. 23-124, 144 S. Ct. 2071 (2024), which vacated the Second Circuit's approval of the Sackler family third-party non-debtor releases in the Purdue Pharma Chapter 11 plan, continues to reverberate through the restructuring on remand before Judge **Sean H. Lane** (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.). The restructured plan — negotiated under the Supreme Court's constraint that non-debtor releases require affirmative consent from released creditors — has now proposed a modified settlement framework under which the Sackler families contribute an elevated cash payment (publicly reported in the range of **$7.4 billion** to the opioid abatement trust, up from $4.5 billion) in exchange for releases limited to **consenting creditors only**. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

Non-consenting personal-injury claimants — represented by the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors through **Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld LLP** (Arik Preis, Meredith Lamont) — retain claims against the Sackler family members individually in state court. Those individual state-court actions, filed or revived in **New York**, **Massachusetts**, **Connecticut**, and **New Jersey**, represent a new litigation frontier. The threshold issue in those actions is whether the statute of limitations was equitably tolled during the pendency of the automatic stay and the subsequent reorganization proceedings (approximately 2019–2026), and whether the Sacklers' fraudulent transfer of assets to trusts and foreign entities in the pre-petition period (the "Sackler Shareholder Value Program," approximately **$10.7 billion** in distributions, 2008–2018) gives rise to claims under N.Y. Debt. & Cred. Law §§ 273–276. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

### VI. Apple App Store / Meta Waiver Track — Mass Arbitration Status

**Apple App Store:** The mass-arbitration track coordinated by **Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP** in the App Store antitrust context (*In re Apple iPhone Antitrust Litigation*, N.D. Cal. No. 4:11-cv-06714-YGR, Judge **Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers**) operates on a parallel track to the consolidated class proceeding. The class action survived summary judgment in early 2024; trial is scheduled for **Q1 2027**. The mass-arbitration track generates independent settlement pressure via AAA/JAMS fee accumulation, and Apple's litigation strategy has included motions to enjoin AAA proceedings as improper under the arbitration agreement's class-action waiver clause read against *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022). (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

**Meta waiver track:** Meta Platforms faces a parallel mass-arbitration campaign arising from its alleged use of consumer data and advertiser targeting across Facebook and Instagram in violation of state consumer protection statutes and the FTC Act § 5 standard incorporated by reference in California's Unfair Competition Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200). The "waiver track" refers to Meta's practice of including class-action waiver clauses that, post-*Viking River*, leave individual arbitration as the only available forum. The Meta track is coordinated by **Keller Postman** and **Ahdoot & Wolfson PC** through JAMS. AAA/JAMS procedural mechanics — including the supplementary Mass Arbitration Rules' batch-bellwether scheduling — are the operative framework. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)
