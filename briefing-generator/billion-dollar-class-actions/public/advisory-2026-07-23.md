### Bartz v. Anthropic — Final Approval Entered; 30-Day Appeal Window Now Running


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment in the case.
 The tracked-case ground truth confirms docket No. 3:24-cv-05417; the most recent docket activity (Entry 681, July 21, 2026) was filed one day after the approval order, consistent with the entry of the judgment itself.

The fee arithmetic is the sharpest analytical data point to carry forward. 
The court denied class counsel's requested 12.5% of the $1.5 billion fund — which would have produced $187.5 million in fees — and instead awarded a 3.75× lodestar multiplier, yielding a total fee payment of $101,561,111, plus approximately $3 million in expense reimbursements and a $18.2 million cost reserve for claims administration.
 
The court determined that "a multiplier of 3.75, for a total fee award of $101,561,111 is reasonable,"
 rejecting the 6.92× multiplier sought by counsel as inconsistent with Ninth Circuit norms. 
$122 million of the settlement money goes toward attorneys and litigation costs in the aggregate.
 Every dollar trimmed from fees remains in the fund for authors.

On claims economics: 
class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works, or approximately 0.03% of the total number of covered works.
 
The settlement pays approximately $3,100 per author of more than 480,000 works, with claims filed covering 440,490 works downloaded from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror by Anthropic.
 That 91.3% claims rate is extraordinary for a class action of this scale — it compresses the unclaimed remainder almost to zero, which matters for anyone monitoring redistribution risk.


The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," found it was "negotiated at arm's length," and overruled all 53 objections filed against it.
 
The court emphasized that the settlement "provides substantial benefits to the Class in light of the novel claims asserted," and that taking the claims to trial could have left the authors "no recourse."
 
The court rejected the class representatives' request for $50,000 service awards each, reducing the amount to $15,000 per representative, while finding that Andrea Bartz, Kirk Wallace Johnson, and Charles Graeber "have adequately represented the Class."


On the structural non-monetary terms: 
every dollar not paid in fees stays in the non-reversionary fund for authors, and if money remains after all valid claims are paid, the parties anticipate redistributing it to class members; any final balance that is economically infeasible to distribute will go — subject to court approval — to charitable purposes.


The 30-day appeal window opened July 20. Given that only 53 objections were filed and all were overruled, appellate exposure appears low, but any objector who filed a timely notice of appeal would stay distribution absent a bond motion. 
The Authors Guild anticipates the distribution process will begin quickly, though no specific commencement date has been announced.
 Claims administrators should be tracking disbursement mechanics now.

One doctrinal note for the advisory record: 
the underlying case turned on Anthropic's source, not its use — Judge Alsup ruled that training an AI model on copyrighted text counts as fair use, but that ruling did not excuse how Anthropic obtained the books, having built its training library from books it purchased (permissible) and books it downloaded from pirate sites like Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror (the basis for liability).
 That fair-use holding — favorable to AI defendants — survives and will be relied upon in parallel AI copyright litigation.

### J&J Talc MDL 2738 — Dismissal Motion Fully Briefed; Court Holds the Ball

The J&J talc MDL (In re Johnson & Johnson Talcum Powder Products Marketing, Sales Practices and Products Liability Litigation, 
MDL No. 2738, before Judge Michael A. Shipp in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey
) has arrived at its most consequential procedural moment since the bankruptcy gambit collapsed.

The sequence: 
J&J's dismissal argument is based on the withdrawal of two specific causation experts — Dr. Daniel Clarke-Pearson and Dr. Judith Wolf — after a hearing before special master retired U.S. District Judge Freda Wolfson.
 
Plaintiffs formally withdrew the experts on June 8. In its filing, J&J is seeking dismissal not only of the six scheduled bellwether cases but also of all remaining ovarian cancer claims in the MDL.
 
According to J&J, the experts acknowledged during recent court hearings that there's no reliable scientific method to determine whether talc was a substantial contributing factor in a specific woman's ovarian cancer.


The PSC's response landed on schedule. 
The Plaintiffs' Steering Committee requested a one-week extension to respond to the court's order, asking in a letter sent to the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey on July 14 for leave until July 22.
 
The court granted that extension on July 15.
 The PSC's brief is therefore now before Judge Shipp as of this morning.


Counsel for those 67,000 victims responded forcefully in a June 29 filing, arguing that J&J had "swung for the fences" in an attempt to avoid remand orders that could set the stage for talc trials across the country.
 
Plaintiffs' attorneys Michelle Parfitt and Christopher Placitella characterized J&J's motion: "Defendants' claim is as breathtaking as it is meritless."


The case count continues to grow despite the dismissal motion. 
Even as J&J pushes for the talc MDL to be dismissed, new lawsuits continue to pour in — as of the start of July, there are 68,435 talc ovarian cancer lawsuits active in federal court, meaning over 400 new lawsuits were filed in just the preceding few weeks.
 That trajectory is notable: 
following Judge Christopher Lopez's rejection of the proposed $8 billion bankruptcy settlement, all MDL claims are proceeding in court rather than through Chapter 11.


State-court verdicts cut both ways as bellwether context. 
In the past year alone, juries have awarded a $1.5 billion verdict in Baltimore, a $966 million verdict in Los Angeles (later reduced to $16 million), a $40 million ovarian cancer verdict in Los Angeles, a $32 million mesothelioma verdict in Los Angeles, and a $65.5 million mesothelioma verdict in Minnesota — all finding that J&J's talc products were a substantial factor in causing plaintiffs' cancers.
 
Bloomberg Intelligence analysts continue to estimate ultimate exposure as high as $11 billion.


The expert-withdrawal gambit is tactically aggressive but structurally risky for J&J: 
earlier this year, special master Wolfson had recommended that all 39 experts in the talc MDL could testify, though she did recommend that some methods used by one general causation expert be struck.
 Judge Shipp's ruling on the dismissal motion will set the bellwether trial trajectory. If he denies dismissal, remand orders to home jurisdictions likely follow — triggering the very dynamic J&J sought to forestall.

### Securities Class Actions — 2026 Settlement Pace at Six-Year High; AI-Related Filings Surge


The value of class action settlements is on track to reach a six-year high of $4.4 billion, fueled in part by a handful of "mega settlements" and other high-dollar deals, according to a Cornerstone Research report released Wednesday, July 22, 2026.


The H1 filing data reinforces the macro trend. 
Federal securities class action filings rose to 118 cases in the first half of 2026, with AI-related litigation reaching a five-year high and average settlement values climbing to $54 million — the highest in a decade.
 
The average settlement value of $54 million in H1 2026 represents a 32% increase relative to the 2025 inflation-adjusted average of $41 million.
 
Market manipulation cases, including pump-and-dump schemes, surged dramatically, while cryptocurrency-related filings fell to their lowest level in five years.


The mid-year class action landscape across all verticals: 
the top-5 settlements at the mid-year point in 2026 across key categories were: $34.875 billion in antitrust, $8.609 billion in products liability/mass tort, $4.25 billion in government enforcement actions, $1.979 billion in securities fraud, and $1.535 billion in consumer fraud.
 The antitrust figure reflects the long tail of the Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement and similar mega-fund distributions still running their claims cycles.

### Mass Arbitration — Structural Watch

No new docket-confirmed developments in the Google advertiser mass arbitration (Keller Postman, ~$218B in claimed exposure) or Apple App Store mass arbitration tracks since the last advisory. The AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules remain operative architecture for batch processing. The pattern to watch: as *Bartz* demonstrates that high claims-rates and non-reversionary fund structures can deliver per-claimant recovery in the thousands of dollars even in novel-theory litigation, plaintiffs' counsel in Big Tech mass-arbitration campaigns will press for similar structural concessions in any global resolution framework. That is the denominator math that makes these campaigns financially viable at scale — and the reason defendants fight batching and bellwether selection so aggressively.
