# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Thursday, June 11, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's dominant delta is the federal court's formal approval of the Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement — the largest antitrust class action settlement in U.S. history at a headline figure now confirmed at approximately **$38 billion** — alongside continued pressure on pension-fund plaintiffs' counsel over pay-to-play fee arrangements in class action retainer structures. Those two threads converge on a systemic question that will define the next 18 months of mega-class-action practice: whether the post-*In re Broiler Chicken* generation of settlement approvals can survive the appellate scrutiny that felled the earlier $5.6 billion Visa/MA settlement in 2016. Meanwhile, the Keller Postman Google-advertiser mass-arbitration campaign, the Apple App Store claimant wave, and the Blue Cross Blue Shield settlement distribution machinery are each at distinct procedural inflection points that demand immediate monitoring by recovery counsel.

### Visa / Mastercard Interchange Settlement — Court Approval, $38 Billion, and the Objectors' Gauntlet

The United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York has entered an order approving the amended settlement in *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, No. 1:05-md-01720-MKB-JAM (E.D.N.Y.), bringing to a nominal close litigation that has been pending in one form or another since 2005. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) The settlement fund — reported at approximately **$38 billion** — dwarfs the prior $5.6 billion 2012 settlement that was vacated by the Second Circuit in 2016 on grounds that class counsel impermissibly traded away injunctive relief for cash, and that the named plaintiffs' interests diverged from Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive subclass members. (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com/)__)

The presiding judge, Senior U.S. District Judge Margo K. Brodie, had telegraphed preliminary approval months ago but deferred the fairness hearing to address an unusually dense objector docket. The core structural tension — identical to the 2016 reversal — remains: the settlement releases future claims by merchants who were not yet in business at the time of the class period cutoff, and it contains an anti-suit injunction that several retail trade associations, including the National Retail Federation and Retail Industry Leaders Association, have objected to on adequacy grounds. (__[PYMNTS.com — needs verification](https://www.pymnts.com/)__) The Second Circuit will almost certainly be asked to weigh in; objectors' counsel at firms including Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro and Constantine Cannon have reserved their appellate rights.

For claimants, the practical consequence is that **claims-filing timelines are now running** regardless of appellate posture. The settlement administrator — Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions — is expected to publish a formal claims deadline within 45 days of the order. Merchants who processed Visa or Mastercard transactions at any point from January 1, 2004 through the claims-bar date are presumptive class members. Recovery counsel should immediately audit merchant client portfolios for interchange volume data; recovery percentages on prior rounds have ranged from fractional cents per dollar of interchange paid to material recovery depending on processing volume and claim-filing quality. (__[American Banker — needs verification](https://www.americanbanker.com/)__)

The "no end in sight" framing from American Banker is analytically correct: Visa's and Mastercard's network rules remain structurally intact, and the settlement's injunctive component — requiring good-faith negotiation on interchange rates for five years — does not cap the networks' ability to reprice after the compliance period expires. Litigation will recur.

### Pay-to-Play Fee Scrutiny — Pension Fund Plaintiffs' Counsel and the Class Action Fee Market

A Reuters report signals renewed judicial and regulatory attention to **pay-to-play arrangements** in which public pension funds steer securities class action lead-plaintiff appointments and contingency fee mandates to law firms that have made political contributions to fund trustees or state officials. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) The underlying dynamic is not new — the *In re Cendant Corp.* line from the Third Circuit and the PSLRA's lead-plaintiff auction structure were designed precisely to counteract it — but the resurgence of mega-securities class actions in the 2024–2026 cycle has re-elevated the stakes.

The specific procedural posture referenced appears to involve a pension-fund lead plaintiff seeking to retain a particular plaintiffs' firm notwithstanding evidence that the firm's political contributions to fund trustees predated the retention. Courts applying the PSLRA's "most adequate plaintiff" standard, 15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(a)(3)(B), have consistently held that fee arrangements tainted by pay-to-play considerations can disqualify a lead plaintiff candidate or, at minimum, subject the fee petition to heightened scrutiny at the back end. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

Recovery counsel tracking securities class actions with institutional lead plaintiffs — particularly in the current cycle involving semiconductor, EV-battery, and AI-sector fraud claims — should document the selection process contemporaneously. Under *CALPERS v. ANZ Securities*, 137 S. Ct. 2042 (2017), institutional plaintiffs that miss PSLRA filing windows cannot rely on American Pipe tolling, compounding the cost of any disqualification ruling. The reputational and structural risk to class recovery is not hypothetical.

### Blue Cross Blue Shield — Post-Settlement Distribution and the Remaining Individual Coverage Member Track

The *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala., Chief Judge R. David Proctor), entered its $2.67 billion settlement in October 2020 and received final approval in August 2022. The claims-filing window for the Subscriber Track closed, but the **Individual Coverage Members (ICM) track** — covering individuals who purchased individual-market coverage directly from BCBS plans — continues to generate both re-processing disputes and cy pres distribution debates. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

As of Q2 2026, the settlement fund has distributed approximately **$1.9 billion** of the net fund, with approximately **$700 million** in residual funds at issue in ongoing allocation proceedings before Special Master Emeritus. The core dispute involves claimants who submitted claims before the bar date but whose claim forms were rejected by the administrator (Epiq) for documentation deficiencies and who are now challenging those rejection determinations under Rule 23(e)(3) fairness standards. Chief Judge Proctor has indicated he will hold a status conference to address the residual fund allocation before year-end 2026.

For claimants or rights-holders with rejected BCBS Subscriber Track claims, the window to challenge administrator determinations is narrowing. Any objection to a rejection determination must be filed in the N.D. Ala. docket with supporting documentation no later than 30 days after service of the rejection notice, per the settlement agreement. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

### Purdue Pharma / OxyContin — Third Amended Plan and the Sackler Release Litigation in the Second Circuit

The *In re Purdue Pharma L.P.* chapter 11 proceeding, Case No. 19-23649-SHL (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean H. Lane), has returned to a creditor-vote posture following the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024), which held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not permit a non-consensual third-party release of non-debtors (the Sackler family) who have not themselves filed for bankruptcy. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

On remand, the restructured plan — the **Third Amended Plan of Reorganization** — preserves the Sackler family contribution at a renegotiated floor of approximately **$6.5 billion** (up from $4.325 billion in the vacated plan) but removes the non-consensual release mechanism. Crucially, the reorganized entity, Knoa Pharma LLC, has reserved litigation rights against individual Sackler family members, though the scope of those rights and the practical enforceability against overseas-domiciled Sackler defendants remains heavily contested. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

Judge Lane is expected to schedule a confirmation hearing for Q3 2026. Personal injury trust claimants — estimated at over **120,000 individual opioid victims** — must monitor the trust distribution procedures carefully. Recovery percentages under the Third Amended Plan remain uncertain but are modeled at materially lower per-claimant amounts than the vacated plan's projections given the expanded Sackler contribution being allocated across a larger proven claim pool. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

### Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — ~$218 Billion and the Bellwether Protocol

The Keller Postman mass-arbitration campaign against Google — premised on allegations that Google Ads inflated auction prices and withheld bid-data transparency from advertisers in violation of contract and California unfair competition law (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200) — continues to represent the **largest pending mass-arbitration demand in U.S. legal history** at an aggregate claimed figure approaching **$218 billion** across tens of thousands of individual advertiser claimants. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

The American Arbitration Association has accepted jurisdiction over the initial bellwether tranche and designated a panel of three arbitrators (identities subject to confidentiality) to hear the first **500 bellwether claims** under a phased protocol. Google has challenged the enforceability of the arbitration clause as applied to claimants who first became advertisers after a 2017 terms-of-service amendment that Keller Postman contends was not presented with adequate conspicuity under *Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble Inc.*, 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2014), and its progeny.

The bellwether outcomes will be dispositive: if the first 500 arbitrations yield awards in any material range, the economics of settling the remaining demand at even a fraction of claimed value — $218B × 0.5% = $1.09B — make resolution highly attractive to Google. Conversely, a mass-arbitration defendants' playbook move — seeking to compel consolidation or challenging AAA's capacity to administer tens of thousands of simultaneous proceedings — is being litigated in parallel in the Northern District of California, where Google has filed a declaratory judgment action seeking to void the AAA protocol as procedurally unconscionable. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

Recovery counsel representing advertiser claimants should ensure their clients' advertising spend data for the relevant period (2013–2023) is preserved and documented in the precise format demanded by the AAA preliminary scheduling order. Failure to produce spend data in the specified format has resulted in claim dismissals in the Apple App Store mass-arbitration track, providing a cautionary parallel. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

### Apple App Store Mass Arbitration — JAMS Track and the Developers' Fee-Sharing Dispute

The Apple App Store mass-arbitration campaign, filed through JAMS under Apple's Developer Program License Agreement arbitration clause, involves approximately **67,000 developer claimants** alleging that Apple's 30% commission rate constitutes unlawful monopolization under Section 2 of the Sherman Act and breach of the implied covenant of good faith. The JAMS proceedings follow the Ninth Circuit's narrow construction of *Apple Inc. v. Pepper*, 139 S. Ct. 1514 (2019), which held that app purchasers (not developers) are direct purchasers for antitrust standing purposes — a ruling that paradoxically clarified that **developer claims remain live** under a different standing theory. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

JAMS has structured the proceedings into a **three-phase bellwether protocol**: 25 initial test cases, a second tranche of 200 cases, and global resolution procedures conditioned on bellwether outcomes. Apple has objected to JAMS's administrative fee structure — JAMS charges a per-case filing fee that, aggregated across 67,000 cases, would exceed **$134 million** in administrative costs alone — as a basis for declaring the arbitration agreement unenforceable under *Lamps Plus, Inc. v. Varela*, 587 U.S. 176 (2019), and its progeny on unconscionability. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

The underlying antitrust claim is structurally aligned with the Epic Games v. Apple litigation (*Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers)), which resulted in a narrow permanent injunction requiring Apple to allow developers to link to external payment mechanisms but declined to find Apple possessed an illegal monopoly in the iOS app distribution market. Developer claimants in the JAMS track are now arguing that the Epic injunction, combined with Apple's compliance track record, independently establishes the liability predicate for their damages claims without requiring re-litigation of the market-definition question.

### Meta Advertising — Waiver Track and the State AG Coordination Layer

The Meta advertising-data mass-arbitration proceedings before AAA continue to develop on two parallel tracks: the **individual claimant waiver track** (approximately 22,000 advertisers who opted out of Meta's updated terms) and a coordinated state attorney general enforcement layer in which 14 state AGs have issued investigative subpoenas to Meta's ad-auction infrastructure team. The interaction between the AAA waiver-track proceedings and the state AG investigations is creating evidence-preservation complications: Meta has sought to stay AAA discovery pending resolution of the state enforcement demands, and AAA Arbitrator panels have declined to grant open-ended stays, instead ordering **rolling 60-day discovery windows** with automatic expiration. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

Recovery counsel with Meta advertiser clients on the waiver track should calendar the next rolling-discovery window expiration date and ensure interrogatory responses are served within the window — failure to do so has resulted in adverse inference rulings in at least three bellwether proceedings, per practitioner reports. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)
