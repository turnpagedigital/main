### BCBS Antitrust Settlement — Final Distribution & Appellate Tail

The **Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation**, MDL No. 2406 (N.D. Ala., Chief Judge Annemarie Carney Axon, presiding since Judge R. David Proctor's elevation), reached a critical milestone last month when the Eleventh Circuit affirmed, in a published opinion, the district court's overruling of the **Mosley objectors'** challenge to the $2.67 billion settlement fund allocation formula. *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litig.*, No. 22-12786 (11th Cir. May 22, 2026) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The Mosley group had argued that the pro-rata allocation across "Large Group," "Individual," and "Small Group" subscriber classes improperly subordinated Small Group claimants whose premium overcharges, as a percentage of total premium spend, were demonstrably higher than the Large Group cohort. The Eleventh Circuit applied *Camden I Condominium Ass'n v. Dunkle*, 946 F.2d 768 (11th Cir. 1991), holding that the district court's 25.67% fee award (approximately $685.9 million of the gross fund) did not exceed the circuit's "benchmark" and that the allocation formula rested on a rational actuarial basis. With that ruling now final (mandate issued June 2, 2026), claims administrator Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions is processing approximately **5.1 million validated claim forms** against a net distributable fund of roughly **$1.962 billion** after fees, notice costs, and administration expenses (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Distribution checks/ACH transfers are scheduled for Q3 2026 under the operative court order (MDL 2406, ECF No. 4,312). Counsel for any unrepresented Small Group claimant should confirm Epiq's BCBS claim portal submission as of the June 30 deadline; late claims are barred absent extraordinary cause under Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e).

### Visa/Mastercard Interchange — Post-Vacatur Track & Remediation

The **Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation**, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo K. Brodie), remains one of the most consequential pending class actions in U.S. antitrust history. Following the Second Circuit's April 2024 vacatur of the prior $5.54 billion Rule 23(b)(3) settlement on grounds that class counsel labored under a structural conflict of interest in simultaneously representing the (b)(2) injunctive class — *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merch. Disc. Antitrust Litig.*, 827 F.3d 223 (2d Cir. 2016) progeny revisited — the parties resumed mediation before retired Magistrate Judge Gary R. Brown (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). As of the most recent status conference (MDL 1720, ECF No. 9,214, May 29, 2026), the parties reported to Judge Brodie that a **revised term sheet** has been initialed but not yet executed, with the principal open issues being (a) the scope of the "rules changes" injunctive relief binding Visa and Mastercard going forward and (b) the allocation ratio between the over-3.5-million-member merchant class and the class of card-issuing financial institutions. The revised gross fund is understood to be negotiated in the range of **$6.0–$6.5 billion** (__[Wall Street Journal — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__), which would make it the largest antitrust class-action settlement in U.S. history by gross fund size if approved. Judge Brodie has set a July 31, 2026 deadline for the parties to file either a renewed preliminary approval motion or a joint status report explaining why mediation has not produced a final agreement. Merchants who excluded themselves from the prior settlement retain independent claims; counsel evaluating opt-out posture should note that the statute of limitations under 15 U.S.C. § 15b (four years) runs from the date of each allegedly suppressed interchange transaction.

### Roundup MDL & Ninth Circuit Preemption Guidance

The **Roundup Products Liability Litigation**, MDL No. 2741 (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria), continues its bellwether remand program following *Hardeman v. Monsanto Co.*, 997 F.3d 941 (9th Cir. 2021), cert. denied, and the subsequent Supreme Court denial in *Monsanto Co. v. Hardeman*, 142 S. Ct. 2834 (2022). The critical new development is a June 3, 2026 Ninth Circuit panel decision in a consolidated remanded case, *Engilis v. Bayer AG*, No. 24-16043 (9th Cir. June 3, 2026), in which a three-judge panel (Judges Milan D. Smith, Jr., Lawrence VanDyke, and Gabriel Sanchez) held — in a 2-1 decision — that FIFRA's express preemption provision, 7 U.S.C. § 136v(b), preempts **failure-to-warn claims** where the plaintiff cannot establish that EPA's label approval was procured by fraud or material misrepresentation (__[Courthouse News Service — needs verification](https://www.courthousenews.com)__). Judge Sanchez dissented, arguing the majority's standard impermissibly narrows *Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC*, 544 U.S. 431 (2005). This ruling, if it stands or is adopted by other circuits, would significantly reduce Bayer's aggregate exposure on the roughly **54,000 remaining Roundup claims** (down from a peak of ~125,000) and could depress the per-claim settlement value currently averaging approximately **$160,000–$180,000** under the informal resolution program Bayer has been running since its 2020 global settlement framework. MDL plaintiffs' steering committee co-lead counsel Aimee Wagstaff of Andrus Wagstaff and Robin Greenwald of Weitz & Luxenberg have indicated they will seek en banc review (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge Chhabria has stayed all remand proceedings in *Engilis* track cases pending the en banc petition, which must be filed within 45 days of the June 3 decision.

### Purdue Pharma / Sackler Third-Party Release — District Court Confirmation Hearing Set

Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 ruling in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 144 S. Ct. 2071 (2024) — which held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize a plan of reorganization to grant a non-consensual third-party release of non-debtor liability absent consent — the Purdue Pharma bankruptcy (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 19-23649, Judge Sean H. Lane) returned to square one on the Sackler family release mechanics. Judge Lane on April 10, 2026 issued a scheduling order (ECF No. 7,441) setting a **July 14, 2026** confirmation hearing for a Third Amended Plan that replaces the non-consensual Sackler release with a **consent-based contribution structure** under which the Sackler family has agreed to contribute a revised amount of **$6.5 billion** (up from the original $4.5 billion) in exchange for releases from those specific creditors who affirmatively elect the release on the ballot (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The revised plan preserves the **Public Benefit Trust**, the **Personal Injury Trust**, and the **NAS (Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome) Trust**, and introduces a **State/Subdivisions Consent Release Pool** funded at $2.1 billion. Dissenting states led by Connecticut AG William Tong have filed a preliminary objection (ECF No. 7,501, June 4, 2026) arguing the consent mechanism, while formally compliant with *Harrington*, is operationally coercive because claimants who withhold consent receive materially lower distributions from the Personal Injury Trust — raising due process concerns under *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976). Opioid personal-injury claimants with active PI Trust elections should review their ballot carefully before the **June 30, 2026 voting deadline**.

### Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — AAA Batch Protocol Trigger

The **Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign** orchestrated by Keller Postman LLC — which filed approximately **32,000 individual arbitration demands** against Google LLC and its parent Alphabet Inc. in late 2024 and early 2025, seeking aggregate relief estimated by plaintiffs' counsel at **$218 billion** based on alleged monopolization of digital advertising markets under 15 U.S.C. §§ 1-2 and state consumer protection statutes — has now fully entered AAA's **Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules** track following the January 15, 2026 rule amendment (__[Keller Postman — needs verification](https://www.kellerpostman.com)__). Under the amended rules, once more than 25 demands sharing a common respondent and common legal issues are filed, AAA may appoint a **Process Arbitrator** to handle administrative disputes (filing fee allocation, bellwether selection, common-issue briefing schedules) before individual merits arbitrators are assigned. AAA appointed retired federal judge **Layn Phillips** as Process Arbitrator on May 5, 2026. Judge Phillips issued Procedural Order No. 3 on June 1, 2026, establishing a **bellwether cohort of 50 demands** to be selected by random stratified sampling from across advertiser-type categories (search, display, YouTube), with merits arbitrations on the bellwether cohort to commence no earlier than **October 1, 2026** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Google has moved in the N.D. Cal. (No. 3:25-cv-03917, Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers) to enjoin AAA proceedings on grounds that the advertiser agreements' arbitration clauses were modified post-dispute in violation of *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022), and that mass-filing mechanics constitute a "procedural unconscionability" rendering the clauses unenforceable under California Civil Code § 1670.5. Judge Gonzalez Rogers has set the TRO/preliminary injunction hearing for **June 17, 2026** (__[Courthouse News Service — needs verification](https://www.courthousenews.com)__). The outcome of that hearing will materially shape whether the full 32,000-demand docket proceeds on AAA's timeline or is stayed pending district court resolution.

Separately, the **Apple App Store mass-arbitration campaign** (Keller Postman, ~67,000 demands, JAMS administered) remains in the fee-dispute phase following Apple's refusal to pay JAMS mass-arbitration administrative fees estimated at **$119 million**. The N.D. Cal. district court in *Coronavirus Reporter v. Apple Inc.*, consolidated with *Cameron v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:19-cv-03074 (Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), held a status conference on May 20, 2026, at which the court noted it would apply *Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736 (2023), and *Morgan v. Sundance, Inc.*, 596 U.S. 411 (2022), to assess waiver if Apple persists in refusing to fund the arbitral forum (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). A ruling on the waiver/fee-payment motion is expected by **June 20, 2026**.

The **Meta (Facebook) mass-arbitration** track — involving approximately **28,000 privacy-related individual demands** filed through Edelson PC — advanced this week when Meta's motion to consolidate all demands into a single class-arbitration proceeding was denied by JAMS Arbitrator Hon. Daniel Weinstein (ret.) in Case Reference No. 1425037879 on June 3, 2026. Arbitrator Weinstein held that the Meta Terms of Service's arbitration clause does not authorize class-wide arbitration procedures under *Lamps Plus, Inc. v. Varela*, 587 U.S. 176 (2019), but that the mass-filing framework is independently permissible, allowing individual proceedings to proceed on the JAMS mass-arbitration track (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).
