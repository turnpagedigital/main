### Securities Class Actions — 2026 Midyear Data (Bloomberg Law, Aug. 3)


Bloomberg Law published this morning a survey finding that large alleged investor losses and increased AI adoption are driving more than **$2 billion in aggregate settlements** at the midyear mark of 2026, per practitioners surveyed.
 The timing matters: 
investors filed roughly **120 new proposed securities class actions** in the first half of the year, on pace to exceed 2025 filings if sustained through year-end, according to recent reports by Cornerstone Research and NERA.
 
Claims involving artificial intelligence arrived at nearly twice the 2025 rate.


The aggregate settlement figure carries a structural caveat worth noting. 
The aggregate settlement amount for the period already represents **75% of the inflation-adjusted total for all of 2025**, per NERA
 — a concentration effect driven by a small number of large resolutions rather than broad-based improvement in per-claimant recoveries. 
Roger Cooper, co-head of Cleary Gottlieb's Americas litigation group, noted that the median settlement as a percentage of plaintiff-style damages in 2026 "is actually lower" than in eight of the past nine years — meaning settlements are not getting higher in relative terms; larger stock drops are simply producing larger absolute dollar figures.



So-called "mega" settlements of $100 million or more — including Didi Global Inc.'s **$740 million** resolution of a suit over its IPO — are the primary driver of the aggregate figure.
 The implication for recovery counsel tracking PSLRA-era cases: headline aggregate numbers overstate claimant-level recovery rates when the denominator math is done. Lead plaintiff appointment deadlines in the current cycle that bear immediate attention include the **Estée Lauder** (NYSE: EL) matter — 
claim deadline **August 5, 2026**
 — and the **Verra Mobility** (NASDAQ: VRRM) matter, where 
Faruqi & Faruqi set the lead plaintiff motion deadline at **August 4, 2026**.


The First Solar (NASDAQ: FSLR) securities action is also in the near-term window. 
Institutional investors holding FSLR positions during February 26, 2025 through February 24, 2026 are evaluating lead plaintiff opportunities.
 
FSLR shares suffered two corrective declines during the class period, falling $27.67 per share (10.29%) on January 7, 2026 and an additional $33.09 per share (13.61%) on February 25, 2026.
 
The court has set **August 24, 2026** as the lead plaintiff deadline.
 The complaint's theory — that management concealed demand deterioration, a 6.6-gigawatt booking termination by BP affiliates, and the cost burden of onshoring production — 
specifically alleged concealment of a $330 million cost burden tied to a new South Carolina facility.

### *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Post-Approval / Distribution Phase

Covered here continuously since preliminary approval. The status line today: final approval entered; distribution timeline still unconfirmed.


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the **$1.5 billion** class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment.
 
The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," overruled all **53 objections**, and found that the proposed settlement "was negotiated at arm's length" and "treats Class Members equitably relative to each other."


Key structural outcomes from the July 20 order: 
class counsel reported a **91.3% claims rate**, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately **0.03%** of the total number of works in the settlement class.
 That claims rate is unusually high for a copyright class action and directly compresses the effective denominator, concentrating the fund among participating claimants. 
The court disallowed opt-outs submitted after the February 9, 2026 deadline, with narrow exceptions for two individual requestors (Esquivel and Castells), and approved expense reimbursement of **$2,635,197.46** plus a cost reserve of **$18,220,000** in anticipated future expenses, and service awards of **$15,000** per named plaintiff ($45,000 total for the three class representatives — down from the requested $50,000 each).


The fee award, reduced from $300 million to $187.5 million, is not contested in any new filing as of today. 
Authors urged the court to grant final approval alongside the revised fee request of $187.5 million, down from the initial $300 million ask, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.


The open question now is distribution timing. 
The settlement website confirms the claim submission deadline passed March 30, 2026, and the opt-out deadline passed February 9, 2026
 — meaning the claims pool is locked. 
The Authors Guild noted it looks forward to the commencement of distribution, though no specific date for payouts has been announced.
 The tracked-case ground truth shows the last docket entry (Entry 681) filed July 21, 2026, the day after final approval — no new filings in the 13 days since. The next material event to watch is either a notice of appeal by one of the 53 overruled objectors or the claims administrator's first distribution report; neither has surfaced as of this writing.
