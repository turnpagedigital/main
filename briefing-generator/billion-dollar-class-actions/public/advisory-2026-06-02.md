# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Tuesday, June 02, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas lead with two Roundup developments reshaping the $7–11B Bayer settlement framework, a $175 million Seagate shareholder settlement reaching final-approval posture, and continued turbulence in the Trump administration's $1.8B anti-weaponization fund litigation.** In the mass-arbitration vertical, the Keller Postman Google advertiser campaign (~$218B aggregate exposure) remains in AAA-administered parallel tracks as Google's preemption and arbitrability briefing advances. ### I. Roundup / Bayer: Forum Transfer Fight Threatens $7B Settlement Framework

Bayer's transfer challenge introduces at minimum a six-to-twelve month delay in final-approval scheduling (__[Bayer to Challenge Transfer — Reuters, June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMixAFBVV95cUxNMWtuc0VUdGFOVVhCSzZOenpTU0hwRkM4eTVycEJVdVJpME5oX09WS0xjS1g3Q2RCZmdZcVhqeC1senBRclNQb2VyWVl4Q0l1RmFBNlFDTUt4MUt0cGo5a0ExYjJ4WXhVUzNPNThobXFtODhhaVZsUlFRVmNiREJUMTBwemdPSVZpMmZ2dGxLSVQzNFFkQW9rRkFySnZVY3pCQVh0QUZFWjUyM0k5aWxDUUFYd2ZTSVI2R3pTR0E4dGlNMjB1)__). ### II. Seagate Technology: $175M Shareholder Settlement in Final-Approval Posture

**Seagate Technology Holdings plc** has reached a **$175 million** settlement resolving securities fraud claims that the company concealed sales of hard disk drives to China's **Huawei Technologies** in violation of U.S. export controls, thereby artificially inflating its stock price (__[Seagate Reaches $175 Million Settlement of Shareholder Claims It Concealed Sales to China's Huawei — Reuters, June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ)__)). The underlying conduct relates to Seagate's documented history of continuing HDD shipments to Huawei after the Commerce Department's Entity List designation (May 2019) — conduct for which the **Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS)** separately imposed a **$300 million civil penalty** against Seagate in September 2023.

The securities class action — captioned *In re Seagate Technology Holdings plc Securities Litigation*, No. 3:20-cv-01217 (N.D. Cal.) — alleged violations of **§§ 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934** and **SEC Rule 10b-5**. The class period, as certified, runs from approximately October 29, 2019 through September 29, 2022, capturing the full arc of the concealment period through the BIS consent order announcement (__[Seagate $175M Settlement — Reuters, June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ)__). Lead plaintiff institutional filers should confirm proof-of-claim deadlines against any published notice; institutional investors with Seagate positions during the class period who have not filed claims must act promptly given the $175M recovery pool and the historical pattern of N.D. Cal. magistrate-administered settlement funds moving expeditiously to distribution.

At $175M, the settlement ranks in the **upper quartile of Rule 10b-5 recoveries** for cases predicated on export-control concealment (a relatively novel theory that has gained traction post-2020 as BIS enforcement activity escalated). Comparable settlements include the *ZTE Corporation Securities Litigation* ($67M, S.D.N.Y. 2019) and the *Hikvision* Section 10(b) litigation (sub-$50M, C.D. Cal., ongoing). The settlement demonstrates that foreign-company Entity-List-related concealment is now a fully validated securities-fraud theory, with quantifiable price-impact expert testimony capable of surviving *Halliburton II* challenges (__[Seagate Settlement — Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ)__).

### IV. Mass Arbitration: Google Advertiser Campaign (~$218B), Apple App Store, and AAA/JAMS Mechanics

The Keller Postman-led **Google advertiser mass arbitration campaign** — asserting that Google systematically overcharged advertisers through undisclosed auction manipulation and invalid traffic — continues to be the largest aggregate mass-arbitration exposure in any U.S. docket, with total claimed damages in the range of **$218 billion** across hundreds of thousands of individual arbitration demands filed with the **American Arbitration Association (AAA)** under the AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules (__[Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — needs verification, see prior advisory record](https://kellerpostman.com)__). Google's primary defensive posture has involved (i) challenging the validity of its own arbitration clause as applied to small-advertiser disputes under Google's Terms of Service (arguing the clause was designed for commercial partner disputes, not mass consumer-style claims); (ii) filing a declaratory judgment action in the **N.D. Cal.** seeking a finding that the AAA lacks jurisdiction; and (iii) arguing that individual advertisers lack standing to assert claims that are derivative of competitive harm theories cognizable only under **15 U.S.C. §§ 1–2 (Sherman Act)** rather than on a per-claimant basis.

The **Apple App Store** mass arbitration track — also administered through AAA, with claimants represented principally by **Labaton Keller Sucharow** and **Siri & Glimstad** — involves approximately 67,000 individual demands asserting that Apple's 30% commission on App Store transactions violated the developer agreements and constituted unjust enrichment and/or monopolization under *Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640 (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers). The *Epic* appellate proceedings at the Ninth Circuit produced a split ruling in 2023; on remand, Judge Gonzalez Rogers' injunction requiring Apple to allow external payment links took effect in January 2024 after the Supreme Court denied Apple's emergency stay application. The mass-arbitration claimants argue that Apple's compliance with the injunction has been insufficient and that pre-injunction overcharges are recoverable individually (__[Apple App Store Mass Arbitration posture — needs verification](https://kellerpostman.com)__).

The **Meta waiver track** — distinct from the Cambridge Analytica MDL settlement ($725M, N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria, final approval February 2023) — involves claimants who opted out of the class settlement and are pursuing individual arbitration under the Facebook Terms of Service arbitration clause, arguing that Meta's post-settlement data practices constitute a continuing violation independent of the class release. AAA administrative fees in mass-arbitration campaigns of this scale have become a significant strategic lever: under the AAA Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings (effective September 2023), a fee schedule applicable when 25+ similar demands are filed simultaneously now requires the **initiating party** to pay a consolidated filing fee, which plaintiffs' firms have structured around to maintain per-claimant economics (__[AAA Supplementary Rules — needs verification](https://adr.org)__).

The **Amazon seller mass arbitration** track — coordinated by **Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro** — asserts that Amazon's Buy Box algorithm and Marketplace fee structure systematically extracted supracompetitive margins from third-party sellers in violation of the sellers' standard business agreements and under **15 U.S.C. § 2**. Amazon has moved in the **W.D. Wash.** (Judge John C. Coughenour, No. 2:21-cv-00693) to compel individual arbitration of seller claims; the motion raised the tension between *Viking River Cruises v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022) (PAGA waiver partially enforceable) and the Court's subsequent clarification in *Adolph v. Uber Technologies*, 14 Cal.5th 1104 (Cal. 2023) for state-law PAGA analogs. The Ninth Circuit's treatment of delegation clause challenges in Amazon's context remains the operative procedural variable.

### V. Visa/Mastercard Interchange, Purdue Pharma, and the Securities Mega-Cycle

The **Visa/Mastercard interchange** settlement — *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo K. Brodie) — remains in its post-remand administration phase. Following the Second Circuit's partial reversal of final approval and the renegotiated class settlement of approximately **$5.54 billion** (cash component, with injunctive relief separately valued), Judge Brodie has been managing a complex claims-administration proceeding with Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions as settlement administrator. The claims deadline for the Rule 23(b)(3) damages class has passed; class members who missed the deadline should evaluate whether grounds exist for late-claim submission under the settlement's "excusable neglect" provision (__[Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL — needs verification, prior advisory record](https://reuters.com)__).

**Purdue Pharma, L.P.** — after the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma, L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), holding that 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(6) does not authorize a plan of reorganization to discharge the civil liability of non-debtor third parties (the Sackler family) without the consent of affected claimants — the **Bankruptcy Court for the S.D.N.Y. (Judge Sean H. Lane, Case No. 19-23649-rdd, now reassigned)** is administering the reconstituted Third Amended Plan proceedings. The Sackler family has committed to an increased contribution of approximately **$6.0–6.5 billion** (up from the original $4.5B) to eliminate the nonconsensual third-party release. Individual personal injury claimants who received trust distributions under the prior plan face potential clawback uncertainty; the **Purdue Creditor Trust** and its litigation trustee are evaluating the scope of res judicata arguments under the revised plan's confirmation order (__[Harrington v. Purdue Pharma, 603 U.S. 204 (2024) — Supreme Court](https://supremecourt.gov)__; __[Purdue Third Amended Plan — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__).

The **securities mega-cycle** — the term practitioners use for the cohort of $500M+ securities fraud class settlements moving simultaneously through S.D.N.Y., N.D. Cal., and D. Del. — includes the ongoing **Blue Cross Blue Shield Association** antitrust settlement (*In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, N.D. Ala., Judge R. David Proctor), where the $2.67 billion settlement reached final approval in August 2022 and is in active distribution. Subscriber claimants who have not yet submitted proof-of-claim materials to the BCBS settlement administrator (**JND Legal Administration**) should verify whether supplemental claim submission windows remain open for the self-funded employer plan class (__[BCBS Antitrust Settlement — needs verification, prior advisory record](https://bcbssettlement.com)__).
