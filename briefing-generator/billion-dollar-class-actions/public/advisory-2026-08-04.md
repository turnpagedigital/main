### *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Appeal Window Running; Distribution Not Before August 19

**Status.** 
On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the N.D. Cal. granted final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment.
 
The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," ruling that it "was negotiated at arm's length" and "treats Class Members equitably relative to each other," and overruled all 53 filed objections.


**Appeal exposure.** 
There is a 30-day window from July 20 during which parties can attempt to appeal the order, meaning no payouts will occur until at least after that date.
 That window closes August 19. No notice of appeal has been confirmed on the docket as of the tracked-case ground truth (last docket entry: July 21, 2026, Docket No. 681). The practical significance: distribution mechanics remain suspended through at least August 19, and any filed appeal would extend that hold indefinitely pending Ninth Circuit briefing.

**Fee award.** 
The court granted class counsel a lodestar multiplier totaling $101,561,111, plus approximately $3 million in expense reimbursements, with a cost reserve of $18.2 million for anticipated claims administration expenses.
 That is a material reduction from the originally requested $300 million and the revised request of $187.5 million. 
Class counsel had urged final approval alongside the revised $187.5 million fee request, arguing the deal was fair notwithstanding multiple objections.


**Claims and opt-out metrics.** 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately 0.03% of the total number of covered works.
 That claims rate is unusually high by claims-made settlement standards and compresses per-claim recovery for claimants relative to what the opt-out denominator would otherwise permit. The fee reduction — from the requested 12.5% of fund down to approximately 6.8% — correspondingly increases the net distributable pool.

**Named-plaintiff enhancement.** 
Although the court found that class representatives adequately represented the class, it rejected their request for $50,000 each and reduced the enhancement to $15,000 per named plaintiff.


**Near-term calendar.** Distribution cannot commence before August 19 at the earliest. If an appeal is filed, any distribution will await either a Ninth Circuit stay ruling or final disposition. No payout date has been announced. 
The Authors Guild has stated it looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process, though no specific payout date has been announced.

### Securities Class Actions H1 2026: Midyear Benchmarks (Cornerstone / NERA Reports, Published July 29)

This is the macro context for every securities matter tracked here. 
Large alleged investor losses and increased AI adoption are the key factors driving more than $2 billion in aggregate settlements at the midyear mark of 2026.


**Filing volume.** 
Securities class action filings jumped 30% in H1 2026, reaching 121 total complaints, up from 93 in the second half of 2025.
 
NERA counted 118 federal court filings in H1 2026, projecting a year-end total of 236 — 15% above the 205 cases filed in 2025 and the highest annual total since 2020.


**AI-specific filings — the dominant driver.** 
Fifteen AI-related securities class actions were filed in H1 2026, nearly equaling the full-year total of 16 recorded in 2025; the annualized pace of 30 filings puts the category on track to nearly double last year's count.
 
NERA counted 18 first-half AI-related filings, already exceeding the 17 AI-related cases filed in all of 2025.
 
Cornerstone's 15 AI filings broke down as seven related to AI development, five to data centers, and four to AI infrastructure and hardware.


**Loss concentration.** 
The Maximum Dollar Loss Index reached $1.858 trillion in H1 2026 — nearly triple the historical semiannual average of $667 billion — with just two of the 15 AI-related filings accounting for $1.235 trillion, or 66%, of the entire MDL Index for the period.
 
AI-related filings represented 13% of core filings, but Disclosure Dollar Loss from those filings ($385 billion) accounted for 73% of the DDL Index for H1 2026 ($529 billion).
 Stanford's Grundfest characterized this as 
"a small number of high-impact matters" dominating securities litigation trends across the period.


**Settlement volume and value.** 
Cornerstone counted 39 securities suit settlements in H1 2026 with a total value of $2.2 billion.
 
The aggregate settlement amount for the period already represents 75% of the inflation-adjusted total for all of 2025, per NERA.
 
NERA reported an average settlement value of $54 million in H1 2026 against an inflation-adjusted 2025 average of $41 million; the median was $18 million, flat to the 2025 inflation-adjusted median.


**Pump-and-dump surge; crypto/SPAC retreat.** 
Cases with pump-and-dump allegations jumped from no more than two filings annually during 2022–2025 to 11 in H1 2026 alone.
 
Crypto-related filings fell sharply, with only two in H1 2026 compared to 14 for full-year 2025; SPAC filings similarly declined to one versus five for full-year 2025.


**Practical read for claims recovery.** The concentration dynamic — a handful of mega-cases distorting aggregate loss and settlement metrics — has direct implications for lead-plaintiff competition and fee awards in the AI securities wave. Cases with nine-figure MDL figures will attract institutional lead plaintiffs under PSLRA § 21D(a)(3)(B), driving lower percentage fees and higher absolute dollar recoveries. The pump-and-dump resurgence is a separate, smaller-dollar pipeline, but the rapid growth from near-zero suggests a structural shift in plaintiff bar strategy, likely driven by accessible AI-analysis tools for market manipulation detection.
