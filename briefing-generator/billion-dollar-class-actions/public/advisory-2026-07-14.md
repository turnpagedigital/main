### Bartz v. Anthropic, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Still Pending, Late Opt-Outs the Blocking Issue


The fairness hearing took place May 14, 2026, before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California.
 
After a 75-minute hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement, though the tone of the proceeding led most observers to expect final approval relatively quickly.
 The court issued a post-hearing order directing Anthropic to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages on the late opt-out question by May 21, 2026. 
By that date, plaintiffs were also required to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026 at docket entry 646-1.
 Docket entry 678, filed July 10, 2026 and identified in the tracked-case ground truth as a brief responsive to the Passman late opt-out request, is the most recent activity — meaning the late opt-out constellation remains unresolved nearly two months after the hearing.

The substantive late opt-out disputes are not trivial. 
Laura Esquivel, the Mexican author of *Como agua para chocolate* (Dkt. 651), challenges Class Counsel's "co-owner consent" requirement, arguing the Long Form Notice expressly provides that a single rightsholder can opt out unilaterally; she further argues excusable neglect — she did not receive notice until March 3, 2026, three weeks after the deadline, received no Spanish-language notice, and suffered a stroke during the relevant period.
 
A separate objector, Chakanga, raises a due process argument: thousands of pages of sealed summary judgment records, class-certification briefs, and expert declarations were not unsealed until January 21, 2026 — eight days before the original opt-out deadline and 19 days before the extended deadline — and relies on the Ninth Circuit's January 28, 2026 decision in *Avery v. TEKsystems* to argue the compressed, holiday-season timeline with a sealed record constitutes a substantive defect.


Fee exposure at the approval stage remains notable. 
Plaintiffs urged the court to grant final approval alongside an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 
Anthropic has separately contested the $75 million in fees requested by non-class counsel, arguing there is "scant justification" for that request.
 At the hearing, 
Class Counsel Justin Nelson reported the payout per work will be approximately $3,100, against a 92.77% participation rate for works on the works list.
 
The final claims figure of 440,490 out of 482,460 eligible works (91.3%) vastly surpassed the 54% rate reported in the March 19, 2026 motion for final approval — eleven days before the March 30 claims deadline — suggesting a large volume of last-minute filings.


Distribution mechanics: 
the settlement timeline contemplates a second-wave check issuance on October 9, 2026 (120 days after the final approval order, assumed April 23) and a second scheduled Anthropic payment of $450 million on September 25, 2026.
 Every week the final approval order remains outstanding compresses the administrative runway before the September payment date.

One granular structural issue warrants continued monitoring. 
Objector Pinder (Dkt. 648) — an author with 40+ independently published novels registered under a single group copyright registration — receives one settlement share rather than 40, because the settlement counts each copyright registration number as one "claimable Work." Class Counsel's response — that "any author or publisher can register their work on an individual basis" — did not address the merits of the group-registration disparity.
 Judge Martínez-Olguín's order on this point, if she addresses it, will have downstream implications for how group-registration authors in future AI-copyright settlements structure their claims processes.

### Keller Postman v. Google (Mass Arbitration) — $218B Campaign, Two Antitrust Foundations, FAA Mechanics Active


Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding Google engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.
 
The $218 billion aggregate figure is based on calculations from a retained economist covering damages from both the search monopoly (Judge Amit P. Mehta, D.D.C.) and the ad tech monopoly (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema, E.D. Va.) — the two separate rulings that established Google's liability.


The legal mechanics matter for anyone evaluating recovery posture. These are not class actions; 
due to contractual arbitration provisions in Google's advertiser agreements, claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation, with Keller Postman coordinating thousands of individual claims nationwide.
 
Alphabet's public filings reflect that U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases have estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs.
 
Potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under Clayton Act § 4.


The campaign's structural leverage derives from the same mechanism Keller Postman weaponized in its prior mass-arbitration campaigns. 
Prior campaigns include the Amazon Alexa arbitration — which prompted Amazon to remove its mandatory arbitration clause entirely after 75,000 demands — and the TurboTax campaign that ran parallel to FTC enforcement and resolved in a confidential settlement alongside a $141 million FTC fund.
 
In addition to representing private claimants in the arbitration, Keller Postman also represents seven state attorneys general in a parallel case.
 The combination of individual AAA arbitration demands at scale and parallel state AG enforcement creates compounding per-claim pressure: as demand volume rises, Google's per-claim processing costs under AAA rules create structural incentives toward global resolution short of adjudication.


The firm cautions that deadlines vary by state and that earlier action increases the probability of a successful claim being filed within applicable statutes of limitations.
 Practitioners advising advertiser clients should note that the Sherman Act § 4-year limitations period under Clayton Act § 4 began running no later than the date each advertiser's injury accrued, and the two liability rulings — not the arbitration filing — are the operative damages anchors.

### In re Payment Card Interchange Fee Antitrust Litigation, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.) — Second Initial Distribution Approved; New Injunctive Settlement Enters Final-Approval Track

Two distinct Visa/Mastercard matters are in motion simultaneously and should not be conflated.

**MDL 1720 damages distribution:** 
On May 26, 2026, class counsel in MDL 1720 filed a motion for a second initial distribution of the $5.54 billion settlement fund; on June 15, 2026, the court approved that motion, and the second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026.
 
The $5.54 billion settlement fund covers merchants that accepted Visa and Mastercard branded cards from January 1, 2004, to January 25, 2019.
 The claim window for that fund has closed.

**New injunctive settlement — preliminary approval granted June 9, 2026:** 
On June 9, 2026, U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan granted preliminary approval to a revised Visa/Mastercard injunctive-relief settlement — a meaningful development given that prior versions of the settlement have been rejected.
 
The $38 billion figure associated with this settlement represents cumulative merchant savings on interchange rates over five years — not a cash fund for payments — a distinction that has caused widespread confusion among merchants.
 
Analysts predict the settlement may not be fully finalized until 2029 as legal challenges continue.


**New post-release litigation:** 
On April 21, 2026, a merchant class filed a new complaint in the Southern District of New York — *Potayto-Potahto, LLC et al. v. Visa Inc. et al.* — asserting federal antitrust claims on behalf of merchants that accepted Visa and Mastercard credit cards since January 25, 2019, the date the original damages-class release expires.
 
In April 2026, a putative class action was filed on behalf of U.S. merchants seeking damages related to interchange fees since January 2019; the named plaintiffs concurrently filed a motion for summary judgment before the MDL 1720 court seeking a declaration that the release from the Damages Class Settlement Agreement does not bar their post-2019 damages claims.
 This is the clean-hands question that has been latent in the MDL 1720 structure since the 2023 Second Circuit affirmance: whether the release clause in ¶ [X] of the Amended Settlement Agreement extending through August 2028 can prospectively bind merchant claims that accrued after final approval. The answer will define the outer boundary of transaction-based release provisions in future antitrust class settlements.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and claim-recovery counsel should take three specific steps this week. First, on *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417): monitor the docket daily for the final approval order from Judge Martínez-Olguín — once entered, the 30-day objector appeal window under FRCP 23 begins, and any objector appeal to the Ninth Circuit will delay the September 25, 2026 distribution installment; if you represent a claimant with a group-registration issue (the Pinder/Dkt. 648 problem), prepare now to pursue individual re-registration or to challenge the allocation formula in the objection/appeal process, since Class Counsel's non-answer on merits leaves this issue open. Second, on the Keller Postman/Google mass arbitration: any business with material Google Ads spend since August 2016 should confirm its applicable state limitations period before year-end — Clayton Act § 4's four-year federal clock is the floor, but several state analogs are shorter, and the firm is prioritizing earlier filers for claim-window certainty; the parallel state AG actions in seven states add settlement pressure but do not toll individual arbitration deadlines. Third, on *MDL 1720* interchange: the September 2026 second-distribution check from the closed $5.54 billion fund requires no further action from approved claimants; however, the new *Potayto-Potahto* post-2019 putative class warrants intake evaluation for any merchant with Visa/Mastercard volume from January 25, 2019 forward — the release expiration argument is live and the summary-judgment motion before Judge Cogan on the release scope is the critical near-term inflection point.
