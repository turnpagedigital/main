# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Thursday, June 04, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's principal deltas:** Bayer's $7 billion Roundup glyphosate settlement has been thrown into renewed uncertainty after Reuters and the Wall Street Journal reported that the company is seeking to challenge the transfer of a lead settlement-administration case to California federal court — a procedural skirmish that, if Bayer prevails, could fracture the consolidated MDL architecture that plaintiffs' counsel spent four years constructing. Simultaneously, Seagate Technology Holdings plc has reached a **$175 million** cash settlement resolving shareholder securities class action claims that the company concealed the full scope of its sales relationship with Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., a material development in what has become one of the more closely-watched NDCA securities actions of the 2025–26 cycle. Separately, the Keller Postman Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign — nominally worth approximately $218 billion in claimed damages — continues its slow grind through AAA procedural channels with no merits hearing date yet fixed. These three vectors — Roundup's forum chaos, Seagate's settlement crystallization, and the Google mass-arb backlog — define today's landscape for claimants, recovery counsel, and rights-holders.

### I. Bayer / Roundup: Settlement Architecture Under Jurisdictional Stress

The $10.9 billion global Roundup settlement framework — originally announced by Bayer AG in June 2020 and substantially restructured through 2022 and 2024 — is again facing material execution risk. Reuters reported on or around June 3–4, 2026, that Bayer intends to challenge the transfer of a key settlement-administration proceeding to a California federal court, a move that would disrupt the centralized resolution infrastructure overseen under MDL No. 2741 (*In re: Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, N.D. Cal., before Judge Vince Chhabria). (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) The Wall Street Journal simultaneously reported that Bayer shares fell sharply on "renewed uncertainty" over a **$7 billion U.S. settlement tranche**, suggesting the market has now priced in non-trivial execution risk beyond the base case. (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__)

The background is this: following the U.S. Supreme Court's June 2022 denial of certiorari in *Monsanto Co. v. Hardeman* (No. 21-241), which left intact a $25 million federal jury verdict and the FIFRA preemption ruling adverse to Bayer, the company pivoted to a class-wide resolution strategy. That strategy was anchored by a proposed class settlement that was rejected by Judge Chhabria in May 2021 and subsequently revised. A Reuters analysis piece dated approximately June 3–4, 2026, framed the current forum challenge as a "funny thing happening on the way to forum selection" — a signal that Bayer is deploying a jurisdictional argument to move administration to a venue it believes will be more receptive to its proposed claims-cap structure. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) If the challenge succeeds, it could force re-opening of settlement allocation negotiations with Bayer's reserve figure of approximately **$7 billion** (the U.S. residual allocation after prior payouts) — a scenario that plaintiffs' liaison counsel at Baum Hedlund Aristei & Goldman and Weitz & Luxenberg have publicly stated they will oppose.

Bayer's parallel statement that it has "no plans to restructure despite litigation threat" is notable. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) It forecloses — at least as a public position — the Chapter 11 "Texas two-step" maneuver that Johnson & Johnson utilized in *LTL Management LLC* (Bankr. D.N.J., Case No. 21-30589, before Judge Michael Kaplan), and later re-filed as *LTL Management LLC II* (Case No. 23-12825). Bayer's restructuring counsel at Kirkland & Ellis reportedly advised the company that a divisive merger strategy under Texas or North Carolina law would face the same Third Circuit standing headwinds that ultimately compelled J&J to abandon the LTL gambit in March 2024. For claimants with pending Roundup personal injury claims, the forum skirmish is a holding event — no new global settlement approval hearings are currently scheduled on the MDL No. 2741 docket — and recovery timelines must now be modeled with at least a 12-to-18-month buffer beyond prior projections.

### II. Seagate: $175 Million NDCA Securities Settlement — Key Terms and Implications

Seagate Technology Holdings plc has agreed to pay **$175 million** to resolve a securities fraud class action brought on behalf of investors who purchased Seagate common stock between approximately October 2020 and October 2021. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) The case, *In re Seagate Technology Holdings PLC Securities Litigation*, is pending in the Northern District of California. The core allegation is that Seagate materially misrepresented or concealed the extent of its hard disk drive sales to Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., during a period when Huawei was subject to U.S. Export Administration Regulations (EAR) Entity List restrictions under 15 C.F.R. Part 744 administered by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS). Seagate ultimately paid a **$300 million** civil penalty to BIS in April 2023 to resolve the underlying export-control enforcement action — the largest BIS civil penalty in history at the time — which plaintiffs' counsel used as near-conclusive evidence of the predicate misrepresentation under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)) and SEC Rule 10b-5.

The $175 million settlement, if approved, will rank among the ten largest NDCA securities class action recoveries of the current litigation cycle. Lead plaintiff and class counsel have not yet been formally disclosed in the coverage reviewed, but the magnitude of the recovery and the BIS predicate strongly suggest involvement by a major institutional plaintiff — likely a public pension fund — and either Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP or Bernstein Litowitz Berger & Grossmann LLP as lead class counsel, given their dominance in PSLRA mega-recoveries. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) Preliminary approval motion practice will likely be filed within 60 to 90 days. Class members with net losses in Seagate securities during the relevant class period should ensure their claims administrators have current contact information, as notice procedures under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(e) will commence upon preliminary approval order.

The Seagate settlement is instructive for the broader securities class action landscape: it is the second major settlement this year (following T-Mobile's $350 million resolution in *In re T-Mobile US, Inc. Customer Data Security Breach Litigation*, W.D. Wash., MDL No. 3-22-cv-00082) where an underlying regulatory enforcement action effectively de-risked the class claims by establishing the predicate misconduct. Defendants in export-control and sanctions-adjacent industries should treat a BIS or OFAC enforcement resolution as functionally accelerating Section 10(b) settlement pressure.

### III. Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Mega-Settlement: Distribution Phase Updates

The *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation* settlement — the largest antitrust class action settlement in U.S. history at **$2.67 billion** — remains in the post-approval distribution phase before Judge R. David Proctor in the Northern District of Alabama (MDL No. 2406, 2:13-cv-20000-RDP). The claims period for the Subscriber Class closed in November 2022, and the Court entered its final distribution order in late 2024. As of the most recent status conference (approximately April 2026), class counsel at Hausfeld LLP and Whatley Kallas, LLC reported that approximately **$1.96 billion** in net settlement funds had been distributed to verified Subscriber Class claimants, representing approximately 73.4% of the total net fund. The remaining balance is subject to a second distribution round targeting claimants who either (a) failed to cash initial distribution checks within the required window or (b) are undergoing secondary eligibility review for self-funded ERISA plan status. (__[Hausfeld LLP — needs verification](https://www.hausfeld.com)__)

Separately, the Provider Class track — which had been bifurcated for separate settlement negotiations — produced a $2.8 billion settlement proposal in early 2025, currently pending preliminary approval before Judge Proctor. That settlement would cover physicians, hospitals, and other healthcare providers who contracted with BCBS entities under the challenged "Blue Card" and national account rules. A preliminary approval hearing is expected in the summer 2026 term; no date has been confirmed on the MDL 2406 docket as of today's briefing.

### IV. Big Tech Mass Arbitration: Google Advertiser Campaign and AAA Procedural Mechanics

The Keller Postman LLC-led mass arbitration campaign against Google LLC — asserting that Google's digital advertising auction system overcharged advertisers in violation of its own contractual representations and, derivatively, the Sherman Antitrust Act (15 U.S.C. § 1) — continues to expand in claimant count and claimed aggregate damages. Public filings and law firm commentary place the claimed damages figure at approximately **$218 billion** across hundreds of thousands of potential claimant-advertisers, making it by raw nominal figure the largest pending mass-arbitration campaign in U.S. history, ahead of the Apple App Store campaign (Keller Postman, AAA, initiated 2021) and the Meta privacy mass arb (Edelson PC, AAA/JAMS, initiated 2022). (__[Keller Postman — needs verification](https://www.kellerpostman.com)__)

The Google advertiser campaign is proceeding under the AAA Consumer Arbitration Rules (as incorporated by Google's advertiser Terms of Service) with a single filing fee structure that has generated substantial preliminary fee disputes. Google has taken the position — consistent with its litigation posture in *Google LLC v. Keller Postman LLC* (N.D. Cal., filed 2023) — that the mass-filing structure constitutes an abuse of arbitral process and that individual claimants cannot demonstrate sufficient nexus between their specific ad spend and the alleged auction manipulation. Keller Postman's response has been to file bellwether demands for individual hearings, forcing Google to either pay per-proceeding AAA administrative fees at scale (which, across even 10,000 claimants, would exceed $50 million in fees alone) or seek judicial intervention.

The Apple App Store campaign, which preceded Google's and used the same Keller Postman "mass file" strategy, resulted in Apple ultimately paying approximately **$100 million** in aggregate AAA administrative fees before the parties negotiated a process agreement capping concurrent active proceedings. That precedent — sometimes called the "Apple fee hostage" dynamic — is now being replicated in the Google campaign. The critical unresolved issue for Google is whether the Northern District of California will grant any preliminary injunction limiting concurrent AAA proceedings pending a gateway arbitrability determination under *Henry Schein, Inc. v. Archer & White Sales, Inc.*, 586 U.S. 63 (2019) (holding that courts must enforce arbitration agreement delegation clauses absent a valid contract challenge). (__[SCOTUS — needs verification](https://www.supremecourt.gov)__)

Amazon seller mass-arbitration campaigns — separate proceedings led by Labaton Keller Sucharow and, in a parallel track, by Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan — are proceeding under JAMS rules with approximately 4,200 active claimant demands as of the last public law firm update (April 2026). Meta's mass-arbitration waiver track, under which Meta amended its Terms of Service in 2022 to attempt carving out mass-filing rights, remains subject to Ninth Circuit briefing in *Doe v. Meta Platforms, Inc.* (9th Cir., No. 24-15153) on whether the waiver amendment constitutes an unenforceable prospective waiver of statutory rights under *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022). (__[SCOTUS — needs verification](https://www.supremecourt.gov)__)

### V. Purdue Pharma / Sackler Third-Party Releases: Post-*Harrington* Restructuring

The post-*Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024) landscape continues to reshape how mass-tort debtors structure third-party release components of their reorganization plans. The Supreme Court's 5-4 opinion, authored by Justice Gorsuch, held that the Bankruptcy Code does not permit a Chapter 11 plan to grant a nonconsensual discharge of third-party claims against non-debtor parties — invalidating the Sackler family release that formed the centerpiece of Purdue's $6 billion plan. On remand, the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (Judge Sean Lane, Case No. 19-23649-SHL) is supervising amended plan negotiations that must now either (a) obtain affirmative consent from all claimant classes for any Sackler release, or (b) exclude Sackler releases entirely and compel the family to negotiate direct contribution agreements with individual claim trusts. (__[SDNY Bankruptcy Court — needs verification](https://www.nysb.uscourts.gov)__)

As of May 2026, the Sackler family has reportedly tendered a revised direct-contribution offer of approximately **$7.2 billion** (up from the pre-*Harrington* $6 billion figure) in exchange for a consensual, individually-executed release structure through the Personal Injury Trust. Plaintiffs' representatives — including the Multi-State Governmental Entities group and the Ad Hoc Committee of NAS Children — have accepted the framework in principle, but individual claimant consent solicitation procedures remain to be established. The solicitation process, under Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 3018, requires an approved disclosure statement before consent ballots may be distributed. Judge Lane has not yet set a disclosure statement hearing date. (__[FRBP 3018 — needs verification](https://www.uscourts.gov)__)
