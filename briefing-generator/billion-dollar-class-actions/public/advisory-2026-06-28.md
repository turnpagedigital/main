# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Sunday, June 28, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**MDL 3047 — Social Media Adolescent Addiction — Breathitt County Pre-Trial Settlement and State AG Track Acceleration**

The most significant development of the past week is the confirmed pre-trial settlement of the first federal school-district bellwether in *In re Social Media Adolescent Addiction/Personal Injury Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 3047, Case No. 4:22-md-03047-YGR, 
before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers in the Northern District of California.
 
The Breathitt County School District (Kentucky) trial was calendared for June 15, 2026. It never reached a jury. Snap, TikTok, and YouTube settled in mid-May 2026; Meta — the last defendant standing — settled days later, on the eve of trial. Local reporting puts the combined value at roughly $27 million for a single rural Kentucky district.
 That figure is unverified by court filings; settlement terms were not docketed. The strategic read is what matters: 
the platforms spent a decade telling courts and Congress that these claims could never survive a motion to dismiss, much less reach a jury. A federal trial, with internal documents and live executives under oath in front of twelve jurors, was the moment that argument would be tested.
 Defendants chose settlement over that test — a significant capitulation given prior posture.

The pre-trial settlement shifts litigation pressure immediately to the **State AG bellwether track**. 
Per the MDL Centrality docket tracker, the Second State AG Pretrial Conference is set for June 26, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. PT, and the Final State AG Pretrial Conference is calendared for July 17, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. PT, with the State AG Bellwether Trial beginning August 6, 2026.
 
The October 2023 multi-state action, joined by 41 attorneys general, targets Meta specifically for platform design choices that allegedly harmed youth in their states. Massachusetts AG Andrea Joy Campbell pursues a separate state-court action with similar claims.
 
Alongside the federal MDL, nearly 800 school district lawsuits and attorneys general from 41+ states remain active.


The pre-trial settlement does not resolve the MDL's personal-injury track or the AG track. 
As of June 2026, MDL 3047 holds roughly 2,664 pending cases, spanning three tracks: individual personal-injury claims by adolescents and parents, claims by nearly 800 school districts, and actions by state attorneys general.
 
The KGM verdict remains subject to post-trial motions and likely appeal.
 
Meta and YouTube have asked a Los Angeles Superior Court judge to set aside the landmark $6 million verdict in the first trial to hold social media companies liable for a teen user's mental health injuries.
 The post-trial motion practice in JCCP 5255 will run concurrently with the August federal AG trial — creating simultaneous appellate and trial exposure for Meta across two venues.

**The KGM Verdict as Settlement Anchor**

The litigation's settlement arithmetic begins with *K.G.M. v. Meta Platforms, Inc. & YouTube LLC*, JCCP 5255, Los Angeles County Superior Court, before Judge Carolyn B. Kuhl. 
On March 25, 2026, a Los Angeles Superior Court jury returned a $6 million verdict against the remaining defendants — $3M compensatory (Meta 70% / Google 30%) plus $3M punitive.
 
Mark Zuckerberg gave his first-ever jury testimony on February 18, 2026.
 The verdict is the operative data point for mass-tort settlement modeling: 
it was the first of three California bellwether cases and the first jury verdict on social-media product-liability claims. The case is a state-court companion to MDL-3047, not part of the federal MDL itself, but the result is widely expected to influence settlement posture across the federal docket and the remaining state cases.


The Breathitt County pre-trial settlement — occurring after the KGM verdict — confirms that the verdict shifted Meta's calculus. A reported ~$27M payout to a single rural Kentucky school district implies per-district settlement values that, extrapolated across 800+ school-district plaintiffs, place aggregate exposure well into the billions before the personal-injury and AG tracks are priced. That extrapolation is inherently uncertain and depends on bellwether outcomes the market has not yet seen. What the data supports is that defendants are no longer willing to let a federal jury adjudicate this theory.

**Bartz v. Anthropic PBC — Final Approval Taken Under Submission; Distribution Timeline Now Operative**

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, Case No. 3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal., 
Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín held the hearing for final approval of the class settlement on May 14, 2026.
 The court did not issue a ruling from the bench. 
Judge Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission and indicated that short orders may follow.
 
Per the post-hearing order, by May 21, 2026, Anthropic was directed to file a supplemental brief of no more than 2 pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored; plaintiffs were directed to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026.
 The docket shows the last filing on June 25, 2026, confirming the matter remains live, with no final approval order yet entered on the public docket as of this run.

The settlement terms are confirmed: 
Anthropic will pay $1.5 billion into a settlement fund.
 
Anthropic will fund the settlement fund in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 
Class counsel filed papers apprising the court that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% participation rate.
 
The high claims rate means that the forecasts about a larger payout based on the March 19 report of 54% will not hold, and the actual payout will likely end up closer to the original $3,000 per work figure.
 Class counsel confirmed at the May 14 hearing that 
the payout per work in the class will be $3,100.


Fee exposure is material for any class member assessing net recovery: 
plaintiff lawyers are seeking 25% of the fund — $375 million — as attorneys' fees.
 The fee cross-check and late opt-out treatment are the remaining judicial work before a final approval order can issue. 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 
Per the settlement's Plan of Allocation, calculation of per-work distributions is scheduled for June 11, 2026, with earliest payments to class members if there are no objectors; summer 2026 is the earliest payment window if objectors exist but no appeals are filed.
 Given that 
at the May 14 hearing, the judge heard from 7 objectors
 — including structured objections through attorney James Bartolomei — an appellate challenge remains a non-trivial risk. If an objector files a Ninth Circuit appeal, payment disbursement will slip well past summer 2026.

**Bartz as Precedent: The Shadow-Library Template**

The Bartz settlement matters beyond its face value because it validated a litigation strategy now being replicated against other AI defendants. 
The Bartz settlement has given plaintiffs' lawyers a roadmap. Law firms like Susman Godfrey have developed what has been called "The Shadow Library Strategy," which attempts to skip the debate over whether AI training is fair use — judges so far seem to think it is — and go straight for the piracy angle. Most new book copyright lawsuits against AI companies, most of which are also class actions, now allege the defendants downloaded from shadow libraries.
 The liability theory is narrower but more durable: 
in June 2025, Judge William Alsup of the N.D. Cal. ruled on summary judgment that using books without permission to train AI was fair use if they were acquired legally, but he denied Anthropic's request for summary judgment related to piracy — finding that piracy was not fair use.
 Any AI defendant whose training corpus includes LibGen or PiLiMi downloads faces substantially the same exposure profile. The settlement fund structure — installment payments over two years — is also becoming a template for AI copyright deals where the defendant cannot absorb a lump-sum payment.

**MDL 3047 State AG Bellwether: What August 6 Means for Settlement Posture**

The August 6 State AG trial date is the next live trigger. The AG track differs structurally from the school-district track: 
over 40 state attorneys general have filed similar claims against Meta.
 AG plaintiffs are not settling for compensatory damages to individual claimants; they seek injunctive relief (redesign mandates, algorithmic restrictions) and civil penalties, which are not resolved by the Breathitt County template. 
Attorney General Letitia James was among the coalition of 33 state attorneys general that sued Meta in federal court in October 2023 over features targeting minors.
 A defense verdict in the AG bellwether would significantly reduce settlement leverage across that track. A plaintiff verdict — or a further pre-trial settlement by Meta — would accelerate the multi-state AG settlement process and likely produce injunctive terms that restructure platform design requirements at scale.

**Broader Mass-Arbitration and Class-Action Housekeeping**

The Keller Postman Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign (estimated at approximately $218B in aggregate claims), Apple App Store proceedings, and Meta waiver-track arbitrations remain in their respective procedural postures from prior advisories. No new public docket developments have been confirmed this run. The AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules continue to govern batching mechanics across these campaigns. Counsel tracking those proceedings should note that the social media MDL's settlement dynamics — particularly Meta's willingness to settle the Breathitt County case for a reported ~$27M rather than face trial — may inform settlement posture on the mass-arbitration tracks where Meta is also a defendant.

Counsel with school-district clients in MDL 3047 (No. 4:22-md-03047-YGR) should note that Breathitt County settled for a reported combined ~$27M before opening statements, establishing an implied per-district floor that will anchor negotiations across the 800+ district docket; the August 6 State AG bellwether before Judge Gonzalez Rogers is the next pressure point, and preparation of preservation notices and expert schedules for the second school-district bellwether wave (Arizona and South Carolina) should be treated as immediate, not deferred. Rights-holders who opted out of the Bartz settlement and are pursuing independent claims against Anthropic retain their infringement claims but face the fair-use headwind from Judge Alsup's June 2025 summary judgment ruling on legally acquired materials; the piracy-only theory is viable, but only for works demonstrably downloaded from LibGen or PiLiMi prior to the cutoff dates.
