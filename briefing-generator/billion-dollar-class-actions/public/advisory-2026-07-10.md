### Bartz v. Anthropic — Final Approval in Limbo, Distribution Math Now Set

*Bartz, et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal., Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín). Docket: CourtListener ID 69058235; last filing synced July 1, 2026.


The fairness hearing took place May 14, 2026, before Judge Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California.
 
After a 75-minute courtroom session, the judge declined to issue final approval from the bench, though the tone of the hearing left most observers expecting relatively prompt approval.
 
Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission and indicated short orders might follow that day.
 As of the docket's last sync date of July 1, 2026, no final approval order has been reflected. The gap between the May 14 hearing and today — nearly eight weeks — is notable; the court has not published an order within the window suggested by post-hearing commentary.

Post-hearing procedural posture: 
By order signed May 14, the court directed Anthropic to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages by May 21 addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored, and directed plaintiffs to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline.
 
The judge stated she would not require anything further from objectors and would not consider additional submissions from them.


**Fee dispute — the number that moved:** 
Plaintiffs' counsel urged final approval alongside an attorneys' fee request revised downward from $300 million to $187.5 million — a meaningful concession from the original 25% ask.
 The revised figure represents 12.5% of the $1.5 billion gross fund; 
Anthropic separately contested the $75 million in fees requested by non-class counsel, arguing there was "scant justification" for that request.
 Resolution of both disputes is presumably among the items Judge Martínez-Olguín is still working through.

**Claims-rate lock-in and distribution denominator:** The claims dynamic is the most operationally significant development in this case. 
Class counsel reported that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a 91.3% rate — vastly exceeding the 54% rate reported in the March 19 Motion for Final Approval, indicating a large wave of last-minute filings.
 
At the hearing itself, lead class counsel Justin Nelson updated the participation figure to 92.77%, with a projected payout of approximately $3,100 per work in the class.
 
The high claim rate extinguishes any prospect of the per-work payout rising beyond the original ~$3,000 figure.
 For claimants tracking recovery value: the denominator is now effectively fixed at ~92.77% of 482,460 works.

**Payment waterfall and installment schedule:** 
The $1.5 billion gross fund must cover attorneys' fees, JND Legal Administration costs, and $50,000 service awards to each of the three named plaintiffs.
 
Anthropic is funding the settlement in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 
The first $300 million has already been deposited in an interest-bearing escrow account.
 
The second scheduled payment of $450 million is due September 25, 2026; a third $450 million payment follows September 25, 2027.
 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.


**Objector and opt-out overhang:** 
At the hearing, seven objectors were heard — each allotted two minutes, with one attorney given five minutes to speak on behalf of multiple objectors.
 The objection from Arizona firm ClaimsHero Holdings LLC was handled separately and pre-hearing: 
a California federal judge warned ClaimsHero against encouraging opt-outs, characterizing its strategy as apparent "extortion" and a bid for a "nuisance settlement."
 The court's prior order on ClaimsHero reduces the risk that a coordinated late-opt-out wave destabilizes the settlement class.

**Forward risk:** The principal residual exposure is appellate. 
Once final approval issues, payouts will only commence after any appeals are fully resolved.
 The objectors who appeared May 14 are the most likely appellants; their two-minute allocations suggest limited record development for a Ninth Circuit challenge, but the fee structure — 12.5% of gross, on a $1.5B fund — remains the sharpest target.

### Visa/Mastercard Interchange — Second Distribution Wave Authorized

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.).


On May 26, 2026, class counsel filed a motion with the court requesting approval of a second initial distribution; on June 15, 2026, the court approved the motion.
 
The second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026.
 
The $5.54 billion settlement applies to merchants that accepted Visa and Mastercard branded cards in the United States at any time from January 1, 2004, through January 25, 2019.



Class counsel sought a partial staged distribution because outstanding legal disputes regarding payment facilitators (PayFacs) and Branded Operators, and challenges to fee amounts or ownership of claims, made full payment impossible at this time.
 
The first approved distribution was approximately $426 million to more than 605,000 claimants, structured as a percentage of each claimant's pro rata share of the net fund to ensure no merchant is overpaid ahead of the resolution of contested claims.


A separate, structurally distinct proceeding merits tracking: 
On June 9, 2026, U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan granted preliminary approval to a revised Visa and Mastercard settlement — a meaningful step because prior versions had been rejected.
 
This newer proceeding is distinct from the MDL 1720 cash fund; the $38 billion figure circulating in coverage represents cumulative merchant savings on interchange rates over a five-year period, not a cash distribution pool.
 
Preliminary approval is not final approval — objections, procedural delays, and appellate review remain possible.


A fresh wave of follow-on litigation has also opened: 
On April 21, 2026, Potayto-Potahto, LLC and two co-plaintiffs filed a class action complaint in the S.D.N.Y. against Visa Inc., Visa U.S.A., Visa International, Mastercard Incorporated, and Mastercard International, alleging violations of federal antitrust laws consistent with MDL 1720 allegations, on behalf of merchants accepting Visa or Mastercard credit cards since January 25, 2019.
 
The named plaintiffs concurrently filed a motion for summary judgment before the MDL 1720 court seeking a declaration that the Damages Class Settlement release — which by its terms prospectively releases claims through August 2028 — does not bar their damages claims.
 That motion is the dispositive procedural question for any merchant in the 2019-to-present damages window.

### BCBS Antitrust — Tier-1 Distribution Active, Tier-2 Wave Underway

*In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala.).


The initial round of payments from the $2.67 billion settlement fund began distribution in May 2026, approximately 13 years after the cases were consolidated as MDL 2406 in 2013 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Alabama, the settlement administrator confirmed.
 
A motion for distribution of settlement funds was filed April 23, 2026 and approved by the court April 24, 2026; payments commenced on or around May 11, 2026.



Approximately $1.9 billion will be distributed to roughly six million claimants, with individual payouts varying based on coverage history and premiums paid.
 
Tier-1 (Individual) Wave 2 of prepaid debit cards was mailed in the week of May 19; paper-check rollout began May 25.
 
Tier-2 (Employee) notification emails started June 1 for the first batch — approximately 15% of validated Tier-2 claims.
 
Total Tier-1 wave completion is estimated by end of July 2026.
 That date is now, operationally speaking, this week — claimants who have not received payment and who elected Tier-1 should be escalating to Epiq Class Action Services.


Final court approval was granted August 9, 2022; appeals were filed shortly after, but the Supreme Court denied review on June 24, 2024, affirming the settlement as final.
 The nearly four-year gap between final approval and first dollar of distribution is a useful benchmarking data point for gauging timeline expectations in *Bartz* — particularly where appellate exposure remains unresolved.

### Keller Postman / Google Mass Arbitration — Claims Campaign Live, Remedies Ruling Pending


Keller Postman announced May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding the company engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.
 
The $218 billion gross damages figure is based on calculations from a retained economist, covering harm from both the search monopoly (Judge Mehta, D.D.C.) and the ad tech monopoly (Judge Brinkema, E.D. Va.).



According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases estimated anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs.
 
Eligible businesses may be entitled to recover up to three times actual damages under the Clayton Act; Keller Postman estimates most qualifying businesses could recover up to 30% of their total Google Ads spend during the relevant period.
 The eligible period runs from August 2016 forward; 
the firm cautions that deadlines vary by state, and that earlier action increases the probability of a successful claim within applicable statutes of limitations.


**Structure and leverage mechanics:** Because Google's advertiser contracts contain mandatory arbitration clauses, 
claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action; Keller Postman is coordinating thousands of individual claims on behalf of businesses nationwide.
 
For large advertisers — some with hundreds of millions of dollars in annual Google media spend — mass anonymous arbitration provides cover to pursue recovery without triggering account relationship concerns.
 
In addition to the private claimants, Keller Postman represents seven state attorneys general in a parallel proceeding.
 That AG track creates a second pressure vector on Google that is not subject to individual arbitration dismissal on procedural grounds.

The campaign's macro pressure depends substantially on the remedies ruling from Judge Brinkema (E.D. Va.) in the ad tech monopoly case, expected in 2026 but not yet issued. Until that ruling is final, the damages quantum in the ad tech sub-claim remains an estimate. Claimants entering the arbitration now are effectively front-running the remedies order — which cuts both ways on timeline and settlement pressure.
