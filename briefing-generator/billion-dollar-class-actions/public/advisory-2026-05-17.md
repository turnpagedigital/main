## I. Blue Cross Blue Shield Subscriber Antitrust ($2.67B) — Distribution Now Live
The Blue Cross Blue Shield subscriber-antitrust settlement is now in its distribution phase. The N.D. Ala. court granted final approval in October 2020; the subscriber plaintiffs filed a [https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/blue-cross-blue-shield-267b-antitrust-settlement-payments-set-to-begin-may-2026](motion on April 23, 2026) asking the court to approve the distribution plan for approximately 7.8 million claimants, with the first payment cycle commencing May 11 and continuing through the weekend of May 15–17. Combined with the BCBS Provider settlement at $2.8 billion that received final approval on August 19, 2025, the total BCBS antitrust resolution exceeds $5.47 billion — one of the largest antitrust settlements in U.S. history, and the controlling reference point for the post-NCAA-style multi-decade-overcharge antitrust class architecture.
The distribution mechanics for the subscriber settlement run through the standard claims-administrator pipeline (Epiq / KCC family). Per-claimant amounts will vary substantially based on the underlying claim type (individual versus self-funded employer subscriber, length of plan participation, premium-history documentation). Practitioners on the defense-side bar should note that the BCBS distribution mechanics — particularly the documentation burden on individual subscribers — will become the de facto template for any subsequent mega-antitrust resolution requiring direct distribution to a large consumer class.

## II. Bayer-Roundup $7.25B Mass-Tort Settlement
The Bayer-Roundup settlement of $7.25 billion received preliminary approval from Judge Timothy Boyer in St. Louis and is now moving toward the July 9, 2026 final-approval hearing. Class counsel are seeking $675 million in attorney fees, equating to 9.3% of the total settlement fund — at the low end of mass-tort fee percentages and likely to clear the lodestar cross-check. The settlement is designed to resolve both existing claims and the future-claims universe tied to long-term glyphosate exposure and non-Hodgkin lymphoma diagnoses.
The settlement is a hybrid mass-tort architecture, leveraging the experience of the prior Roundup MDL waves to produce a unitary disposition with a future-claim trust component. Objector-practice activity has been muted relative to the early Roundup waves; the principal contested terrain is the future-claim fund's adequacy and the medical-causation criteria for the future-claimant pool. The final-approval ruling is expected at or shortly after the July 9 hearing.

## IV. Settlement-Administrator Litigation Watchlist
Settlement-administrator litigation — disputes over notice adequacy, claims-processing accuracy, and breach-reporting under settlement-administrator engagements — continues to be a live tail risk in the mega-settlement architecture. No new SA-litigation rulings of consequence over May 15–17. Practitioners should note that the post-Equifax data-breach SA-litigation landscape has begun to set the working standards for SA-engagement contracting, and any large 2026 settlement should expect SA-engagement language to be much more carefully negotiated than in the early-2020s mega-deal cycle.

## V. Objector-Practice Cycle
The objector-practice cycle in mega-deals continues to follow the post-Andrew and post-In re Subway BMTs framework, with court-supervised case-management orders effectively constraining the volume and substance of contested objections. No new published objector-cycle rulings over May 15–17. The Eighth Circuit and Third Circuit objector-procedure doctrines remain the operative cross-jurisdictional split that practitioners should monitor.
