### I. Blue Cross Blue Shield Subscriber Settlement — Eleventh Circuit Objector Wave & Distribution Hold

The $2.67 billion BCBS subscriber settlement (*In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, N.D. Ala., Judge R. David Proctor) entered final approval on October 16, 2024, but has not achieved the clean post-approval trajectory class counsel anticipated. On May 22, 2026, lead counsel at Hausfeld LLP and Whatley Kallas filed an emergency motion requesting Judge Proctor hold distribution triggers pending resolution of a consolidated objector appeal docketed at the Eleventh Circuit as *Moody v. Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama*, No. 24-14023 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The objector coalition, led by former MDL steering committee member Adam Levitt of DiCello Levitt, argues that the plan of distribution's "large group" exclusion — which fences out self-insured ERISA plans with fewer than 51 employees — violates the equal-treatment principle articulated in *Sullivan v. DB Investments*, 667 F.3d 273 (3d Cir. 2011) (en banc), and is structurally inconsistent with the anti-steering injunctive relief flowing to all subscriber classes (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

At stake is approximately $620 million in distribution tranche proceeds that claims administrator Epiq Class Action Solutions has staged but not yet dispersed to small-employer class members. Class counsel's emergency brief argues the distribution hold would cost the certified class an estimated $4.2 million in escrow drag per month and that the objectors' equitable arguments were fully aired before Judge Proctor at the October 2024 fairness hearing (__[Hausfeld LLP — needs verification](https://hausfeld.com)__). The Eleventh Circuit's procedural motion panel has a response deadline of June 3, 2026. This is a high-watch matter: if the Circuit grants a distribution stay pending appeal, the entire post-judgment timeline slips into 2027.

### II. Roundup MDL — Post-*Loper Bright* Preemption Order & Implications for Mass-Tort Class Architecture

On May 23, 2026, Judge Vince Chhabria (N.D. Cal., Courtroom 4) issued a 34-page order in *In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2741, declining to certify a consolidated preemption question under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) for interlocutory review by the Ninth Circuit (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). The ruling is analytically significant beyond the MDL itself. Monsanto/Bayer had argued that in the wake of *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 144 S. Ct. 2244 (2024), courts owe EPA's glyphosate registration findings no *Chevron* deference, and that de novo statutory construction of FIFRA's express preemption clause, 7 U.S.C. § 136v(b), compels dismissal of state-law failure-to-warn claims across all 54,000+ pending cases.

Judge Chhabria's order rejected that framing on two independent grounds. First, the Court held that *Loper Bright*'s reach does not retroactively destabilize *pre-Chevron* FIFRA preemption doctrine established in *Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC*, 544 U.S. 431 (2005), which conducted its own statutory construction without Chevron's aid. Second, the Court found that the § 1292(b) "controlling question of law" prong was not satisfied because resolution of the preemption defense requires individualized state-law labeling records that vary across plaintiffs (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The denial means Bayer's preemption argument remains a case-by-case defense rather than an MDL-wide dispositive motion, preserving the mass-tort litigation's individual-causation trial track. Bayer has reserved the right to seek mandamus relief in the Ninth Circuit, a step its outside counsel at Wilkinson Stekloff previewed in a post-hearing statement.

For class-action practitioners: the Chhabria order has spillover relevance to *Atrazine* and *Paraquat* MDLs, where defendants have filed analogous post-*Loper Bright* preemption motions premised on EPA registration actions. It also reinforces the view — increasingly common in mass-tort circles — that *Loper Bright*'s practical litigation effect in preemption-heavy MDLs has been overstated: courts simply revert to *Bates*-style text-first analysis and reach largely the same result.

### III. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — AAA Hearing Scheduling & Fee Architecture

The Keller Postman-led Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign — the largest coordinated mass-arbitration effort in U.S. history by claimed damages, with aggregate demands estimated at approximately $218 billion across more than 25,000 claimants — reached a structural inflection on May 21, 2026 when AAA formally issued scheduling orders for the first wave of 14,221 individual arbitration hearings (__[The Block / Tech Policy Press — needs verification](https://techpolicypress.com)__). Hearings are slated on a rolling basis beginning August 11, 2026, before a panel of more than 800 AAA arbitrators pre-qualified under the Commercial Arbitration Rules, as modified by the parties' arbitration agreement embedded in Google Ads Terms of Service (revised October 2022).

The practical bottleneck is administrative cost. Under AAA's Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules (effective August 1, 2023), Google is required to pay the filing fees for all claimants who have complied with the pre-arbitration notice protocol — a per-claimant administrative fee of $2,750 for commercial disputes plus an $825 per-claimant hearing deposit. With 14,221 first-wave claimants, Google's aggregate AAA administrative exposure for this wave alone is approximately $50.5 million, before any merits engagement (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). Keller Postman's strategy mirrors the template it deployed against DoorDash in 2020 (*Abernathy v. DoorDash, Inc.*, No. 3:19-cv-07545, N.D. Cal., Judge William Alsup), where fee-pressure leverage forced a settlement structure.

Google's opposition posture — documented in filings before AAA's administrative panel — is threefold: (1) challenge claimant-identity verification under the pre-arbitration notice protocol; (2) seek a "bellwether" scheduling modification under AAA Rule MA-3 that would defer all but 100 test cases; and (3) preserve the right to seek California state-court consolidation under Code Civ. Proc. § 1281.11 if arbitration proceeds produce irreconcilable awards. Keller Postman has opposed all three tracks, arguing that the October 2022 ToS revision that Google relies on is itself unconscionable under *McGill v. Citibank, N.A.*, 2 Cal. 5th 945 (2017), because it retroactively eliminated the class-action carve-out (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). The merits theory underlying all 25,000+ claims is that Google's dominance in display advertising (AdSense, DV360, Google Ad Manager) constitutes an unlawful monopoly under Sherman Act § 2, with each advertiser sustaining overcharge damages calculable from bidding-auction logs (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### IV. Purdue Pharma — Second Circuit Argument on Plan Injection Standing & Settlement-Class Intersection

The Purdue Pharma restructuring (*In re Purdue Pharma L.P.*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 19-23649, Judge Sean Lane) returned to the Second Circuit's oral argument calendar on May 20, 2026, in a proceeding captioned *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, No. 23-1098 (2d Cir.), addressing the singular question left open after the Supreme Court's June 2024 reversal (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__): whether the reorganized debtor, operating as Knoa Pharma, may invoke the confirmed plan's third-party release and channeling injunction against tort claimants who did not affirmatively opt out — a question the SCOTUS majority in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024), expressly declined to answer, limiting its holding to the Sackler non-debtor release.

Judge Lane approved a modified plan on December 3, 2025 that restructures the settlement into a bifurcated structure: a $6.0 billion Global Settlement Trust (distributing cash to governmental, hospital, and NAS claimant subclasses) and a $1.8 billion Individual Victims Trust (IVT) operating under a point-matrix claims-resolution facility (__[Weil Gotshal — needs verification](https://weil.com)__). The Second Circuit's May 20 argument focused on whether the channeling injunction governing IVT claims — which bars parallel state-court litigation — exceeds § 524(g)'s authorization outside the asbestos context, and whether CAFA's mass-action jurisdiction provision, 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(11), independently gives the district court (Judge Colleen McMahon, S.D.N.Y.) appellate supervisory authority over IVT awards. The DOJ's Office of the U.S. Trustee, represented at argument by Civil Division AUSA Rachel Smorto, took the position that § 524(g) is limited to asbestos debtors by its plain text and structural context, and that extension to opioid mass-tort claimants requires congressional action (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). A Second Circuit decision is expected by late September 2026.

### V. Visa/Mastercard Interchange, Meta Waiver-Track & Apple App Store — Continuing Fronts

**Visa/Mastercard Interchange:** *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo Brodie) remains in a holding pattern on the Rule 23(b)(3) merits class, after Judge Brodie on April 30, 2026 declined to compel production of Visa and Mastercard's internal "network fee" modeling documents, ruling them protected under the common-interest doctrine as between the card networks and their issuing-bank co-defendants (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Class counsel at Robins Kaplan have noticed an immediate appeal to the Second Circuit under the collateral-order doctrine. The Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive class — through which merchants recover going-forward interchange caps — is separately proceeding toward a June 30, 2026 trial readiness conference.

**Meta Waiver-Track Mass Arbitration:** Following the Northern District of California's February 2026 order in *In re Meta Platforms Biometric Data Privacy Litigation*, No. 3:20-cv-08570 (N.D. Cal., Judge James Donato), which upheld Meta's arbitration clause but carved out pre-2021 "waiver track" claimants who filed before the clause became effective, approximately 68,000 claimants are proceeding in JAMS arbitration (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__). JAMS has assigned dispute-resolution batches of 500 claimants per arbitrator; the first batch determinations are due August 1, 2026. Claimant-side counsel at Dicello Levitt and Edelson PC have flagged that JAMS's "Streamlined Arbitration Rules" — which Meta invoked to cap per-session hearing time at 3 hours — may be procedurally unconscionable as applied to Illinois BIPA claims that require individual damages proof under 740 ILCS 14/20.

**Apple App Store:** *Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640 (N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers) continues its post-contempt trajectory. Apple's May 8, 2026 compliance filing disclosed that it has implemented the anti-steering injunction by permitting out-of-app purchase links but imposes a 27% commission on external transactions, a rate the court's May 2026 compliance order characterized as potentially inconsistent with the injunction's purpose (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The developing class-action overlay — *Cameron v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:19-cv-03074 (N.D. Cal., Judge Gonzalez Rogers) — awaits the court's ruling on whether Apple's revised commission structure triggers a renewed settlement-class reopening motion under the 2021 $100 million developer settlement's modification clause.

### VI. Securities Mega-Cycle — NVIDIA, Boeing, Block Inc.

**NVIDIA:** *In re NVIDIA Corporation Securities Litigation*, No. 5:24-cv-01898 (N.D. Cal., Judge Beth Labson Freeman) is proceeding toward the lead plaintiff's amended complaint deadline of June 16, 2026. The derivative complaint filed by the Oklahoma Firefighters Pension & Retirement System alleges that NVIDIA's disclosures regarding export-control compliance with the October 2022 and October 2023 BIS chip rules, 15 C.F.R. § 744.11, were materially false in light of evidence that H100 and H800 GPUs were being routed to restricted Chinese end-users through Singaporean intermediaries (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). Aggregate market-cap decline during the class period (September 14, 2023 to February 21, 2025) is estimated at approximately $212 billion at its trough, placing this case firmly in the first tier of securities mega-cycle candidates.

**Boeing:** *In re Boeing Co. Aircraft Securities Litigation*, No. 1:19-cv-08514 (N.D. Ill., Judge Jorge Alonso) has a class certification briefing schedule running through August 2026. Lead plaintiff Arkansas Teacher Retirement System seeks certification of a putative class alleging § 10(b) violations based on Boeing's MCAS-related communications through the 737 MAX crisis period.

**Block Inc.:** *Seltzer v. Block, Inc.*, No. 3:24-cv-02989 (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria) — the Hindenburg Research-triggered securities fraud action — has a motion-to-dismiss argument scheduled for June 9, 2026.
