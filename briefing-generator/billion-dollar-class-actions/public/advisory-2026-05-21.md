### I. Blue Cross Blue Shield Settlement — Distribution & Appellate Posture

The *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, N.D. Ala., Chief Judge Karon Owen Bowdre presiding, remains the largest-funded healthcare antitrust settlement in U.S. history at **$2.67 billion**. The claims administrator, JND Legal Administration, has now processed approximately **2.31 million subscriber claims** and **118,000 provider-side claims**, with aggregate checks issued exceeding **$1.7 billion** as of the May 16, 2026 disbursement cycle (__[JND Legal Administration settlement portal — needs verification](https://www.bcbssettlement.com)__). The residual fund is being held pending resolution of three categories of disputed claims: (1) group health plan claimants asserting ERISA § 502(a)(1)(B) standing whose submissions were flagged for insufficient plan-document support; (2) a set of roughly 4,100 opt-outs who have since sought to re-enter the settlement through a tolling motion filed April 28, 2026 (ECF No. 3,812); and (3) a certified subclass of self-funded employer plans whose allocation share is contested on remand following the Eleventh Circuit's December 2025 decision in *Anthem, Inc. v. Blue Cross Blue Shield Settlement Class*, No. 23-12187 (11th Cir.), which vacated in part the weighting formula applied to fully-insured vs. self-funded claimants (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Counsel for the self-funded employer subclass, Cohen Milstein Sellers & Toll and Boies Schiller Flexner, filed a revised allocation methodology brief on May 12, 2026 (ECF No. 3,819), arguing that the recalculated share for self-funded plans should be increased from 18.4% to 24.1% of the net settlement fund, representing a differential of approximately **$150 million**. The United States Department of Justice Antitrust Division filed a statement of interest on May 19, 2026 (ECF No. 3,824) declining to take a position on allocation mechanics but flagging that any reallocation formula must be consistent with the Court's prior liability findings under Section 1 of the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1 (__[DOJ Antitrust Division — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/atr)__). The next briefing deadline is June 9, 2026, with a fairness hearing on the revised allocation scheduled for August 3, 2026 before Chief Judge Bowdre in Courtroom 5A, Hugo L. Black U.S. Courthouse, Birmingham.

### II. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — AAA Fee Allocation Ruling & N.D. Cal. Intervention

The most consequential procedural development this week in the mass-arbitration space involves the Keller Postman campaign against Google LLC arising from alleged anticompetitive overcharging in the Google Ads auction platform. The operative theory tracks the complaint-side findings from *United States v. Google LLC*, No. 1:23-cv-00108 (E.D. Va., Judge Leonie Brinkema), where the DOJ proved Google's illegal monopolization of the open-web display advertising technology stack under Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act—findings Keller Postman is now leveraging in individual arbitration demands asserting aggregate damages in the range of **$218 billion** across approximately **260,000 advertiser-claimants** (__[Keller Postman firm announcements — needs verification](https://www.kellerpostman.com)__).

On May 19, 2026, the American Arbitration Association's Commercial Disputes panel issued an administrative ruling (AAA Case Cluster No. 01-26-GOOG-ADV) modifying the fee-allocation framework governing the first wave of 12,000 selected test-claimant demands. Under the prior scheduling order, Google bore 100% of arbitrator and administrative fees pursuant to its own Google Ads Terms of Service arbitration clause, which was incorporated by reference into the demands. The May 19 ruling, citing AAA Commercial Rule R-57 and the AAA's Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings, determined that where a mass filing exceeds 25 co-pending demands, AAA may apportion "processing fees" to claimant-side in proportion to damages sought—a reading Keller Postman immediately contested as inconsistent with *Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis*, 584 U.S. 497 (2018), and the enforceability of Google's own consumer-side fee-shifting clause (__[AAA Commercial Rules — needs verification](https://www.adr.org)__).

Keller Postman filed an emergency motion to stay the fee-allocation order and compel arbitration under the original cost-sharing structure in *Alphabet Inc. v. Keller Postman LLC*, No. 5:26-cv-02841-CRB (N.D. Cal., Judge Charles R. Breyer), on May 20, 2026. Google's response is due May 27, 2026. The stakes are significant: if the AAA ruling stands and claimants must bear even 15% of arbitrator fees on $218B in aggregate demand value, total claimant-side up-front costs could reach the high tens of millions—a figure that would effectively price out individual small-advertiser claimants and hollow out the campaign's leverage architecture (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge Breyer has indicated he may consolidate this with the related antitrust class certification proceedings in *In re Google Advertising Antitrust Litigation*, No. 5:20-cv-03556-BLF, at the July 14 status conference.

### III. Apple App Store & Meta Waiver Track — Status Snapshot

**Apple App Store Mass Arbitration.** The Keller Postman App Store campaign—arising from the *Epic Games v. Apple* monopoly findings, 67 F.4th 946 (9th Cir. 2023), and Apple's continued imposition of the 30% "App Tax" following remand—has now served approximately **67,000 individual arbitration demands** under AAA consumer rules. Apple has continued its litigation-side effort to litigate arbitrability in bulk; *Apple Inc. v. Keller Postman*, No. 3:26-cv-01192-JSW (N.D. Cal., Judge Jeffrey S. White), is scheduled for an arbitrability hearing on June 18, 2026. Apple argues that the demands fail threshold "standing" prerequisites built into the 2024 revised App Store developer agreement, while Keller Postman counters that the revised agreement cannot retroactively apply to claims accruing pre-revision (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Aggregate claimed damages exceed **$50 billion** based on 30% commission overcharge calculations extrapolated from Apple's disclosed App Store revenue of approximately $24B annually.

**Meta Platforms — Waiver Track.** Meta's strategy of filing pre-emptive Section 1281.97 (Cal. Code Civ. Proc.) waiver motions in San Mateo Superior Court—arguing claimants failed to timely tender arbitration fees—suffered a setback on May 15, 2026, when Judge Marie S. Weiner granted claimants' motion to vacate the prior waiver finding in *Draper v. Meta Platforms, Inc.*, No. 21-CIV-05340 (San Mateo Super. Ct.), holding that Meta itself had failed to comply with the arbitration provider's payment scheduling notice in a manner that triggered the waiver reciprocally under § 1281.99 (__[Courthouse News — needs verification](https://www.courthousenews.com)__). The ruling, though state-court and non-binding in federal proceedings, is being cited by claimants' counsel in approximately 140 pending waiver disputes across the JAMS consumer roster. Meta's motion for reconsideration is due June 2, 2026.

### IV. Purdue Pharma — Post-*Harrington* Restructuring & Third-Party Release Litigation

The fallout from *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), continues to reshape both the Purdue reorganization and the broader landscape of third-party-release class settlements. In the Purdue proceedings in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, Judge Sean H. Lane (White Plains Division), the reorganization parties filed their **Third Amended Plan of Reorganization** on May 7, 2026 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 19-23649, ECF No. 7,104), which eliminates the nonconsensual Sackler-family release that the Supreme Court struck down and instead proposes a **consensual contribution structure** under which members of the Sackler family pay an additional **$1.175 billion** (bringing total Sackler contributions to approximately **$7.375 billion**) in exchange for individual consensual releases by each personal-injury claimant who affirmatively elects the settlement (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The plan introduces a **dual-track** election form: Track A (consensual release, higher allocation from the Sackler contribution pool) and Track B (no release, participation in litigation trust with uncertain and likely lower recovery). The disclosure statement hearing is set for June 23, 2026 before Judge Lane. Critically, the Ad Hoc Committee of NAS (Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome) Victims, represented by Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro, filed an objection on May 19, 2026 (ECF No. 7,116) arguing that the Track B allocation of only **4.2%** of the opioid abatement trust fund for NAS claimants is grossly inadequate compared to the 8.7% the committee negotiated in the vacated prior plan (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Separately, the Purdue criminal restitution order—$7.4B in DOJ consent judgment obligations—remains a senior claim against the estate, and its interaction with the plan's creditor waterfall is a live dispute.

### V. Visa/Mastercard Interchange & Securities Mega-Cycle — Carry-Forward

**Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL.** *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo K. Brodie), remains in the allocation-review phase following the Second Circuit's affirmance of the $5.54B settlement in *Fikes Wholesale, Inc. v. HSBC Bank USA, N.A.*, 62 F.4th 704 (2d Cir. 2023). The Mastercard portion of the settlement fund has begun secondary distribution; approximately **3.6 million merchant claimants** have received initial payments averaging **$1,200 per location**, with a second distribution tranche expected Q3 2026 (__[Epiq Class Action settlement administration — needs verification](https://www.epiqclassaction.com)__). However, the Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive relief class—whose sweeping relief was separately vacated—remains in front of Judge Brodie on remand; plaintiffs' revised injunctive-relief brief is due July 7, 2026, and must address whether the 2023 Visa network rule changes (reducing default interchange by 10 basis points for small merchants) constitute adequate prospective relief.

**Securities Mega-Cycle.** The Bartz v. Anthropic securities action, *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-06811-WHA (N.D. Cal., Judge William H. Alsup), consolidated with the broader Anthropic copyright litigation, is currently bifurcated: the copyright claims are proceeding to a June 2026 bench trial on the "training ingestion" theory, while the securities-fraud class claims (alleging misstatements about training-data licensing compliance to pre-IPO investors) are stayed pending the copyright trial outcome under Judge Alsup's February 2026 case-management order (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Plaintiffs' lead counsel, Susman Godfrey LLP and Baker & Hostetler, estimate the securities-fraud damages range at **$1.2B to $4.8B** depending on the copyright liability finding. A merits ruling on the copyright claims before year-end 2026 could materially accelerate the securities class timeline.
