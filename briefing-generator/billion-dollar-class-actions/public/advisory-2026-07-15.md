### *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Gate: Still Open as of July 13, 2026

The core posture has not changed since our last advisory: 
Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement; in a supplemental filing ordered after the May 14 fairness hearing, Anthropic urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs, arguing the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect," and that the remaining three late opt-out requests should also be denied; final approval remains pending.



Judge Martínez-Olguín held the fairness hearing on May 14, 2026. Post-hearing, the court ordered Anthropic to file by May 21, 2026 a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored; Plaintiffs were simultaneously directed to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026 (Dkt. 646-1).


The most recent docket activity confirmed by the tracked-case ground truth is Dkt. 679 (filed July 13, 2026), described as Anthropic's opposition to a related brief. 
CourtListener shows the docket last updated July 2, 2026.
 The final approval order has not been confirmed entered in any publicly available source as of July 15, 2026.

**Fee trajectory — the number that controls net per-work recovery.** 
Class counsel urged the court to grant final approval along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 
Anthropic separately contended that $75 million in fees requested by nonclass counsel was "far too high," arguing there was "scant justification" for the request.
 The fee dispute — with two competing fee stacks still pending resolution — is one reason the approval order has not yet issued. The delta between $187.5 million and any further reduction meaningfully affects the per-work distribution: 
class counsel stated at the May 14 hearing that the payout per work will be approximately $3,100, with a 92.77% participation rate for works on the works list.
 
Class counsel filed papers showing 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% rate — vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in the March 19, 2026 motion for final approval filed 11 days before the March 30 claims deadline.


**Installment schedule and appellate exposure.** 
Anthropic will fund the settlement fund in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The April 30, 2026 installment (the $300 million tranche per the detailed timeline) would have been triggered upon issuance of the final approval order; that tranche's timing now depends on when the order issues and whether any objector files a timely notice of appeal. 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 Any appeal to the Ninth Circuit tolls distribution entirely. Given the 7 objectors at the fairness hearing, appellate risk — while modest given the claims rate — is live.

**Late opt-out and ClaimsHero dynamic.** This remains the discrete litigation sub-issue blocking the order. 
A California federal judge earlier blasted Arizona law firm ClaimsHero Holdings LLC for encouraging authors to opt out of Anthropic's $1.5 billion deal, saying the firm appears to be seeking "a nuisance settlement" and warning against a legal strategy he called "extortion."
 The five late opt-out requests now pending before Judge Martínez-Olguín are the residue of that dynamic. The court's resolution of those requests — accept them and narrow the class, deny them and lock the release — will appear in the final approval order.

**Structural precedent.** 
Judge Alsup held on summary judgment that Anthropic's use of lawfully acquired books for AI training was "quintessentially transformative" and thus protected by fair use; however, he simultaneously ruled that Anthropic's creation and retention of a "central library" comprised in part of pirated works was not transformative and constituted infringement.
 The bifurcation — fair use for licensed ingestion, no fair use for shadow-library piracy — is the liability theory every downstream AI copyright plaintiff is now building on. 
There are dozens of other AI copyright suits at varying stages — including consolidated actions against OpenAI and Microsoft, and active putative class actions against Meta, Apple, Salesforce, and Adobe — and several appear to be adopting the strategy that proved effective in *Bartz*: focus on the underlying piracy rather than challenging AI training as such.

### Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Keller Postman Campaign (FAA § 2; Clayton Act § 4)

This is the most consequential mass-arbitration development in the current cycle. 
On May 11, 2026, Keller Postman announced it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding the company engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.


The legal architecture is unusual: 
while many companies that displayed ads purchased through Google — including USA Today Co. and Advance Publications Inc. — have sued for damages since the antitrust rulings in 2024, advertiser contracts with Google require mandatory arbitration over legal disputes.
 
The Google mass arbitration may be the first to seek to represent corporate plaintiffs, as most prior mass arbitrations have sought to arbitrate consumer or labor-related claims; Keller noted that arbitration clauses in Google's advertiser contracts effectively prevent damages recovery via class action litigation.


**Damages quantum.** 
The $218 billion figure is based on calculations from an economist Keller Postman retained, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly — the two separate rulings that established Google's liability.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs.
 The firm's working estimate for per-claimant recovery: 
potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law (Clayton Act § 4).


**Underlying liability predicates.** 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) ruled in favor of the DOJ, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in its advertising business.
 
Both DOJ and Google filed competing remedies proposals in November 2025, with the government seeking forced divestiture of Google's AdX exchange and publisher ad server, while Google argued behavioral changes would suffice.
 
Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling remains expected; Keller Postman is actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines.


**Timeline and mechanics.** 
Similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between filing of claims and resolution.
 
The firm is targeting companies of all sizes that spent money on Google search or display advertising on or after August 2016.
 The campaign is proceeding before private AAA/JAMS panels; because arbitration demands are not filed in court, the public record is limited to intake data and firm disclosures. The liability predicates are unusually strong by mass arbitration standards — two confirmed federal rulings, not merely regulatory allegations — but Google retains the right to contest causation and damages quantum in each individual proceeding.

### *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala.) — Distribution Underway

This settlement has crossed the threshold that *Bartz* has not yet reached: money is moving. 
A $2.67 billion BCBS antitrust settlement began paying out on May 11, 2026; on April 23, 2026, subscriber plaintiffs filed a motion in the Northern District of Alabama asking the court to approve a distribution plan for nearly 7.8 million claimants.
 
Of the $2.67 billion total, approximately $1.9 billion will be distributed to valid claimants after legal and administrative fees are deducted, under the oversight of U.S. District Judge R. David Proctor.


The antitrust theory: 
the BCBS payout stems from a 2013 lawsuit alleging that more than 35 BCBS affiliates violated the Sherman Antitrust Act by dividing the U.S. into exclusive "service areas," effectively agreeing not to compete with one another.
 Claims denominator math for BCBS is largely resolved now that distribution has commenced; the residual litigation risk sits with the separate provider settlement track at bcbsprovidersettlement.com.

### Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL — Class B Exchange Offer Triggered; New Merchant Suits Filed

The Visa interchange complex (MDL 1720, E.D.N.Y.) generated two concrete developments in Q1-Q2 2026. First, the Class B exchange offer trigger: 
as disclosed in Visa's Form 10-K filed November 6, 2025, the estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved damage claims was approximately $49.6 billion as of October 1, 2023, and approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025.
 
Visa believes that, together with recent dismissals and upon dismissal of certain claims in *7-Eleven, Inc. v. Visa Inc.*, No. 13-cv-4442 (AKH) (S.D.N.Y.), the estimated interchange fees at issue in unresolved claims will fall below 50% of the October 2023 amount
 — satisfying the condition for a successive Class B exchange offer authorized by Visa's board on February 13, 2026.

Second, new litigation filings: 
on April 21, 2026, Potayto-Potahto LLC and two other merchants filed a class action complaint in the S.D.N.Y. against Visa, Visa U.S.A., Visa International, Mastercard, and Mastercard International, asserting federal antitrust violations on behalf of merchants that accepted Visa and/or Mastercard credit cards since January 25, 2019.
 
On April 20, 2026, a group of merchants from across Europe filed a claim in the UK High Court against several Visa entities, alleging that interchange fees on European transactions from January 1, 2019 to present are an unlawful restriction of competition.
 The injunctive-relief class settlement entered November 10, 2025 is separately subject to court approval: 
on November 10, 2025, Visa entered into a superseding and amended settlement agreement to resolve the interchange MDL injunctive relief class claims, subject to court approval; on December 23, 2025, Visa deposited $500 million into its litigation escrow account under its U.S. retrospective responsibility plan.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and claimants should prioritize the following in the current cycle: In *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417), monitor PACER Dkt. 679 and immediately downstream entries for the final approval order — once it issues, the 30-day appeal window starts running, and any objector appeal to the Ninth Circuit tolls the entire $1.5 billion distribution including the September 25, 2026 installment ($450 million); watch whether Judge Martínez-Olguín accepts or rejects the five late opt-outs before or in the approval order, as the outcome affects the released-claims scope. For the Google advertiser mass arbitration, any U.S. business that purchased Google Search or display advertising on or after August 2016 should evaluate whether its Google advertiser agreement contains a mandatory arbitration clause (virtually all post-2016 agreements do) and, if so, assess claims before state statutes of limitations run — Keller Postman is publicly citing state-specific filing deadlines as a reason for urgency; recovery projections of up to 30% of ad spend subject to Clayton Act § 4 trebling are aggressive but grounded in confirmed federal liability. For BCBS (MDL 2406), the May 11, 2026 distribution wave is underway and approved claimants need no further action; the residual risk is payment timing across the roughly 7.8 million claimant pool, not substantive approval risk. On the Visa interchange front, any merchant with claims post-January 25, 2019 should confirm whether it is covered by the existing MDL 1720 damages class settlement or falls into the new post-cutoff litigation cohort now represented by the April 21, 2026 Potayto-Potahto filing, as the two tracks have different recovery and timing profiles.
