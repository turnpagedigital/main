### *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 — Final Approval Pending, Distribution Timeline Slipping


Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín presided over the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and heard from class counsel and objectors.
 
After a 75-minute hearing, the judge declined, for now, to issue the final approval order, though the tone of the proceeding led most observers to expect approval will follow.


The structural facts remain unchanged from prior coverage. 
Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion into a settlement fund; plaintiff's counsel sought 25% of the fund — $375 million — in fees, together with administrative costs to JND Legal Administration and $50,000 service awards per named plaintiff.
 
Prior to the hearing, class counsel revised the fee request downward to $187.5 million.
 The fee-to-fund ratio at that revised figure is 12.5%, meaningfully below the 25% benchmark, which should ease the court's lodestar cross-check concern.

The claims rate is the central distributional variable. 
Class counsel reported that 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a 91.3% rate — vastly exceeding the 54% rate reported in the March 19, 2026 motion for final approval, filed eleven days before the March 30 claims deadline.
 
That high participation rate means earlier forecasts of an above-$3,000 per-work payout will not materialize; the per-work distribution will land near the original $3,100 figure.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson confirmed at the May 14 hearing that the expected payout per work is $3,100.


On installment sequencing: 
Anthropic funds the $1.5 billion in four installments — October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The first two installments have landed. The third ($375 million tranche) is due September 25, 2026, which creates a hard backstop for distribution mechanics. 
The Settlement Administrator targeted June 11, 2026 for distribution calculations, with payment disbursement beginning June 2026 or later, subject to the final approval order being entered.



Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 Two risk vectors remain open: (1) whether any objector files a notice of appeal from the final approval order, which would stall distribution while the Ninth Circuit adjudicates; and (2) the ClaimsHero opt-out litigation. 
A California federal judge previously rebuked Arizona law firm ClaimsHero Holdings LLC for encouraging authors to opt out of the settlement, characterizing its strategy as "extortion" and suggesting it was seeking "a nuisance settlement."
 The ClaimsHero posture — opt-outs who may demand separate consideration from Anthropic — is a residual drag on finality, though the court has been pointedly hostile to it. 
By order following the May 14 hearing, Anthropic was directed to file by May 21, 2026 a supplemental brief on whether late opt-outs should be honored, and plaintiffs were directed to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections.


The scope limitation that practitioners must track: 
Judge Alsup's fair-use ruling covers AI training on legally acquired books; the class was certified only for the piracy theory — unauthorized acquisition from LibGen and PiLiMi — not for training itself.
 That means the settlement resolves the shadow-library infringement track, not the AI training fair-use question, which remains live in parallel proceedings against OpenAI, Meta, and others.

### *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala.) — Tier-1 Wave Nearing Completion


Initial distribution of payments to damages class members began May 11, 2026 in the BCBS antitrust settlement, Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP.
 Distribution is proceeding in waves administered by JND Legal Administration.


Millions of Americans who filed claims are now receiving payments from the $2.67 billion settlement; approximately $1.9 billion will be distributed to roughly six million claimants, with individual payouts varying based on coverage history and premiums paid.
 
Claimants with a calculated distribution of $200 or more are receiving 90% in the initial round, with 10% held in reserve for a second distribution; payment methods include check, PayPal, Venmo, and electronic debit card.



Tier 1 (individual) Wave 2 of prepaid debit cards mailed the week of May 19; paper checks began rolling out May 25; Tier 2 (employee) notification emails started June 1 for the first batch, covering approximately 15% of validated Tier-2 claims.
 
The Tier-1 wave is expected to complete by end of July 2026.
 Practitioners advising institutional claimants should note the 10% holdback — the second distribution timeline is not yet published.

The underlying case — 
plaintiffs alleged that BCBS and its regional affiliates violated antitrust laws by agreeing not to compete and limiting competition among the regional Blue plans
 — was filed in 2013. The separate provider settlement (No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, provider track) is on a distinct timeline: 
the Provider Settlement Claims Administrator began issuing claim notices on February 16, 2026, acknowledging receipt of claims.

### *In re: Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 — Second Distribution Approved for September 2026


On May 26, 2026, class counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds; on June 15, 2026, the court approved the motion, finding the proposed distribution is in the best interests of eligible claimants; the second initial distribution is anticipated for September 2026.



The $5.5 billion settlement covers merchants that accepted Visa and Mastercard-branded cards in the United States at any time from January 1, 2004, to January 25, 2019.
 
The next distribution would be "at least $182 million" for approximately 84,000 claimants; the initial distribution was approved in October 2025.



The process has been periodically affected by fraudulent, incomplete, or noncompliant claims solicited by third parties; the first disbursement occurred roughly four months ago — approximately one year after the February 2025 claims deadline.
 
The partial distribution structure was necessitated by outstanding legal disputes regarding payment facilitators and branded operators, and challenges to fee amounts or ownership of claims that made full payment impossible.
 Claims counsel should confirm their clients' status in the portal before the September wave; the second distribution population is limited to the cohort identified in the May 26 motion.

### Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Campaign Filed, Remedies Ruling Pending


On May 11, 2026, Keller Postman announced it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.


The predicate liability findings are from two separate courts. 
Judge Amit P. Mehta (D.D.C.) and Judge Leonie M. Brinkema (E.D. Va.) each ruled in favor of the United States, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in search and display advertising, respectively.
 
The $218 billion aggregate damages figure is based on calculations from an economist retained by Keller Postman, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly across the two separate rulings.


The legal mechanics: 
because of contractual arbitration provisions implemented by Google, these claims proceed through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation; Keller Postman is coordinating thousands of individual claims, allowing each claimant to pursue recovery based on its own advertising spend.
 
Eligible businesses may be entitled to recover up to triple the amount of actual damages under federal antitrust law (Clayton Act § 4); Keller Postman estimates that most qualifying businesses could recover up to 30% of their total Google Ads spend during the relevant period.



Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling in the ad tech monopoly case is expected in 2026
, and that ruling will set the structural predicate for quantum calculations in the arbitrations. Until the remedies order issues and any appeal is resolved, the aggregate damages figure remains a theoretical ceiling rather than a liquidated claim. 
On the search side, Google filed an appeal in January 2026 seeking to pause data-sharing mandates imposed by Judge Mehta's September 2025 remedies ruling
, which introduces additional appellate uncertainty into the search-monopoly damages track.


Keller Postman also represents seven state attorneys general in a parallel case
, which means arbitration claimants are operating alongside a government enforcement track — historically a dynamic that accelerates global settlement conversations.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders with claims pending in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417) should monitor the N.D. Cal. docket daily for the final approval order and note whether any objector files a notice of appeal within 30 days of entry; if an appeal is filed, distribution delays of 12–18 months are plausible and the September 25, 2026 Anthropic installment may sit in escrow rather than flowing to claimants. Merchants in the Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement (MDL 1720) should confirm their claim status in the administrator portal before September and identify whether they fall within the population covered by the June 15-approved second distribution motion — only the cohort described in class counsel's May 26 filing qualifies for the September wave. Businesses that purchased Google advertising on or after August 2016 and have not enrolled with Keller Postman should evaluate participation given state-law statute of limitations variance; the firm's published guidance indicates deadlines vary by state, and delay may foreclose otherwise viable claims. BCBS claimants still awaiting Tier-2 distribution (notification emails began June 1 for the first 15% of that cohort) should be prepared for the remainder of Tier-2 payments to run through late 2026 into 2027, with the 10% holdback on Tier-1 claims subject to a second-distribution timeline not yet announced by JND.
