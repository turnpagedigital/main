## Analysis & Developments

**The week's headline is a closed one:** *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), has its final-approval order in hand as of Monday. The week's live wire is *In re Johnson & Johnson Talcum Powder Products*, MDL 2738 (D.N.J.), where a July 22 show-cause order by Magistrate Judge Rukhsanah Singh has placed 68,435 ovarian-cancer claims on a causation-or-dismissal countdown. ### I. Bartz v. Anthropic — Final Approval Entered; Distribution Clock Starts

**DELTA — closed this week.** 
On July 20, 2026, Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín (N.D. Cal.) entered a final-approval order in the $1.5 billion copyright infringement class action *Bartz v. Anthropic*, calling the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate" and overruling all 53 objections filed with the court.
 The order runs 23 pages and 
resolves several outstanding administrative matters, effectively closing the case.


The claims profile is unusually strong for a copyright settlement at this scale. 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate and 350 valid opt-outs, representing 1,802 works — approximately 0.03% of the total number of works in the class.
 That opt-out figure is minimal and removes almost no claimant exposure from the fund. 
Each eligible work in the settlement carries a payout of approximately $3,000 to $3,100.
 The fee award was negotiated down: 
class counsel's fee request was revised from $300 million to $187.5 million before final approval was sought.


One late-breaking procedural artifact merits tracking. 
Music industry attorney Donald S. Passman filed a conditional objection and late opt-out request (Dkt. entry filed June 30 / entered July 1, 2026), attaching declarations from Passman himself and Kenneth D. Freundlich along with email correspondence with Simon & Schuster.
 The court's final-approval order and the July 21 docket entry (Dkt. 681 per the tracked-case ground truth) appear to have resolved or mooted that application. 
The court reaffirmed that disputes that "cannot be resolved by mutual agreement with the settlement administrator" will be submitted to the Special Master, and that all submissions "should remain confidential and under seal."


Critically for rights-holders in the AI training corpus space: the order's risk framing is instructive for downstream cases. 
The court characterized the $1.5 billion settlement as providing "substantial benefits to the Class in light of the novel claims asserted," noting that success at trial was not assured, and that a loss would have left the class with no recourse.
 The court also noted that 
Anthropic's Rule 23(f) petition was fully briefed in the Ninth Circuit when the settlement was reached
 — confirming that both parties faced appellate exposure that accelerated resolution. Bartz now moves into distribution administration; no trial-level risk remains.

### II. J&J Talc MDL 2738 — Show-Cause Order Puts 68,435 Claims at Risk

**DELTA — major procedural shift this week.** This is the most consequential single development in active mass-tort MDL practice right now. On July 22, 
Magistrate Judge Rukhsanah Singh issued an order directing attorneys leading nearly 70,000 lawsuits against Johnson & Johnson to show why their claims should not be dismissed — the direct result of plaintiffs' counsel withdrawing causation experts from the six bellwether plaintiffs selected for the first federal trials.


The order's framing signals skepticism in both directions. 
The court wrote that "the visceral reaction to J&J's motion may be that it seeks instant dismissal of all cases in this MDL," but added "The undersigned views it differently."
 That language is judicial restraint, not capitulation to J&J — but it is a meaningful pivot. The court is treating the expert withdrawal as a systemic causation event, not a case-specific failure.

The underlying expert dispute has been building since January. 
Plaintiffs formally withdrew the two experts on June 8, and J&J is seeking dismissal not only of the six bellwether cases but of all remaining ovarian cancer claims in the MDL.
 
The motion follows evidentiary hearings held in May concerning the admissibility of expert testimony; J&J contends that testimony provided during those hearings demonstrated significant limitations in the experts' ability to determine whether talc use caused ovarian cancer in a specific individual.
 The key distinction J&J is pressing: specific causation (individual linkage) versus general causation (population-level association), which Special Master Freda Wolfson addressed favorably for plaintiffs in her January 2026 Daubert recommendation. 
Retired U.S. District Judge Wolfson concluded that plaintiffs' experts used reliable, mainstream scientific methods and that the epidemiological evidence shows a statistically significant association between genital talc use and the disease.


The docket count tells you the volume at stake. 
As of early July, there are 68,435 talc ovarian cancer lawsuits active in federal court, with over 400 new lawsuits filed in just the preceding few weeks.
 
On June 11, 2026, J&J filed a motion asking the court to dismiss all cases in the MDL with prejudice.
 Plaintiffs' leadership has not publicly commented on the motion, per prior reporting. 
Mediator Fouad Kurdi convened the rescheduled April 27 settlement session under Judge Michael Shipp's order requiring representatives with full settlement authority on both sides; J&J has continued to publicly maintain it does not currently intend to offer a global settlement.


The structural backdrop that makes this motion high-stakes: J&J's three bankruptcy attempts to cap liability — including the $9 billion Red River Talc proposal — have all been dismissed. 
A Texas judge dismissed the Chapter 11 bankruptcy case of J&J's subsidiary Red River Talc in March 2025 — the third bankruptcy attempt by a J&J subsidiary that has been dismissed.
 With the bankruptcy channel closed, a successful dismissal motion in MDL 2738 would be J&J's only remaining path to large-scale claim elimination short of a global MDL settlement. The show-cause deadline and plaintiffs' response will be the next hard trigger to watch.

### III. Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Scale, Structure, and the Forum-Shopping Pressure Point

**CARRY FORWARD — no new ruling this week, but the Keller Postman campaign's mechanics are worth consolidating given the Google Ads TOS amendment that followed.**


Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.
 
Ashley Keller estimates potential claims for online search and display ads could reach $218 billion or more, based on calculations from an economist the firm retained.
 That figure is a theoretical damages ceiling, not a single filed demand — but its magnitude drives the leverage mechanics.


The $218 billion figure is an estimate. In mass arbitration, the leverage comes from the volume of individual filings and the per-claim fee obligations they trigger. The scale of the estimate signals that the advertiser claimant pool is potentially enormous: every business that purchased Google search ads or display advertising during the monopoly period at inflated prices is a potential claimant.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade.


The forum mechanics are what distinguish this from a conventional class action. 
Arbitration clauses in Google's advertiser contracts effectively prevent damages via class action litigation; businesses can instead join together in a group arbitration, which is usually handled by a single law firm and provides more leverage in settlement negotiations.
 
Google's advertiser contracts specify a particular arbitration forum — likely AAA or JAMS, both of which have mass arbitration rules triggered at 25 or more simultaneous coordinated claims.


One material downstream development: Google amended its Ads Terms of Service in July 2026, reportedly modifying or dropping arbitration clauses in some contexts and adding a 30-day opt-out window for future disputes. 
Google acknowledged that in May 2026, Keller Postman commenced its mass arbitration effort pointing to antitrust and the $728 billion in U.S. ad spend, targeting thousands of companies — and characterized its TOS update as being "proactive about how we settle things with advertisers."
 Any business that has not yet opted out of the new arbitration clause within the 30-day window and intends to preserve litigation rights should act immediately. This structural change may affect future claims but almost certainly does not reach pre-amendment ad spend covered by the existing campaign.

### IV. Securities Class Actions — H1 2026 Market Data

**CARRY FORWARD — macro context; new NERA/Cornerstone data published July 23.**


According to NERA, there were 118 federal court securities class action lawsuit filings in the first half of 2026, projecting to a year-end total of 236, which would be 15% greater than the 205 cases filed in 2025 and would represent the highest annual total since 2020.
 
Cornerstone Research reported 39 securities suit settlements in the first six months of 2026 with a total value of $2.2 billion; if the settlement pace continues, 2026 will exceed 2025 both in number of cases settled and total settlement value, with projected annual values being the highest since 2020.


On valuation: 
the average settlement value was $54 million in the first half of 2026, a 32% increase relative to the 2025 inflation-adjusted average settlement value of $41 million.
 
AI-related suits continued to grow, with 18 filings in the first half of 2026 already exceeding the 17 cases filed in all of 2025.
 That AI-securities overlap is generating a new category of exposure that sits across the LLM/Copyright and $1B+ Class Actions verticals simultaneously. 
Pump-and-dump cases — in which shareholders accuse company leaders of failing to disclose share-price manipulation — increased sharply, with 11 cases filed in the first half.


The broad-market settlement picture confirms the acceleration. 
As of the end of H1 2026, the aggregate settlement total across all areas of class action litigation and government enforcement lawsuits is $53.795 billion (accounting for the top five settlements in various substantive areas).
 
The top-five antitrust class action settlements at mid-year total $34.875 billion; products liability/mass tort totals $8.609 billion; and securities fraud totals $1.979 billion.
 
On an aggregate basis across all litigation areas, class actions and government enforcement lawsuits garnered more than $79 billion in 2025, $42 billion in 2024, $51.4 billion in 2023, and $66 billion in 2022.
