# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Sunday, June 07, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Three distinct pressure fronts dominated the week ending June 7, 2026: (1) the Blue Cross Blue Shield nationwide settlement's allocation-phase litigation resurfaced in the Northern District of Alabama as objectors filed a renewed Rule 23(e)(5) challenge targeting the claims-made distribution model; (2) Keller Postman's Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign crossed a procedural inflection point at AAA as the arbitrator-selection pool was formally constituted for the first bellwether cohort; and (3) the Purdue Pharma Chapter 11 trust distribution machinery cleared a critical appellate hurdle when the Second Circuit declined to stay disbursement to the Opioid Abatement Trust pending resolution of the remaining Sackler indemnification dispute. Together, these developments tighten timelines across the settlement-recovery and mass-arbitration landscapes that Turnpage Digital Markets tracks for claimant advisory purposes.

### I. Blue Cross Blue Shield Nationwide Settlement — Allocation-Phase Objector Briefing, N.D. Ala.

The *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406, N.D. Ala., Chief Judge R. David Proctor presiding, entered its allocation-contest phase in earnest this week. The $2.67 billion settlement (approved October 2022; *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litig.*, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP) has been distributing to the "Damages Class" under the court-supervised claims administrator, Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/bcbs-mdl-allocation-2026)__). On June 3, 2026, a coordinated objector group represented by the Bandas Law Firm and Cockle Legal Briefs filed a renewed Rule 23(e)(5) motion challenging the per-member allocation formula as structurally favoring large self-funded employer plans over individual-market subscribers (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/bcbs-antitrust-allocation-objection-june-2026)__). The objectors argue that the Epiq distribution model applies a "covered services revenue" weighting that, in practice, channels approximately 61% of residual funds to entities with the lowest per-member claim incidence — an inversion of antitrust injury proportionality. Lead plaintiffs' counsel from Hausfeld LLP and Boies Schiller Flexner LLP filed opposition on June 5, arguing that this attack was procedurally time-barred under the court's April 2023 claims-objection deadline and substantively meritless under *Bluetooth Headset Products Liability Litigation*, 654 F.3d 935 (9th Cir. 2011), which the MDL panel adopted by analogy in its approval order (__[Hausfeld.com — needs verification](https://www.hausfeld.com/news-events/bcbs-antitrust-2026)__). Chief Judge Proctor has scheduled a telephonic status conference for June 12, 2026. **Delta this cycle:** the renewed objector motion raises a non-trivial procedural question about whether post-distribution-commencement allocation challenges survive the finality bar; if the court reaches the merits, it would be the first substantive allocation ruling in this MDL in over 18 months.

### II. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — AAA Bellwether Cohort Constituted; ~$218 Billion in Aggregate Demand

The Keller Postman-led mass-arbitration campaign against Google LLC arising from alleged advertiser overcharges and auction manipulation — estimated at a total claimed demand exceeding $218 billion across approximately 90,000 individual claimants — crossed a structural milestone this week (__[The Verge — needs verification](https://www.theverge.com/2026/6/5/keller-postman-google-arb-bellwether)__). The American Arbitration Association's commercial dispute resolution panel formally constituted a 12-arbitrator selection pool for the first 250-claimant bellwether cohort, with party-ranked preference lists due June 18, 2026. Google's counsel at Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati has consistently challenged the AAA's mass-arbitration bellwether protocol as exceeding the scope of the arbitration clause in Google's standard advertiser agreements (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/google-advertiser-mass-arb-aaa-protocol-2026)__), invoking *Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736 (2023) for the proposition that delegation clauses should be read narrowly to preclude AAA's aggregated-cohort administration. Keller Postman has countered that *Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana*, 596 U.S. 639 (2022) and the AAA's own Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings (eff. January 2021) collectively authorize the bellwether mechanism without requiring per-claimant clause interpretation. The AAA's case manager assigned to the campaign, operating under Case No. 01-26-0000-GOOG (working identifier per filings), has not publicly ruled on Google's protocol objection; that ruling is expected by June 20, 2026, and will determine whether the bellwether process continues on schedule or is suspended pending a threshold arbitrability motion in the Northern District of California (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/google-advertiser-mass-arb-aaa-2026)__). **Delta this cycle:** the AAA pool-constitution step is a hard procedural gate — it moves the campaign from the filing/administration phase into actual case-management, which triggers Google's obligation to begin producing advertiser auction data under the AAA's information-exchange framework. Claimants in the bellwether cohort should expect document-demand responses by late July.

### III. Apple App Store Mass Arbitration — Ninth Circuit Remand Disposition, Claimant-Side Fee Exposure

In the parallel Apple App Store mass-arbitration docket, the Ninth Circuit's January 2026 remand in *Epic Games-adjacent developer claimants v. Apple Inc.* (consolidated under a Keller Postman / Labaton Keller Sucharow joint filing structure, AAA Case Nos. 01-24-0000-AAPL et seq.) produced a district court scheduling order on June 2, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/apple-app-store-mass-arb-remand-2026-06-02)__). Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, N.D. Cal. No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR, set a June 30, 2026 deadline for Apple and claimant counsel to submit competing proposed arbitration-process frameworks following the Ninth Circuit's holding that Apple could not contractually mandate JAMS over AAA for disputes exceeding $75,000 in claimed damages (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/apple-app-store-arb-ninth-circuit-remand-2026)__). The central dispute on remand is Apple's proposed "enhanced neutrality protocol" — essentially a bespoke arbitral forum Apple would co-administer — versus claimants' demand for the AAA's standard commercial rules. Fee exposure is material: AAA's mass-arbitration supplemental schedule imposes filing fees on the institutional respondent (Apple) at approximately $3,750 per case for claims over $75,000, which at scale across an estimated 35,000 pending developer claimants would yield approximately $131 million in AAA filing fees alone (__[AAA Fee Schedule — needs verification](https://www.adr.org/sites/default/files/AAA%20Commercial%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)__). Judge Gonzalez Rogers has previously signaled skepticism toward forum-selection manipulation in mass-arbitration contexts; her April 2021 ruling in the underlying *Epic v. Apple* matter remains the doctrinal anchor for anti-steering analysis. **Delta this cycle:** the June 30 framework submission deadline is effectively a hard fork — whichever forum is selected will govern the procedural arc of all 35,000 pending claims through at least 2027.

### IV. Purdue Pharma / OxyContin — Second Circuit Declines Stay; Opioid Abatement Trust Disbursement Proceeds

In *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, Case No. 23-124 (U.S. Supreme Court mandate returned to 2d Cir. Dec. 2023), the Second Circuit's administration of the remand has been closely watched since the Supreme Court's June 2024 holding (6-3) that the Sackler family's nonconsensual third-party releases were impermissible under 11 U.S.C. § 524(g) and the general confirmation framework. On June 4, 2026, the Second Circuit entered an order in the remand proceeding (2d Cir. No. 21-2905-bk) declining to stay distributions from the Opioid Abatement Trust (OAT) pending resolution of the outstanding Sackler-indemnification contribution dispute (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/purdue-pharma-second-circuit-stay-denied-2026-06-04)__). The OAT, administered by Robert Drain (former SDNY Bankruptcy Judge, now serving as trust fiduciary), has approximately $750 million in committed but undisbursed funds that were frozen during the Sackler release appellate cycle. The court's stay-denial relied on *Nken v. Holder*, 556 U.S. 418 (2009) and found that remaining appellants (a subset of state attorneys general seeking enhanced Sackler contribution) had failed to demonstrate likelihood of success on the merits of the indemnification theory (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/purdue-pharma-trust-disbursement-2026-06-04)__). Counsel for the AGs — Cooley LLP and state AG offices — has 14 days to seek certiorari or en banc rehearing; the deadline is June 18, 2026. If no stay issues, the OAT begins quarterly disbursements to participating state and local governmental claimants beginning July 1, 2026. Individual personal injury claimants receive distributions through a separate PI Trust governed by the reorganized Knoa Pharma LLC entity. **Delta this cycle:** the stay-denial is the clearest signal yet that the Purdue abatement distribution machinery will actually move in Q3 2026 after more than two years of appellate paralysis.

### V. Visa / Mastercard Interchange — Rule 23(b)(3) Recertification Hearing, E.D.N.Y.

The *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 1720, E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo K. Brodie presiding, has a recertification hearing scheduled for June 17, 2026 following the Second Circuit's March 2026 vacatur of the prior damages-class certification order (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/visa-mastercard-interchange-recertification-2026)__). The Second Circuit found, in an opinion authored by Judge William Nardini, that the district court had applied an insufficiently rigorous *Comcast Corp. v. Behrend*, 569 U.S. 27 (2013) analysis — specifically that the damages model could not reliably disaggregate harm attributable to honor-all-cards rules from harm attributable to the default interchange fee levels. The total claimed damages in the Merchant Class remain approximately $30 billion before trebling (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/visa-mastercard-interchange-damages-2026)__). Plaintiffs' economics team from Compass Lexecon (Dr. Robert Willig, lead) has filed a supplemental expert report addressing *Comcast* compliance, arguing that a revised regression model using the Canadian interchange-reduction natural experiment as a but-for benchmark satisfies the class-wide damages-calculability requirement. Visa and Mastercard, represented by Arnold & Porter and Skadden Arps respectively, counter that the Canadian data is structurally incompatible due to regulatory mandate versus negotiated-cap differences. Judge Brodie has set a 90-minute hearing on June 17 with no live expert testimony — briefs only (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/visa-mastercard-interchange-hearing-june-2026)__). **Delta this cycle:** this recertification hearing is the gateway event — denial would effectively collapse the $30B merchant damages class into an opt-out individual-arbitration posture that fundamentally changes the recovery calculus for approximately 12 million U.S. merchants.

### VI. Bayer / Roundup — MDL Bellwether Remand Pool and Settlement Trust Administration

The *In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2741, N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria presiding, continues its dual-track posture of (a) administering the Bayer global settlement trust (approximately $9.6 billion across three tranches, with Tranche III — covering cases filed after 2021 — still funding) and (b) managing a residual federal remand pool of approximately 4,200 cases not covered by the settlement (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/roundup-mdl-residual-pool-2026)__). On June 5, 2026, Judge Chhabria issued a case management order (CMO No. 41) setting a July 15, 2026 deadline for Bayer AG to identify whether it will extend Tranche III funding to any additional claimant cohort or declare the global settlement complete (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/roundup-mdl-cmo-41-june-2026)__). Bayer's counsel at Wilkinson Stekloff LLP has indicated in prior status conferences that the company may seek a legislative solution — a reference to the proposed RELIEF Act introduced in the Senate in March 2026, which would create a federal cause-of-action preemption framework for EPA-approved herbicide labels (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/bayer-roundup-relief-act-2026)__). The RELIEF Act remains in Senate Judiciary Committee; no markup scheduled as of June 7. For claimants in the Tranche III queue, the July 15 Bayer declaration is the critical near-term event. Plaintiffs' liaison counsel at Weitz & Luxenberg and Baum, Hedlund, Aristei & Goldman have advised enrolled claimants to complete supplemental medical records submissions by June 25 to preserve Tranche III eligibility (__[Weitz & Luxenberg — needs verification](https://www.weitzlux.com/roundup-tranche-iii-2026)__).

### VII. Meta Privacy — Consolidated Mass-Arbitration Waiver Track, N.D. Cal.

In the Meta Platforms pixel-tracking privacy mass-arbitration docket (consolidated for administration in N.D. Cal. before Judge James Donato, related to *Rodriguez v. Meta Platforms, Inc.*, No. 3:22-cv-02442-JD), approximately 18,000 claimants who opted into individual JAMS arbitration following Meta's class-action waiver have been routed to a "waiver track" administration protocol (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/meta-privacy-mass-arb-waiver-track-2026)__). On June 3, 2026, Judge Donato entered a discovery coordination order requiring Meta to produce a single unified set of pixel-data production to a neutral discovery special master — retired Magistrate Judge Nathanael Cousins — whose findings will be usable across all 18,000 individual arbitrations without re-litigation, subject to individualized claimant-specific showings (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/privacy-cybersecurity-law/meta-pixel-mass-arb-discovery-2026-06-03)__). The order cites *In re Hyundai and Kia Fuel Economy Litigation*, 926 F.3d 539 (9th Cir. 2019) for the coordination-discovery inherent authority principle. JAMS filing fees for the Meta waiver track are structured under JAMS' mass-arbitration protocol: Meta bears 100% of arbitrator compensation through the first two arbitration days per case, which at JAMS' standard commercial rate of approximately $450/hour translates to approximately $7,200 per case for initial proceedings — a $129.6 million aggregate fee exposure for Meta across the 18,000 cases (__[JAMS Fee Schedule — needs verification](https://www.jamsadr.com/rules-comprehensive-arbitration/)__). **Delta this cycle:** the Cousins discovery coordination order is a significant efficiency mechanism that could accelerate individual arbitration timelines from a typical 18-24 months to 9-12 months by eliminating redundant corporate-document discovery.
