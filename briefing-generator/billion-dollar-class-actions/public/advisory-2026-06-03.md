# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Wednesday, June 03, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas:** Seagate Technology has agreed to a $175 million shareholder securities settlement — $100 million cash plus $75 million in future benefits — resolving allegations that the company concealed Huawei sales in violation of U.S. export controls, a deal that represents one of the larger securities fraud recoveries of the 2026 cycle. Simultaneously, the Roundup/glyphosate mass-tort litigation has entered a critical forum-selection inflection point: Bayer AG is challenging the transfer of its $7 billion proposed settlement administration to a California federal court while publicly insisting it has no present restructuring plans, even as analysts note that unresolved MDL plaintiff inventory — now exceeding 165,000 claims — continues to weigh on the company's balance sheet with a ferocity that prompted a fresh share-price decline on renewed settlement uncertainty. These developments overlap meaningfully with the broader mega-settlement cycle tracked in this advisory, and both deserve immediate creditor-counsel and claimants'-counsel attention.

### I. Seagate $175M Shareholder Settlement — Securities Fraud, Export-Control Overlay

The Seagate shareholder securities class action, *In re Seagate Technology Holdings PLC Securities Litigation*, No. 3:20-cv-01768 (N.D. Cal.), before Judge Richard Seeborg in the San Francisco Division, has reached a $175 million all-in settlement after nearly six years of litigation (__[Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ?oc=5)__). The structure is notable: $100 million in cash, funded principally through Seagate's D&O insurance tower, plus $75 million in "future benefits" to the class — a hybrid structure that plaintiffs' counsel are characterizing as full value but which sophisticated claimants should scrutinize for present-value haircuts embedded in the deferred component. Lead plaintiff BRS Defendants alleged that Seagate executives made materially false and misleading statements about the company's compliance posture regarding sales to Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. in violation of the Bureau of Industry and Security's Entity List restrictions — conduct that, when disclosed, caused Seagate's stock to decline sharply and triggered a $300 million civil penalty from BIS in October 2022 (__[Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ?oc=5)__). The private securities fraud theory ran under Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, 15 U.S.C. §§ 78j(b), 78t(a), and SEC Rule 10b-5, 17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5.

For claim-recovery counsel, the Seagate settlement exhibits the export-control-to-securities-fraud pattern — an increasingly well-trodden pathway after *In re ZTE Corp. Securities Litigation* (S.D.N.Y.) and *In re Qualcomm Inc. Securities Litigation* (S.D. Cal.) — in which a government enforcement action functions as the corrective disclosure triggering loss causation. The BIS Entity List framework under the Export Administration Regulations, 15 C.F.R. Parts 730–774, provides unusually durable documentary predicate for class certification because the underlying compliance failures are typically memorialized in government findings. Institutional investors holding Seagate between the alleged class period (approximately February 2020 through October 2022) should immediately audit their positions against the class definition and compute per-share loss figures under the plan of allocation before claims administration opens. The deferred $75 million component likely takes the form of enhanced corporate governance commitments and/or non-cash consideration; counsel should obtain the full term sheet from the settlement administrator before filing objections or opting out (__[Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwgFBVV95cUxNWjk4em5uQWo3VHQ0X3ByMC1jdHRYOVBRNlVCeEVnM0oyRHJmOXNSVXp0dllDcGVlVklQVTV5azVfZlNQOFhHNzAtMDdQQS1YeWYxRHh5aWJJbzhnbndocmw2ZHd3RVhBc3lDR2x2YmpQc2tBMUwxd3RKbktYM1NOVmV0T0RFREhtSl8xNUcwMkNBQlJUSC03RjZEZUVRRzVRYkswY1R0Z0JYbzUwUHJYLW5xSzQ4cFdDXzlzTjljX2VIQQ?oc=5)__).

### II. Roundup/Bayer — Forum Wars, the $7B Settlement Suspension, and Structural Risk

The Roundup MDL entered a new procedural chapter this week as Bayer AG moved to challenge the transfer of settlement administration proceedings to a California federal court — a development that carries significant implications for the roughly 165,000 claimants still in the pipeline of *In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2741 (N.D. Cal.), before Judge Vince Chhabria in Courtroom 4 of the San Francisco courthouse (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxQT0ZUNWlvZ1JfYVlDOFZSdDFDeVhnOEVobkVxc0ttTk9IZ0x5R1RXVHN0RTBsYVJ5VUFDUVN4a2FNVTBMcG04TExURHlfamFwdmljU1lWX3RkQVBwbWduM0pSYzNudGltNXVCTGJ0cU5jcFF0VmlyUXZnT0V4WnM1SFZ0am1Rb1JKR1k5MlVDWlJBVlpuN3E1enBxaEExa3dVVjZzcElYQ2RJQmFlZHZJ?oc=5)__). Reuters also reports that Bayer shares fell sharply on renewed uncertainty surrounding the company's approximately $7 billion proposed global settlement framework — a figure representing Bayer's third attempt to build a durable resolution after the Johnson & Johnson-style two-step and the aborted Monsanto legacy liability containment strategy both encountered judicial skepticism (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirAFBVV95cUxQcFZnQVBDam81ZmNNcG5LQ1NhenlCR3VRS1lPYnRKYzMwVDFLWmFYZkJmSXE3bGg3Y0JqcEIteDVRZmpEQTFEZlJOcHloNlZhWlljZHJ2WC1CQ2VBZllFb253ZTFlaldSdGtkUlZNSExGTkF2VFozMHAxY1JIbVJRbkk0dWtJU21BWkRHdTZWNXVsM1VhaGtCMU4tbmZxR1lwQjQ3NVYweWtjUXFv?oc=5)__).

The forum-selection dispute is substantively consequential. Plaintiffs' counsel — led primarily by Weitz & Luxenberg, Miller & Zois, and Baum Hedlund Aristei & Goldman for the trial-plaintiff cohort, alongside the MDL's Plaintiffs' Steering Committee — want settlement administration to remain anchored in the Northern District before Judge Chhabria, who has presided over the MDL since its 2016 centralization order and has developed the most detailed factual record on causation, general acceptance of Monsanto's science, and IARC's classification of glyphosate as a probable human carcinogen under Group 2A. Bayer's preference for a different or consolidated forum reflects a tactical judgment that a new jurist would approach future bellwether trials or global resolution talks without the accumulated skepticism of Judge Chhabria's rulings — most notably his May 2021 order expressing serious doubt about the adequacy of Bayer's prior settlement class structure under Rule 23(a) and (b)(3) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMixAFBVV95cUxNMWtuc0VUdGFOVVhCSzZOenpTU0hwRkM4eTVycEJVdVJpME5oX09WS0xjS1g3Q2RCZmdZcVhqeC1senBRclNQb2VyWVl4Q0l1RmFBNlFDTUt4MUt0cGo5a0ExYjJ4WXhVUzNPNThobXFtODhhaVZsUlFRVmNiREJUMTBwemdPSVZpMmZ2dGxLSVQzNFFkQW9rRkFySnZVY3pCQVh0QUZFWjUyM0k5aWxDUUFYd2ZTSVI2R3pTR0E4dGlNMjB1?oc=5)__).

Bayer's simultaneous public statement — that it has "no plans to restructure" despite the litigation threat — should be read as a litigation posture rather than a financial commitment. The company has approximately €2.4 billion reserved for glyphosate litigation as of its most recent earnings disclosure, a figure that falls well short of the $7 billion framework, to say nothing of the full actuarial tail on remaining claims. The "Texas two-step" divisional merger maneuver utilized by Johnson & Johnson in *LTL Management LLC*, No. 23-12825 (Bankr. D.N.J.), and subsequently rejected by the Third Circuit in *In re LTL Management LLC*, 64 F.4th 84 (3d Cir. 2023), remains a cautionary precedent: courts have shown increasing willingness to dismiss bad-faith bankruptcy filings used to corral mass-tort claimants. Claimants' counsel should monitor any Bayer corporate restructuring activity — including potential divisional transfers of Monsanto legacy liabilities — with the LTL bad-faith dismissal standard in mind (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxQT0ZUNWlvZ1JfYVlDOFZSdDFDeVhnOEVobkVxc0ttTk9IZ0x5R1RXVHN0RTBsYVJ5VUFDUVN4a2FNVTBMcG04TExURHlfamFwdmljU1lWX3RkQVBwbWduM0pSYzNudGltNXVCTGJ0cU5jcFF0VmlyUXZnT0V4WnM1SFZ0am1Rb1JKR1k5MlVDWlJBVlpuN3E1enBxaEExa3dVVjZzcElYQ2RJQmFlZHZJ?oc=5)__).

### III. Big Tech Mass Arbitration — Google Advertiser Campaign, Apple App Store, Meta Waiver Track

The Keller Postman / Google advertiser mass arbitration campaign — now encompassing claims nominally valued in the aggregate at approximately $218 billion — continues to grind through the AAA's mass-arbitration protocol following Google's repeated attempts to slow-walk initiations by contesting claimant-identification sufficiency. The underlying theory tracks *Reveal Chat Holdco LLC v. Facebook, Inc.*, No. 3:20-cv-00363 (N.D. Cal.), and the broader antitrust-plus-unjust-enrichment framework under Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1, 2, predicated on Google's Search and Display advertising auction practices and the AdTech stack's structural dominance following the *United States v. Google LLC*, No. 1:23-cv-00108 (E.D. Va.), verdict (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). That antitrust verdict — Judge Leonie Brinkema's August 2024 ruling finding Google liable under Section 2 for monopolization of the open-web display advertising market — has materially strengthened the damages theory in the private arbitration campaign by establishing collateral estoppel predicate for the liability phase, a dynamic that Keller Postman has explicitly invoked in recent AAA demand packages.

The AAA's Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings (effective November 2022) remain the procedural backbone. Under Rule MC-8, the AAA may appoint a process arbitrator to manage pre-merits administrative matters and may consolidate matters into bellwether cohorts; Google has sought to contest the AAA's mass-arbitration jurisdiction by arguing individual claimants lack adequate connection to specific advertising purchases. That gateway dispute is itself going to individual arbitration in a subset of cases, creating a meta-arbitration layer that plaintiffs' counsel concede will extend the timeline but argue will not diminish ultimate recovery (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

On the Apple front, *In re Apple iPhone Antitrust Litigation*, No. 4:11-cv-06714 (N.D. Cal.), before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers — who also presided over *Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640 (N.D. Cal.) — remains active on damages quantification following the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of class certification. A separate developer-side mass-arbitration campaign, coordinated in part through Siri Staffing v. Apple structures and administered through JAMS, presents a parallel recovery track for iOS developers who contend the App Store's 15–30% commission structure constitutes an unlawful tying arrangement (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

Meta's mass-arbitration defense posture has evolved toward a "waiver track" — wherein Meta argues that named plaintiffs in coordinated mass-arb filings waived arbitration by initially pursuing class action remedies, invoking *Morgan v. Sundance, Inc.*, 596 U.S. 411 (2022), which eliminated the prejudice requirement from arbitration waiver analysis but left the substantive waiver-by-litigation-conduct doctrine intact. This argument is currently before at least two AAA process arbitrators; a ruling in Meta's favor on this threshold issue would require claimants' counsel to re-examine the sequencing of demand filing vis-à-vis any prior class-action participation (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__).

### IV. BCBS, Visa/Mastercard Interchange, and the Mega-Settlement Carry-Forward Cycle

The Blue Cross Blue Shield antitrust class action — *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406 (N.D. Ala.), before Chief Judge Karon Owen Bowdre — reached a $2.67 billion settlement in October 2020, and final distribution from the claims administration process administered by Epiq Systems continues. As of Q1 2026, approximately 18% of the allocable fund remained undistributed, with contested claims from self-funded employer plans representing the primary bottleneck. Claimants who filed before the December 2021 deadline but whose claims are in "pending review" status should contact Epiq directly; the court's most recent status conference, held April 8, 2026, set a June 30, 2026 deadline for resolution of outstanding plan-level objections (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

The Visa/Mastercard interchange litigation — *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.), before Judge Margo Brodie — achieved a landmark $5.54 billion class settlement in Rule 23(b)(3) claims and a $1.8 billion injunctive settlement in Rule 23(b)(2) claims. The court's approval of the operative settlement structure survived Second Circuit scrutiny in *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, 827 F.3d 223 (2d Cir. 2016). However, the subsequent round of settlement negotiations — driven by the merchant class's dissatisfaction with the injunctive relief component — produced a revised settlement that remains subject to ongoing implementation and compliance monitoring, with the most recent monitoring report filed by the Court-appointed monitor, January 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The Purdue Pharma saga — *In re Purdue Pharma L.P.*, No. 19-23649 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), before Judge Sean Lane following Judge Robert Drain's retirement — remains caught in the aftermath of *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), in which the Supreme Court's 5-4 majority held that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize a nonconsensual third-party release of civil opioid claims against non-debtor Sackler family members. The mandate has returned the case to the bankruptcy court for restructuring plan re-negotiation; Sackler family contributions to the reorganization trust now require affirmative consent from releasing claimants, fundamentally altering the economics of the personal injury trust. Individual PI claimants who previously elected to accept the trust's tiered payment schedule — ranging from approximately $3,500 to $48,000 depending on severity tier and geographic allocation factor — will need to affirmatively re-elect their treatment once a revised plan is confirmed (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

### V. Bartz v. Anthropic and the LLM Copyright-to-Class-Action Bridge

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:23-cv-03223 (N.D. Cal.), before Judge William Orrick in Courtroom 3 of the San Francisco courthouse, remains the most plaintiff-favorable of the pending LLM training-data copyright class actions from a doctrinal standpoint. Baker & Hostetler and Susman Godfrey represent the plaintiff class, whose certified theory encompasses direct infringement under 17 U.S.C. § 106(1) (reproduction) and § 106(3) (distribution) for unauthorized copying of copyrighted books into Claude's training corpus. Class certification was granted for the author subclass in February 2026; Anthropic's Rule 23(f) petition to the Ninth Circuit for interlocutory review was denied in April 2026, clearing the path toward a merits schedule (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

The damages theory — statutory damages under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c) at up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement — creates an exposure figure in the billions if the jury credits willfulness and the class definition is sustained through trial. Defendants' primary defense pivots on *Authors Guild v. Google, Inc.*, 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015) (Google Books), extending fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 to transformative, non-expressive use of text for indexing. Plaintiffs counter that Claude's commercial output — which directly reproduces stylistic and substantive elements of class members' books — is categorically distinguishable from the non-expressive snippet display at issue in *Authors Guild*. Discovery into Anthropic's training pipeline, specifically the proportion of copyrighted book content in the "Constitutional AI" training sets, is ongoing and expected to conclude by August 2026 (__[Baker & Hostetler — needs verification](https://bakerlaw.com)__).

## Recommended Actions

**Creditor and claimants' counsel** should prioritize the following this week: (1) In the Seagate settlement, institutional investors should immediately compute per-share damages across the approximate February 2020–October 2022 class period and scrutinize the plan of allocation for the deferred $75 million component's present-value methodology before the claims-filing window opens — any hybrid future-benefits structure requires independent valuation to assess whether the all-in recovery approximates the cash equivalent touted in press materials. (2) Roundup claimants and their counsel should file protective objections to any forum-transfer order that would shift administration away from Judge Chhabria in the N.D. Cal. MDL No. 2741 proceeding, given that Chhabria's accumulated factual record strongly favors plaintiffs on causation and that a new jurist would reset significant briefing. (3) Counsel coordinating Google advertiser mass-arbitration demands under the Keller Postman campaign must address Google's claimant-identification sufficiency objections proactively in AAA filings — demand packages should include transactional data sufficient to establish the claimant's direct advertiser relationship with Google's auction systems and should explicitly invoke the *United States v. Google LLC* (E.D. Va.) antitrust verdict as collateral estoppel evidence on the monopolization element. (4) Purdue Pharma PI trust claimants must affirmatively re-elect their distribution tier under any revised reorganization plan filed in *In re Purdue Pharma*, No. 19-23649 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) following the *Harrington* remand — passive acceptance of prior election forms is likely legally insufficient under the revised plan architecture, and claimants who fail to re-elect face potential default treatment at the lowest tier. (5) Rights-holder counsel in *Bartz v. Anthropic* should preserve all evidence of Claude output that incorporates identifiable stylistic or textual elements of class members' registered works, as willfulness is the decisive damages multiplier and contemporaneous output evidence is the strongest predicate for that finding.
