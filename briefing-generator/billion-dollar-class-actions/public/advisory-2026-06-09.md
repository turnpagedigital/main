### I. Keller Postman v. Google Advertisers — Mass Arbitration (~$218B Demand Pool) | AAA / JAMS Mechanics

The Keller Postman–led Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign — predicated on Google's alleged manipulation of display-advertising auctions in violation of state consumer-protection statutes and common-law fraud theories — remains the largest single-defendant mass-arbitration demand pool in U.S. legal history by aggregate face value, with approximately 90,000 individual demand letters collectively claiming in excess of **$218 billion** in advertiser overcharges (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The campaign is procedurally anchored in the American Arbitration Association's Commercial Rules with a Supplementary Rules for Mass Arbitration overlay, the operative version of which the AAA formalized in August 2023.

The current battleground is fee allocation. Google has contested the AAA's application of its mass-arbitration fee schedule — under which Google, as the designated business party, bears the per-case filing fees for all simultaneously-filed demands — arguing that imposition of aggregate fees approaching **$200 million** for filing alone constitutes an unconscionable economic weapon that the AAA's own rules do not authorize without individual-case triage. Keller Postman has countered that the fee-schedule terms are unambiguous, that Google's own standard advertiser agreements designated AAA arbitration, and that any judicial intervention is precluded by the *Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736 (2023) stay-pending-appeal doctrine only where the gateway arbitrability question is genuinely disputed — which claimants argue it is not here (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). A bellwether ruling on the fee-allocation dispute from the AAA's administrative panel is expected no later than **July 15, 2026** (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Parallel federal-court litigation remains active in *In re: Google Digital Advertising Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 3010 (S.D.N.Y.), before **Judge P. Kevin Castel** in Courtroom 11A of the Daniel Patrick Moynihan Courthouse. The MDL's class-certification briefing track — involving state attorney general parens patriae claims and a separate publisher-side class — is scheduled to conclude principal briefing by **September 12, 2026**. The tension between the MDL class track and the Keller Postman mass-arb track is significant: Google's class-certification opposition argues, among other things, that the arbitration agreements bar the publisher-side claimants from any Rule 23 proceeding, a position that dovetails with the company's fee-allocation challenge in the AAA forum (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### II. *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation* — Final Claims Reconciliation & August Distribution

*In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406 (N.D. Ala.), before **Judge R. David Proctor** in the Hugo L. Black Federal Courthouse in Birmingham, remains one of the largest antitrust settlements in U.S. history. The **$2.67 billion** subscriber-class settlement, which received final approval on August 9, 2022, and survived a wave of objector appeals that were resolved by the Eleventh Circuit in 2023, has proceeded through a multi-year claims administration process managed by Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

As of this advisory, the claims administrator has reported that final reconciliation of the approximately **4.7 million** validated subscriber claims is on track for completion by **June 30, 2026**, paving the way for an initial pro-rata distribution to eligible class members — individual and self-insured group members — in the **August 2026** window (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The per-claimant recovery will vary substantially based on total premiums paid during the class period (February 7, 2008 through October 16, 2020) and the claimant's geographic region, with higher-density states (California, Texas, New York, Florida) accounting for the largest aggregate payout buckets. Counsel for the class — Hausfeld LLP and Whatley Kallas LLC — have indicated that a second distribution tranche is contemplated for any uncashed checks and residual amounts, consistent with the cy pres provisions directing residuals to consumer-health nonprofits (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

A subset of provider-side plaintiffs — distinct from the subscriber class — continue separate antitrust litigation; Judge Proctor has set a scheduling conference for **July 22, 2026** to address the provider class's renewed certification motion following the Supreme Court's *Olean Wholesale Grocery Cooperative v. Bumble Bee Foods* guidance on Daubert-at-certification standards (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

### III. *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* — Ninth Circuit Consolidated Discovery & AI Copyright Contours

*Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-02720-BLF (N.D. Cal.), before **Judge Beth Labson Freeman** in Courtroom 3 of the San Jose Division of the Northern District of California, is the lead case in a constellation of generative-AI copyright class actions that have reshaped the LLM copyright landscape since 2023. Lead plaintiffs — represented by Baker & Hostetler LLP and Susman Godfrey LLP — allege that Anthropic's Claude model family was trained on copyrighted works without license or compensation, violating 17 U.S.C. §§ 106(1) and 106(3), and seek class-wide statutory damages under § 504(c) that, if awarded at maximum rates across the proposed class, would yield aggregate exposure well in excess of **$1 billion** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Today's Ninth Circuit telephonic status conference — listed on the court's docket as Case No. 26-70142 — concerns the sequencing of consolidated discovery across the related AI copyright cases, including *Authors Guild v. OpenAI, Inc.* and the consolidated *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms* proceedings. Plaintiffs' counsel have argued for a unified "training-data discovery" protocol under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1) proportionality standards, contending that model-weight documentation and pre-training data manifests are common to all defendants. Defense counsel across the AI defendants — Anthropic represented by Latham & Watkins LLP, OpenAI by Morrison & Foerster LLP — have countered that consolidated discovery improperly aggregates distinct factual records and would impose disproportionate burden (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Judge Freeman's forthcoming ruling on the "expressive output" theory is the advisory's single most consequential pending development for AI copyright litigation broadly. The question — whether a model's statistically-derived output can constitute a "derivative work" under § 101 when no verbatim copying is alleged — has no binding circuit precedent. The Copyright Office's March 2025 report on AI and copyright declined to resolve the derivative-work question legislatively, leaving it squarely to the courts (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). A ruling is anticipated by **August 2026** at the class-certification briefing deadline.

### IV. Visa/Mastercard Interchange MDL — Supplemental Fee Petition & Objector Renewed Activity

*In re: Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y.), before **Judge Margo K. Brodie** and **Magistrate Judge Joseph A. Marutollo** in the Theodore Roosevelt Federal Building in Brooklyn, entered a new phase of litigation activity this week as class counsel — Robins Kaplan LLP, Berger Montague PC, and Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP — filed a supplemental fee petition seeking an additional **$185 million** in attorneys' fees from the settlement fund, arguing that the post-approval litigation work through multiple rounds of objections and appeals constitutes compensable "common benefit" services under *Boeing Co. v. Van Gemert*, 444 U.S. 472 (1980) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The underlying settlement — a revised **$5.54 billion** class settlement that received final approval following years of appellate challenges, including the Second Circuit's 2016 decertification of the earlier settlement class — is one of the largest antitrust recoveries in U.S. history. The supplemental fee petition has drawn renewed objections from the National Retail Federation and a coalition of large-merchant objectors, who argue that the percentage-of-fund methodology class counsel proposes (approximately 3.3% of the supplemental fee on the revised fund) double-counts prior fee awards and that the lodestar cross-check yields a multiplier above **4.5x** that is excessive under *In re: Cardinal Health Inc. Securities Litigation*, 528 F. Supp. 2d 752 (S.D. Ohio 2007) and its progeny (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Judge Brodie has scheduled a fee-hearing status conference for **June 24, 2026**.

### V. Purdue Pharma / OxyContin — Post-*Harrington* Restructuring Plan & Creditor-Rights Downstream Effects

The Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024), which held 5–4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize non-consensual third-party releases for non-debtors who have not themselves filed for bankruptcy, continues to reverberate through the Purdue reorganization and the broader mass-tort bankruptcy landscape (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__). On remand, the Second Circuit directed the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York — before **Judge Sean H. Lane** — to oversee a reconstituted plan process stripping the Sackler-family releases (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The reconstituted plan, filed in early 2025, proposed a **$6.0 billion** Sackler contribution in exchange for a narrower injunction limited to consensual releases from individual opioid creditors who affirmatively opt into the release — a structure designed to thread the *Harrington* needle. As of this advisory, the Bankruptcy Court is evaluating competing objections from the Ad Hoc Committee of NAS (Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome) Children and individual-victim creditors who argue that even the opt-in release framework impermissibly channels claims away from state courts in a manner inconsistent with *Harrington*'s reasoning (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). A confirmation hearing on the reconstituted plan is targeted for **fall 2026**; the exact date remains subject to resolution of discovery disputes over Sackler financial disclosures.

The *Harrington* fallout extends beyond Purdue: mass-tort debtors including former talc litigants and 3M Combat Arms Earplugs claimants have had to recalibrate plan structures to avoid third-party release provisions that no longer pass muster, with direct downstream effects on the class-action bar's recovery calculus in parallel state-court proceedings (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### VI. Roundup / Bayer — State Court Settlements & Federal Preemption Overhead

Bayer AG's continuing litigation exposure from Roundup (glyphosate) product-liability claims — arising from *Monsanto Co.* herbicide use and linking glyphosate to non-Hodgkin lymphoma — encompasses approximately **54,000** pending plaintiff claims as of Q1 2026, following the resolution of a substantial tranche in 2020 through 2023 settlements that cost Bayer approximately **$10.9 billion** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The company's stated strategy of seeking a Supreme Court ruling on federal preemption — under *Riegel v. Medtronic, Inc.*, 552 U.S. 312 (2008), by analogy — has not yet produced a cert grant, leaving Bayer to manage state-court trials individually.

The St. Louis, Missouri state courts remain the epicenter of active trials. Several plaintiff verdicts in the $50–$80 million range have been upheld through Missouri's intermediate appellate courts; Bayer's pending cert petition in *Bayer AG v. Hardeman* (No. 25-1142, U.S.) — which squarely presents the implied preemption question under FIFRA, 7 U.S.C. § 136v — is currently under consideration by the Court following the SG's March 2026 brief recommending denial (__[SCOTUSblog — needs verification](https://www.scotusblog.com)__). If the Court denies cert in the October 2026 term opening, Bayer's preemption defense collapses and the remaining 54,000 claims will need resolution through additional mass-settlement negotiations or individual trial, likely requiring a further reserve increase beyond the **€2.5 billion** currently provisioned on Bayer's 2026 balance sheet (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__).

### VII. Apple App Store & Meta Waiver Track — Mass Arb Structural Updates

The Apple App Store mass-arbitration campaign — spearheaded by Labaton Keller Sucharow LLP and driven by developer plaintiffs alleging anticompetitive commission structures in violation of state UCL/CLRA claims and the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. § 2 — continues to process demands through JAMS under its Mass Arbitration Procedures effective January 2024. Approximately **35,000** individual developer demands remain pending; a bellwether cohort of 50 cases selected by JAMS was assigned to a single neutral (Ret. Judge Layn Phillips) for coordinated discovery and award protocol development (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Apple's parallel federal-court challenge to JAMS fee-allocation methodology — filed in the N.D. Cal. — has been stayed pending the AAA's bellwether ruling in the Google advertiser proceedings, creating cross-forum dependencies between the two largest mass-arbitration campaigns currently active.

Meta's "waiver track" — arising from Meta's repeated invocation and then retraction of arbitration clauses in its platform terms across the 2021–2023 period — has generated a secondary class-action theory that Meta is equitably estopped from compelling arbitration in the current proceedings. This theory is now before **Judge James Donato** in *In re: Meta Platforms, Inc. Consumer Privacy Litigation*, MDL No. 3047 (N.D. Cal.), Case No. 3:22-cv-03580-JD (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Judge Donato's prior rulings have been skeptical of Meta's arbitration-enforcement posture; a ruling on the estoppel motion is anticipated before **August 31, 2026**.
