### Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust — Settlement Administration & Objection Cycle (N.D. Ala.)

The *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 2406 (N.D. Ala., Judge R. David Proctor), remains the single largest U.S. antitrust class settlement in healthcare, with the operative **$2.67 billion settlement fund** covering subscriber and provider classes. Judge Proctor's March 2026 order set a **June 18, 2026** final-approval hearing date following a remand-related housekeeping cycle on the provider-class carve-out (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The claims administrator, Epiq Class Action & Claims Solutions, reported as of May 9, 2026 that approximately **4.1 million subscriber claims** had been filed against the roughly 9.2 million class members identified in the notice database—a 44.6% participation rate that class counsel at Whatley Kallas and Hausfeld LLP characterize as "historically strong for a complex antitrust settlement of this scope" (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The outstanding objector bloc centers on a coalition of large self-funded employer plans that contend the **Blue Card program injunctive relief**—which caps service-area geographic restrictions among BCBS licensees—does not reach their administrative services only (ASO) contracts with member plans. Lead objector counsel at Proskauer Rose LLP filed a supplemental brief on May 14, 2026 (Dkt. 2988-1) arguing that the injunctive provisions, as drafted, will not prevent BCBS licensees from re-imposing de facto market-allocation through data-sharing side agreements. Class counsel's opposition is due **May 29, 2026**. The fund has been drawing Treasury bill interest since initial escrow deposit in late 2023; as of Q1 2026, accrued interest is estimated to have added approximately **$112 million** to the distributable pool, bringing total expected distribution capacity to approximately **$2.78 billion** before administrative fees (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — AAA Threshold Decision & Delegation Clause Fight (~$218 Billion Demand)

The **Keller Postman** Google advertiser campaign—which has styled itself as a **~$218 billion** aggregate demand based on overcharge theories under Section 1 of the Sherman Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1, tied to Google's alleged Search and Display monopoly—achieved a significant procedural milestone this week when AAA's Commercial Arbitration Tribunal issued a **mass-arbitration threshold ruling** in a bellwether tranche of approximately 1,400 individual advertiser demands (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). AAA's threshold decision, issued by a three-member administrative panel on May 19, 2026, determined that Google's standard advertiser terms of service—specifically the **Google Ads Terms, Section 13(b)** governing dispute resolution—contain a valid delegation clause that assigns gateway arbitrability questions to the arbitrator rather than to a court, rejecting Google's argument that the AAA's mass-arbitration supplementary rules (the "MA Rules," effective March 2024) themselves create unconscionability that should be resolved judicially.

This ruling has outsized significance because it means Keller Postman can proceed to merits selection across the full campaign without first obtaining a federal-court order compelling arbitration. Google's lead outside counsel at **Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati** has signaled it will seek emergency review in the **Northern District of California** under 9 U.S.C. § 10 (FAA Section 10) on the theory that AAA exceeded its authority by issuing a consolidated threshold decision rather than individual arbitrator rulings per demand (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The Northern District docket has not yet assigned a judge as of press time. Practitioners should monitor whether Chief Judge Richard Seeborg's standing order on related-case designations routes this to the existing Google antitrust MDL, *In re Google Digital Advertising Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 3010 (S.D.N.Y., Judge P. Kevin Castel), where a separate class track is proceeding—a split-venue dynamic that could generate conflicting rulings on delegation and unconscionability.

### Visa / Mastercard Interchange — $5.54 Billion Settlement, E.D.N.Y. Renewed Objector Campaign

*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Margo K. Brodie), returned to the docket this week with a **renewed objection** filed May 20, 2026 by the **National Retail Federation** and a coalition of 38 large merchant plaintiffs-turned-objectors (Dkt. 9112) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The coalition argues that the **revised $5.54 billion** settlement—down from the $7.25 billion figure vacated by the Second Circuit in *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee*, 827 F.3d 223 (2d Cir. 2016) on adequacy-of-representation grounds—still fails to adequately compensate merchants for prospective interchange overcharges because the injunctive surcharging provisions contain a **MFN clause** permitting Visa and Mastercard to grant exceptions to preferred networks that would effectively hollow out the surcharging right. Judge Brodie is currently evaluating whether to re-open the fairness hearing or accept supplemental briefing in lieu of live testimony; her order is expected by **June 6, 2026** (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Background: the settlement class covers U.S. merchants who accepted Visa or Mastercard cards between **January 1, 2004 and January 25, 2019**, representing an estimated **12 million merchant locations**. Average per-merchant recovery under the current allocation formula is approximately **$450**, with large-volume retailers potentially recovering six to seven figures individually. The claims window has been extended twice; the current deadline is **July 31, 2026**. Defense counsel at **Paul, Weiss, Rifkind, Wharton & Garrison** (Mastercard) and **Arnold & Porter Kaye Scholer** (Visa) have opposed re-opening the hearing, arguing that the renewed objection is time-barred under Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e)(5) and constitutes collateral attack on Judge Brodie's prior conditional-approval order.

### Purdue Pharma / Sackler — Post-*Harrington* Plan Amendment Proceedings (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.)

Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 144 S. Ct. 2071 (2024), which held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize non-debtor third-party releases of the kind that shielded the Sackler family from civil opioid liability, the **reorganized Purdue / Knoa Pharma** entity has been navigating plan-amendment proceedings before Judge Sean H. Lane in the **Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York** (Case No. 19-23649-SHL) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The amended plan filed April 3, 2026 (Dkt. 6778) proposes a **$6.0 billion revised Sackler contribution**, up from the $4.325 billion figure in the vacated plan, in exchange for a narrowed injunction covering only debtor-estate claims rather than the broader channeling injunction struck down in *Harrington*.

The official committee of unsecured creditors—anchored by the states' attorneys general coalition and personal-injury victim advocates represented by **Motley Rice LLC** and **Gilbert LLP**—has filed conditional support for the revised structure while preserving objections to the Sackler payment schedule, which contemplates **nine annual installments** beginning Q1 2027 (Dkt. 6801). The U.S. Trustee, represented by the Office of the United States Trustee Region 2, has raised renewed concerns that even the narrowed injunction may run afoul of *Harrington* to the extent it sweeps in successor-liability claims held by future claimants (Dkt. 6815, filed May 12, 2026). A confirmation hearing on the amended plan is tentatively scheduled for **September 8, 2026**. Key risk: if the amended plan is confirmed and a single creditor class is deemed to have voted in bad faith, the Second Circuit's prior reversal history in this case—*In re Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 69 F.4th 45 (2d Cir. 2023), rev'd sub nom. *Harrington*—creates appellate uncertainty that could extend resolution into 2027 or beyond (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### Roundup / Glyphosate — Bayer Settlement Machinery & Missouri Bellwether

**Bayer AG's** effort to achieve global peace on Roundup / glyphosate litigation continues to operate on two tracks simultaneously. On the settlement-administration side, the voluntary settlement program managed by **Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe** (plaintiff liaison) and **Covington & Burling** (Bayer) has processed claims from approximately **125,000 claimants** across the Roundup MDL, *In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2741 (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria), with a reported **$10.9 billion aggregate reserve** set by Bayer (reduced from the initial $11.6 billion reserve after several thousand claimants opted out) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Judge Chhabria has taken no further action on Bayer's 2022-era attempt to achieve a **science panel** mechanism following his rejection of that structure; the MDL now runs on an individual bellwether track.

The more immediate litigation risk surfaces in Missouri state court, where a **St. Louis City Circuit Court** jury returned a **$2.25 billion** verdict against Bayer in *Stephens v. Monsanto* in February 2026—later reduced by the trial judge to **$611 million** on remittitur under Missouri's punitive-damages cap proportionality analysis (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Bayer has noticed appeal to the **Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District**. The Missouri appellate posture is relevant to federal MDL claimants because Bayer's global-peace reserve modeling depends on keeping individual state-court verdicts within a range that does not trigger adverse accounting events; the *Stephens* remittitur, if reversed on appeal and the full $2.25 billion reinstated, could force a reserve restatement (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__).

### Big Tech Mass Arbitration — Apple App Store, Meta Waiver Track, Amazon Seller, AAA/JAMS Fee Mechanics

**Apple App Store:** The **Keller Postman** Apple developer mass-arbitration campaign—approximately **67,000 demands** filed with AAA asserting monopolization theories under *Epic Games v. Apple*, 67 F.4th 946 (9th Cir. 2023)—remains in a pre-merits fee-dispute posture. AAA issued a May 5, 2026 administrative letter to Apple's counsel at **Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher** noting that Apple had failed to pay **$36.7 million** in upfront filing fees for the pending demands and that AAA would decline to administer further demands unless remittance was made within 30 days. Apple's position, articulated in its May 15, 2026 response letter filed as an exhibit in the parallel N.D. Cal. proceeding (*Epic Games v. Apple*, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR), is that AAA's mass-arbitration fee schedule constitutes an unconscionable penalty that frustrates the arbitral process itself (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers has set a **June 10, 2026** case management conference to address whether to compel Apple to pay AAA fees or permit transfer to JAMS under the alternative-forum clause in Apple's developer agreement.

**Meta Arbitration Waiver Track:** Following the Ninth Circuit's 2024 decision in *Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc.*, 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. 2024), which invalidated certain mass-arbitration fee-shifting structures as unconscionable, Meta Platforms pivoted to a **waiver track** in which it voluntarily waives arbitration for claims below $15,000 and channels them into a dedicated Small Claims Track administered through AAA's Consumer Rules. In the **Northern District of California** proceeding coordinating Facebook privacy claims, *In re Facebook, Inc. Consumer Privacy User Profile Litigation*, MDL No. 2843 (N.D. Cal., Judge Vince Chhabria), Meta has argued this waiver eliminates standing to seek judicial determination of arbitrability for the small-claims cohort (Dkt. 1044, May 7, 2026). Plaintiffs' counsel at **Lieff Cabraser Heimann & Bernstein** and **Keller Rohrback** counter that the waiver is illusory because the Small Claims Track's confidentiality requirements prevent aggregation of decisions, insulating Meta from collateral estoppel exposure (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

**Amazon Seller:** The Amazon seller mass-arbitration campaign—approximately **20,000 demands** filed by merchants alleging Section 2 monopolization in the Buy Box algorithm—has stalled at JAMS over fee non-payment. JAMS issued a **mass-arbitration case-management order** on May 1, 2026 requiring Amazon to submit a **$4.1 million** administrative deposit by May 30, 2026 or JAMS will administratively close the pending demands without prejudice. Amazon's counsel at **Sidley Austin LLP** has filed a motion in the **Western District of Washington** (No. 2:24-cv-00891-RSL, Judge Robert S. Lasnik) seeking a stay of JAMS proceedings pending resolution of Amazon's argument that the seller agreements' arbitration clauses were superseded by the 2023 Amazon Business Solutions Agreement amendment, which carves out antitrust claims for federal-court resolution (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

**AAA/JAMS Mechanics Update:** The AAA's **Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules** (effective March 11, 2024) introduced a bellwether process under which parties must arbitrate a representative tranche (default: 25–50 cases) before proceeding to the broader mass. JAMS' **Mass Arbitration Procedures** (effective August 2024) similarly require a phased bellwether, but with a higher default tranche of 100 cases. The divergence in tranche sizing has created strategic forum-selection dynamics: Keller Postman has filed Google and Apple campaigns at AAA (smaller tranche, faster merits exposure for defendants) while plaintiffs in the Amazon campaign filed at JAMS (larger tranche, more pre-trial discovery leverage per claimant). Courts have not yet uniformly resolved whether a respondent's failure to pay arbitral fees under the AAA/JAMS mass-arbitration schedules triggers the "materially breached" waiver standard under **9 U.S.C. § 3** and the Supreme Court's *Smith v. Spizzirri*, 601 U.S. 472 (2024) framework (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).
