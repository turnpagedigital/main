### NextEra Securities Fraud Settlement: $150M Proposed Fund, S.D. Fla., Judge Cannon


Defendants in the case — NextEra Energy, its subsidiary Florida Power & Light, and former top executives from both companies — would pay $150 million into a fund to be recovered by investors who purchased or acquired NextEra securities between December 2, 2021, and February 1, 2023.
 The theory of liability is specific: 
plaintiffs pursued a novel theory of recovery — that after NextEra and FPL denied reports of involvement in political wrongdoing, NextEra's subsequent disclosure of elevated risks and the resignation of a top executive revealed the truth and caused investor losses.
 The claims arise under Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and SEC Rule 10b-5, per the complaint as previously documented by Paul, Weiss. 
Plaintiffs' counsel characterized the proposed settlement as the largest securities fraud recovery in decades in the Southern District of Florida; the proposal arrives as NextEra moves to acquire Dominion Energy in a transaction that would make it the world's largest regulated power company.



The settlement was presented to U.S. District Judge Aileen M. Cannon in court filings posted Monday.
 The case is *Jastram v. NextEra Energy, Inc.*, No. 9:23-cv-80833 (S.D. Fla.), 
assigned to Judge Aileen M. Cannon.
 
There are likely at least 200,000 eligible members of the settlement class, according to court filings.
 
The case had previously been dismissed by Judge Cannon in 2024, when she found that lead investors had failed to demonstrate that any company statements during the political controversy coverage were sufficiently misleading; a panel of Eleventh Circuit judges resurrected it.
 That reversal — which forced settlement — is a direct lesson in appellate persistence under the PSLRA's scienter pleading standard.

The denominator math at this stage matters. With approximately 200,000 eligible class members and a $150 million fund (before attorneys' fees and expenses, which in a case of this size typically run 25–33% of the gross fund), net per-share recovery will depend critically on claims filing rates. Historically, retail-investor claims rates in securities settlements run 20–40% of eligible shares; institutional lead plaintiffs generally file at higher rates. 
The City of Hollywood Police Officers' Retirement System and the Pembroke Pines Firefighters & Police Officers Pension Fund serve as lead plaintiffs and urged the court to issue preliminary approval.
 No preliminary approval hearing date has been confirmed in public filings reviewed for this run; the next procedural trigger is Judge Cannon's preliminary approval order, which will set the claims deadline and objection window.

### J&J Talc MDL: Dismissal Motion After Expert Withdrawal — D.N.J., Judge Shipp / Special Master Wolfson

This development is the most consequential near-term risk event in the tracked MDL universe. 
Johnson & Johnson urged the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey to toss all pending cases in the sprawling multidistrict litigation after plaintiffs withdrew their two "marquee" experts on the link between the disease and talc use.
 The MDL is assigned to Judge Michael A. Shipp. The case caption is *In re: Johnson & Johnson Talcum Powder Products Marketing, Sales Practices and Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 2:16-md-02738 (D.N.J.).

The strategic context is critical. 
Talc claims pending in the MDL increased by 9,375 cases from last year; with at least 68,029 claims pending, the J&J talc litigation is the largest active MDL in the U.S.
 Earlier in the proceeding, 
retired U.S. District Judge Freda Wolfson, serving as special master, concluded that plaintiffs' experts used reliable, mainstream scientific methods and that epidemiological evidence shows a statistically significant association between genital talc use and the disease.
 That Daubert clearance — issued January 22, 2026 — was the foundational ruling enabling the trial track. The withdrawal of the two marquee experts now directly undermines that foundation. 
In a May 29 motion before Judge Shipp, J&J argued that Drs. Daniel Cramer, Daniel Clarke-Pearson, and Judith Wolf changed their opinions during a recent 702/Accutane hearing and therefore should be excluded.


The interplay here is technically complex: plaintiffs apparently withdrew certain experts preemptively rather than face exclusion, but J&J is now arguing that withdrawal itself is grounds to dismiss the entire MDL for lack of general causation. If Judge Shipp grants a broad dismissal motion — or even limits the surviving expert pool severely enough — the aggregate exposure for J&J on the ovarian cancer track, which plaintiffs have valued in the tens of billions based on individual trial verdicts (including a Baltimore jury's $1.5 billion verdict in December 2025 in a mesothelioma case), could contract materially. 
The number of pending cases grew from 58,205 in 2025 to 68,029 in 2026.
 That case-volume growth now faces an inflection point: J&J's motion is effectively a request for summary disposition of the entire ovarian cancer docket. Briefing schedule on that motion has not been confirmed in sources reviewed for this run.

### Depo-Provera MDL: In-Principle Global Settlement, Daubert Hearings June 24–25 — N.D. Fla., Judge Rodgers

The Law360 headline referenced a Pfizer deal to end the Depo-Provera MDL. Confirmed: 
both sides recently agreed in principle to a global settlement for the Depo-Provera lawsuits that could resolve thousands of cases.
 
Exact details, including compensation amounts, have not yet been revealed as both sides work to finalize the agreement.
 This advisory treats the financial terms as unverified and will not characterize scope or amount pending formal disclosure.

What is confirmed: 
the Depo-Provera MDL (No. 3140) is in the Northern District of Florida, with 5,508 cases pending before Judge M. Casey Rodgers as of June 2026.
 
Daubert causation hearings are currently scheduled for June 24 and 25, 2026, at which Judge Rodgers will hear arguments on whether Depo-Provera and meningiomas are scientifically linked.
 The timing of the in-principle settlement announcement — one week ahead of Daubert — is consistent with the pattern observed in 3M earplug litigation (also before Judge Rodgers, who managed that case to its $6 billion resolution): 
Judge Rodgers previously managed the massive 3M earplug litigation that resulted in a $6 billion settlement.



As of May 22, 2026, Pfizer and plaintiffs' counsel were actively battling over what expert testimony will be used in the upcoming trials — a normal and critical part of the MDL process.
 The expert battle was the final pre-settlement pressure point; Pfizer's pending motion seeks to narrow plaintiffs' general causation testimony on timing, duration of use, and new versus accelerated meningioma formation. 
As of June 1, 2026, there are 5,508 lawsuits in the MDL, a large uptick that includes 1,831 more lawsuits than were present on May 1, 2026.
 The rapid case velocity — nearly 2,000 new filings in a single month — gives plaintiffs' steering committee considerable leverage in any global allocation negotiation.
