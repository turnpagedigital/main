Today's Briefing

Three mega-deals move simultaneously; claims rates rewrite the playbook.

Mega-Class Action Desk10:00 AM ETMonday, August 3, 2026

Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Post-Approval / Distribution Phase

Covered here continuously since preliminary approval. The status line today: final approval entered; distribution timeline still unconfirmed.

On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement in Bartz v. Anthropic and entered final judgment.

The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," overruled all 53 objections, and found that the proposed settlement "was negotiated at arm's length" and "treats Class Members equitably relative to each other."

Key structural outcomes from the July 20 order: class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately 0.03% of the total number of works in the settlement class. That claims rate is unusually high for a copyright class action and directly compresses the effective denominator, concentrating the fund among participating claimants. The court disallowed opt-outs submitted after the February 9, 2026 deadline, with narrow exceptions for two individual requestors (Esquivel and Castells), and approved expense reimbursement of $2,635,197.46 plus a cost reserve of $18,220,000 in anticipated future expenses, and service awards of $15,000 per named plaintiff ($45,000 total for the three class representatives — down from the requested $50,000 each).

The fee award, reduced from $300 million to $187.5 million, is not contested in any new filing as of today. Authors urged the court to grant final approval alongside the revised fee request of $187.5 million, down from the initial $300 million ask, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.

The open question now is distribution timing. The settlement website confirms the claim submission deadline passed March 30, 2026, and the opt-out deadline passed February 9, 2026 — meaning the claims pool is locked. The Authors Guild noted it looks forward to the commencement of distribution, though no specific date for payouts has been announced. The tracked-case ground truth shows the last docket entry (Entry 681) filed July 21, 2026, the day after final approval — no new filings in the 13 days since. The next material event to watch is either a notice of appeal by one of the 53 overruled objectors or the claims administrator's first distribution report; neither has surfaced as of this writing.
