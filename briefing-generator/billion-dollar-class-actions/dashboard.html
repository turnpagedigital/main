J&J Talc MDL-2738 — $5.5 Billion Settlement Announced; Structural Approval Process Ahead

This is the largest new development in the mass-tort space since the prior advisory. The procedural trigger matters: the settlement follows a July 22 ruling by U.S. Magistrate Judge Rukhsanah Singh in the multidistrict litigation in New Jersey, granting Johnson & Johnson's motion to show cause why all the talc cases shouldn't be dismissed, after lead plaintiffs' lawyers withdrew their two key specific causation experts from the bellwether cases. That ruling — a show-cause order covering the entire docket — created maximum settlement pressure on the plaintiffs' bar within days, and the deal emerged publicly on July 27.

Johnson & Johnson said it would pay an estimated $5.5 billion to resolve tens of thousands of lawsuits alleging its baby powder and other talc products cause ovarian cancer, in a deal that could end a legal battle that has dogged the company for a decade.

The settlement resolves 99.75% of the talcum powder litigation in federal and state courts, coming 15 years after the first suits alleged J&J's baby powder caused ovarian cancer.

Chris Seeger, an attorney who represents approximately 2,500 clients with talc claims and helped negotiate the agreement, said J&J could ultimately pay $7 billion or more.

$3 billion is set to be paid out as soon as 2027.

The MDL sits before Chief Judge Michael A. Shipp and Magistrate Judge Rukhsanah L. Singh in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey. As of July 2026, there are 68,435 pending cases in MDL No. 2738, up from 68,029 cases in June — more than 400 additional lawsuits added in a single month.

The talc litigation featured dozens of verdicts for both sides and three failed bankruptcies by J&J's subsidiaries.

The move comes after three failed attempts by J&J to settle the litigation through the Texas Two-Step bankruptcy procedure.

The settlement is not yet before the court for approval. The deal is structured as a direct MDL resolution rather than a bankruptcy plan — a structural choice forced on J&J after its third Texas Two-Step attempt (Red River Talc LLC, Chapter 11) was also rejected. This matters for claims-rate mechanics: unlike a bankruptcy trust, an MDL global settlement typically requires individualized claimant sign-offs via plaintiffs' counsel, meaning the 99.75% resolution figure is a participation target, not a confirmed opt-in rate. Watch for: (a) a Case Management Order from Judge Shipp setting a settlement approval schedule; (b) plaintiffs' counsel submission of evidence of aggregate claimant authorization; and (c) any motion to stay the show-cause response deadline (currently running at 120 days from July 22) pending formalization of the settlement agreement. Plaintiffs have 120 days to respond to the show-cause order and identify admissible expert evidence supporting specific causation. The leverage dynamic is clear: without a settlement framework filed before that deadline, unrepresented plaintiffs face potential mass dismissal.

Keller Postman / Google Mass Arbitration — Campaign Architecture; Google ToS Response

No new forum ruling or remedies decision issued in the 72-hour window. The structural picture, updated from the prior advisory: following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic and anticompetitive conduct, Keller Postman is representing U.S. businesses in a coordinated mass arbitration seeking recovery for alleged advertising overcharges.

According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade.

The $218 billion figure is an estimate, not a filed demand amount; in mass arbitration, the leverage comes from the volume of individual filings and the per-claim fee obligations they trigger — but the scale signals that every business purchasing Google search ads or display advertising during the monopoly period is a potential claimant.

The firm represents approximately 10,000 clients with total spend exceeding $20 billion since 2016, and has commenced arbitration proceedings for the majority of those claimants; the recovery period spans from 2016 onward, with potential damages ranging from 15% to 30% of total spend, requiring individual arbitration for each client.

The firm operates on a contingency fee basis of 40% with no upfront costs to clients.

The development worth flagging: Google revised its advertiser Terms of Service in July 2026. In May 2026, Keller Postman commenced mass arbitration after 2016, pointing to antitrust and the $728 billion in U.S. ad spend; Google's updating of the framework — and in some places, dropping the clause — is described by Google as being proactive about how it settles things with advertisers.

Google's advertiser contracts specify a particular arbitration forum — likely AAA or JAMS, both of which have mass arbitration rules triggered at 25 or more simultaneous coordinated claims. The ToS revision does not affect the vested claims of existing Keller Postman clients whose arbitration demands were filed before the amendment became effective, but it is worth verifying whether the revised terms include a 30-day opt-out window giving new advertisers the ability to elect court instead of arbitration. The FAA's Concepcion / Epic Systems framework preserves pre-amendment rights; post-amendment claimants will need independent analysis. The Google mass arbitration may be the first to seek to represent corporate plaintiffs, as most group legal proceedings to date have sought to arbitrate consumer or labor-related claims. Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling in the ad tech antitrust case (United States v. Google, E.D. Va.) — expected in 2026 — remains the downstream liability anchor for this campaign.

MDL Landscape — Supporting Data Points

AAA closed 26 mass arbitrations with 37,648-plus claimants in H1 2025 alone; the 9th Circuit upheld JAMS consolidation of 7,300 Starz claims in Jones v. Starz Entertainment. The pipeline of BIPA, VPPA, and CIPA privacy claims is accelerating independent of the Big Tech campaigns and warrants continued monitoring under this tab. Separately, on June 29, 2026, a Minnesota judge denied J&J's request for a new trial after a jury awarded $65.5 million to a 37-year-old plaintiff who alleged that exposure to asbestos-contaminated Johnson's Baby Powder caused her mesothelioma, following the court's earlier decision upholding the verdict based on sufficient evidence. That verdict — now twice-sustained — is one of the pressure-point data points driving the economics of the July 27 MDL settlement.
