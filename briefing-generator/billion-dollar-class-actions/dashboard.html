II. Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Final Approval Entered July 20; Distribution Clock Starts Now

This one crossed the finish line while still carrying its own tail risk. On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the landmark $1.5 billion class action settlement in Bartz v. Anthropic and entered final judgment in the case. The court issued a 23-page order finding the settlement fair, reasonable, and adequate and overruling all 53 objections. The court emphasized that the $1.5 billion settlement provides "substantial benefits to the Class in light of the novel claims asserted," and noted that taking the claims to trial could have left the authors "no recourse" despite their strong downloading-related claims.

The claims picture is unusually strong for a class action. Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works, or 0.03% of the total number of works. That participation rate is exceptional — typical mega-fund consumer class actions see single-digit claim rates; a 91.3% rate here compresses the per-claim denominator and should produce materially higher per-work recoveries for participating authors. The court's final judgment triggers the appeal window: any objector who preserved their standing at the May 14 fairness hearing has 30 days from entry of judgment to file a notice of appeal to the Ninth Circuit. Given that Judge Martínez-Olguín overruled all 53 objections, any appellate path is uphill, but it delays distribution until the window closes or any appeal is resolved.

On the residual handling: every dollar not paid in fees stays in the non-reversionary fund for authors, and if money remains after all valid claims are paid, the parties anticipate redistributing it to class members; if a final balance remains that is economically infeasible to distribute, it will go — subject to court approval — to charitable purposes. No distribution date has been publicly announced, per the Authors Guild's post-approval statement: the Authors Guild looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process so that authors can start getting paid, noting that while a specific date for commencement of payouts has not been announced, they hope and expect it will happen quickly.

One live docket item: a late opt-out application by Donald S. Passman was filed at Dkt. 676 (filed June 30, entered July 1), asserting objections and seeking leave to submit a late request for exclusion from the class action settlement. Class counsel opposed. Judge Martínez-Olguín's final approval order addressed this — the court confirmed the Special Master mechanism for dispute resolution: any disputes "that cannot be resolved by mutual agreement with the settlement administrator" will be submitted to the Special Master, and all submissions "should remain confidential and under seal," with the Special Master's decisions binding. Passman's situation and similar late-exclusion requests will run through that channel.

III. Depo-Provera MDL — In re Depo-Provera (Depot Medroxyprogesterone Acetate) Products Liability Litigation, MDL No. 3140 (N.D. Fla., Judge M. Casey Rodgers) — Settlement Administration Underway; New Pilot Plaintiffs Approved July 29

The Pfizer Depo-Provera litigation is in post-agreement mechanics, with a fresh procedural development yesterday. The Florida federal judge overseeing the MDL — asserting claims of a link between brain tumors and the hormonal contraceptive Depo-Provera — approved three new pilot plaintiffs on Wednesday for the cases remaining after a global settlement, calling the settlement an "excellent outcome." That approval, entered July 29, reflects the MDL's bifurcation: the global agreement covers federal claims meeting eligibility criteria, but a population of cases falling outside those criteria requires its own pretrial structure.

The underlying global agreement: Pfizer reached an agreement in principle to resolve the federal Depo-Provera MDL, and Judge M. Casey Rodgers, who oversees the MDL in the Northern District of Florida, vacated the deadlines in the first bellwether case.

The settlement covers all plaintiffs with cases pending in the MDL who meet the eligibility criteria agreed to by the parties. Financial terms have not been publicly disclosed. The settlement does not include cases filed in state court, most of which are filed in New York and Delaware.

The docket count underscores the scale: there are now 5,830 lawsuits consolidated in the Depo-Provera MDL.

As of July 2026, more than 5,800 Depo-Provera lawsuits have been consolidated in the MDL.

Pfizer argued it cannot be blamed for failing to warn customers of a meningioma risk because the FDA rejected a label update in 2024 that would have included that information, but that argument shifted materially in December 2025 when the FDA approved a new label for Depo-Provera warning customers of a potential connection to meningiomas.

Recovery lens for state-court claimants. The federal MDL global settlement expressly excludes state-court cases. Significant parallel activity is occurring in state courts, with Delaware hosting 340 cases, New York hosting 119, California 91, and Minnesota 72, with smaller groups in Pennsylvania, Illinois, Connecticut, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Wisconsin, and Missouri. These claimants are outside the settlement envelope; the appointment of new pilot plaintiffs for non-settling federal cases creates a parallel track that will develop its own Daubert and expert record. That track may generate settlement gravity of its own — or serve as leverage to extract improved global terms for late joiners.

IV. Mass Arbitration — Keller Postman / Big Tech Campaigns: Steady State

No material docket movements this cycle in the Keller Postman Google advertiser mass arbitration (estimated exposure ~$218B under Sherman Act §§ 1–2 and Clayton Act § 4 treble-damages framework) or the Apple App Store, Meta waiver-track, and Amazon seller campaigns. The Google campaign remains in the batching and scheduling phase at AAA under the Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules; the central legal question — whether arbitrators may coordinate discovery across thousands of contemporaneous JAMS/AAA individual proceedings — continues to develop at the individual arbitration enforcement level in the N.D. Cal. and S.D.N.Y. district courts. No new bellwether rulings or global settlement signals in the last 72 hours.

Structural note. The Bartz final approval is relevant context here: a 91.3% claims rate in an author class of works confirms that sophisticated rights-holders, when mobilized with direct notice, file claims. Mass arbitration claimants who have not confirmed receipt of their demand tracking numbers through AAA's online portal should do so now; lapsed demand deadlines in mass arbitration campaigns are rarely extended.

Recommended Actions

For creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel tracking these matters today: in the J&J talc MDL, clients holding qualifying ovarian cancer claims should confirm within the next 30–45 days whether their lead counsel is among the firms that have already signed on to the 95% threshold and, if not, understand that a failure to reach that threshold unwinds the deal entirely and returns the case to MDL 2738 trial tracks before Judge Shipp; the per-claim value assigned in the settlement grid should be benchmarked against the uncapped upside Seeger is projecting before any recommendation is made to accept; mesothelioma claimants should verify expressly that their injuries fall outside this agreement, as those claims were handled in a prior track and are reportedly not covered here. In Bartz v. Anthropic (No. 3:24-cv-05417), the 30-day appellate window from the July 20 judgment is the critical path item — any party who filed a preserved objection at the May 14 hearing should assess whether a Ninth Circuit appeal serves their interests or whether accepting the claims administrator process (routed to the Special Master for disputes) is the more efficient path; authors who filed claims before the deadline should expect to receive payment notices once the appeal window closes or any appeal is resolved. In the Depo-Provera MDL (MDL No. 3140), state-court claimants in Delaware, New York, California, and Minnesota are outside the Pfizer global agreement and should treat their cases as active litigation: the preemption and Daubert questions that drove the federal settlement remain live issues in state court, and the December 2025 FDA label update warning of meningioma risk substantially weakens Pfizer's failure-to-warn preemption defense in those forums.
