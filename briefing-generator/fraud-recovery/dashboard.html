OneCoin Remission — Claims Window Has Closed; Post-Deadline Processing Begins (S.D.N.Y.)

The DOJ formally opened a remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-orchestrated a large international cryptocurrency investment scheme through a global multi-level-marketing network, with victims investing over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.

The OneCoin Remission Program is a DOJ initiative using over $40 million in forfeited assets seized from multiple key figures in the scheme.

The June 30, 2026 petition deadline has now passed. The program is in post-filing review. No payment date has been announced.

The remission process was open to victims worldwide who purchased OneCoin during the covered period and still showed a net loss after accounting for withdrawals or other recoveries.

The $40 million fund against a $4 billion loss pool is the critical recovery-pricing variable: even if every submitted petition is approved, pro-rata recoveries will be deeply fractional. Claims-market pricing on secondary OneCoin interests should reflect this arithmetic. Ruja Ignatova, the figure most closely associated with OneCoin, remains on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list, meaning further criminal forfeitures are theoretically possible but speculative and undated. The DOJ has stated it will continue working to seize criminal proceeds, but no supplemental fund has been announced. (DOJ Press ReleaseDDOJ Announcement — OneCoin Remission Process (OJ Office of Public Affairs, April 13, 2026))

Madoff / Picard — No Movement This Week; Seventeenth Distribution Tracking Continues (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 08-01789-LGB)

No new filings or announcements from the BLMIS liquidation in the last 24 hours. The last material event was the seventeenth pro-rata interim distribution, which commenced February 27, 2026. Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro-rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, with the SIPA Trustee distributing more than $253 million on a pro-rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.

The seventeenth interim distribution represented 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions in aggregate, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.

As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.

The next milestone to watch is the eighteenth distribution motion, which has not yet been filed. The fiftieth fee application — referenced in a February 13, 2026 trustee statement — is also pending; fee orders are signal events for claim pricing and worth monitoring when scheduled.
