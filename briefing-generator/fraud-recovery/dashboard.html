I. BLMIS / Picard: Seventeenth Distribution Underway; Fee Application Signal

The Madoff estate remains the benchmark for long-duration Ponzi recovery pricing. On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to account holders with allowed claims, bringing aggregate customer distributions to nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances.

The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and will be paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; combined with prior distributions, approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid, unless previously satisfied in full.

The case docket is Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB), before Judge Loretta G. Bagarozza (successor to Judge Stuart M. Bernstein). As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.

As of the same date, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation in customer advances.

The near-term pricing constraint is fee-driven, not recovery-driven. On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for work performed August 1 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest-discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour. The 10% public-interest discount continues to apply but the application volume at round fifty signals that litigation assets remain in play. With the seventeenth distribution, SIPA Chief Counsel David J. Sheehan confirmed that 1,531 accounts will be made whole, and noted that additional funds recovered between October 31, 2025 and January 31, 2026 will also be distributed.

For claims-pricing purposes: BLMIS is at 72.848 cents recovered on the allowed claim dollar, with recovery litigation continuing. The incremental distribution rate per round has been declining (1.302% in round seventeen versus 1.975% in round eleven), consistent with diminishing marginal recoveries from residual litigation. Claims trading below par on time-value grounds remain defensible at current recovery pace, but there is no catalyst visible for an eighteenth distribution motion before Q4 2026 at the earliest.

II. Stanford Receivership: Final Distribution Checks Void This Week; Fee Appeal Briefing Complete

The Stanford receivership — SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd., No. 3:09-CV-0298-N (N.D. Tex., Judge David C. Godbey), with Receiver Ralph S. Janvey — is now executing its final distribution while simultaneously litigating a material fee dispute at the Fifth Circuit. Both tracks have immediate operational relevance.

Final Distribution Payments — Void Deadline This Week. On March 9, 2026, Janvey filed the Final Distribution Schedule with the N.D. Tex., covering payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claim groups, to be issued on a rolling basis beginning 14 days after filing.

Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash them by July 26, 2026. Checks not negotiated by those deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline. This is a hard operational cut-off. Any claims-fund position carrying uncashed Stanford checks in its portfolio must act before Thursday.

On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted to the N.D. Tex. a Status Report Concerning Final Distribution Payments, providing an update on payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan. That filing is the most recent docket entry and confirms distribution issuance is ongoing.

SG Suisse Settlement — Still Contingent. The $157 million Société Générale Private Banking (Suisse) settlement remains in escrow pending resolution of the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal. On February 25, 2025, the N.D. Tex. entered an order closing all proceedings with respect to the Bar Order and barring further objections. Once that Order becomes final, the parties can proceed to dismiss the pending cases against SG Suisse — and only upon finality of those dismissals will SG Suisse be obligated to fund the $157 million settlement. The Antiguan appeal continues to gate that payment.

Fee Appeal at the Fifth Circuit — Briefing Closed. The N.D. Tex. awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees at the October 8, 2025 Final Distribution Order — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed to the Fifth Circuit, filing their opening brief on March 23, 2026.

The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.

The Examiner, having been denied party status, was granted leave to participate as amicus curiae and filed his brief in support of the district court's order on June 9, 2026. With the Examiner's June 9 brief, the Fifth Circuit briefing is now fully submitted. A ruling reducing the fee award further would modestly increase the residual pool available to claimants; a ruling reversing and awarding closer to the $43 million sought would reduce it. Fee appeals in receivership contexts are classically priced out of claim valuations — they should not be, particularly where the spread between requested ($43M) and awarded ($14.9M) is this wide.

III. OneCoin / Ignatova: DOJ Remission Deadline Passed; Post-Filing Administration Begins

The DOJ remission process for OneCoin victims — prosecutions in the S.D.N.Y., with Kroll Settlement Administration LLC as administrator — is now in claims-review posture. The DOJ announced the commencement of the remission compensation process for victims of the international OneCoin investment scheme, covering conduct orchestrated between 2014 and 2019 by Ruja Ignatova, Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and others.

As a result of misrepresentations about OneCoin, victims invested over $4 billion worldwide; the DOJ filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York.

The DOJ launched the remission process with over $40 million in forfeited assets available for distribution; the petition filing deadline was June 30, 2026. That deadline has now passed. Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator. The key structural fact for recovery-sizing purposes: $40 million against $4+ billion in victim losses implies a recovery rate well below 1% of actual losses at the current fund size. The DOJ has signaled continued pursuit of additional forfeiture. While the $40 million fund represents only a fraction of the total $4 billion lost, officials have stated they are committed to identifying and seizing further criminal proceeds to expand the compensation pool.

On the asset-tracing front, Europe remains the primary recovery theater while Ignatova is at large. On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.

The German authorities initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings" which led to the overseas forfeiture order, which applies to the remaining proceeds of sale of two London flats owned by Guernsey-registered companies.

The seized funds will be paid into Guernsey's seized assets fund and then returned to the German authorities, where they will be used to compensate victims.

The collaboration between the Public Prosecutors Office in Bielefeld and international jurisdictions has set a precedent for non-conviction-based asset forfeiture, allowing the state to seize criminal proceeds even when the primary defendant has not been convicted. This is the procedural hook that other recovery counsel should be tracking: non-conviction-based forfeiture under German law, enforced in third-country jurisdictions via mutual legal assistance, is replicable in other markets where Ignatova-linked assets are suspected. Ignatova herself remains a fugitive; she has been on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list for several years and has evaded capture since October 2017.

IV. SEC Enforcement Posture: Recalibration and Cross-Border Focus

Upstream enforcement matters because it determines the pace of new receivership appointments. The pipeline is narrowing. The SEC filed 456 enforcement actions in FY 2025, a significant decline from 583 in FY 2024, driven by a deliberate refocus away from cases that do not meaningfully advance investor protection or market integrity, including actions based primarily on technical or strict-liability violations.

David Woodcock was appointed Director of the Division of Enforcement on April 8, 2026, joining from Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP where he chaired the firm's securities enforcement practice; he replaced Acting Director Sam Waldon effective May 4, 2026.

The stated enforcement priority going forward is "first principles" fraud — offering fraud, insider trading, financial accounting fraud, and investment adviser breach of fiduciary duty. The SEC underscored its continued objective to return funds to harmed investors and combat fraud wherever it occurs, citing in particular its Cross-Border Task Force dedicated to addressing the threat that fraudsters located abroad pose to U.S. investors. For the recovery market, the Cross-Border Task Force is a meaningful signal: it is the structural feeder for new § 21 receivership appointments and 28 U.S.C. § 1782 discovery orders, particularly in crypto-adjacent offering fraud cases where assets have been moved offshore.

Under Director Margaret Ryan, who resigned after just over six months, the Division of Enforcement issued a comprehensive update to the Enforcement Manual on February 24, 2026 — the first such update since 2017. That manual revision is operationally significant for receivership practitioners: it governs how the Division presents asset-freeze and receivership-appointment requests to district courts, and updates to the Wells process may affect the pace at which targets settle versus litigate, with corresponding implications for when receiver-appointment motions get filed.

Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should take three specific steps this week. First, any position in the Stanford receivership that includes uncashed distribution checks dated April 24 or April 27, 2026 must be negotiated before July 23 and July 26, respectively — the void deadlines are hard, and the Stanford receivership website (stanfordfinancialreceivership.com) is the operative source for reissuance procedures; funds from voided checks revert to the estate and the practical path to reissuance is neither guaranteed nor fast. Second, OneCoin claimants who submitted petitions before the June 30 deadline should verify receipt with Kroll Settlement Administration (via onecoinremission.com) and begin sizing their pro-rata position against the current $40 million fund, bearing in mind that the DOJ intends to pursue additional forfeitures that could increase the pool — a secondary-market position in OneCoin remission claims at near-zero pricing may warrant evaluation against the non-conviction-based forfeiture trajectory in Europe. Third, track the Fifth Circuit fee appeal in the Stanford matter: the spread between the $43 million sought and $14.9 million awarded is wide enough that a reversal meaningfully affects net distributable assets; with all briefs now filed including the Examiner's June 9 amicus submission, a ruling is possible in Q3–Q4 2026 and it will price into the final distribution residual. For Madoff positions, no near-term catalyst is visible — the eighteenth distribution motion has not been filed and the 50th fee application cycle suggests ongoing litigation overhead — but the 72.848% recovery level means late-stage claims at deep discounts have limited upside compression remaining unless a material new settlement surfaces. Monitor the BakerHostetler fee application cycle as a forward-looking signal for settlement activity.
