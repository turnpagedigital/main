Power Block Coin — Creditor Committee Liquidation Plan, Judge Transfer, and the $133 Million Celsius Overhang

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing the plan on January 30, 2025. The plan proposes winding down the company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee, with the trustee empowered to pursue avoidance and other claims.

The tracked-case ground truth identifies the current presiding judge as Judge Cathleen D. Parker — consistent with the case designation 24-bk-23041-CDP visible in later docket entries — rather than Judge Joel T. Marker, who presided over the early Subchapter V eligibility fight. The judge reassignment is itself a procedural marker: Subchapter V cases convert to standard Chapter 11 or Chapter 7 when the debtor's eligibility is successfully challenged, and the CDP designation signals that transition has occurred.

The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million. The Celsius claim is the structural complexity that has slowed this case. Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against Power Block Coin in connection with the Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy case (No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. The allowance and priority treatment of those claims is the single largest variable in the distribution waterfall.

Power Block Coin LLC filed a voluntary petition under Chapter 11 of the Bankruptcy Code on June 20, 2024, represented by Parsons Behle & Latimer.

With less than three hours to spare before the Subchapter V debt limit dropped from $7,500,000 to $3,024,725, the debtor filed its Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024, electing to proceed under Subchapter V as an entity with aggregate noncontingent liquidated secured and unsecured debts of less than $7,500,000. The committee's challenge to Subchapter V eligibility succeeded, removing debtor-favorable plan exclusivity and enabling the committee's own plan.

The chapter11cases.com publication notes a July 19, 2026 analysis on "Pricing the Transfer: A First-Impression Framework for Valuing Appreciating Digital Assets in Avoidance Actions," which is directly relevant to this estate's posture: the liquidating trustee will need to pursue avoidance actions, and the valuation of crypto assets transferred pre-petition at potentially depressed prices (versus current values) is a live legal question. The last confirmed docket filing was July 23, 2026 (Dkt. 532, described as a "Complaint" — likely an adversary proceeding initiation) per the live data feed. That filing warrants attention: adversary complaint filings at this stage are typically the trustee or committee initiating preference, fraudulent transfer, or other avoidance litigation.

BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Eighteenth Cycle Underway

The Madoff estate provides the baseline benchmark against which all other Ponzi recovery timelines are measured. On February 27, 2026, Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee for the liquidation of Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC (BLMIS), announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.

Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.

As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation, and SIPC-committed advances will continue to increase as claims currently in litigation are allowed as a result of settlements or the conclusion of litigation. The estate is now in its eighteenth recovery cycle — no new distribution has been announced, which is consistent with the pattern of 12–18 month intervals between distributions as litigation settlements accumulate. Active clawback adversary proceedings continue in Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB). Net winners who received fictitious profits remain exposed; the Ponzi presumption under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) has been affirmed through 17 years of litigation and is not being challenged in any pending appeal of significance this desk has confirmed.

Cross-Cutting: Clawback Exposure and the Ponzi Presumption

Two structural themes warrant standing attention across all active receiverships and bankruptcies in this desk's portfolio:

Net-winner exposure. In every case above — Goliath, Power Block Coin, BLMIS — the Ponzi presumption converts ordinary distributions into constructively fraudulent transfers under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) (or state UFTA/UVTA equivalents). The two-year look-back under § 548 and the longer state-law look-back periods (up to six years under New York DCL, four years under Utah UFTA) create layered exposure. Net winners — defined as investors whose lifetime withdrawals exceed lifetime principal deposits — should treat their "profits" as contingent liabilities, not confirmed income. The recent Goliath plea acceptance (July 22) is a trigger event: it confirms the scheme's existence as a matter of judicial record, which satisfies the factual predicate for Ponzi-presumption clawback without further litigation.

Cross-border tracing in crypto schemes. Goliath's crypto-wallet discovery demands (Dkt. 61, issued April 9, 2026, seeking all documents concerning "cryptocurrency wallets belonging to Goliath") signal the estate is pursuing on-chain tracing. The mechanics — subpoenas to Coinbase and Uniswap Labs (both named in the related civil complaint record), combined with blockchain analytics — are consistent with how crypto Ponzi estates have operated in post-FTX cases. Mareva-style injunctions under CPR 25 or § 1782 discovery orders directed at offshore exchanges are available if assets migrated offshore, though no such application has been confirmed this cycle.
