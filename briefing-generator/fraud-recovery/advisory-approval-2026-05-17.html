Ponzi / Fraud Recovery

I. Goliath Ventures (M.D. Fla.) — Post-Arrest Recovery Posture

The Goliath Ventures crypto-Ponzi matter, in which federal agents arrested CEO Christopher Alexander Delgado on February 24, 2026 on wire-fraud and money-laundering charges, continues to run through its parallel civil-criminal recovery architecture. Prosecutors allege the Orlando-based cryptocurrency firm operated as a $328 million Ponzi scheme from January 2023 through January 2026, with the standard fact pattern — promised yields backed by purported algorithmic trading; lifestyle-spending diversion; classic Ponzi rotation of investor funds. The SEC's parallel civil complaint produced the asset-freeze and federal-receiver appointment under the recovery-grade template that the Middle District of Florida has now used in multiple recent crypto-Ponzi matters.

As of May 17, the receiver's asset-tracing work-stream is producing the expected USDT/USDC flow analysis through offshore exchanges. The cross-border friction point — particularly with respect to wallets routed through non-cooperating jurisdictions — remains the central operational challenge for the recovery posture. Section 1782 applications for foreign-jurisdiction discovery are the working tool; experienced fraud-recovery counsel continue to recommend early and parallel Section 1782 filings in BVI, Cayman, and Hong Kong to compress the cross-border timeline.

II. Paramount Management / Prestige Investment Group ($400M, ~2,700 Investors)

The Paramount/Prestige matter remains the second active major federal-court receivership in the recovery-grade docket. The Heller-led receivership continues to marshal the recovered asset pool against the ~2,700 victim universe. Distribution-waterfall mechanics remain pending; the standard rising-tide distribution template — under which all victims share pro-rata up to a recovery threshold — is the working assumption. Net-winner clawback work-stream is now active under both actual and constructive fraudulent-conveyance theories.

III. Clawback Doctrine — Norton Rose "Even the Winners Lose" Framing

The Norton Rose Fulbright "even the winners lose" framing — that investors who exited Ponzi schemes with apparent gains face clawback exposure under § 548 actual-fraud and constructive-fraud theories irrespective of their good faith — continues to anchor the doctrinal landscape. The Ponzi-scheme presumption (Bayou Group / Sharp v. Lukehart line) remains the controlling authority for treating all transfers from a Ponzi vehicle as carrying the requisite actual-fraud intent for § 548(a)(1)(A) purposes. No new appellate decisions over May 15–17.

IV. Cross-Border Tracing — Venezuela / Caribbean Friction

The Venezuela and broader Caribbean cross-border tracing friction continues to challenge the recovery posture in matters with offshore-exchange routing. The Latin America jurisdictional friction has not improved meaningfully through 2026. Practitioners should continue to model Caribbean and South American tracing legs as multi-year recovery timelines. The MLAT pathway remains slow; Section 1782 discovery remains the more reliable operational tool.

V. SEC Enforcement Cycle

The SEC fiscal-year-end enforcement push pattern is approaching its standard May/June ramp. No new high-profile complaints with asset-freeze components surfaced over May 15–17, but the typical pattern — a wave of new filings in late May and June timed to fiscal year-end metrics — remains the working watchlist baseline. Practitioners should expect a new wave of Ponzi-related complaints to surface over the next four to six weeks.

VI. SIPC Liquidations / Broker-Dealer Fraud Recovery

No new SIPC liquidations of consequence over May 15–17. The legacy Madoff trustee-action precedent continues to govern the secondary clawback doctrine. The Picower-style "good faith for value" defense limits remain the controlling authority on net-winner clawback in the SIPC context.

Recommended Actions

Victim-side counsel with Goliath Ventures exposure should: (i) file proofs of claim and section 1782 supporting affidavits if cross-border tracing requires non-U.S. discovery; (ii) coordinate with the federal receiver's tracing work-stream rather than running parallel forensic streams. Net-winner defense counsel in active Ponzi receiverships should: (iii) preserve good-faith documentation and review § 548(c) value-for-value defenses early. Receivership counsel should: (iv) calibrate distribution-waterfall planning to the rising-tide template absent matter-specific factors; (v) file early Section 1782 applications in BVI / Cayman / Hong Kong to compress cross-border timelines. Recovery-fund managers should: (vi) maintain the SEC fiscal-year-end watch for new enforcement complaints with asset-freeze components.
