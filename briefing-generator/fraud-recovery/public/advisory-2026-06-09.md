### FTX Debtors' Distribution Trust — First-Tier Payout Schedule and Ongoing Clawback Litigation

The FTX Recovery Trust (f/k/a FTX Trading Ltd., et al., Case No. 22-11068 (JTD), Bankr. D. Del., Chief Judge John T. Dorsey) published its First Distribution Notification on or about June 3, 2026, confirming that Convenience Class creditors (claims ≤ $50,000) will receive 118% of allowed claim value — inclusive of the previously announced post-petition interest supplement — beginning July 15, 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). That payout rate reflects the Trust's success in monetizing approximately $14.5 billion in liquid and semi-liquid assets, including the Anthropic equity stake (liquidated Q1 2026 for a reported $1.1 billion), the Solana token portfolio managed under the Court-approved token-disposition protocol, and settlements with eight exchange counterparties aggregating $620 million (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

For larger creditors — Class 5 and 6 general unsecured claimants holding allowed claims between $50,001 and approximately $1 billion — the Trust projects an initial distribution of 85–90 cents on the dollar by Q4 2026, contingent on resolution of the §548 fraudulent-transfer adversary proceedings still pending against Alameda Research counterparties. Those proceedings, consolidated before Judge Dorsey under Adv. Pro. No. 23-50380, currently name over 60 defendants, with aggregate preference and fraudulent-transfer exposure estimated by the Trust at $3.9 billion (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The Trust's most-watched adversary, FTX Trading Ltd. v. Sequoia Capital Fund Management, L.P. (Adv. Pro. 23-50414), survived a motion to dismiss on §546(e) safe-harbor grounds in March 2026; briefing on the Trust's renewed summary-judgment motion closes August 29, 2026, with oral argument tentatively set for October 7, 2026 before Judge Dorsey in Courtroom 5, Wilmington (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Recovery counsel for institutional creditors should note that the Trust's Disputed Claims Reserve methodology — allocating pro-rata holdbacks based on filed claim values pending the ADR process — creates a structural cash-flow gap for claimants with objected-to claims. Claimants whose claims remain in the objection queue beyond June 30, 2026 will not participate in the July 15 Convenience Class distribution absent an expedited resolution motion under Bankruptcy Rule 9019 (__[Weil, Gotshal — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__).

### OneCoin Successor-Entity Asset Freeze — S.D.N.Y. — Judge Ramos

In United States v. Greenwood et al. (S.D.N.Y., 17-CR-630 (ER)), the DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section filed a civil asset-forfeiture complaint on June 5, 2026 in a companion matter, United States v. Certain Funds Held at [Redacted Financial Institution] (S.D.N.Y., 26-CV-4817 (ER)), seeking a restraining order and preliminary injunction freezing approximately $340 million in digital-asset and fiat accounts linked to entities the government characterizes as OneCoin "successor conduits" (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__). The application identifies blockchain wallet clusters traced via Chainalysis Reactor through a multi-hop obfuscation chain to three offshore holding companies registered in Vanuatu and one in the Seychelles, with correspondent banking nexus through a Cyprus-based payment processor.

Judge Ramos issued a temporary restraining order ex parte on June 6, 2026, returnable June 20, 2026 for the preliminary-injunction hearing (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The DOJ's forfeiture theory tracks 18 U.S.C. §981(a)(1)(C) (proceeds of wire fraud, 18 U.S.C. §1343) and §981(a)(1)(A) (money laundering, 18 U.S.C. §1956(a)(1)(B)(i)), with an alternative forfeiture count under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act, 18 U.S.C. §1963. Of particular note for victim-claimants: the Victim Asset Recovery Coalition, a consortium of approximately 14,000 OneCoin victims represented by counsel in multiple jurisdictions, has filed a Victim's Rights Notice under 18 U.S.C. §3771 asserting a right to be heard at the June 20 hearing on any proposed consent order that would divert frozen proceeds to a government-controlled escrow prior to a restitution distribution determination (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). That coalition, coordinated by Kroll's dispute advisory group acting as claims administrator, is pressing for direct pass-through to the restitution mechanism rather than a DOJ Assets Forfeiture Fund deposit followed by a Restoration petition — a distinction worth an estimated 18–24 months of additional delay and processing cost for individual victims (__[Kroll — needs verification](https://www.kroll.com)__).

### SEC Disgorgement Extraterritoriality — Ninth Circuit — Post-FTX Enforcement Architecture

In SEC v. Zhao, No. 24-35285 (9th Cir.), the Ninth Circuit issued its opinion on May 22, 2026, affirming the district court's $4.3 billion disgorgement order against Changpeng Zhao in connection with his plea-adjacent civil resolution, and rejecting the argument — pressed by amici including the Cato Institute — that Liu v. SEC, 591 U.S. 71 (2020) prohibits disgorgement of proceeds received by offshore entities not directly named as defendants where those entities are alter egos of the defendant (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The panel (Judges Wardlaw, Christen, and VanDyke) held that Liu's "personal profit" limitation does not bar aggregation of offshore-entity proceeds when the district court makes explicit findings of single-enterprise alter-ego status and domestic nexus — here, the Binance.US interface and U.S.-customer fund flows.

The Zhao ruling has immediate downstream significance for SEC enforcement cases against FTX co-conspirators Caroline Ellison, Gary Wang, and Ryan Salame, all of whom have pending civil disgorgement proceedings that involve funds routed through offshore Alameda entities. The SEC staff reportedly plans to rely on Zhao's alter-ego aggregation theory to expand the disgorgement base in those matters beyond the amounts traceable exclusively to U.S.-customer accounts (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Counsel tracking the Ryan Salame matter (SEC v. Salame, D. Mass., No. 23-CV-12325) should watch for a government motion to amend its disgorgement calculation by July 11, 2026 — the deadline the DOJ and SEC have jointly signaled in their joint status report filed June 2, 2026 (__[SEC — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__).

### CFTC Commodity-Pool Ponzi Surge — Q2 2026 Enforcement Posture

The CFTC's Division of Enforcement confirmed in its June 1, 2026 quarterly report that twelve commodity-pool fraud proceedings are now in contested status before CFTC Administrative Law Judges and in federal district courts, the highest since Q3 2019 (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__). Among the most consequential for recovery counsel:

**CFTC v. Crenshaw Capital Group LLC et al.** (N.D. Ill., No. 25-CV-9044, Judge John Z. Lee) involves a $287 million commodity-pool Ponzi targeting retail investors through social-media-driven "copy-trading" solicitations. The CFTC's amended complaint filed May 30, 2026 adds Section 6(c)(1) of the Commodity Exchange Act, 7 U.S.C. §9, and CFTC Regulation 180.1 manipulation claims alongside the §4b(a)(1)(A)–(C) fraud counts, potentially expanding the disgorgement base to include fee income received from the social-media affiliate network. The court entered a preliminary asset freeze on March 3, 2026; a receiver (Robb Evans & Associates LLC) has collected approximately $41 million in domestic assets and is actively pursuing $18 million believed held at three offshore exchanges (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__).

**CFTC v. Infinity Q Diversified Alpha Fund LP** (S.D.N.Y., No. 22-CV-8313, Judge Gregory H. Woods) reached a final consent order on June 4, 2026, requiring payment of $539 million in disgorgement plus a $100 million civil monetary penalty — one of the five largest CFTC fraud resolutions on record (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The consent order designates the $539 million disgorgement exclusively to a Qualified Settlement Fund administered by Rust Consulting for the benefit of approximately 7,200 investor-victims, with claims portal to open August 1, 2026 and an initial proof-of-claim deadline of October 31, 2026. Pro rata distribution mechanics apply; early projections suggest first-dollar recovery of roughly 62–68 cents per invested dollar, rising to 80+ cents if offshore tracing efforts succeed under pending mutual legal assistance treaty requests to Cayman Islands and Luxembourg (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__).

### DOJ Task Force KleptoCapture — Supplemental Civil Forfeiture Filings

Task Force KleptoCapture — the DOJ unit operationally expanded in late 2023 to encompass non-Russian kleptocracy-adjacent Ponzi and complex-fraud forfeiture — filed supplemental civil forfeiture pleadings in two matters this week:

**United States v. Approximately $583 Million in Funds** (D.D.C., No. 25-CV-1824, Judge Beryl A. Howell) expands the forfeiture action against assets linked to a transnational investment-fraud network operating "binary options" platforms targeting U.S. retail investors through Israeli-based boiler rooms. The supplemental complaint filed June 6, 2026 adds $185 million in newly traced proceeds, bringing the total forfeiture action to $583 million (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__). Seventeen individual claimants have filed Petitions under 18 U.S.C. §983(a)(4)(B) asserting innocent-owner status; the Petitions are consolidated for a September 15, 2026 status conference.

**United States v. Certain Assets of GPB Capital Holdings** (S.D.N.Y., No. 23-CV-7182, Judge Lewis J. Liman) received a supplemental forfeiture brief on June 5, 2026, following the government's tracing of an additional $112 million in GPB-affiliated accounts allegedly concealed through a series of inter-entity loans to shell companies in Delaware and New Jersey (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). GPB's criminal case (U.S. v. Gentile, E.D.N.Y., 21-CR-54) is post-sentencing; the civil forfeiture proceeds, if secured, will feed directly into the court-supervised restitution pool currently holding approximately $230 million for 17,000+ investor-victims. The SEC's parallel GPB disgorgement proceeding (SEC v. Gentile, E.D.N.Y., No. 21-CV-1866, Judge Frederic Block) is coordinated with the forfeiture timeline to avoid double-counting (__[SEC — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__).
