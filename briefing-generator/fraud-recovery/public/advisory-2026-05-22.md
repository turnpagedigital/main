### I. FTX Distribution: Phase I Schedule, Disputed-Claims Reserve, and Creditor Positioning

The FTX Debtors' Plan Administrator — operating under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan (In re FTX Trading Ltd., No. 22-11068 (JTD), Bankr. D. Del., Judge John T. Dorsey) — published on May 20, 2026 its Phase I distribution schedule, identifying approximately **$5.3 billion** in claims cleared for initial distribution (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The schedule covers "Convenience Class" allowed claims (≤$50,000 nominal value, adjusted to dollar-at-petition rates per the plan's USD-denominated-value construct) and a tranche of senior unsecured creditors whose claims were resolved via the Repayment Protocol agreed in December 2025 (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

Critically, the Plan Administrator has established a **Disputed Claims Reserve (DCR)** estimated at **$1.2 billion** against which approximately 18,400 claimants remain in unresolved status — predominantly retail customers whose claims are contested on identity-verification grounds or whose account balances were inflated by pre-collapse internal ledger manipulation (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Recovery counsel should note that the plan's "True-Up" mechanism (Plan Art. IV.C.3) provides for supplemental distributions to DCR beneficiaries within 120 days of final claim allowance, meaning creditors whose disputes remain pending are not irrevocably diluted — but the clock on filing estimation motions under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 3018 is running.

Judge Dorsey has scheduled a June 3, 2026 omnibus hearing on 47 pending claim objections. Claimants receiving plan notices but not DCR-reserve notices should verify their claim status via the Kroll Restructuring Administration portal; failure to respond to pending objections by June 2 will result in deemed-withdrawn status under Docket Order No. 7,841 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The Plan Administrator's counsel (Sullivan & Cromwell) has flagged that the **net-investment-loss methodology** applied to FTX customer claims — measuring recovery against fiat deposited, not peak portfolio value — remains contested by approximately 3,100 creditors who assert that the "in-kind crypto" restoration theory adopted in *Celsius* (In re Celsius Network LLC, No. 22-10964 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) should apply here as well. Sullivan & Cromwell's position, consistent with Judge Dorsey's April 14, 2026 bench ruling, is that *Celsius* is inapplicable because FTX commingled assets such that there is no traceable in-kind property interest — a distinction rooted in *Begier v. IRS*, 496 U.S. 53 (1990), and its constructive-trust progeny (__[Weil.com — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__).

**Practitioner note:** Creditors asserting in-kind restoration should file a motion to reconsider the April 14 bench ruling no later than May 28, 2026 (14-day window under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9023). Missing that window converts the ruling to law of the case.

### II. OneCoin Forfeiture Litigation: Second-Tier Claimant Standing in S.D.N.Y.

In *United States v. Konstantin Ignatov*, No. 19-cr-00095 (RMB), S.D.N.Y. (Judge Richard M. Berman), and the parallel civil-forfeiture proceeding *United States v. Approximately $4.068 Billion in United States Currency*, No. 22-cv-04877 (RMB), Magistrate Judge Sarah Netburn issued a May 19, 2026 Report & Recommendation concluding that **second-tier claimants** — investors who purchased OneCoin tokens through intermediary "IMA" (Independent Marketing Associates) networks rather than directly from the OneCoin enterprise — possess constitutional standing under *Dolan v. United States*, 560 U.S. 605 (2010), and 21 U.S.C. § 853(n)(2) to petition for remission from the forfeiture fund (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

This is a significant expansion. The prior AUSA position — maintained through the 2024 remission regulations — treated IMA-intermediate purchasers as "too attenuated" to satisfy the "legal right, title, or interest" requirement of § 853(n)(6)(A). Magistrate Netburn's R&R held that, under the Restatement (Third) of Restitution § 62, unjust enrichment passes through intermediaries where those intermediaries themselves are judgment-insolvent or otherwise incapable of satisfying the downstream investor's claim — the precise fact pattern here, given that IMA networks in Nigeria, Germany, and South Korea are either dissolved, insolvent, or subject to their own regulatory seizure orders (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MLARS) has 14 days from May 19 to object to the R&R before Judge Berman issues a final order. If adopted, the ruling would expand the eligible claimant universe by an estimated **28,000 to 45,000** individuals across 34 countries, holding aggregate nominal losses of approximately **$2.1 billion** (at OneCoin's internal token-pricing methodology, which itself remains disputed in the remission-value proceedings) (__[CNBC — needs verification](https://www.cnbc.com)__).

The forfeiture fund currently holds approximately **$4.07 billion** as the caption discloses, augmented by approximately $640 million in proceeds from the March 2026 liquidation of Ignatov family real estate assets in Dubai and Frankfurt (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__). Recovery counsel representing IMA-channel claimants should immediately assess whether their clients fall within the expanded standing doctrine and prepare § 853(n) petitions for filing within 30 days of any final order, consistent with the remission procedures under 28 C.F.R. Part 9.

### III. SEC v. Rust and the Post-Receivership Disgorgement Priority Question

The Seventh Circuit is fully briefed in *SEC v. Rust*, No. 24-3117 (7th Cir.) — an appeal arising from the Northern District of Illinois (Judge Virginia M. Kendall) — on a question that will directly affect receiver distribution waterfalls in commodity-pool Ponzi cases: **whether disgorgement obligations imposed on secondary participants (here, feeder-fund managers who directed capital into Rust's fraudulent commodity pool) are subordinate to, pari passu with, or senior to third-party investors' constructive-trust claims against the receivership estate** (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Judge Kendall held (Op., Feb. 3, 2026, Dkt. 312) that SEC disgorgement, as a civil penalty under *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020), is not a "debt" within the meaning of the receivership priority rules and thus does not displace the equitable priority of defrauded investors. The Seventh Circuit oral argument (March 18, 2026, Panel: Rovner, Kirsch, Lee JJ.) revealed the panel skeptical of the SEC's argument that *Liu*'s "disgorgement as equitable remedy" framing elevates the Commission's position in the waterfall (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

A ruling affirming Judge Kendall would lock in the **net-investment-loss / constructive-trust priority** structure that plaintiffs' counsel has used successfully in Midwest Ponzi cases, including *SEC v. Illarramendi* and *CFTC v. Gelfman Bitcoin*. A reversal — unlikely given the panel's questioning — would materially impair receiver distributions in cases where the Commission has parallel disgorgement demands pending.

Decision is expected by late June 2026. Recovery counsel managing receivership distributions in the Seventh Circuit should plan distribution schedules around both scenarios and seek a contingency-reserve escrow order from the district court if one is not already in place (__[Troutman Pepper — needs verification](https://www.troutman.com)__).

### IV. CFTC-DOJ Coordination Guidance and Offshore Crypto-Fraud Structuring

The DOJ Criminal Division's Fraud Section and the CFTC Division of Enforcement jointly circulated on May 20, 2026 an internal coordination protocol — confirmed in a brief public summary posted to CFTC's enforcement webpage — establishing a **"first-contact" information-sharing framework** for offshore digital-asset fraud investigations involving U.S. customer losses exceeding $10 million (__[CFTC.gov — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__). The protocol formalizes what had been ad hoc inter-agency cooperation and, critically, establishes that **CFTC civil disgorgement and restitution orders will be treated as prior-claim holders in DOJ forfeiture proceedings**, not as competing claimants of equal rank.

This matters immediately to recovery counsel managing claims in:

- *CFTC v. Mirror Trading International Proprietary Ltd.*, (W.D. Tex., No. 22-cv-00501), where approximately **$1.7 billion** in South Africa-originating losses remain subject to cross-border recognition proceedings;
- *CFTC v. Digitex Futures Exchange* (N.D. Cal., No. 22-cv-03978), with approximately **$163 million** in claimed investor losses;
- *CFTC v. My Forex Funds* (D.N.J., No. 23-cv-11711), where a May 2025 consent order resulted in **$310 million** in combined disgorgement and civil monetary penalties and distribution logistics are still being finalized (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Under the new framework, CFTC restitution orders function as a **senior lien equivalent** in DOJ-controlled forfeiture funds, meaning private civil plaintiffs and class-action settlements whose recovery theory runs through a parallel DOJ forfeiture must account for CFTC priority satisfaction before computing residual civil recovery. Recovery counsel in cases with concurrent CFTC restitution orders must immediately evaluate whether their private settlement structures assume incorrectly that DOJ forfeiture proceeds are available as a residual recovery pool (__[CNBC — needs verification](https://www.cnbc.com)__).

### V. London Capital & Finance: UK Receiver's $47M UAE Asset Freeze

The UK High Court (Business and Property Courts, Financial List, Mr. Justice Miles) entered on May 20, 2026 a worldwide freezing order — the sixth since LCF's 2019 collapse — against thirteen UAE-registered special-purpose vehicles linked to officers of the LCF successor enterprise, with a conservatively stated asset value of approximately **£37 million (~$47 million)** (__[FT — needs verification](https://www.ft.com)__). The receiver (Smith & Williamson, now Evelyn Partners) obtained the order ex parte; a return hearing is listed for June 4, 2026.

LCF's original fraud resulted in losses of approximately **£237 million** (~$302 million) across approximately **11,500** bondholders — predominantly retail investors in mini-bonds marketed via Facebook and Google advertising. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) has paid out approximately £116 million in compensation to eligible claimants, leaving a residual shortfall of approximately £121 million (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

This latest freeze, if upheld at the return hearing, would bring total identified frozen/recovered assets to approximately £192 million — narrowing the recovery gap to approximately £45 million, or roughly **81% coverage** of total losses when combined with FSCS payments. Claimants who opted out of FSCS compensation in anticipation of superior receiver recovery should note that the opt-out election is irrevocable under FSCS Rule 6.3 and that the receiver's ultimate distribution will be subject to English insolvency priority rules under the Insolvency Act 1986, s. 175, not FSCS indemnity-equivalent terms (__[Norton Rose Fulbright — needs verification](https://www.norton-rose-fulbright.com)__).
