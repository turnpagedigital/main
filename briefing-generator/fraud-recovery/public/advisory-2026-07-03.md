### OneCoin / DOJ Remission: Petition Window Now Closed — Recovery Math Remains Deeply Unfavorable


Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud were eligible to apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the U.S. Department of Justice. OneCoin was co-founded by fugitive Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, defrauding as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 The gap between those two numbers — $4 billion in investor losses and $40 million in the remission pool — frames the recovery trajectory precisely: 
victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 A fully subscribed $40 million pool therefore represents recovery of roughly 1 cent on the dollar before any pro rata haircut imposed by the volume of petitions filed.


The filing deadline was June 30, 2026.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator in this matter.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission compensation process.
 The SDNY prosecution docket — *United States v. Ignatova*, S.D.N.Y. — remains open with Ignatova a fugitive. 
In June 2024, the DOJ offered a new $5 million reward for the missing Cryptoqueen.


Cross-border asset tracing continues to yield small but meaningful increments. 
German prosecutors in Bielefeld, working with the Royal Court of Guernsey, traced two of Ignatova's London properties — a Kensington penthouse sold for £10 million and a smaller flat for £1.4 million — through shell companies registered in the Channel Islands. After Ignatova ignored a 28-day ultimatum, the Royal Court on January 16, 2026 registered an overseas forfeiture order and released £8,590,200.92 plus interest into the Seized Asset Fund, to be claimed by Bielefeld for OneCoin victims.
 
In early 2026, a total of £8,590,200.92 was forfeited from the Guernsey-registered property structures; these funds will now be returned to Germany to compensate global victims of the OneCoin fraud.
 The Guernsey/Bielefeld recovery is independent of the U.S. DOJ remission pool — victims seeking to participate in the German compensation track will need to monitor the Bielefeld Regional Court proceedings separately.

A notable development on the facilitator front: 
Frank Schneider, a former deputy chief of Luxembourg's foreign-intelligence service who advised Ignatova through his firm Sandstone, fled French house arrest in May 2023, was returned, and in April 2026 saw the Nancy Court of Appeal side with Washington's extradition request; he has indicated he will appeal.
 Schneider's extradition posture is worth tracking — cooperation or a plea in the SDNY proceeding would be the most likely path to meaningful new asset identification.

**Claims-pricing note:** With the U.S. DOJ remission window now closed and no near-term prospect of a second U.S. remission tranche (absent new seizures), OneCoin claims have limited secondary-market utility unless denominated in the German or Guernsey proceedings, neither of which currently supports a liquid claims-trading market. The most actionable near-term development would be Ignatova's apprehension — a low-probability but high-magnitude event.

### Stanford Receivership: Final Distribution Progressing; ~8,400 Groups Still Pending; $8.1M Swiss Tranche Unresolved

The Stanford receivership — *SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd. et al.*, N.D. Tex., presided over by Judge David C. Godbey — is executing its court-approved Final Distribution Plan. 
On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.



On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 As of May 4, 2026, 
the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups
 pursuant to the court-approved Final Distribution Plan. That leaves approximately 8,400+ of the 14,000+ scheduled groups still awaiting payment — rolling disbursement at this pace suggests completion could extend well into Q3/Q4 2026, or beyond, depending on any administrative complications.

A discrete unresolved item concerns the Swiss assets. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank has concluded. As a result, the JLs transferred to the Receiver, pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol, $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding. The JLs further informed the Receiver that no further distributions of funds are expected.
 As of this run, there is no public docket entry confirming how or when the $8.1 million Swiss tranche will be incorporated into the existing distribution waterfall — the COViSAL claimant coalition submitted a formal second request on the status of these funds as of March 11, 2026, and as of the April 2026 update, the Receiver had not publicly responded. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026, titled: "SECOND REQUEST: Status of $8.1 Million Swiss Funds (Stanford Receivership)." To date, the Receiver and his legal team had not responded to that request as of April 7, 2026.


On the litigation backstop: 
on April 1, 2026, the Fifth Circuit affirmed the SEC civil judgment against Stanford in the Commission's enforcement action. Stanford challenged the judgment on several grounds, including arguments related to SEC jurisdiction, the offshore certificates of deposit, and the Antigua-based bank. The Fifth Circuit rejected his arguments, found no reversible error, and left the civil judgment in place.
 That affirmance closes a potential appellate disruption to the receivership's final distribution authority.

**Claims-pricing note:** At $339 million total final distribution against approximately $7 billion in allowed claims (the receivership's admitted loss figure), the blended recovery rate remains in the low single-digit percentage range, consistent with prior interim distributions. Claims buyers who acquired at low basis through the interim-distribution cycle are now in the execution phase — the key remaining variable is the timing of the rolling payment schedule and the disposition of the $8.1 million Swiss increment.

### BLMIS / Picard (Madoff): 17th Distribution Digesting; 50th Fee Application Filed; Gross Recoveries at $15.456B

The BLMIS liquidation proceeding — Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789 (LGB), before Judge Loretta G. Brozman's successors — remains the benchmark against which all Ponzi recoveries are measured. The most recent operative events: the 17th pro rata interim distribution, which commenced February 27, 2026, and the 50th BakerHostetler interim fee application, filed February 13, 2026.

On the distribution: 
the SIPA Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims. Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 
The seventeenth interim distribution represented 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts. Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid when combined with the prior 16 distributions in aggregate, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


Gross recoveries have continued to accrete modestly since the 17th distribution: 
as of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 The delta between the $15.38 billion distributed and $15.456 billion recovered represents the reservoir for the eventual 18th distribution — approximately $76 million in incremental recoveries accumulated since February 27, 2026. Whether that increment is sufficient to trigger an 18th distribution motion depends on the Trustee's internal threshold calculus; historically, distributions have been filed when new recoveries approach or exceed $50–100 million.

On fees — the signal event for claims-pricing discipline: 
on February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his Counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for their work on the global liquidation of BLMIS from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025. The Application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 All trustee, legal, and accounting fees are borne by SIPC, not the Customer Fund — 
none of the money recovered is used to pay administrative costs; all Trustee, legal and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 That structural feature means fee burn does not directly impair claim value, but it does inform the Trustee's continued operational incentive to litigate rather than wind down.


As of March 2026, the trustee had recovered $15.3 billion toward these claims through legal action against Madoff associates, feeder funds and beneficiaries of the scheme, and had made seventeen distributions to investors. Action by the Department of Justice recovered an additional $4.3 billion.


**Claims-pricing note:** At 72.848% paid-to-date on a $20.3 billion admitted-claims base, the residual claim value represents the present-value computation of: (a) future Picard litigation recoveries, (b) timing of the 18th distribution, and (c) the incremental SIPC advance reimbursements as smaller claims are fully satisfied. The case is structurally in late innings — active litigation targets have shrunk substantially — but the Trustee's consistent posture is to pursue remaining defendants. Secondary claim prices should continue to reflect a relatively tight discount to par on the remaining 27.152% unpaid balance.

### SEC Enforcement Posture: "Quality over Quantity" — Offering Fraud Remains Core Priority

The current SEC enforcement program is directly relevant to claims investors monitoring the next wave of Ponzi-driven receiverships. 
At the 2026 SEC Speaks Conference, Acting Enforcement Director Sam Waldon declared that the Commission's Enforcement Division is moving "full steam ahead" by focusing on quality over quantity and bringing actions against those who "lie, cheat, and steal."
 
The Division will focus on core case types including insider trading, financial accounting and disclosures, offering fraud, market manipulation, and fiduciary duty violations by investment advisers.



During fiscal year 2025, the Commission filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions and 69 follow-on administrative proceedings, obtaining orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.
 The offering-fraud category — the statutory home of Ponzi-scheme enforcement — is confirmed as a continuing priority under 
Chairman Atkins and the new Enforcement Director, Judge Margaret Ryan, who arrived in September 2025; the anticipated shift is away from technical or process-based errors and toward cases alleging intentional wrongdoing.


For claims investors, the practical read is this: the Atkins/Ryan era is unlikely to generate the high-volume receivership pipeline of the 2010–2022 period, but individual offering-fraud actions — particularly in the crypto-adjacent and digital-asset space — remain active. The Tron / Justin Sun resolution (proposed final judgment filed March 5, 2026, Rainberry consenting to a $10 million civil penalty under Securities Act § 17(a)(3)) is illustrative of the current settlement architecture: 
as part of a global resolution, the SEC filed a proposed final judgment with respect to its claims against Rainberry, Inc., Justin Sun, Tron Foundation Limited, and BitTorrent Foundation Ltd., noting that the SEC had charged the Tron Defendants with, among other things, violating Section 10(b) of the Exchange Act and aiding and abetting violations of Section 17(a)(1) of the Securities Act, but the settlement included only violations of Section 17(a)(3) of the Securities Act, which requires only negligence in engaging in any transaction, practice, or course of business.
 The absence of disgorgement in that settlement is the notable feature — it reflects the current Commission's skepticism about disgorgement as a remedial tool in the post-*Liu* environment.
