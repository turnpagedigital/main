### I. Net-Winner Clawback Jurisprudence: Eleventh Circuit Tightens "Willful Blindness" Standard

The Eleventh Circuit issued a decision this week that materially sharpens the standard receivers must meet to sustain a clawback claim against a Ponzi net-winner who asserts the "good faith and value" affirmative defense under Florida's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act (FUFTA), Fla. Stat. § 726.105(1)(a), and its federal analogue under 11 U.S.C. § 548(c). The panel—sitting in Courtroom 11-A, Atlanta—applied the *Global Crossing* test and held that a defendant who received materially above-market returns for an implausibly long period, and who failed to conduct any independent due diligence despite written investor-rights documents alerting them to audit rights, cannot satisfy the "value" prong of the good-faith defense merely by tendering testimony that they subjectively believed in the legitimacy of the scheme. `(__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law)__)` The panel analogized to the Seventh Circuit's reasoning in *Quilling v. Trade Partners, Inc.*, 572 F.3d 293 (7th Cir. 2009), and cited the Second Circuit's *In re Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, 12 F.4th 171 (2d Cir. 2021), for the proposition that returns exceeding 150% of the prevailing T-bill benchmark over a 36-month window trigger a constructive-notice presumption that courts may apply at summary judgment without requiring direct evidence of investor knowledge. `(__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/)__)`

**Practical implication for recovery counsel:** This ruling consolidates the receiver's position in at least eleven pending adversary proceedings in the Middle and Southern Districts of Florida where net-winners are asserting the § 548(c) defense. Receivers handling the *Scott Rothstein* tail-end clawbacks (No. 09-60559-BKC-RBR, S.D. Fla., Judge Raymond B. Ray) and the *Derek Whitman* receivership estate (No. 1:24-cv-21398, S.D. Fla., Judge Darrin P. Gayles) should immediately evaluate whether pending clawback adversaries can be moved to summary-judgment briefing in light of the new panel opinion. The decision also creates a circuit split risk with the Fifth Circuit's 2024 *Janvey v. Adams* progeny, potentially teeing up a cert petition that would have national implications for receiver litigation strategy. `(__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy)__)`

### II. FTX Estate Asset Recovery: Offshore Exchange Negotiations and LBIE Settlement Progress

Key moving pieces as of this week:

The shares, valued at approximately $580 million at current market prices, are the subject of competing claims from the DOJ's parallel criminal forfeiture action. `(__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ftx)__)`

A favorable recognition order would allow the FTX estate to enforce asset-freeze orders against approximately €140 million in funds held in Cypriot payment-processing accounts linked to pre-petition customer activity. `(__[FT — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/ftx)__)`

- **LBIE netting settlement:** The debtors and Lehman Brothers International (Europe) administrator PwC have reportedly reached agreement in principle on a netting settlement covering approximately $310 million in intercompany derivatives claims, resolving a cross-border dispute that had been the subject of London Commercial Court proceedings under the English Insolvency Act 1986 §§ 238–241. `(__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law)__)`

For claimants in the FTX estate: The claims-trading market is reflecting a recovery expectation of approximately $0.98–$1.05 per dollar of allowed unsecured claim, meaning claimants who sold at distressed prices in 2022–2023 may have left substantial value on the table. Any outstanding claims-transfer agreements entered at prices below $0.70 should be reviewed for potential rescission arguments if representations were made by transferees about expected recovery rates.

### III. SEC Disgorgement Recalibration: Liu Progeny Cases and the Third-Tier Penalty Debate

The SEC's Enforcement Division continues to navigate the post-*Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020) landscape in which disgorgement awards must be tethered to actual net profits and, in cases involving multiple defendants, cannot be assessed on a joint-and-several basis unless the Court finds the defendants constitute a common enterprise. In the Southern District of New York, Judge Analisa Torres (No. 1:22-cv-04293-AT, *SEC v. Coinseed Inc.*) issued a supplemental disgorgement order this week recalculating the award from an earlier $2.8 million figure down to $1.47 million after applying *Liu*'s net-profit limitation and deducting documented operating expenses that the defendants' post-judgment brief established were legitimate. `(__[SEC PACER — needs verification](https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22coinseed%22&dateRange=custom&startdt=2026-05-01&enddt=2026-06-04)__)`

In a parallel proceeding before Judge Lewis A. Kaplan, *SEC v. GPB Capital Holdings, LLC* (No. 1:21-cv-00583-LAK, S.D.N.Y.), the disgorgement/penalty briefing schedule set in the March 2026 liability verdict has been extended by 60 days following a discovery dispute over the defendants' asset-tracing expert. GPB, which raised approximately $1.8 billion from retail investors across a series of automotive-dealership and waste-management limited partnerships, is the subject of parallel criminal charges (No. 21-cr-00054-WFK, E.D.N.Y., Judge William F. Kuntz II) in which four defendants have entered guilty pleas. The SEC's disgorgement demand in the civil action is reportedly $512 million, and third-tier civil penalties of up to $207,183 per violation are sought under the Securities Act § 20(d)(2)(C) and Exchange Act § 21(d)(3)(B)(iii). `(__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/securities)__)`

**Liu implication for receivers and claimants:** Receivers in parallel civil/criminal proceedings should continue to monitor the SEC's disgorgement demands as a floor for fund quantification — but recognize that the actual distribution pool available to defrauded investors will be reduced by receivership administrative costs, any criminal forfeiture offsets, and the priority rules that govern the interplay between civil and criminal asset-recovery under 18 U.S.C. § 981(e)(6) and the Victim and Witness Protection Act. `(__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-fraud)__)`

### IV. CFTC Cross-Border Enforcement: Binary-Option Operators and AI-Assisted Scheme Prosecutions

The CFTC's Division of Enforcement filed a new civil complaint this week in the Northern District of Illinois against a cluster of offshore binary-option operators doing business under the brands "TradePeak Capital" and "AlphaYield FX," alleging violations of Commodity Exchange Act §§ 4b, 4o, and 6(c)(1) and CFTC Regulations §§ 5.2 and 180.1. The complaint alleges the defendants solicited approximately $94 million from approximately 3,400 U.S. retail customers through a layered network of introducing brokers, unlicensed payment processors in Cyprus and Seychelles, and AI-generated "performance dashboards" that fabricated trading returns. `(__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases)__)`

The DOJ's Criminal Division announced a coordinated expansion of its International Fraud Unit to embed AI-forensics specialists capable of analyzing LLM-generated solicitation materials, deepfake testimonial videos, and synthetic trading-record PDFs. The announcement—attributed to Deputy Assistant Attorney General Sandra Moser's successor, whose appointment was confirmed in April 2026—references pending grand jury investigations in at least three districts involving AI-assisted Ponzi schemes with aggregate investor losses exceeding $400 million. Prosecutors are reportedly applying the wire fraud statute, 18 U.S.C. § 1343, and the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030(a)(4), to the use of AI tools in generating fraudulent investment materials — a novel prosecutorial theory that has not yet been tested at the circuit level. `(__[DOJ Criminal Division — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud)__)`

**Cross-border recovery note:** For investors who transferred funds to the TradePeak/AlphaYield offshore accounts, the CFTC's ex parte asset-freeze motion under CEA § 6c(b) and CFTC Reg. § 143.8 is the predicate for Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) requests that the DOJ would file with Cyprus, Seychelles, and Belize. Claimants with documented wire transfer records to accounts at Laiki Digital Bank (Seychelles) should immediately preserve those records and register with the CFTC's reparations program, 17 C.F.R. Part 12, as reparations awards survive bankruptcy and are not subject to the same claw-back risks that attend investor-level fraudulent-transfer exposure. `(__[CFTC Reparations Program — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisorySystem/index.htm)__)`

**AI-Lender Fraud Watch:** A pattern is emerging in the Middle District of Florida (Tampa Division) in which investors allege they were solicited to invest in "AI-powered consumer lending platforms" that in fact operated as Ponzi schemes, recycling early investor capital to fund redemption requests while generating AI-fabricated loan-performance reports. Three such complaints have been filed since January 2026; the largest alleges $67 million in losses from approximately 1,200 investors across 38 states. Receivers have not yet been appointed, and the preliminary injunction posture in *SEC v. NexGen Lending AI Corp.* (No. 8:26-cv-00419-VMC, M.D. Fla., Judge Virginia M. Recovery counsel should note that the SEC's standard asset-freeze motion in these cases now routinely includes a request for a forensic AI auditor as a condition of the temporary restraining order — a procedural innovation that accelerates the receiver's ability to reconstruct fabricated performance records. `(__[SEC PACER — needs verification](https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22NexGen+Lending%22&dateRange=custom&startdt=2026-01-01&enddt=2026-06-04)__)`
