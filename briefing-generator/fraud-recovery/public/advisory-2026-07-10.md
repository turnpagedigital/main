### I. BLMIS / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789) — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Eighteenth Not Yet Filed


On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, aggregate payments to eligible BLMIS customers reached nearly $15.38 billion — including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances — representing 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid across the seventeen interim distributions in aggregate, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.



As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 The gap between that recovery figure and total distributions to date represents the unallocated recovery balance held pending future allocation motions — the raw material for an eighteenth distribution motion. No eighteenth allocation or distribution motion has been publicly filed as of this advisory's preparation. The pace of prior motions (typically annual or bi-annual filings, with the seventeenth filed in December 2025 for a January 2026 hearing) suggests a motion in late 2026 or early 2027 is the base case.


As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation, with further advances expected as litigated claims are resolved.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


For claims investors, the pricing implication is unchanged from last week: at 72.848% already distributed and total recovery agreements reaching $15.456 billion, the remaining upside on unsatisfied claims is bounded. The clawback posture is the more live issue for net winners — Picard's clawback litigation against feeder fund investors and redemption recipients continues under § 548 of the Bankruptcy Code and SIPA § 78fff-2. Nothing filed in the past 72 hours alters that posture.

### II. Stanford Financial Receivership (N.D. Tex., Dallas Div.) — Final Distribution Active; Swiss Asset Fate Unresolved


On March 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey filed with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
The Final Distribution Schedule reflects payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claim groups, issued on a rolling basis.



On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver had issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups.
 Execution is still rolling; a significant portion of the $339 million schedule has not yet been paid. No updated payment count has been published since May 4.

The open question for claims buyers is the $8.1 million Swiss-asset tranche. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and transferred $8.1 million in funds to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol — with no further distributions from the Swiss proceeding expected.
 
As of early April 2026, COViSAL (a Latin American victim advocacy group) sent a second formal written request to the Receiver regarding the status of those $8.1 million Swiss funds, and the Receiver and his legal team had not responded to that request.
 Whether those funds are folded into the Final Distribution Plan or require a separate amendment is unresolved on the public docket. Distribution methodology uncertainty of this type is a standard basis for claims discount in the secondary market.

The Venezuela delivery issue has been resolved. 
Beginning December 11, 2025, the Receivership had been unable to arrange courier delivery to Venezuelan addresses; as of March 11, 2026, courier delivery to Venezuela was restored.
 Claims with Venezuelan addresses that were in the distribution queue should now be processing.

### III. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — Guernsey Forfeiture Executed; DOJ Remission Petition Window Closed June 30

The most structurally significant development in the OneCoin recovery track this quarter is the Guernsey forfeiture order and the simultaneous closure of the DOJ's formal remission petition window.


On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The German authorities initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings" — non-conviction-based asset forfeiture — which led to the overseas forfeiture order, applying to the remaining proceeds of sale of two London flats owned by Guernsey-registered companies.
 
The German proceedings found that OneCoin was released with fraudulent intent by Ignatova and others; criminal proceedings against Ignatova could not conclude because her whereabouts remain unknown.
 The Guernsey proceeds flow back to German authorities for victim compensation, not directly to the DOJ remission pool — cross-border coordination among jurisdictions (Germany, Guernsey, U.S. SDNY) remains fragmented, which creates recovery-sequencing risk for claimants expecting a unified distribution.

On the U.S. side, the DOJ launched a formal victim remission process. 
The DOJ announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the international OneCoin investment scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood orchestrated a large, international cryptocurrency investment scheme defrauding investors from around the globe.
 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 
Through the remission process, victims who purchased OneCoin between 2014 and 2019 may be eligible for compensation; petition forms were available at onecoinremission.com, with a filing deadline of June 30.


That June 30, 2026 deadline has now passed. Any victim or claims investor who did not submit a petition form by June 30 is presumptively ineligible for the DOJ remission pool. The size of the remission fund relative to the $4 billion-plus victim pool is not publicly stated; the fraction is almost certainly small. 
In April 2026, authorities issued a court ultimatum giving Ignatova a 28-day window to object to the confiscation of additional assets — these proceedings are part of a broader international effort to dismantle the financial infrastructure left behind after the OneCoin collapse.



The Royal Court of Guernsey's forfeiture of over £8.5 million followed a mutual legal assistance request from prosecutors in Bielefeld, Germany — demonstrating that non-conviction-based asset forfeiture is viable even when the primary defendant remains a fugitive.
 That precedent matters for the broader recovery posture: further jurisdictions holding OneCoin-linked assets can now be targeted through similar MLAT channels.

Ignatova remains a fugitive. 
The U.S. Department of State offers a reward of up to $5 million under the Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program for information leading to her arrest.
 
By 2026, the enforcement environment has changed dramatically, as governments now examine crypto exchanges, mixers, token issuers, stablecoins, wallets, decentralized finance platforms, and laundering pathways with far greater sophistication.
 That is a net positive for future asset tracing.

### IV. GPB Capital Holdings Receivership (E.D.N.Y., No. 1:21-cv-00583) — Post-Initial Distribution; Subsequent Distributions Pending


On December 7–8, 2023, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York converted the GPB Capital monitorship into a full receivership and appointed Joseph T. Gardemal III as receiver; the Second Circuit affirmed that order on December 3, 2024.
 
On April 9, 2025, Chief Judge Brodie approved a substantial initial distribution of $400 million to investors in three specific funds.
 
The initial distribution was mailed on April 25, 2025, to investors in GPB Automotive Portfolio, LP, GPB Holdings II, LP, and GPB Cold Storage, LP.


The receiver filed a Q1 2026 status report via SEC Form 8-K. 
In Q1 2026, the Receiver continued to focus on protection, preservation, and maximization of distributable proceeds for investors while implementing the Distribution Plan, including accounting, banking, insurance, asset control, and litigation management.
 
The Receiver anticipates making subsequent distributions from available receivership assets as the receivership progresses, remaining assets are liquidated, and claims involving third parties are pursued or resolved.



At present, the dollar amount of asserted claims, legal fee advancement demands from individuals or entities formerly associated with GPB, and identified potential liabilities such as pending litigation and government enforcement proceedings, is substantially larger than the dollar amount remaining in the receivership estate.
 That is a material disclosure for claims pricing: the estate faces a structural overhang of asserted claims and fee demands that is larger than remaining assets. Secondary claims in GPB continue to carry meaningful distribution-timing and quantum risk.


President Trump commuted founder David Gentile's prison sentence in November 2025; the commutation does not reverse his conviction, does not affect investors' civil rights, and does not change the financial status of GPB funds.
 FINRA arbitration claims against selling broker-dealers remain active and are, for most retail investors, the more realistic recovery path than the receivership itself.

### V. Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) — Liquidation Plan on File; Docket Quiet This Week

Per the tracked-case ground truth, the most recent docket entry is Dkt. 531, filed July 6, 2026, with no new substantive filings in the 72-hour window ending today. The case remains active before Judge Parker (previously assigned to Judge Joel T. Marker at petition; current presiding judge is Cathleen D. Parker per the authoritative ground truth file).


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company via a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including Celsius Network LLC claims exceeding $133 million, with a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to pursue potential legal claims and oversee distributions.



The Committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning insider transactions — including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, about which the Committee expressed concern regarding unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property.


That Solara loan remains the operative clawback/avoidance target. Whether it is prosecuted as a fraudulent transfer under § 548 or as a preference under § 547 turns on timing relative to the June 20, 2024 petition date. If the Solara transaction occurred within two years of petition and the debtor was insolvent, a § 548 constructive-fraud claim is available without requiring actual intent. The outcome of that adversary proceeding, whenever filed, will materially shape distribution recovery for unsecured creditors holding the $192 million claims pool.

For claims investors and recovery counsel active in this week's material cases: **OneCoin** — confirm that any client or portfolio position with victim exposure submitted a DOJ petition by the June 30, 2026 deadline; absent that submission, remission-pool participation is foreclosed and recovery is limited to whatever European or other jurisdictional proceedings produce in the coming years; the Guernsey MLAT precedent is worth monitoring as a template for additional jurisdictions. **Stanford** — contact Receiver Janvey's team (Baker Botts, Kevin Sadler) directly regarding the $8.1 million Swiss tranche's disposition before the Final Distribution Plan is closed; claims bought or priced on the assumption that the Swiss funds increase per-claimant recovery should be revisited until the allocation methodology is public. **GPB Capital** — treat the receivership's own disclosure that asserted claims exceed remaining assets as a hard cap on distribution expectations; FINRA arbitration against selling broker-dealers (primarily regional B-Ds that placed GPB funds) remains the more actionable recovery path and should be pursued on parallel track rather than sequentially with the receivership. **BLMIS** — at 72.848% recovered on allowed claims, the residual upside is modest for pure distribution plays; the clawback-defense posture for net winners is the live risk, and any client who received more from BLMIS than they deposited should confirm with counsel whether Picard's remaining adversary proceedings could reach them under § 548 or SIPA § 78fff-2. **Power Block Coin** — the next substantive development to watch is any adversary proceeding targeting the Solara Communities loan or other insider transactions; file-watch Bankr.
