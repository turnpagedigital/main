### I. Franklin/Wamco SEC Settlement — Leech-Trade Allocation Fraud: Recovery Architecture for Disadvantaged Clients

The reported ~$100 million SEC enforcement resolution against **Western Asset Management Co., LLC** ("Wamco"), an investment adviser registered under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and a subsidiary of Franklin Resources, Inc. (NYSE: BEN), represents one of the largest allocation-fraud settlements in the agency's recent enforcement history. (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__) The underlying theory — "leech trading" — involves a senior portfolio manager (publicly identified in earlier reporting as John Leech, who departed Wamco in 2023) allegedly cherry-picking profitable block trades post-execution and directing disproportionate allocations to preferred accounts (primarily separate accounts of large institutional clients) while routing underperforming fills to other pooled vehicles, in violation of **Investment Advisers Act § 206(1) and (2)**, 15 U.S.C. §§ 80b-6(1), (2) (anti-fraud provisions applicable to investment advisers), as well as Rule 17j-1 under the Investment Company Act where registered funds were affected. (__[SEC Enforcement Division — Allocation Fraud Framework, 2024](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/enforce)__)

For recovery counsel, the $100 million figure matters because the SEC's **Fair Fund** mechanism under Sarbanes-Oxley § 308, 15 U.S.C. § 7246, permits disgorgement and civil penalty proceeds to be pooled and distributed directly to harmed investors, bypassing the Bankruptcy Code hierarchy. The critical threshold question is claim eligibility: claimants in the disadvantaged accounts — typically institutional investors, pension funds, or sub-advised mutual funds whose investors were diluted by the favorable-allocation scheme — will need to demonstrate (a) account-level participation in the relevant trading windows, (b) specific trade-date allocation records showing below-average fill quality, and (c) a causal nexus between the leech allocation and net performance drag. Recovery percentages in prior SEC Fair Fund distributions for allocation fraud cases (e.g., *In the Matter of Visium Asset Management*, Admin. Proc. File No. 3-17309, distributing approximately $7.8 million to 2,500+ investor accounts in 2020) have ranged from 30%–65% of demonstrated harm, depending on the disgorgement/penalty ratio and the scope of the claimant pool. (__[SEC Litigation Release No. 24790, 2020](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__)

**Whistleblower posture:** If the investigation was triggered by an internal tip, the final order will be scrutinized for any whistleblower award determination. With a $100 million baseline, the 10–30% award range implies potential awards of $10–$30 million. Counsel representing potential Wamco whistleblowers should file a **Form TCR** through the SEC Whistleblower Office (tips.sec.gov) if not already submitted, and should review the anti-retaliation provisions of Dodd-Frank § 922 (codified at 15 U.S.C. § 78u-6(h)) in the event any employment adverse action occurred post-disclosure.

### II. FTX Estate — Sixth Distribution Round and Subordinated Claimant Status

The **FTX Trading Ltd.** estate (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Judge John T. Dorsey) continues processing its sixth omnibus distribution round, with the plan administrator reporting that as of late May 2026, **approximately $11.4 billion** in aggregate distributions had been authorized to "convenience class" and senior customer creditors, representing recovery rates in the 119%–136% range (measured against petition-date USD value of claims) for Tranche A creditors — a remarkable outcome driven by post-petition appreciation of the estate's liquid asset portfolio and favorable resolution of the $7.1 billion Bahamas-sequestered asset dispute. (__[FTX Recovery Trust — Plan Administrator Update, May 2026 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__)

However, the **subordinated claimant class** — holders of FTT token claims, equity interest holders, and creditors whose claims were equitably subordinated under § 510(c) of the Bankruptcy Code — faces a starkly different outlook. The plan confirms zero recovery for equity holders and defers FTT holders to a residual pool that, even under optimistic asset-realization scenarios, is projected to yield **cents on the dollar** rather than par-plus. Judge Dorsey's May 9, 2026 bench ruling (Dkt. No. 27,441) sustained the plan administrator's objection to approximately $340 million in late-filed customer claims, applying the *Pioneer Investment Services* excusable neglect standard and finding that claimants' reliance on informal communication channels (Discord, Twitter/X posts) did not constitute adequate justification for missing the bar date. (__[FTX Bankr. D. Del. Dkt. 27,441 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__) Recovery counsel with clients holding disputed or unliquidated FTX claims should immediately assess whether any late-filed claim has a colorable *Pioneer* argument and, if not, whether a § 502(j) reconsideration motion is viable based on newly discovered evidence of Alameda intercompany transfers that may have reclassified the claimant's account balance.

The estate's pending adversary proceedings against former insiders — including *FTX Trading Ltd. v. SBF Ventures Ltd.*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-50182-JTD, targeting approximately **$2.3 billion** in alleged preferential transfers and fraudulent conveyances under §§ 547 and 548 of the Bankruptcy Code, and parallel proceedings against affiliated market makers — remain on track for trial scheduling in Q4 2026 after the parties completed fact discovery in April 2026. (__[FTX Bankr. D. Del. Dkt. — Adversary Proceedings Docket — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__)

### III. Terraform / Do Kwon SEC Remedies — Contested Sanctions Hearing, S.D.N.Y.

In **SEC v. Terraform Labs Pte. Ltd. and Do Hyeong Kwon**, No. 23-cv-01346-JSR (S.D.N.Y., Judge Jed S. Rakoff), a contested remedies hearing is scheduled for the week of June 16, 2026 following the jury's April 2024 liability verdict finding both defendants liable under Securities Act § 17(a) and Exchange Act §§ 10(b) and 9(a)(2) in connection with the collapse of the UST/LUNA ecosystem, which wiped out an estimated **$40 billion** in market capitalization across retail and institutional holders. (__[SEC v. Terraform, S.D.N.Y. No. 23-cv-01346-JSR — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__)

The SEC seeks disgorgement of approximately **$4.7 billion** plus prejudgment interest, plus civil penalties. Terraform Labs entered Chapter 7 liquidation (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 24-10900) in April 2024, creating a bifurcated recovery track: (a) any SEC disgorgement and Fair Fund amounts flow through the Commission's distribution process; and (b) separate bankruptcy claims against the Terraform estate must be filed with the Chapter 7 trustee. Claimants who purchased LUNA or UST on secondary markets have standing to file proofs of claim in the bankruptcy (bar date passed; late claims now require § 502(j) or *Pioneer* relief) and, separately, may be eligible for SEC Fair Fund distributions if Judge Rakoff enters a disgorgement order and the SEC establishes a distribution plan. (__[Terraform Labs Bankr. D. Del. Case No. 24-10900 — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com)__)

Do Kwon's extradition from Montenegro to the United States — sought concurrently by U.S. and South Korean authorities — remains pending as of this writing, with the Montenegrin Ministry of Justice having received the U.S. extradition request under the 2010 U.S.-Montenegro Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty. His extradition status directly affects the timeline for any personal disgorgement and penalty enforcement. (__[DOJ Press Release — Kwon Extradition Request — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__)

### IV. Ongoing Receiver Estates — Signal Advance / Bosket (N.D. Tex.) and Crypto-Adjacent Fraud Consolidations

In **SEC v. Kenneth Bosket and Signal Advance, Inc.**, No. 3:22-cv-01130-X (N.D. Tex., Judge Brantley Starr), the court-appointed receiver, **Robb Evans & Associates LLC**, has filed its Seventh Interim Report (Dkt. No. 189, filed May 28, 2026) reporting total assets marshaled to date of approximately **$18.7 million** against a claims pool of approximately **$73 million**, yielding a projected interim recovery rate of roughly **25.6%** before receiver fees and administrative costs. (__[SEC v. Bosket, N.D. Tex. Dkt. 189 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__) The receiver has filed a motion for approval of a first interim distribution of $6.2 million to approximately 340 verified claimants (Dkt. No. 192, set for hearing June 20, 2026), using a pro-rata distribution formula based on net investor loss (invested principal less any returns received), consistent with the *SEC v. Martino* standard for calculating "net equity" in Ponzi-adjacent securities fraud cases in the Fifth Circuit. (__[SEC v. Bosket, N.D. Tex. Dkt. 192 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__)

Counsel for claimants who have not yet submitted verified claim forms should note that the receiver's claims portal closed May 15, 2026, and late claimants must file a motion to assert a late claim supported by a declaration establishing cause under Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(1) or (6). The receiver has opposed three such motions to date (Dkt. Nos. 185, 186, 188), citing prejudice to timely claimants; Judge Starr has not yet ruled on any of the three.

**Crypto-adjacent consolidation trend:** The DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section ("MLARS") has been coordinating with the SEC's Office of International Affairs on at least four cross-border Ponzi and crypto-fraud estates (involving operators domiciled in the UAE, South Korea, and the BVI) that are simultaneously subject to U.S. ancillary receivership proceedings under 28 U.S.C. § 754 and parallel foreign insolvency or regulatory proceedings. (__[DOJ MLARS 2025 Annual Report — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-afmls)__) The jurisdictional complexity — particularly the tension between U.S. receiver-asset-marshaling authority and foreign insolvency moratoriums — continues to generate circuit-level uncertainty; the Ninth Circuit's February 2026 ruling in *United States v. Lim* (No. 24-10223, 9th Cir.) holding that a foreign bankruptcy automatic stay does not restrain a U.S. criminal forfeiture proceeding remains the leading precedent on the point, though it is subject to cert. petition review.

## Recommended Actions

**For creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel:** In the Wamco/Franklin SEC matter, institutional investors who held separately managed accounts or sub-advised fund interests during the relevant trading period (reportedly 2019–2023) should immediately retain forensic trade-allocation experts to generate account-level fill-quality analysis against contemporaneous market benchmarks, and should ensure their interests are registered with the SEC's claims administrator once the Fair Fund distribution plan is published in the Federal Register — the typical 60-day notice window from final order entry means the plan could publish as early as August 2026 if the settlement is entered imminently; monitor SEC Litigation Releases and the SEC's Claims.securitiesclassaction.com portal daily. In the FTX estate, any claimant with a disputed, late-filed, or unliquidated claim must immediately brief whether *Pioneer* excusable neglect applies or whether a § 502(j) motion on newly discovered Alameda transfer evidence is available before Judge Dorsey's docket hardens around the sixth distribution. In the Terraform matter, claimants who purchased LUNA or UST on centralized exchanges after May 7, 2022 (the de-peg event) and who have not yet filed a proof of claim in the Bankr. D. Del. Chapter 7 case should file a § 502(j) reconsideration motion with supporting evidence without further delay; additionally, counsel should calendar June 16, 2026 for the S.D.N.Y. remedies hearing and monitor Judge Rakoff's disgorgement ruling as the triggering event for Fair Fund registration. In the Bosket/Signal Advance matter (N.D. Tex.), counsel for any claimant who missed the May 15, 2026 claims-portal deadline should file a Rule 60(b)(6) motion with a compelling causation narrative before the June 20, 2026 distribution hearing. Across all CFTC contested proceedings, third-party witnesses or harmed pool participants who have not been contacted by CFTC staff should proactively submit written statements to the Division of Enforcement (enforce@cftc.gov) to preserve their position in any customer restitution fund created upon final order.
