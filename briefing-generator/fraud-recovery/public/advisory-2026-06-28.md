### I. SEC Whistleblower Award — $20M for Securities Manipulation (June 25, 2026)

The most operationally significant development of the past 72 hours is a whistleblower award order dated June 25, 2026. 
An SEC order filed June 25 says the claimant will receive a payment of approximately $20 million.
 
Florida-based attorneys Silver Law Group and the Law Firm of David R. Chase announced the award, saying their client provided information connected to an alleged securities manipulation scheme.
 
The order states that the claimant provided significant information about misconduct that would have been difficult to detect without the whistleblower's assistance, and the enforcement action exceeded $75 million in sanctions.
 
Scott Silver of Silver Law Group said the client reported alleged misconduct involving a major financial institution and retail mutual funds.
 The underlying respondents and docket references are not publicly disclosed in the order, which is standard practice under Exchange Act § 21F(h)(2).

**Why this matters for claims investors:** 
Whistleblowers can receive between 10% and 30% of sanctions collected in successful actions of more than $1 million.
 A $20M award on a $75M+ sanctions base implies a payout rate in the range of 22–26% — toward the upper end of the statutory band, suggesting the SEC's Office of the Whistleblower assessed the information as materially original and the claimant's cooperation as strong. Whistleblower awards at this magnitude are early-pipeline indicators: they signal active enforcement targets where receiver appointments, disgorgement orders, or restitution funds may follow in the 12–24 months ahead. The retail mutual fund / major financial institution nexus is worth tracking for any downstream fair-funds distribution. The underlying covered action is not yet publicly docketed in a resolvable way; this advisory will update when the Notices of Covered Action page posts the matching entry. 
The SEC Office of the Whistleblower posted activity on June 26, 2026.

### II. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Claims Window Closes June 30, 2026

This is the most time-sensitive item in the desk's coverage universe this week. 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud can now apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the U.S. Department of Justice.
 
OneCoin, co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 
Between 2014 and 2019, Ignatova, Greenwood, and others operated an international cryptocurrency investment scheme defrauding up to 3.4 million investors from around the globe.


**Claims mechanics:** 
Victims may obtain a petition form at www.onecoinremission.com, as directed by U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process, with Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serving as the designated administrator.
 
The filing deadline is June 30, 2026
 — 48 hours from today. Any eligible victim or aggregator holding assigned claims who has not yet filed should treat this as a hard stop.

**Asset-tracing backdrop:** Earlier this year, the Germany-Guernsey enforcement corridor produced a concrete forfeiture. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held at a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies, and Ignatova had commissioned the purchase of shares in both companies to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 
Criminal proceedings against Ignatova could not conclude because her whereabouts remain unknown; accordingly, German authorities initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings" which led to the overseas forfeiture order.
 The Guernsey forfeiture — roughly $10.7M at current GBP/USD — is small relative to the $4B+ scheme but is relevant as a proof-of-concept for multi-lateral confiscation without a principal defendant present. Whether proceeds from the Guernsey forfeiture are channeled into the DOJ $40M fund is unverified and has not been confirmed by DOJ or Kroll.

The aggregate recovery-to-claim ratio here is sobering. A $40M DOJ fund against a $4B+ fraud implies a recovery rate below 1% for any claimant filing through the remission process. Recovery-focused claim pricing on OneCoin positions should reflect that ceiling unless and until Ignatova is apprehended and additional forfeiture orders are executed.

### III. Madoff / Picard (SIPA Trustee) — Seventeenth Distribution Static; Recoveries at $15.4B

No new distribution motion or settlement announcement has posted to madofftrustee.com since the seventeenth distribution commenced on February 27, 2026. The estate's current posture:


The SIPA Trustee commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution on February 27, 2026, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including the seventeenth distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion — including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances — and the seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid when combined with all seventeen distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.



As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion.
 The $45M gap between the May 26 recovery figure ($15.411B) and the February 27 distribution total ($15.38B) reflects settlements reached but not yet allocated to a distribution — consistent with the Trustee's practice of batching allocations before seeking court approval for each successive distribution motion. The case remains before Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB), with BakerHostetler as Trustee's counsel and SIPC funding all professional fees. 
All trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.


**Clawback posture:** The Ponzi-presumption doctrine under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) and the Trustee's settled authority to avoid transfers within the two-year look-back (extended under § 548(a)(1)(B) against constructive fraud theory) continues to govern pending adversary proceedings. Net winners who received fictitious-profit withdrawals remain exposed regardless of good faith; the Second Circuit's *Picard v. Citibank* line foreclosed the good-faith affirmative defense for transfers not made "for value." Claims investors acquiring positions in the BLMIS estate should net down any acquisition price against potential clawback liability — the two exposures are not always held by the same party after claim assignment, and assignment agreements vary materially in how they address § 548 contingencies.

The "final" designation on both the CFO Solutions application and the case numbering under Judge Parker (a reassignment from Judge Joel T. The committee's filings raised concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and several insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC — an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah — and questioning its unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property.



The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing the plan on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 Whether the June 9 final fee approval constitutes a predicate to plan confirmation or trust establishment has not been confirmed from the docket entry description alone. Claims investors should monitor for a trust establishment order and any adversary proceedings targeting the Solara Communities loan or the insider transactions flagged by the committee.

### V. Cross-Border Asset Recovery — Structural Watch

Two structural mechanics are worth brief note for the current environment:

**§ 1782 discovery:** Several active receiverships continue to use 28 U.S.C. § 1782 applications to compel discovery from U.S.-based financial institutions in support of foreign forfeiture proceedings. The Guernsey-Germany OneCoin corridor is the most recent example of successful multi-lateral enforcement without a criminal conviction of the principal defendant — a pattern that recovery counsel should expect to replicate in BitConnect international tracing matters, where offshore asset pools remain partially unaccounted.

**FinCEN AML whistleblower program expansion:** The proposed rulemaking published at Federal Register Docket No. FINCEN-2026-0067 (RIN 1506-AB57) implements the AML Whistleblower Improvement Act's framework under 31 U.S.C. § 5323. 
The AML Whistleblower Improvement Act expanded the scope of the whistleblower program to allow awards for information relating to violations of IEEPA, TWEA, and the Kingpin Act, substantially expanding coverage beyond BSA violations to include U.S. trade and economic sanctions.
 The comment period closed June 1, 2026. Once finalized, this creates a parallel federal incentive structure for informants in cross-border Ponzi and fraud cases with a sanctions nexus — material for cases like Stanford (Antigua/UK coordination) where sovereign and sanctions dimensions overlap.
