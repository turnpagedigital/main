# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Monday, July 27, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The primary delta this week sits across two fronts: the Stanford receivership has crossed into final-distribution execution — checks issued in late April are now at or past their void deadlines — and the OneCoin DOJ remission window closed on June 30, shifting that matter from claims-filing posture to administrative adjudication. Power Block Coin (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) logged a filing at docket entry 532 on July 23 but the docket summary returned no description, keeping that case in a monitoring posture. The Madoff/BLMIS estate remains in the distribution tail following its seventeenth pro rata interim distribution in February; no new allocation motion has been filed as of the July 26 data pull. Each of these is addressed in turn below.

### Stanford Receivership (N.D. Tex. No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, Judge David C. Godbey) — Final Distribution: Check Void Deadlines Passed This Weekend

The Stanford receivership has moved into what may be its closing administrative chapter. 
According to the Receiver's update posted on April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 The distribution vehicle is the court-approved Final Distribution Plan entered by Judge Godbey; the docket entries confirming the plan are Doc. 3555 (original order) and Doc. 3562 (amending order). 
Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 were required to cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 had until July 26, 2026.
 Both deadlines have now passed as of today, July 27. 
The Receiver recently contacted certain claimants whose Final Distribution checks remain uncashed, because those checks are approaching the deadline to be cashed; checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and the funds represented by those checks will revert to the Receivership.


For claims investors who acquired Stanford CD positions in the secondary market, this is an irreversible haircut event: any transferred claim whose assignee failed to present the check is now a reversion. Counsel should immediately verify presentment status against their position schedule before moving on.

One unresolved thread overhangs the final distribution: 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026, titled "Second Request: Status of $8.1 Million Swiss Funds (Stanford Receivership)." To date, the Receiver and his legal team had not responded to that request; the letter asked, among other things, whether and when the $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025 will be distributed, and whether those funds will be included in a future distribution separate from the current Final Distribution Plan.
 That $8.1 million residual — if distributed — would represent a supplemental recovery layer beyond the checks already issued.

Fee litigation is separately pending. 
The court entered an order on October 8, 2025 approving the Receiver's proposed final distribution and addressing other matters; among those other matters, the Court awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees, which was substantially less than the approximately $43 million that was sought. The Receiver and certain of his professional firms appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals.
 
The Receiver and certain of his professional firms filed their brief on appeal on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting also filed a separate brief in support of the appeal.
 Resolution of that appeal will not alter distributions to claimants — fees are capped by the court's October order — but a reversal in the Fifth Circuit could extend administrative life of the receivership by several months and delay any residual distribution tied to a remanded fee calculation.

### OneCoin / DOJ Remission — Claims Window Closed; Now in Adjudication Phase

The DOJ's OneCoin remission program, announced April 13, 2026, has crossed its filing deadline. 
The Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process to provide recovery for victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd.; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-founded OneCoin and, with others, orchestrated a large, international cryptocurrency investment scheme defrauding investors from around the globe.
 
As a result of misrepresentations made about OneCoin, victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.



The OneCoin Remission Program is a U.S. Department of Justice initiative to compensate victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd.; the program will use over $40 million in forfeited assets seized from multiple key figures in the scheme.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process; Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 
The OneCoin victims filing deadline was June 30.


The arithmetic is sobering for recovery pricing purposes: $40 million available against $4 billion in total victim losses implies a maximum recovery rate of approximately 1% before Kroll's administrative fees and any pro-rata dilution from the total claim pool. That is a structurally different recovery profile from BLMIS (which has achieved approximately 75 cents on the dollar) and Stanford (which recovered over $2.7 billion against a $7 billion fraud). Claims investors who priced OneCoin positions against a BLMIS-style trajectory should revisit those models. 
Ruja Ignatova remains wanted, with authorities offering $5 million for information leading to her arrest
, which means the asset pool is frozen at whatever forfeiture has already been executed — there is no credible near-term pathway to materially enlarging the $40 million fund absent a capture.

### BLMIS / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789) — Post-Seventeenth Distribution Maintenance Mode

The Madoff estate is in a distribution-tail phase with no new allocation motion filed as of the July 26 data pull. The last material event was the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution, which commenced February 27, 2026. 
The SIPA Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims; including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 
Two lawyers — Irving Picard and David Sheehan — have recovered 75 cents on the dollar of the stolen money, many times the usual rate in such cases.


Fee visibility is the next actionable signal. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his Counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for their work from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025; the application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
The public interest discount applied represents a reduction of 10 percent from standard rates.
 That application will be the primary fee-order signal for claim pricing in the coming quarter. 
All trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC
 — meaning the fee order does not dilute the Customer Fund directly, but the hearing cadence signals how aggressively the Trustee expects to continue recovery litigation.


As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 That figure has been stable for several months, indicating the remaining litigation-driven recoveries are still working through the pipeline rather than landing in the fund.

### Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) — Liquidating Trust Plan; Judge Reassignment Note

This case merits a factual clarification from the tracked-case ground truth. The case was originally assigned to Judge Joel T. Marker, before whom the Subchapter V eligibility dispute was litigated in October 2024. 
The Honorable Cathleen D. Parker's calendar for November 18–19, 2025 included the Power Block Coin matter at case No. 24-23041, with matters including the Committee's motion for approval of the disclosure statement (docket No. 277), a motion to appoint a Chapter 11 Trustee, and a motion to convert the case.
 The tracked-case ground truth confirms Judge Parker as the current presiding judge — the ground truth controls.

The case's operative posture flows from the Committee's liquidation plan. 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, LLC filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah under Chapter 11 protection (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM); filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, the plan outlines a strategy to maximize value for creditors through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million; a key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors.


The most recent docket activity is entry 532, filed July 23, 2026, per the tracked-case ground truth. No description was captured in the data pull. Monitoring for adversary-proceeding filings, retention applications for the Liquidating Trustee, and any asset-sale motions remains warranted given the insider-transaction concerns flagged in the Committee's plan. 
The Committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities.
 Those insider-transaction concerns are potential § 548 avoidance targets, and the Celsius $133M preference claim (referenced in the Celsius Network LLC et al. case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) will be the primary distribution variable for non-Celsius unsecured creditors.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should take three immediate steps this week. First, verify Stanford check presentment: if you hold a transferred Stanford CD claim and received a check dated April 24 or April 27, 2026, the void deadlines were July 23 and July 26, respectively — confirm with your custodian whether the check was negotiated before those dates, because the reversion is now final and there is no cure mechanism under the court's order. Second, reassess OneCoin positions against the $40 million fund ceiling: with the June 30 filing deadline now closed and Kroll in adjudication mode, the maximum pro-rata recovery is structurally capped below 1% of total losses, and secondary-market pricing that assumed a BLMIS-style recovery trajectory should be revised downward accordingly; the sole remaining upside lever is the DOJ's ongoing pursuit of Ignatova, which is speculative. Third, monitor Power Block Coin (No. 24-23041, Judge Parker) for the first post-July-23 docket entry with a substantive description — the Celsius preference claim ($133M+) and the insider-transaction avoidance universe are the two variables that will determine whether unsecured non-Celsius creditors recover anything material under the Committee's Liquidating Trust plan, and retention of Liquidating Trustee counsel is typically the earliest signal that adversary proceedings are being authorized and scoped.
