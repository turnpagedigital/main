## Analysis & Developments

The most time-sensitive item this cycle is the OneCoin DOJ claims window, which closes **June 30, 2026** — ten days out. ### I. OneCoin / Ignatova — DOJ Claims Window Closes June 30; Guernsey Forfeiture Becomes Distributable

The DOJ's formal compensation process for OneCoin victims is the most pressing near-term event on this desk. 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud can now apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the U.S. Department of Justice.
 The fund is modest relative to estimated investor losses — roughly one cent on the dollar — but it is real, denominated in seized cash, and administered through a structured petition process. 
The filing deadline is June 30, 2026.
 
Petition forms are available at www.onecoinremission.com.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process.



OneCoin, co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 Ignatova remains a federal fugitive. 
The FBI continues its investigative pursuit alongside IRS-CI and the Southern District of New York, with Ignatova listed as an FBI Top Ten Fugitive.


The parallel cross-border recovery track is more substantive for secondary claim investors than the DOJ petition fund. 
German prosecutors in Bielefeld, working with the Royal Court of Guernsey, traced two of Ignatova's London properties through shell companies registered in the Channel Islands; after Ignatova ignored a 28-day ultimatum, the Royal Court on January 16, 2026 registered an overseas forfeiture order and released £8,590,200.92 plus interest into the Seized Asset Fund, to be claimed by Bielefeld for OneCoin victims.
 These funds sit outside the DOJ petition process and will be administered through German authorities under their own restitution framework.

Separately, 
Frank Schneider, a former deputy chief of Luxembourg's foreign-intelligence service who advised Ignatova, fled French house arrest in May 2023, was returned, and in April 2026 saw the Nancy Court of Appeal side with Washington's extradition request; he has indicated he will appeal.
 An extradited Schneider would be a material witness in asset-tracing proceedings — he is among the few remaining principals with operational knowledge of the scheme's financial infrastructure. The extradition appeal, if it fails, moves this thread meaningfully.

**Claims-investor note.** The $40 million DOJ fund proration across 3.4 million potential victims makes individual recoveries from this pool negligible for most claim positions. The Guernsey / Bielefeld track — and any future recoveries through § 1782 discovery against financial intermediaries or Mareva injunctions in additional jurisdictions — represents the real recovery upside. Monitor the Bielefeld proceedings and any MLAT-facilitated asset freezes in jurisdictions where Ignatova's network is known to have placed funds.

### II. Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Plan in Execution; $339M+ in Payment Schedules, Swiss $8.1M Deployment Unresolved

The Stanford receivership (Ralph Janvey, receiver; N.D. Tex., Dallas Division, before Judge David C. Godbey) has entered the final distribution phase. 
On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule — totaling more than $339 million and including payments to over 14,000 claim groups — are being issued on a rolling basis beginning 14 days after the filing of the Schedule, with the process expected to take several weeks.



On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups.
 The cadence suggests ongoing rolling disbursements are still working through the 14,000+ claim group list.

Two open items material to claim pricing. First, the Swiss asset tranche: 
on December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank has concluded, and transferred to the Receiver, pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol, $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding.
 As of the April 6, 2026 update, the $8.1 million had not been assigned to a specific distribution tranche, and claimant groups have publicly complained that the Receiver has not responded to formal inquiries about its deployment. 
The Antiguan JLs further indicated that no further distributions of funds are expected
 from the Swiss proceeding, making these $8.1 million the residual tail from that track.

Second, there is a continuing operational friction around payment method clarity. 
Claimants cannot determine the issuance date of a check if one was sent, yet the 90-day deadline to cash or request reissuance runs from that unknown date
 — a practical stale-check risk that secondary investors holding assigned claims should monitor, as a lapsed 90-day window may require reissuance procedures that delay actual receipt.

The 16-year litigation arc on the Stanford case reflects the structural timeline reality for this asset class. At this stage, the primary recovery risk is operational rather than legal: missed checks, uncashed distributions, and undepleted Swiss proceeds are the remaining exposure for claimants who haven't yet received payment.

### III. Madoff / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete at $253M; Fee Posture Stable; No New Clawback Docket Activity Since February

The seventeenth distribution is complete. 
Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee for the liquidation of BLMIS, announced on February 27, 2026 that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to account holders with allowed claims, bringing the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers to nearly $15.38 billion.
 
As of May 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.


The overall recovery rate is notable context for claims pricing. 
The Trustee has recovered approximately 75 cents on the dollar of the stolen money — many times the usual rate in such cases.
 The seventeenth distribution marks the capstone of the most recent round; the next distribution depends on continued clawback litigation settlement and recovery. 
As of January 31, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has amassed approximately $15.366 billion as a result of recoveries and settlement agreements since his appointment in December 2008.


Fee posture: 
a February 13, 2026 statement from the Picard office concerned the Fiftieth Fee Application.
 That application was not separately newsworthy — BakerHostetler's ongoing counsel compensation has been a routine quarterly event — but the fiftieth application milestone signals the extraordinary duration of the engagement. Fee disputes have historically been a source of friction in SIPA cases; as of this date, no contested fee ruling is pending before Judge Loretta A. Preska (who succeeded Judge Stuart M. Bernstein on the BLMIS docket). The case remains Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789. Absent a new distribution motion filed post-February 2026, the next material docket event will be a new allocation and distribution motion — timing unconfirmed at this date.

### V. SEC Enforcement Pipeline — Atkins Posture Narrows New Receivership Supply; Fraud Cases Remain Priority

The broader enforcement context matters for new-case deal flow. The SEC under Chairman Paul Atkins has narrowed crypto-adjacent enforcement, with several Biden-era actions dismissed or settled on reduced terms. 
On March 5, 2026, the SEC filed a proposed final judgment against the Tron Defendants — Rainberry, Inc., Justin Sun, Tron Foundation Limited, and BitTorrent Foundation Ltd. — dismissing all remaining claims as part of a global resolution, settling on a Section 17(a)(3) negligence theory rather than the Section 10(b) scienter-based violations originally charged.
 
The settlement would permanently enjoin Rainberry from violating Section 17(a)(3) and require Rainberry to pay a $10 million civil penalty
 — a substantially reduced outcome from what the original charges contemplated.

On pure fraud (Ponzi/offering fraud), the Enforcement Division's posture is more stable. 
Acting Enforcement Director Ryan reaffirmed the Division's focus on core fraud and market integrity cases, emphasizing fairness, transparency, and timely resolution.
 
During fiscal year 2025, the Commission filed 456 enforcement actions and obtained orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.
 The volume is substantial, but the quality of new receivership appointments in crypto-adjacent Ponzi cases will depend on whether the Enforcement Division under its current leadership treats digital-asset frauds as "core fraud" matters or continues to treat them as regulatory-boundary cases subject to the crypto-pullback trend.

The practical implication for claim investors: the pipeline of SEC-initiated receiverships with meaningful asset-recovery potential — the primary source of new Ponzi claim inventory — may thin in the near term. Existing cases (Stanford, Power Block, BLMIS) remain in distribution execution. New large-ticket receivers are less likely to emerge from SEC enforcement than they were in the 2020–2024 cycle.
