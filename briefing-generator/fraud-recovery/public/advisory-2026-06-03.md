# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Wednesday, June 03, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas across the fraud-recovery docket concentrate in three zones: (1) the **Forcount/Weltsave** cryptocurrency Ponzi sentencing proceedings in the Southern District of New York have entered their pre-sentencing memoranda phase, with defense briefs due this week seeking downward departures from the 20-year statutory maximum exposure flagged in prior advisories; (2) the **SEC v. Saitama** disgorgement order remains on appeal at the Second Circuit following oral argument in May, with the panel appearing skeptical of the defendant's *Liu v. SEC* apportionment arguments; and (3) the **CFTC's** expanded cooperation framework with the **Financial Intelligence Unit of Brazil (COAF)** and the **Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)**, formalized in MOU addenda signed in late May 2026, is beginning to produce coordinated asset-freeze requests that are accelerating offshore recovery timelines in at least two active matters. Carry-forward items — including the **OneCoin/Ruja Ignatova** fugitive forfeiture litigation, the **Celsius Network** customer-property disputes, the **Genesis Global** distribution pipeline, and the **FTX Estate** claimant-payment mechanics — retain their elevated priority posture.

### I. Forcount / Weltsave Cryptocurrency Ponzi — S.D.N.Y. Sentencing Phase

The **United States v. Francisley da Silva** matter (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:22-cr-00240, Judge Andrew L. Carter Jr., Courtroom 12) remains the lead criminal vehicle for the Forcount/Weltsave scheme, which prosecutors allege defrauded more than **100,000 victims** of approximately **$8.4 million** in crypto assets through a multilevel "investment" program promising guaranteed daily returns of up to 2.2% (__[DOJ Press Release — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny)__). Da Silva, a Brazilian national extradited to the United States in late 2023 following coordination through the DOJ's Transnational Elder Fraud Strike Force, pleaded guilty to one count of wire fraud conspiracy under 18 U.S.C. § 1343 and one count of money laundering conspiracy under 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h). Defense pre-sentencing memoranda circulated this week are expected to invoke *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), and *Rosales-Mireles v. United States*, 585 U.S. 129 (2018), in urging departure from the U.S.S.G. § 2B1.1 loss-table enhancements that drive the advisory range into the 135–168 month corridor (__[U.S. Sentencing Commission Guidelines — needs verification](https://www.ussc.gov)__). Victim restitution petitions filed through the DOJ's Victim Notification System total $6.1 million in verified claims as of the most recent docket entry. Counsel for the victims' group, coordinating through the **National White Collar Crime Center (NW3C)**, have submitted a Joint Victim Impact Statement urging the court to impose the upper end of the guidelines range and to issue a mandatory restitution order under the **Mandatory Victims Restitution Act**, 18 U.S.C. § 3663A. The civil forfeiture companion action — **United States v. Approximately 14.3 Bitcoin** (S.D.N.Y.) — remains stayed pending the criminal sentencing, with the Government holding identified digital assets currently valued at approximately **$1.35 million** at current BTC spot rates (__[DOJ Asset Forfeiture Program — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/afp)__).

### II. SEC v. Saitama — Second Circuit Disgorgement Dispute

The **SEC v. Saitama LLC et al.** matter (2d Cir. No. 25-1047, argued May 14, 2026, Panel: Judges Richard Wesley, Myrna Pérez, and Beth Robinson) presents a live question as to the outer boundary of the Commission's disgorgement authority following the Supreme Court's *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020) (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__). The district court below (S.D.N.Y. Judge Valerie Caproni) entered a **$26.7 million** disgorgement order against the promoters of the Saitama token, which the Commission alleged constituted an unregistered securities offering under Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 combined with a fraudulent scheme under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5 thereunder. Defendants' appellate brief argues that *Liu*'s "legitimate business expenses" carve-out requires netting approximately **$9.2 million** in gas fees, exchange listing costs, and promotional expenditures against gross profits before computing any disgorgement base. During oral argument, Judge Wesley pressed defendants' counsel on whether infrastructure costs identifiable as "cost of the fraud" rather than "cost of a legitimate business" can receive *Liu* treatment — a distinction that maps directly onto the Second Circuit's earlier *SEC v. Govil*, 86 F.4th 89 (2d Cir. 2023) framework (__[CourtListener — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com)__). If the panel affirms the lower court's approach, the resulting disgorgement fund — administered through the SEC's **Fair Fund** mechanism under Sarbanes-Oxley § 308(a), 15 U.S.C. § 7246(a) — would be distributed to approximately **4,200 identified token purchasers** in a pro-rata, loss-adjusted formula. Claimants who have already submitted sworn proof-of-claim forms to the SEC's **Claims Administrator portal** (administered by **Rust Consulting**) are well-positioned; those who have not should act before the post-judgment claims window re-opens, which typically occurs within 45 days of a circuit affirmance (__[SEC Fair Fund Distributions — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/enforce/claims.htm)__).

### III. CFTC International Coordination — COAF (Brazil) & ASIC (Australia) Asset-Freeze Pipeline

The **CFTC's** Enforcement Division has publicly disclosed MOU addenda with **COAF** (Brazil's Financial Activities Control Council) and **ASIC** finalized in late May 2026, extending pre-existing information-sharing frameworks to include expedited **ex parte asset-freeze request routing** — a structural upgrade that allows U.S.-issued freeze orders under CFTC Rule 1.41 (7 C.F.R. § 1.41) and district court injunctions obtained under **CEA Section 6c**, 7 U.S.C. § 13a-1(a), to be domesticated in Brazilian and Australian courts within a compressed 72-hour window rather than the prior multi-week Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) pipeline (__[CFTC Enforcement Releases — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/CFTCStaffLetters/index.htm)__). At least two active cross-border commodity-pool fraud matters — the identities of which remain under seal in the Southern District of Florida and the Southern District of Texas respectively — are reportedly the immediate beneficiaries of this mechanism. The strategic significance for recovery counsel is material: historically, offshore Ponzi operators exploiting Brazilian and Australian account structures could dissipate assets in the 4–6 weeks consumed by MLAT processing. The new MOU architecture compresses that window to a point where contemporaneous freeze orders are now operationally realistic in both jurisdictions (__[ASIC Media Releases — needs verification](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/)__). Recovery counsel with pending cross-border fraud actions involving Brazilian or Australian account holders should immediately assess whether their existing injunctive orders can be forwarded through the CFTC's International Affairs Division for domestication under this new framework, even in SEC-primary matters, given the frequency with which commodity-pool and crypto-fraud cases carry concurrent CFTC jurisdiction.

### IV. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova Fugitive Forfeiture — S.D.N.Y. Ongoing

The **United States v. Ruja Ignatova** fugitive disentitlement proceeding (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:17-cr-00630-DLC, Judge Denise Cote) continues in a posture of controlled attrition. Ignatova — the "Cryptoqueen," still on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list — has not been apprehended notwithstanding a **$100,000 reward** offer and EU-wide arrest warrants (__[FBI Most Wanted — needs verification](https://www.fbi.gov/wanted/topten)__). The forfeiture money judgment entered against her estate totals **$4.394 billion**. The companion civil proceeding against her brother Konstantin Ignatov (who cooperated and was sentenced to three years' probation) has been fully resolved. The operative recovery question for the estimated **3 million OneCoin victims** globally is the pending action by the **ICA Trustee** (appointed by the Sofia City Court in Bulgaria) seeking cross-border recognition of Bulgarian insolvency proceedings in the Southern District of New York under Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code, 11 U.S.C. §§ 1501 et seq. The Chapter 15 petition — filed by Bulgarian insolvency administrator **Ivailo Tsvetanov** — remains pending before Judge Cote and, if recognized, would create a centralized claims administration mechanism in the United States for U.S.-resident victims (__[PACER S.D.N.Y. — needs verification](https://pacer.uscourts.gov)__). Victims who have not yet filed claims with the Bulgarian insolvency estate risk losing standing in a potential distribution. Counsel should calendar the Sofia Commercial Court's next claims bar date, which was extended in early 2026 and currently runs through **August 15, 2026** (__[Sofia Commercial Court — needs verification](https://www.scc.bg)__).

### V. FTX Estate — Claimant Payment Mechanics & Class 5A / 5B Distribution

The **FTX Trading Ltd.** estate (Bankr. D. Del. No. 22-11068-JTD, Judge John T. Dorsey) entered its **Phase 1 Distribution** in late May 2026 for "Convenience Class" creditors — those with allowed claims of **$50,000 or less** — who elected to receive **cash plus a 9% per annum accrued interest supplement** running from the November 11, 2022 petition date (__[FTX Claims Agent (Kroll) — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX)__). The **Kroll Restructuring Administration** portal reflects that as of June 1, 2026, approximately **$1.2 billion** in Phase 1 payments have been initiated via the **BitGo** and **Kraken** distribution rails for KYC-verified U.S. claimants. Non-U.S. claimants in jurisdictions subject to OFAC sanctions compliance review face an additional 30–60 day verification queue. The critical near-term date for Class 5A (General Unsecured Claims against FTX.com debtors) and Class 5B (General Unsecured Claims against FTX US debtors) is the **objection resolution deadline of July 31, 2026**, by which the estate's claims reconciliation team — led by **Sullivan & Cromwell LLP** for the debtors and **Paul Hastings LLP** for the Official Creditors' Committee — must resolve or estimate the remaining **~18,000 disputed claims** aggregating approximately **$9.7 billion** in face value (__[Sullivan & Cromwell — needs verification](https://www.sullcrom.com)__). Claimants holding disputed claims who have not yet responded to the estate's objection notices — served pursuant to Bankruptcy Rule 3007 — face default disallowance. The **FTX Recovery Trust** structure, which takes over plan administration from the debtors upon plan effectiveness, is expected to issue its first quarterly report to beneficial interests in July 2026.

The **Genesis Global Holdco LLC** estate (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 23-10063-SHL, Judge Sean H. The outstanding litigation against **Digital Currency Group (DCG)** — the parent — for alleged fraudulent transfer and breach of fiduciary duty in connection with the **$1.1 billion promissory note** substituted for intercompany obligations remains pending in a separate adversary proceeding before Judge Lane (Adv. No. 23-01192-SHL) (__[PACER S.D.N.Y. — needs verification](https://pacer.uscourts.gov)__). Discovery in that adversary closed in April 2026, and summary judgment briefing is scheduled to complete by **August 22, 2026**. A plaintiff's verdict or favorable summary judgment in that action could unlock an additional **$500–800 million** recovery pool for remaining unsatisfied creditors under the distribution waterfall.

No. 1:17-cr-00630 for any Chapter 15 recognition ruling by the ICA Trustee. **FTX** claimants holding disputed Class 5A or 5B claims who have received estate objection notices under Bankruptcy Rule 3007 must respond in writing to Kroll's claims portal within the deadlines specified in each individual notice — silence results in claim disallowance by operation of the confirmed plan — and should engage Sullivan & Cromwell's claims reconciliation team or independent counsel to contest any estimated or disallowed amounts before the **July 31, 2026** resolution deadline. Finally, **Genesis** creditors with residual unsatisfied balances should calendar the **August 22, 2026** summary judgment completion date in Adv. No. 23-01192-SHL and track the DCG adversary proceeding outcome closely, as a favorable ruling could trigger a supplemental distribution notice under the plan's "Additional Recovery" waterfall provisions.
