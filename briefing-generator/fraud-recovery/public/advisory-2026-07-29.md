### 1. Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution at the Forfeit-or-Lose Inflection Point

The Stanford receivership (*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Judge David Godbey; Baker Botts lead counsel) has entered the final-distribution execution phase, and this week is a hard deadline moment for a subset of claimants.


In preparation for commencing the Final Distribution, and during the first two months of 2026, the Receivership issued payments to eligible claimants totaling more than $100 million pursuant to Distribution Plans 1–11.
 
Payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.


The check-cashing deadlines are now at or past their limits: 
claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 were required to cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 faced a July 26, 2026 deadline.
 Both of those dates have now passed. 
It is critical that payees cash checks no later than 90 days after the date of issuance; payees may request reissuance by submitting a written request to the Receiver within 90 days of the original issuance date, and reissuance may be requested only once. If a payee does not cash the check or request reissuance within 90 days, that payee will not be eligible to receive any other payment under the Final Distribution Plan.
 
All funds represented by void checks not subject to a timely request for reissuance revert to the Receivership Estate.


The cross-border asset recovery tail on Stanford continues to generate incremental funds. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and the JLs transferred to the Receiver $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding, noting that no further distributions of funds from that channel are expected.
 The $157 million Société Générale settlement — the largest of the five bank settlements announced in February 2023 that brought total receivership recoveries to more than $2.7 billion — has its distribution held pending resolution of the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal. 
Distribution of funds from the $157 million Société Générale settlement must await the final resolution of the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal.


For claims investors holding Stanford CD positions: the Final Distribution Plan is executing now, recovery trajectory is largely fixed, and the residual upside is the JL-appeal resolution and any uncashed-check reversion that increases per-claim distributions. The Venezuelan-address payment logistics issue (
beginning December 11, 2025, the Receivership was not able to arrange delivery of distribution payments via physical checks to Venezuelan addresses
) remains a watch item for claimants in that jurisdiction.

### 2. OneCoin — DOJ Remission Window Has Closed; Administration Phase Begins


On April 13, 2026, the Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process to provide recovery for victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd.
 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud could apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets; OneCoin was co-founded by fugitive Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.


The petition deadline was June 30, 2026. 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section managed the process; Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 
Eligible victims included those who purchased OneCoin or invested in the system between 2014 and 2019 and suffered a net loss, accounting for any withdrawals or recovered amounts; the programme had international scope, with non-U.S. investors permitted to apply.


The $40 million remission pool is materially disproportionate to the $4 billion aggregate fraud — a recovery rate below 1% against total losses — which underscores the structural limitations of DOJ forfeiture-based remission relative to civil receivership or bankruptcy distribution mechanisms. The forfeiture pool reflects only what has been seized and forfeited to date; Ignatova remains a fugitive. 
Co-founder Ruja Ignatova remains on the FBI's wanted list.
 Parallel asset-recovery proceedings continue in other jurisdictions: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies, through which Ignatova commissioned the purchase of shares to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.


For claims investors and victim-advocates: the June 30 deadline has lapsed. Anyone who missed the window should monitor the Kroll administration process for any late-petition exception notices. The Guernsey forfeiture and ongoing German proceedings suggest the asset-recovery perimeter continues to expand, but given the fugitive status of the primary defendant and the multi-jurisdictional complexity, the conversion of seized assets to victim distributions will take time.

### 3. Power Block Coin — No New 72-Hour Filings; Case Posture and Structural Risk


Power Block Coin LLC filed a voluntary petition for relief under Chapter 11 on June 20, 2024.
 Per the tracked-case ground truth, the case is pending before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the Bankr. D. Utah, Case No. 24-23041; the last docket entry was ECF No. 532 on July 23, 2026, and no new filings have appeared in the 72-hour window ending July 28.

The structural features of the case that matter for recovery analysis are well-established: 
the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, represented by Greenberg Traurig, LLP, filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041) on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.


The Celsius preference-claim overhang is the primary recovery-discount driver here. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending Celsius bankruptcy (Case No. 22-10964) in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The $17.4 million Solara Communities loan remains a contested asset: 
a significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah; the Committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.


The professional-fee record is relatively lean for a case of this complexity. 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker set a June 9, 2026 hearing on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for Allowance of Compensation (ECF No. 506), with Amplēo requesting final fees of $10,436.50 for the period April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026.
 The modest Amplēo fee application — relative to the $192 million claims pool — is consistent with a case that transitioned from Subchapter V through a UCC-driven liquidation plan without extensive contested litigation on the professional-fees side. The prior Subchapter V trustee, Joli A. Lofstedt, received final fees of 
$8,925.00 and $0.00 in expenses, allowed as Chapter 11 Administrative Claims pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 330 on a final basis.


Recovery outlook: the Celsius preference claim pool exceeds 69% of total scheduled claims and will functionally set the ceiling on non-Celsius creditor recoveries unless those claims are settled or subordinated in plan confirmation. The Solara loan recoverability — dependent on the Toquerville real estate market and the encumbrance resolution — is a second-order variable. Claim pricing on Power Block Coin positions should reflect meaningful illiquidity risk given the absence of a confirmed plan and the Celsius overhang.

### 4. BLMIS / Madoff — Post-Seventeenth Distribution; Watching for Eighteenth Motion

The seventeenth BLMIS distribution is the current baseline. 
Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution on February 27, 2026.
 
The Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims; including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.



Eligible BLMIS customers have now received approximately 72.848% of their allowed claims from the seventeen interim distributions, plus SIPC advances of up to $500,000 per allowed claim.
 
Subject to court approval, 100% of the SIPA Trustee's recoveries are allocated to the Customer Fund for distribution; none of the money recovered is used to pay administrative costs, as all trustee, legal, and accounting fees are paid by SIPC.
 The fee-structure point is material for claims investors: the SIPC cost-absorption model means the Customer Fund is not eroded by professional fees — a structural advantage compared to most receivership or Chapter 7 estates.


As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
The Trustee's office issued a February 13, 2026 statement regarding the Fiftieth Fee Application.
 The fiftieth fee application is a signal that the professional engagement is deep into its tail; the distribution cadence has historically been annual-to-biannual, and the eighteenth motion has not yet been filed. The case is pending before Judge Stuart M. Bernstein, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789-SMB. 
Once the next anticipated distribution occurs, 1,531 accounts will be made whole.


For claims investors holding BLMIS positions: at 72.848% recovery with SIPC advances, the remaining upside is approximately 27% of allowed claim face value, subject to litigation outcomes on still-contested claims. The ongoing clawback adversary proceedings — particularly against offshore feeder funds — continue to be the primary recovery engine for incremental distributions.

## Recommended Actions

Stanford Final Distribution claimants with checks dated April 24 or April 27, 2026, must treat those instruments as void as of this week; any claimant who received a check in that window and has not yet cashed or requested reissuance should contact the Receiver through Baker Botts counsel and the Gilardi claims administration team immediately to determine whether a late-reissuance exception is available — the funds will otherwise revert to the estate permanently. Claims investors with Power Block Coin (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041) positions should monitor ECF for a plan-confirmation hearing date, given the last filed docket entry is ECF No. 532 (July 23, 2026) and a confirmed plan remains outstanding; the $133 million Celsius preference claim and the Solara Communities $17.4 million loan recoverability are the two variables that most directly determine non-Celsius creditor recovery percentages, and both warrant diligence before pricing or acquiring positions. OneCoin claimants who submitted DOJ remission petitions by the June 30, 2026 deadline should monitor Kroll Settlement Administration for processing updates at onecoinremission.com; those who missed the deadline should preserve documentation of losses pending any announced exception window or supplemental distribution round driven by continued asset seizures, including the multi-jurisdictional forfeiture actions still in progress in Guernsey and Germany. On BLMIS, the clawback adversary docket — particularly the offshore-feeder-fund actions — remains the primary variable driving the timing of an eighteenth distribution motion; net-winner claimants in those adversaries should review their § 548 / SIPA § 78fff-2(c)(3) exposure with counsel as settlement pressure typically increases ahead of distribution motions.
