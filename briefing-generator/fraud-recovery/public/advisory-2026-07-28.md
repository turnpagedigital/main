# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Tuesday, July 28, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Three developments define the current posture of the fraud-recovery landscape this week. First, the Stanford receivership's Final Distribution Plan has now crossed its most immediate check-cashing deadlines, with material reversion risk crystallizing for uncollected payments — a timing event with direct consequences for claims investors holding secondary positions. Second, the SEC's newly formed Retail Fraud Working Group, stood up July 7, changes the forward pipeline of receiver appointments and restitution pools; the mandate explicitly covers offering frauds and Ponzi-style schemes, and its cross-border coordination brief suggests near-term enforcement activity with international dimensions. Third, the OneCoin matter continues to generate incremental asset-recovery actions at the jurisdictional perimeter — Guernsey forfeitures executed in January 2026, a DOJ victim-claim process opened in April 2026 — while Ruja Ignatova herself remains at large, keeping the largest unsatisfied restitution exposure in modern crypto fraud in limbo.

### Stanford Receivership (*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, et al.*, No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Judge David Godbey) — Final Distribution in Wind-Down; Reversion Deadlines Have Passed

The Stanford receivership is in its final operational phase. 
According to the Receiver's update posted on April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 The distribution proceeds from the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan, with an amendment entered November 25, 2025. 
On March 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey filed with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.


A hard reversion deadline has now passed. 
Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 were required to cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 faced a July 26, 2026 deadline. Checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines became void and will be rejected if presented for payment.
 Both of those dates are now behind us as of July 28. Claimants holding uncashed checks from those two issuance dates have no further recourse under the current plan absent extraordinary court relief — reverted funds return to the receivership estate for subsequent allocation.

The receivership is not fully wound down. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026 regarding the status of $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025; as of the April 2026 filing, the Receiver and his legal team had not responded, leaving open whether those funds will be distributed in a tranche separate from the current Final Distribution Plan.
 That $8.1 million remains a discrete open item.

The receivership's fee appeal is live at the Fifth Circuit. 
The district court awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed to the Fifth Circuit, filing their brief on March 23, 2026.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 That appeal remains pending; resolution will determine whether fee-reduction savings flow back to claimants or simply close the books. 
Separately, on May 12, 2026, the court granted the Receiver's motion for authorization to dispose of certain Stanford records and equipment
 — an operational wind-down step that signals the estate is moving toward administrative closure even while the fee appeal turns.

For claims investors: secondary-market Stanford CD claims are effectively in the final cash-out window. The open items — Swiss $8.1 million, Fifth Circuit fee appeal outcome — represent residual upside whose timing and magnitude remain unresolved. Positions acquired at a discount to the Final Distribution payment amounts have now largely run their course.

### BLMIS / Picard (*In re Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, No. 08-01789 (LGB), Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; BakerHostetler 50th Fee Application Pending

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution closed in February. 
Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee, announced on February 27, 2026 that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers had commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.



As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 That figure has held flat since the February distribution, indicating no new SIPC advance commitments have been triggered in the interim — consistent with the posture that remaining disputed claims are still in litigation, not yet resolved to the point of triggering advances.

On the fee side: 
on February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the Bankruptcy Court for work performed from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest-discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 10% public-interest discount applied by BakerHostetler is a recurring feature of this engagement; the application is pending. Fee application cadence in this case has been roughly quarterly since 2009; there have now been fifty applications, and the docket (No. 08-01789 (LGB)) before Judge Lori Grinker Bradfield (who replaced the late Judge Stuart Bernstein) will carry at minimum one or two more before the estate is resolved.

The Madoff recovery percentage — roughly 75 cents recovered on the dollar of stolen principal — remains the benchmark against which all other Ponzi recovery programs are measured. 
Irving Picard and David Sheehan have recovered 75 cents on the dollar of the stolen money, many times the usual rate in such cases.
 The next distribution trigger will depend on further settlement or litigation resolution; no eighteenth distribution motion has yet been publicly noticed.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — Guernsey Forfeiture Executed; DOJ Victim Claim Process Open; Fugitive Status Unchanged

The OneCoin matter is generating incremental cross-border asset recovery even in the absence of its principal defendant. 
Following international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany, on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies, through which Ignatova had commissioned the purchase of shares to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 This forfeiture executed under Guernsey's Proceeds of Crime Law, enabled by German criminal court findings — a clean example of a Norwich Pharmacal / overseas-forfeiture-order chain producing tangible recovery without the primary defendant's presence.


In April 2026, the DOJ announced the opening of a formal claim process for defrauded investors to seek restitution from a $40 million fund consisting of seized assets; while this represents only a fraction of the total losses, officials stated they are committed to identifying and seizing further criminal proceeds to expand the compensation pool.
 At roughly $40 million against an estimated $4 billion in fraud losses, the current victim fund represents approximately 1 cent on the dollar — a figure that underscores how dependent the recovery program is on eventual capture and prosecution of Ignatova or continued co-conspirator asset tracing.


As of April 2026, Ignatova's exact whereabouts remain unconfirmed; despite being on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list for several years, she has successfully evaded capture since her disappearance in October 2017, with INTERPOL and the German BKA continuing to pursue leads across multiple continents.
 
U.S. authorities have announced a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to her arrest and/or conviction.
 The co-founder, Karl Sebastian Greenwood, 
was sentenced to 20 years in prison for his role in orchestrating the fraud.


For recovery claimants: the DOJ's April 2026 victim-claim process is the actionable filing event. Participation deadlines, if any, should be confirmed directly with the DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section. The Guernsey forfeiture proceeds' disposition — whether they flow into the DOJ fund or are separately administered under Guernsey law — has not been publicly clarified and warrants direct inquiry.

### SEC Enforcement Pipeline — Retail Fraud Working Group (July 7, 2026): Signal for Near-Term Receiver Appointments


The SEC announced the creation of a Retail Fraud Working Group within its Division of Enforcement on July 7, 2026, consistent with Chairman Paul S. Atkins' stated focus on protecting retail investors.
 
The working group's mandate covers offering frauds, pump-and-dump schemes, market manipulation, and breaches of duties to customers by investment advisers and broker-dealers.
 
The group is led by Kate Zoladz, Deputy Director, West, Division of Enforcement, and Kim Frederick, Assistant Director, Asset Management Unit.



In its fiscal year 2025 enforcement report released in April 2026, the SEC said it was moving away from measuring success through case counts and aggregate penalty totals, instead emphasizing cases tied directly to fraud and investor harm, including Ponzi-style schemes that targeted retirees, veterans, and religious communities.
 The working group is a structural expression of that reorientation. 
The initiative represents a structural expansion of the SEC's enforcement capabilities and is consistent with prior statements made by SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins about focusing the Division of Enforcement on protecting retail investors.


The practical consequence for recovery-desk practitioners: the Retail Fraud Working Group's "proactive case generation" mandate, combined with its cross-border coordination brief, is the most reliable leading indicator of new receiver appointments over the next six to eighteen months. Historically, each SEC offering-fraud action that results in a temporary restraining order and asset freeze is followed within days by a receiver motion under FRCP 66 and SEC Act § 21. The working group's data-and-technology approach (
Enforcement Director David Woodcock indicated the group will draw on data and technology to identify potential violations
) suggests pattern-matching across tip intake — the SEC received a record 53,753 tips, complaints, and referrals in fiscal year 2025, 
nearly 19 percent more than in the prior fiscal year.
 New receivership estates are the most probable near-term outcome.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should treat the following as time-sensitive action items: (1) **Stanford Final Distribution** — confirm immediately whether any held or monitored secondary-market positions correspond to checks issued April 24 or April 27, 2026; both reversion deadlines have passed as of this briefing, and any such checks are now void absent court intervention; the open $8.1 million Swiss tranche and pending Fifth Circuit fee appeal (briefing closed May 23, 2026 for the SEC's response) represent the only remaining upside in this estate, and positions should be marked accordingly. (2) **OneCoin DOJ Victim Claim Process** — if you have clients with provable OneCoin losses, confirm filing status with the DOJ Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section against the $40 million seized-asset fund announced April 2026; the fund is small relative to total losses, but pro rata participation preserves optionality as further seizures expand the pool; separately, monitor the Guernsey forfeiture proceeds (GBP 8,590,200.92) for disposition orders. (3) **Madoff / BLMIS** — the eighteenth distribution has not been noticed; the 50th BakerHostetler fee application (No. 08-01789 (LGB)) is pending before the court and, if approved, will draw from SIPC rather than the Customer Fund — no distribution dilution risk — but continued litigation-phase recoveries from remaining defendants drive timing of the next distribution trigger. (4) **SEC Retail Fraud Working Group** — map existing client portfolios for exposure to issuer types flagged in the mandate (offering frauds, unlicensed investment advisers, Ponzi-adjacent structures); the group's data-driven, cross-border brief increases the probability of surprise TRO/asset-freeze actions in the near term, and clients who are net winners in subsequently-charged programs face clawback exposure under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and state UVTA from the moment a receiver is appointed.
