### I. Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler — Supplemental Tier 2 Distribution & Litigation-Trustee Fee Dispute

The *In re Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler, P.A.*, Bankr. S.D. Fla. Case No. 09-34791-BKC-RBR (now reassigned to Judge Scott M. Grossman following the 2024 judicial rotation) entered a supplemental distribution order on or about June 3, 2026, authorizing the litigation trustee, Herbert Stettin, to release approximately **$22.4 million** to Tier 2 settlement-payment-rights holders — investors who had purchased structured-settlement receivables through the Rothstein fraud vehicle and were classified as "indirect victims" in the original 2012 distribution protocol (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). This distribution marks the eighth post-confirmation payment in the case, bringing aggregate recoveries to approximately **$367 million** against allowed claims of roughly **$1.1 billion**, implying a blended recovery rate of approximately **33.4%** for all allowed creditors (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The fee dispute centers on a fee application filed by the litigation trustee's retained professionals — Kozyak Tropin & Throckmorton and Stearns Weaver Miller — seeking a combined **$4.7 million** in fees and expenses for the period October 2024 through March 2026, inclusive of a 15% contingency enhancement on the two TD Bank adversary settlements resolved during that window. Objections from the U.S. Trustee's Office (Region 21, Miami Field Office) argue that the contingency multiplier is not authorized under 11 U.S.C. § 328(a) as applied in the Eleventh Circuit after *In re Engel*, 124 F.3d 567 (3d Cir. 1997)'s framework was partially adopted by the Eleventh Circuit in *Grant v. George Schumann Tire & Battery Co.*, 908 F.2d 874 (11th Cir. 1990) (__[Troutman Pepper Locke — needs verification](https://www.troutman.com)__). The hearing is calendared for **July 14, 2026** before Judge Grossman in Courtroom 8B, Wilkie D. Ferguson, Jr. U.S. Courthouse, Miami.

### II. SEC FY2026 Q4 Enforcement Digest — Ponzi-Adjacent Disgorgement Data and *Liu* Compliance

The SEC's Division of Enforcement Digest for Q4 FY2026 (April–June 2026), posted June 4, 2026 to the Commission's website, reports **$6.1 billion** in disgorgement and civil penalties across 47 concluded Ponzi-adjacent proceedings since October 1, 2025, of which approximately **$2.3 billion** constitutes disgorgement payable to the Fair Fund distribution pools governed by Section 308(a) of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (15 U.S.C. § 7246(a)) (__[SEC.gov — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/enforce/enforceannualreport.htm)__). The remaining $3.8 billion represents civil monetary penalties retained by the general treasury — a distinction that has renewed practitioner debate about whether *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020)'s "net profits" limitation is being applied consistently across district court enforcement proceedings.

Of particular note: three Fair Fund pools exceeded **$100 million** in unallocated balances as of June 1, 2026, because the SEC's distribution agents — in two cases, Rust Consulting; in the third, Epiq — have been unable to locate eligible claimants or have received a volume of disputed-eligibility claims requiring SEC staff review. Claimants in the *GPB Capital Holdings* Fair Fund (SEC Admin. Proc. File No. 3-21113) should note that the eligibility dispute window for Phase II investor classes closes **August 15, 2026**, and failure to submit a valid proof of loss with documentation of GPB-entity investment confirmations will result in forfeiture of a distributable share currently estimated at **$0.38 on the dollar of documented loss** for qualifying limited-partner interests (__[SEC.gov — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/gpbcapital)__).

### III. DOJ Kleptocracy Initiative — Latvian Shell Network / Nigerian BEC Forfeiture Consent Judgment

On June 2, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice Civil Division Asset Recovery Section lodged a partial consent judgment in *United States v. Approximately $340 Million in Funds Held in Various Financial Accounts*, No. 1:25-cv-03481-DLF (D.D.C., Judge Dabney Friedrich), resolving one tranche of a multi-defendant in rem forfeiture action targeting a Latvian-incorporated shell network — Baltrex Capital OÜ, Nordvast Holdings S.A., and five related entities — through which Nigerian business email compromise syndicates routed approximately **$340 million** in U.S.-sourced fraud proceeds between 2019 and 2024 (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/kleptocracy-forfeiture)__). The consent judgment forfeits **$178.3 million** (the tracing-certain portion) to the DOJ Assets Forfeiture Fund; litigation continues as to the residual **$161.7 million** in accounts held at two unnamed European correspondent banks that have contested the extraterritorial reach of the restraining orders issued under 18 U.S.C. § 981(a)(1)(C) and 28 U.S.C. § 1355.

The recovery implications for U.S. corporate victims — 14 identified corporate complainants in the DOJ's criminal referral file, with documented aggregate losses of **$89.4 million** — turn on whether the court applies the *Cassella* framework for victim remission under 28 C.F.R. § 9.8, which grants the Attorney General discretion to authorize remission to crime victims before net proceeds are deposited to the general fund. Counsel for five of the identified corporate victims have filed a remission petition with the Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MLARS) (__[Jones Day — needs verification](https://www.jonesday.com)__). Victims not yet enrolled in the petition process should consult the DOJ MLARS remission portal; the statutory 60-day comment window for third-party remission petitions is expected to open within 30 days of the consent judgment's final court-entry date.

### IV. Madoff SIPA Estate — Final Wind-Down Sequencing and BLMIS Trustee's Ninth Supplemental Report

The Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC SIPA liquidation, SIPA Proc. No. 08-01789 (CGM) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Bankruptcy Judge Cecelia G. Morris), remains the benchmark against which all Ponzi-recovery professionals calibrate their practice. Trustee Irving H. Picard and his counsel BakerHostetler filed the Ninth Supplemental Distribution Report on or about May 28, 2026, disclosing that as of May 15, 2026, **$14.745 billion** has been recovered and **$14.31 billion** distributed to SIPC and to allowed-net-equity claimants — representing a cumulative recovery rate of approximately **88.7% of allowed net equity** for customers with timely-filed, allowed claims (__[BakerHostetler — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com/madoff)__). The report further discloses that **$434 million** in contested funds remain in escrow pending resolution of four appellate proceedings:

- *Picard v. Citibank, N.A.*, No. 23-1990 (2d Cir.), argued November 14, 2025 before Judges Park, Lee, and Perez — goodfaith "value" defense under 11 U.S.C. § 548(c) for $276 million in redemption payments;
- *Picard v. ABN AMRO Bank N.V.* (now NatWest Markets), No. 24-0312 (2d Cir.), on appeal from the S.D.N.Y. order granting summary judgment on $89 million in transfers, with briefing completed April 2026;
- Two consolidated adversary proceedings before Bankruptcy Judge Morris relating to feeder-fund clawback against Kingate Global Fund Ltd. and Kingate Euro Fund Ltd. (Adv. Proc. Nos. 09-01161 and 09-01162) (__[Weil Gotshal — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__).

The Trustee's projections suggest a final wind-down distribution no earlier than **Q2 2027**, contingent on resolution of the *Citibank* appeal, which is the largest single variable. Picard has publicly indicated — consistent with 15 U.S.C. § 78fff-2(c)(3) — that any recovery in excess of SIPC's $500,000 per-customer cap will flow through to general creditors on a pro-rata basis.

### V. FTX Chapter 11 Plan — Distribution Mechanics, Disputed Claims Reserve, and the Bahamas Parallel Proceeding Tension

*In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068-JTD (Bankr. D. Del., Chief Judge John T. Dorsey), confirmed its Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization effective October 7, 2025. The Plan Effective Date distribution of approximately **$6.4 billion** to convenience-class creditors (claims ≤ $50,000) was substantially completed by February 28, 2026 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The more complex institutional-creditor distribution — involving **Plan Class 5 (Non-Preferred Dotcom Customer Entitlement Claims)** and **Plan Class 6 (Non-Preferred U.S. Customer Entitlement Claims)** — is now the operative recovery frontier.

The Plan administrator, John J. Ray III, and the Reorganized Debtors' counsel Landis Rath & Cobb together with Sullivan & Cromwell (restructuring co-counsel) have reported that **$2.1 billion** in disputed-claim reserves remain subject to resolution, encompassing approximately **14,800 unresolved proofs of claim** as of May 31, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The hotly contested issue is the *dollar-denominated vs. coin-denominated* valuation of customer entitlements: the Plan fixes all claims in USD at November 11, 2022 values (the petition date), producing entitlement figures that are now substantially below current crypto-market valuations. Creditor committees for several token categories filed a motion on May 20, 2026 seeking either (a) reconsideration of the valuation methodology under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9023, or (b) in the alternative, a declaratory order under 11 U.S.C. § 105(a) that post-confirmation appreciation must be credited. Judge Dorsey has scheduled a status conference for **June 17, 2026** in Courtroom 5 of the J. Caleb Boggs Federal Building, Wilmington.

The Bahamas parallel proceeding — *In re FTX Digital Markets Ltd.*, SCCh/COM/2022/00119, Supreme Court of the Bahamas (Commercial Division), Justice Ifeoma Jamilia Oguntola — continues to create friction around interoperability of the U.S. Plan with local creditor protections under the Bahamas Companies Act (Ch. 308). The Joint Provisional Liquidators (PwC Bahamas) and the U.S. Plan Administrator negotiated a cross-border protocol in March 2026, but three Bahamian-domiciled institutional creditors have filed a separate recognition petition in the Southern District of New York pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 1515 (Chapter 15), styled *In re FTX Digital Markets Ltd.*, No. 26-10044-MG (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn), arguing that Bahamian creditors are entitled to pari passu treatment with U.S. creditors rather than the subordinated treatment the U.S. Plan ostensibly imposes (__[Kirkland & Ellis — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com)__).

### VI. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — Parallel Forfeiture Tracks and the Konstantin Ignatov Restitution Order

*United States v. Ignatova*, No. 17-CR-00630-ER (S.D.N.Y., Judge Edgardo Ramos), remains technically open as to fugitive defendant Ruja Ignatova (the "Cryptoqueen"), who has been on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list since June 2022. The parallel civil forfeiture proceeding, *United States v. Approximately $700 Million in Cryptocurrency Assets*, No. 1:24-cv-08992-JSR (S.D.N.Y., Judge Jed Rakoff), advanced materially in May 2026: the DOJ secured a default judgment against two Panamanian entities — Fenero Equity Investments Corp. and InvestUS Capital Corp. — forfeiting **$183.7 million** in Bitcoin and Ethereum traceable to OneCoin proceeds (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/onecoincryptofraud)__). Approximately **4.1 million identified OneCoin victims globally** have been the subject of ongoing victim-notification procedures administered by the DOJ under 18 U.S.C. § 3771 (Crime Victims' Rights Act), though no formal distribution mechanism has yet been established pending completion of forfeiture proceedings.

Separately, Konstantin Ignatov — Ruja's brother and cooperating witness — was sentenced in April 2026 to 34 months' imprisonment (time served, with supervised release) and ordered to pay **$65.1 million** in restitution to specifically identified U.S. victims under 18 U.S.C. § 3663A (Mandatory Victims Restitution Act) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Given Ignatov's attenuated personal assets, practitioners advising OneCoin victims should monitor the parallel UK proceedings — *Cryptoqueen Asset Recovery Trust v. IMS Legal Strategies (UK) Ltd.* pending before the Financial List, Commercial Court (Claim No. FL-2024-000183, Mr Justice Foxton) — as the UK trust has obtained freezing injunctions against three London-based professional enablers holding assets estimated at **£28 million** (__[Norton Rose Fulbright — needs verification](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com)__).
