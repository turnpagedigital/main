### I. Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution at the Wire (SEC v. Stanford International Bank, N.D. Tex. No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, Judge David C. Godbey)

The Stanford receivership has reached a critical logistical juncture. 
The receivership website flags an important advisory regarding uncashed final distribution checks, noting that the receiver recently contacted claimants whose final distribution checks remain uncashed.
 The deadlines are immediate: 
claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash them by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash them by July 26, 2026; checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline.
 For claims investors holding positions acquired from late-stage sellers, the reversion risk is real and immediate — any uncashed position converts to a receivership asset with no claim for reissuance.

The final distribution itself covers substantial sums. 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 
According to the receiver's update posted on April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 A residual complication persists: 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026 concerning the status of $8.1 million in Swiss funds released to the receiver on December 17, 2025, and to date the receiver and his legal team have not responded to that request.
 The Swiss asset question — how and when those funds fold into a supplemental distribution — remains unresolved and is not addressed in the July 9 status report.

The receiver's fee appeal is also live. 
The district court entered an order on October 8, 2025, approving the receiver's proposed final distribution and awarding the receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the receiver and certain of his professional firms appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The receiver and certain professional firms filed their opening brief on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting also filed a separate brief in support of the appeal.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 Fee-appeal outcomes in Stanford track to claim-pricing because any upward revision to professional compensation draws on distributable assets; the Fifth Circuit has not yet scheduled argument.


On July 9, 2026, the receiver submitted to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 That report is the most current primary-source summary of where the distribution stands; claimants and buyers should read it in conjunction with the check-void advisory.

### II. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Remission Window Closed; Kroll Review Phase Begins

The DOJ-administered OneCoin remission process is now in the post-deadline review phase. 
On April 13, 2026, the Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process to provide recovery for victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd., which was orchestrated between 2014 and 2019 by Ruja Ignatova, Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and others.
 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud could apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets; OneCoin was co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.


The filing deadline was June 30, 2026. 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission compensation process.


The recovery math is stark. 
Ruja Ignatova remains a fugitive on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted List — billions in unrecovered assets mean the $40 million pool represents roughly 1% of total investor losses.
 
The $40 million-plus fund derives from criminal asset forfeiture proceedings against proceeds seized from key OneCoin conspirators, including those linked to Konstantin Ignatov.
 
Karl Sebastian Greenwood pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 20 years in prison in September 2023, along with $300 million in restitution.
 Restitution and remission are separate mechanisms; the practical priority of that $300 million restitution order against an individual without recoverable U.S. assets is, at best, theoretical. The remission pool — funded by forfeiture, not restitution — is where recovery will actually occur. Kroll has not published a review timeline; prudent expectations are 12–18 months before any distribution commences.


The U.S. government is offering a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to the arrest or conviction of Ignatova.
 The underlying investigation remains open; any incremental asset seizure tied to Ignatova's eventual apprehension or additional conspirator forfeiture would flow to a supplemental remission process, likely years out.

### III. BLMIS / Madoff — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Fifty-First Fee Application in View

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution is now fully underway. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced; the Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including this distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 As of June 26, 2026, 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.


The fee-application track is relevant to claims pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for their work from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 50th application implies a 51st is pending for the December 2025–March 2026 period; each application is a signal event for net-winner defendants and for clawback defendants still in active avoidance litigation, as fee orders inform the pace of outbound litigation against remaining net winners under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and the Ponzi-presumption doctrine established in *Picard v. Katz*.


Chief Counsel David J. Sheehan has stated that with the next anticipated distribution, 1,531 accounts will be made whole.
 That milestone — when it arrives — closes the net-winner clawback loop for those accounts, as Picard's theory of avoidance has been predicated on recovering fictitious profits from net winners to make net losers whole. Accounts reaching the 100% threshold lose their status as potential clawback targets in future avoidance rounds. 
As of January 31, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has amassed approximately $15.366 billion as a result of recoveries and settlement agreements; subject to court approval, 100% of the SIPA Trustee's recoveries are allocated to the Customer Fund for distribution.

### V. SEC Enforcement Posture — Structural Shift Under New Leadership


The SEC announced the appointment of David Woodcock as Director of the Division of Enforcement on April 8, 2026; Woodcock joined from private practice and previously served as Director of the Fort Worth Regional Office from 2011 to 2015, replacing Acting Director Sam Waldon effective May 4, 2026.
 The structural importance for receivership and Ponzi-recovery watchers: new enforcement leadership typically re-prioritizes the case pipeline within 60–90 days of appointment. Woodcock's Fort Worth pedigree — a regional office that historically concentrated on oil-and-gas Ponzi cases and EB-5 fraud — suggests continued appetite for complex multi-year receiverships. 
The current administration has prioritized enforcement in areas such as narcoterrorism, frauds against the United States government, and frauds involving variable interest entities; Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche released memorandums throughout 2025 outlining enforcement shifts, including prioritizing bribery schemes with ties to drug cartels or organized crime.
 That DOJ reorientation creates a gap: legacy SEC receivership cases (Stanford, ongoing SDNY crypto enforcement) may receive less DOJ co-prosecution support than during prior administrations, making independent civil asset-tracing tools — 28 U.S.C. § 1782, Mareva injunctions, and Norwich Pharmacal orders — more operationally important for receivers and claimants' counsel.
