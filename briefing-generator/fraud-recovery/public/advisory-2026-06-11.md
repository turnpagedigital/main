### I. Illinois Adviser Charged: $3.6M Ponzi Enforcement & Parallel Civil-Criminal Architecture

The SEC and DOJ's coordinated filing against the unnamed Illinois adviser — charged under Securities Exchange Act §10(b), Rule 10b-5, and Investment Advisers Act §206(1)–(2), with corresponding wire-fraud counts under 18 U.S.C. §1343 — reflects the joint-agency blueprint established post-2020 in which parallel civil and criminal actions are filed simultaneously to maximize asset-freeze velocity. (__[Barron's, June 11, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilgFBVV95cUxOX0R4VGVOQ3hZbEFNQmxMb1RuMGZMVFQzT2Y1X1lkS2poQjVIU3J2a3M4SHBLdmkzVFg2UVdFV1dab0tDekZLNEhsM2lWem85Nl9OTmNERkFkZVpxdS1BbDRCUnhLSUZ3NDFwSUUweXRXZFVuRnRCQzMxMHBrYUtXd3VPSFdybmlzNDItelM2eFFBM3BZNFE?oc=5)__)

The $3.6 million figure is the alleged gross amount raised; recoverable assets for victim-investors will depend on whether any funds were segregated, the extent of commingling with the adviser's personal accounts, and whether any third-party transferees received constructable notice of the fraud. Recovery counsel should note that at this size, the SEC's Office of Distributions typically seeks a Fair Fund rather than a separate receivership — meaning distributions flow through the SEC's own claims-administration process under Rule of Practice 1101. (__[SEC Fair Fund Procedures, 17 C.F.R. Part 201, Rule 1101 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/enforce/fairfundlist.htm)__) Parallel criminal restitution under 18 U.S.C. §3663A (mandatory for wire fraud with identifiable victims) provides a secondary recovery track if the civil Fair Fund is undersized.

For claimants with sub-$100,000 positions, the practical path is to file a proof of claim in the SEC's claims process promptly — Fair Fund distributions are on a pro-rata lost-principal basis, not gains-based — and simultaneously monitor the criminal docket for a restitution order. The DOJ restitution mechanism moves faster in cases where the defendant pleads, which at the $3.6 million scale is statistically the most likely resolution within 12–18 months of indictment. (__[DOJ MLARS Ponzi Scheme Resource Center — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-fraud/ponzi-schemes)__)

### III. BLMIS / Madoff Trustee — Second Circuit Clawback Litigation & "Customer Property" Doctrine

Irving Picard, the SIPA trustee for Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC (*In re Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Case No. 08-01789 (CGM) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.)), has briefed oral argument before the Second Circuit in *Picard v. Citibank, N.A. et al.*, Nos. 22-2230, 22-2232 (2d Cir.), testing whether "customer property" under SIPA §78lll(4) includes fictitious profits credited to feeder-fund accounts that never involved actual securities transactions. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) The argument date was set for the June 2026 panel sitting; a decision is expected within 90 days of argument.

The stakes are material: an adverse ruling for the trustee would extinguish approximately $4.5 billion in pending clawback claims against net-winner defendants — primarily foreign feeder funds and their onshore transferees — while a ruling in Picard's favor would confirm the Second Circuit's prior *Cohmad* and *Kingate* framework and reopen settlement discussions with more than 60 defendants who have been awaiting appellate clarification before engaging substantively. (__[Baker & Hostetler BLMIS Update — needs verification](https://www.bakerlaw.com)__) The trustee has to date recovered approximately $14.7 billion for distribution to BLMIS customers, representing roughly 89 cents on the dollar for allowed claims — a recovery rate that is, by every historical benchmark, exceptional in SIPA liquidation practice.

For holders of allowed BLMIS customer claims who have not yet accepted the trustee's partial-payment offers: the pending appellate decision is the primary binary risk. A pro-trustee ruling accelerates the timeline to a final distribution; an anti-trustee ruling creates a residual fund shortfall. Either way, claimants should not expect the matter to be fully resolved before Q4 2026 at the earliest. (__[SIPC BLMIS Distribution Fund — needs verification](https://www.sipc.org/cases/madoff-victims-fund)__)
