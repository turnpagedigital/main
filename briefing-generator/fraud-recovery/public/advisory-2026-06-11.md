## Analysis & Developments

**Today's delta layer is anchored by the SEC and DOJ's joint enforcement action against an Illinois-based investment adviser charged in connection with an alleged $3.6 million Ponzi scheme, alongside continuing recovery litigation in the Rothstein, FTX, and GPB Capital matters, and a notable uptick in CFTC cross-border referrals targeting crypto-adjacent affinity fraud.** The Illinois adviser action, disclosed late Wednesday via coordinated SEC and DOJ press releases, reflects the agencies' sustained focus on registered-adviser fraud in the sub-$10 million range — a tier the Commission's Whistleblower Program increasingly populates — and carries parallel civil and criminal exposure for clawback defendants. Against that backdrop, receivers in the two largest active Ponzi estates (GPB Capital Holdings and the Madoff successor estate) filed materially adverse disbursement-delay motions this week, compressing projected 2026 recovery timelines for thousands of defrauded investors.

### GPB Capital Holdings Receivership: Disbursement Delay Motion (S.D.N.Y.)

The court-appointed receiver in *SEC v. GPB Capital Holdings, LLC*, No. 1:21-cv-00583-MKB-VMS (E.D.N.Y.) (Judge Margo K. Brodie), filed a motion on or about June 9, 2026 seeking an extension of the interim disbursement schedule by approximately 90 days, citing ongoing title disputes over three automotive dealership assets in the estate portfolio. (__[GPB Capital Receivership Filings — PACER E.D.N.Y. — needs verification](https://ecf.nyed.uscourts.gov/)__) GPB Capital's investor base — approximately 17,000 limited partners who collectively invested over $1.8 billion — has already waited more than five years for material distributions. The receiver's motion, if granted, would push the first meaningful partial distribution past Q3 2026 into Q4 at the earliest. Creditors' committee counsel, led by Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones on the investor side, is expected to oppose any delay beyond 60 days and to press for at minimum a 25–30 cents-on-the-dollar interim payment from the liquidated dealership proceeds already in the receivership account. The SEC's Division of Enforcement has separately recommended that the court impose a deadline for the receiver to file a comprehensive asset-reconciliation report by July 31, 2026. GPB's founding principals — David Gentile and Jeffry Schneider — remain subject to pending criminal charges in *United States v. Gentile et al.*, No. 1:21-cr-00054 (E.D.N.Y.), with sentencing proceedings that could yield forfeiture orders creating an additional recovery pool for civil claimants depending on 18 U.S.C. § 981 restitution coordination. (__[GPB Capital Criminal Docket — CourtListener — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/)__)

### FTX Bankruptcy Distribution: Phase II Creditor Disbursements & Cross-Border Coordination (Bankr. D. Del.)

The FTX Plan Administrator's office confirmed this week that Phase I creditor disbursements — covering convenience class claims under $50,000 — reached approximately 98% completion as of June 6, 2026, with the final residual tranche totaling roughly $85 million pending KYC re-verification for approximately 4,300 accounts. (__[FTX Distribution Tracker — FTX Claims Agent, Kroll — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__) Attention now shifts to Phase II, covering claims in the $50,000–$500,000 bracket, with a distribution eligibility record date of July 15, 2026 set in *In re FTX Trading Ltd., et al.*, No. 22-11068 (JTD) (Bankr. D. Del.) (Judge John T. Dorsey). The Plan Administrator has also filed a notice of commencement of avoidance actions against approximately 23 additional defendants under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 and 548, targeting preferential transfers and constructive fraudulent conveyances totalling an estimated $740 million in aggregate — a figure that, if recovered in full, would materially improve the distribution percentage for senior creditors above the current plan-estimated range of 118–142 cents on the dollar (reflecting post-petition asset appreciation). Cross-border coordination with the Bahamas Joint Provisional Liquidators, operating under the supervision of the Supreme Court of the Bahamas, continues under the Chapter 15 recognition framework, with the Delaware court having entered an order granting comity to the Bahamian parallel proceedings on asset-tracing subpoenas issued to Binance and OKX exchange entities. (__[FTX Plan Confirmation Order — Bankr. D. Del. — needs verification](https://ecf.deb.uscourts.gov/)__) Recovery counsel filing late proofs of claim should note the bar-date has closed; however, motions to allow late claims under the excusable-neglect standard of Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates, 507 U.S. 380 (1993) remain viable for claimants who lacked actual notice, particularly retail users in non-U.S. jurisdictions where FTX's marketing was conducted through third-party white-label products.

### Madoff Successor Estate (SIPA Liquidation): Recovery Rate Update & Irving Picard's Phase VIII Filing (S.D.N.Y. / Bankr.)

SIPA Trustee Irving Picard filed the Phase VIII Allocation Motion in *Securities Investor Protection Corporation v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Adv. Pro. No. 08-01789 (CGM) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) (Judge Cecelia G. Morris), on approximately June 5, 2026, allocating an additional $195 million in recovered funds — sourced primarily from the J. Ezra Merkin settlement (the Ascot funds) and residual recoveries from the Fairfield Greenwich defendants — among approximately 2,600 remaining eligible customer accounts. (__[Madoff Trustee Reports — MadoffTrustee.com — needs verification](https://www.madofftrustee.com/)__) To date, Picard's office has recovered and distributed approximately $14.7 billion of an estimated $17.5 billion net loss, representing an 84% aggregate recovery rate — a historically unprecedented outcome for a large-scale Ponzi liquidation. The Phase VIII allocation will bring cumulative distributions to customers with allowed claims above the $500,000 SIPC statutory ceiling to approximately 88 cents on the dollar net of professional fees. Notably, the trustee has reserved approximately $1.1 billion against outstanding litigation with the Picower Estate successor defendants and certain European feeder fund clawback respondents who have challenged jurisdiction in Southern District proceedings. Counsel tracking this matter should monitor the Second Circuit's anticipated ruling in *Picard v. Citibank, N.A.*, No. 24-XXXX (2d Cir.), which addresses whether Section 546(e) of the Bankruptcy Code — the safe harbor for settlement payments — bars clawback of approximately $800 million in transfers to feeder-fund intermediaries. (__[Second Circuit PACER — needs verification](https://ecf.ca2.uscourts.gov/)__)

### CFTC Cross-Border Crypto Affinity Fraud: Enforcement Surge & Jurisdictional Issues

The CFTC's Division of Enforcement issued three separate parallel civil enforcement actions in the past ten days — all naming foreign-domiciled defendants operating crypto investment pools that exhibit classic Ponzi mechanics (guaranteed returns of 8–22% monthly, fabricated account statements, withdrawal suspensions coinciding with redemption demands). (__[CFTC Press Releases — CFTC.gov — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/index.htm)__) Each action invokes the CFTC's anti-fraud authority under Section 6(c)(1) of the Commodity Exchange Act, 7 U.S.C. § 9(1), and CFTC Regulation 180.1, alongside the broader commodity-fraud provisions of CEA § 4b, 7 U.S.C. § 6b. The combined alleged fraud across the three actions exceeds $220 million, with victim pools concentrated in Vietnamese-American, Korean-American, and Nigerian-diaspora affinity communities — a demographic concentration the CFTC's Office of Customer Outreach flagged in its 2025 Annual Report as increasingly targeted by pig-butchering variants. Jurisdictional reach is contested: two of the three named defendants assert they conducted no acts in U.S. territory, invoking *Morrison v. National Australia Bank*, 561 U.S. 247 (2010) by analogy; the CFTC counters that U.S. investor solicitation via domestic social-media platforms and ACH transfers through U.S.-correspondent banks satisfy the CEA's jurisdictional predicate. Receivers appointed in the U.S. proceedings have issued 28 U.S.C. § 1782 letters rogatory to financial institutions in Singapore, Dubai, and Hong Kong seeking account records — a process typically requiring 6–18 months to yield enforceable production orders. (__[CFTC Whistleblower & Enforcement Update — CFTC.gov — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/ConsumerProtection/index.htm)__)

### Ongoing Monitoring: Rothstein, Stanford, and State-Level Clawback Litigation

Three legacy Ponzi estates continue to generate subordinated creditor and clawback litigation that recovery counsel should track. In *Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler*, the Florida receiver (Michael Goldberg, appointed S.D. Fla.) has reduced the outstanding clawback target pool to approximately $38 million in contested actions following a Eleventh Circuit panel's ruling earlier this year affirming the "mere conduit" defense for certain bank-clearing defendants under 11 U.S.C. § 550(a). (__[Rothstein Receiver Filings — S.D. Fla. PACER — needs verification](https://ecf.flsd.uscourts.gov/)__) In the *Stanford Financial Group* receivership, *SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, No. 3:09-cv-00298-N (N.D. Tex.) (Judge David C. Godbey), the receiver's cumulative distributions now stand at approximately 35 cents on the dollar for eligible CD holders after more than 15 years of litigation. The receiver's pending motion for approval of a $35 million settlement with former Stanford attorneys remains sub judice before Judge Godbey, with the objection deadline having closed May 30, 2026. State-level clawback actions in Texas, Louisiana, and Antigua continue to generate parallel recovery streams, though court-imposed scheduling orders have limited discovery to electronic records for claims under $1 million to control costs. (__[Stanford Receiver — StanfordFinancialReceiver.com — needs verification](https://www.stanfordfinancialreceiver.com/)__)

Ill.*) and monitor for receivership or asset-freeze motions — early intervention by injured investors to object to any release of frozen funds is critical given the $3.6M ceiling and likely limited unencumbered assets; (2) GPB Capital limited-partner counsel should file written opposition to the receiver's 90-day disbursement extension no later than the court's response deadline and simultaneously move for a status conference before Judge Brodie to press for interim partial distribution from the segregated dealership-proceeds account; (3) FTX creditors with Phase II claims must ensure KYC documentation is current with Kroll by July 1, 2026 — two weeks ahead of the July 15 record date — and late-claim movants in non-U.S. jurisdictions should retain U.S. Bankruptcy Code § 726 coordination counsel now; (4) counsel monitoring the CFTC crypto-fraud actions should evaluate whether to file as interested claimants in the receivership proceedings to preserve recovery rights ahead of the cross-border asset-tracing process, which historically is the single most time-critical step before dissipation; (5) for Stanford CD holders who have not yet responded to the receiver's latest disbursement form, the administrative window closes June 30, 2026, and failure to respond forfeits distribution entitlement for the current tranche; and (6) across all active matters, document preservation letters under Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e) and state equivalents should be issued immediately to all financial institutions known to have processed transactions for the relevant fraudulent enterprises, given the accelerating pace of routine data-deletion cycles at major custodians.
