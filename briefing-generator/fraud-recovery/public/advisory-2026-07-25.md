### Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution: The July 26 Check Deadline

The most time-critical item in today's briefing concerns the Stanford receivership (SEC v. Stanford Int'l Bank, N.D. Tex., No. 3:09-cv-00298-N, Receiver Ralph S. Janvey). 
According to the Receiver's April 6, 2026 update, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 The official receivership site is now tracking two negotiation deadlines in rapid succession: 
claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 had until July 23, 2026 to cash them; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash those checks by July 26, 2026.
 That second deadline falls tomorrow. Any check not negotiated by the applicable cutoff becomes void under the distribution order, and the proceeds revert to the receivership estate — not the claimant.

For claims investors, this is an operational risk item, not a legal question. Any position acquired since April that includes a specific check payment needs immediate confirmation of negotiation. 
The Final Distribution Schedule covers payments totaling more than $339 million across more than 14,000 claim groups, to be issued on a rolling basis.
 The receivership administrator filed a July 2026 Status Report with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division: 
on July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 That report has not been publicly posted in full as of this writing.

On the litigation-funding side, 
the five major bank settlements — including $1.205 billion from TD Bank, $157 million from SG Suisse, $100 million each from Trustmark and Independent Bank/Bank of Houston, and $40 million from HSBC — total more than $1.6 billion.
 Combined with earlier recoveries, total distributions now exceed $2.7 billion against the original $7 billion fraud. A residual $8.1 million in Swiss-held funds has been flagged by the Latin American victim coalition COViSAL as unaccounted for; the Receiver had not publicly addressed that inquiry as of mid-March 2026. That tranche, if recovered, would add incrementally to distribution proceeds.

### Power Block Coin / SmartFi (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Parker) — Dkt. 532 Filed July 23

The tracked-case ground truth confirms a single new docket entry this week. 
Power Block Coin, LLC's case is pending before the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, District of Utah, as Case No. 2:24-bk-23041.
 Entry 532, filed July 23, 2026, carries no public description. The nature of the filing — whether a further fee application, adversary proceeding development, or plan-related motion — is unverified at this time.

The case history through the most recent confirmed filings provides the essential backdrop. 
Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, requested to convert its Chapter 11 case to Chapter 7 in November 2025, arguing that approximately $2 million in unpaid professional fees rendered the estate "grossly administratively insolvent."
 That conversion motion was heard before 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker, under Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.
 Simultaneously, the Committee moved to appoint a Chapter 11 trustee — two competing motions that set the trajectory of this estate.

The Committee's earlier liquidation plan, filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP, provides the claims-buyer's baseline: 
the plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including Celsius Network LLC claims exceeding $133 million.
 
The plan contemplated appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage estate assets, pursue legal claims, and oversee distributions.
 Celsius's $133 million claim dominates the waterfall — any recovery analysis for smaller creditors depends almost entirely on whether Celsius's claim is subordinated, settled, or pursued through avoidance litigation.

A notable parallel track: 
claims against Power Block Coin are stayed in a separate investor action, while founder-CEO Aaron Tilton's request for dismissal was denied by Magistrate Judge Christopher B. Brown in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Pennsylvania as of November 15, 2024.
 That case — with investors alleging SmartFi misrepresented its lending business — remains paused pending resolution of the main bankruptcy proceeding.

The prior judge on this case — 
Judge Joel T. Marker, who noted that the debtor filed its Chapter 11 petition with less than three hours to spare before the Subchapter V debt limit dropped from $7,500,000 to $3,024,725
 — has been succeeded on active matters by Judge Parker. The case's administrative insolvency means that any remaining professional fee administrative claims will consume available liquidity before general unsecured creditors see a dollar. Claims-buyers pricing net-winner clawback risk here face a secondary issue: with an administratively insolvent estate, the trustee's incentive to pursue § 548 avoidance against net winners is heightened precisely because those recoveries are the only viable path to funding distributions.

### BLMIS / Madoff — Distribution 17 Complete; Fee Application on File

The Madoff track carries no new delta this week but warrants a clean status marker. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, aggregate payments to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances.
 
As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.


The most recent fee event: 
on February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (No. 08-01789 (LGB)), seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public-interest-discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 10% public interest discount applied by BakerHostetler has been consistent across prior applications; it does not affect the all-in fee quantum meaningfully for claims-pricing purposes, since 
none of the money recovered is used to pay administrative costs — all trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 That structure is the reason the BLMIS Customer Fund recovery rate tracks to nearly dollar-for-dollar against gross recoveries.

Picard has now distributed or allocated more than $15.38 billion out of estimated principal losses of approximately $17.5 billion — a recovery rate approaching 75 cents on the dollar, 
many times the usual rate in comparable cases.
 The remaining universe of active litigation targets is contracting; expect distributions 18 and beyond to be smaller tranches absent a breakthrough in the feeder-fund litigation still pending.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Remission Window Closed; Claims Now in Adjudication

The DOJ's OneCoin Remission Program entered a new phase as of July 1, 2026. 
The Department of Justice announced the remission compensation process for victims of the international scheme involving OneCoin Ltd., co-founded by Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood between 2014 and 2019.
 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 The petition filing deadline was June 30, 2026 — it has now passed. 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC was designated as administrator.


The claims recovery math is stark. 
The $40 million-plus fund derives from criminal asset forfeiture against proceeds seized from key OneCoin conspirators; claimants must document their losses and submit through the DOJ portal, with restitution amounts to be prorated against total verified claims — meaning the $40 million pool represents roughly 1% of total investor losses.
 
Karl Sebastian Greenwood pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 20 years in prison in September 2023, along with $300 million in restitution.
 The $300 million restitution order against Greenwood substantially exceeds the current forfeiture pool available for distribution, but collections against incarcerated defendants with dissipated assets are speculative at best.


Ignatova, often called the "Cryptoqueen," has been a fugitive since 2017 and remains on the FBI's wanted list.
 
Authorities are offering $5 million for information leading to her arrest.
 The bulk of unrecovered OneCoin proceeds almost certainly remain outside U.S. enforcement reach. For claims investors, the $40 million fund is a settled, fixed quantity — recovery per dollar of loss will be determined by the volume of qualifying petitions submitted before the June 30 deadline. Adjudication and pro rata distribution will take additional months.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should treat tomorrow, July 26, as the hard operational deadline for Stanford Final Distribution checks dated April 27 — any uncashed checks become void and revert to the receivership estate, with no apparent mechanism for re-issuance. On Power Block Coin (No. 24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah), the uncharacterized Dkt. 532 filed July 23 warrants a PACER pull before end of day Monday; given the estate's administrative insolvency and the pending conversion/trustee motions before Judge Parker, any further professional fee application or avoidance proceeding filing would materially affect the pricing of sub-Celsius general unsecured positions. On BLMIS, the 50th fee application (No. 08-01789 (LGB), S.D.N.Y.) merits a calendar note for its objection deadline — fee application timing is a leading indicator of the next distribution cycle, and BakerHostetler's $588.88 average rate and 63,774 hours logged confirm the pace of ongoing clawback litigation. On OneCoin, with the June 30 petition window closed, the adjudication timeline from Kroll Settlement Administration is the next inflection point; sophisticated buyers of post-remission residual claims should be tracking petition volume signals from the SDNY Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section. Net-winner clawback exposure under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and applicable state UVTA remains the most underpriced risk in early-stage receivership claims portfolios — the Power Block Coin estate is a live example of how trustee incentive and administrative insolvency converge to prioritize avoidance litigation over conventional creditor outreach.
