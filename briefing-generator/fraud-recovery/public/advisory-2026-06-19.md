### BLMIS / Picard — 17th Distribution Closed; Fee Application Pending; 18th Cycle Likely Late 2026

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund commenced February 27, 2026, in Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, before Judge Lorna G. Schofield (following the January 21, 2026 court authorization). 
Trustee Irving H. Picard distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims, bringing the aggregate customer payout to nearly $15.38 billion.
 
Combined with the prior sixteen distributions, the seventeenth distribution brings the cumulative recovery to 72.015 percent of each customer's allowed claim amount, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.



The seventeenth distribution also carried a milestone: with it, 1,531 accounts will be made whole, according to David J. Sheehan, Chief Counsel to the SIPA Trustee.


The fee file is the near-term signal event for claims pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, covering work on the global BLMIS liquidation from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025. The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
Subject to court approval, 100 percent of the SIPA Trustee's recoveries are allocated to the Customer Fund for distribution; none of the recovered money is used to pay administrative costs, with all trustee, legal, and accounting fees paid by SIPC.
 That structure — SIPC bearing fee expense entirely — means the 50th fee application does not directly dilute the Customer Fund, but the volume of hours confirms that active litigation and settlement discussions on the recovery side remain robust. 
As of May 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.


**Claims-market posture:** The 72% recovery rate against allowed net-equity claims, combined with SIPC's explicit statement that additional litigation and settlements remain pending, suggests residual value in unsatisfied claim positions. The 18th distribution cycle has not been announced; based on the prior cadence (roughly twelve months between the 16th and 17th distributions), the next motion is unlikely before Q4 2026 at the earliest. Time-value discount on positions not yet made whole remains the primary pricing variable.

### Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution Executing; Swiss Tranche Unresolved

The Stanford receivership (N.D. Tex.) has reached the execution phase of its terminal distribution, but open questions on a Swiss asset tranche create modest additional value optionality. 
As of May 4, 2026, the receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups totaling more than $112 million under the court-approved Final Distribution Plan.
 
Under the Final Distribution Plan, the receiver will be making payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claimant groups.



On March 9, 2026, the receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving the Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule, which total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups, are being issued on a rolling basis, with the issuance process expected to take several weeks.


The Swiss tranche is a discrete unresolved item. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and transferred to the receiver $8.1 million in funds from that proceeding; the Joint Liquidators further informed the receiver that no further distributions of funds are expected.
 As of this writing, 
COViSAL — a Venezuelan claimant organization — sent a formal second written request to the receiver on March 11, 2026, concerning the status of the $8.1 million in Swiss funds released on December 17, 2025, and the receiver had not responded to that request as of early April 2026.


**Practical risk for claims holders:** 
Payees must cash checks issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Orders no later than 90 days after the date of issuance; a single reissuance request is permitted if submitted in writing within 90 days of original issuance. Failure to cash the check or request reissuance within the 90-day window results in forfeiture of that payment.
 Claims investors who acquired positions from Venezuelan-domiciled claimants should note that delivery logistics to Venezuelan addresses were disrupted from December 11, 2025, through March 11, 2026, 
when courier delivery was restored.
 The 90-day clock on any checks issued during that disruption period warrants immediate verification against the specific issuance date.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Opens $40M Remission Fund; Guernsey Forfeiture Adds GBP 8.59M

The most structurally significant development across this desk since the last advisory is the April 2026 opening of a formal DOJ victim compensation process for OneCoin. 
The U.S. Department of Justice opened a compensation process for victims of the OneCoin crypto Ponzi scheme, through which victims of the alleged $4 billion fraud can now apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets.
 
OneCoin was co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 Victims may submit petitions at www.onecoinremission.com, per the DOJ announcement. The underlying criminal dockets in the S.D.N.Y. are captioned United States v. Ruja Ignatova et al., Nos. 17 Cr. 556 (ER), 17 Cr. 630 (ER), 18 Cr. 746 (KPF), and 18 Cr. 793 (ER).

The $40 million remission pool represents a small fraction of the alleged $4 billion in losses — roughly one percent — but it establishes the formal U.S. recovery mechanism and sets the administrative precedent for incorporating future forfeitures. The Guernsey channel is now the most active near-term source of additional funds. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order against funds totaling GBP £8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The Guernsey proceedings commenced following mutual legal assistance requests from the Public Prosecutors Office in Bielefeld, Germany, which initially sought assistance to restrain the funds and then to forfeit them under Guernsey law.
 
Guernsey's Economic and Financial Crime Bureau announced that the GBP £8.59 million will return to Germany for victim compensation efforts.


Whether and when those German-recovered funds are coordinated into the U.S. DOJ remission process — or remain in a parallel German victim compensation track — is unresolved. The MLAT chain between Germany's Bielefeld prosecutors and SDNY is active but opaque from the public record.

Ignatova herself remains at large. 
The FBI's New York Field Office confirmed that its investigative pursuit continues, with IRS-CI and the SDNY, with Ignatova listed as an FBI Top Ten Fugitive.
 
The manhunt remains active as victims still seek answers, recovered funds are still being processed, related defendants have been sentenced, and U.S. authorities continue to treat Ignatova as one of the world's most wanted financial crime suspects.

### Structural Theme: The Recovery Gap and Cross-Border Coordination Costs

Across all three of the desk's primary recovery vehicles — BLMIS, Stanford, and OneCoin — the same structural tension recurs: meaningful principal has been recovered, but the gap between gross losses and distributed proceeds is wide, and the cost of multi-jurisdictional coordination compounds the time discount. 
The DOJ's OneCoin victim compensation process underscores the scale of the challenge, because recovered funds represent only a fraction of estimated investor losses.
 The BLMIS estate, by contrast, is the gold standard: 
two lawyers — Irving Picard and David Sheehan — have recovered approximately 75 cents on the dollar of the stolen money, many times the usual rate in such cases.


The instrument most commonly used to generate that kind of recovery rate is the fraudulent transfer clawback. The BLMIS estate has deployed § 548 of the Bankruptcy Code and Ponzi-scheme presumption doctrine extensively against net winners — a point that has sustained the trustee's settlement pipeline for seventeen years. Claims investors acquiring positions in any of these estates should continue to model clawback exposure for net-winner sellers before closing: in any proceeding where the Ponzi-scheme presumption is in play, the seller's prior net receipts may be subject to avoidance even after transfer, creating embedded credit risk in the purchased claim.
