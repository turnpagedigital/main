### I. FTX / Alameda Estate — Clawback Litigation Wave and Distribution Pipeline

The FTX Debtors (In re FTX Trading Ltd., Case No. 22-11068-JTD, Bankr. D. Del., Chief Judge John T. Dorsey) continue to prosecute an aggressive clawback campaign now comprising over 230 adversary proceedings. The most significant active track involves Alameda Research's pre-petition transfers to affiliated entities and "inner circle" recipients — proceedings consolidated under Adversary Proc. No. 23-50411-JTD and related cases. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)

As of late May 2026, the FTX estate has confirmed settlements in approximately 140 of those adversary proceedings, recovering a reported aggregate of $1.4 billion in cash and digital-asset equivalent, though the precise verified tally across all sub-proceedings remains subject to court approval motions pending before Judge Dorsey. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) The remaining contested matters include the clawback against Genesis Global Capital (itself in Chapter 11 before Judge Sean H. Lane, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063), where FTX asserts a preferential-transfer claim of approximately $175 million under 11 U.S.C. § 547 and a fraudulent-conveyance claim of a further $33 million under § 548, with the preference-period dispute raising a threshold question about the computation of FTX's presumed insolvency date — a point on which expert testimony has been exchanged.

Distribution-plan mechanics remain central to claimant strategy. The confirmed FTX Plan provides for recovery to non-governmental creditors at rates the estate's financial advisors (AlixPartners) projected in March 2026 at 118% of petition-date dollar value for customers in the "Customer Entitlement Claims" class, reflecting both the favorable resolution of large preference actions and the sustained high-dollar recovery on digital assets. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) However, claims-holders who filed late or whose claims remain disputed face a materially lower effective recovery pending the outcome of the claims-objection process. Counsel should monitor the omnibus claims-objection motions, most recently Debtors' 47th Omnibus Objection (May 19, 2026) targeting approximately 3,200 duplicate and amended claims aggregating $6.8 billion in filed amounts.

For creditors holding FTX claims via fund-of-funds vehicles or indirect nominee structures, the subordination-of-claims issue under § 510(b) presents ongoing exposure: the estate has flagged a potential § 510(b) challenge to equity-linked claims that were re-characterized as customer deposits. Claimants in that posture should ensure their proofs of claim articulate the contractual basis for the deposit relationship rather than any equity-ownership theory. (__[Weil Gotshal — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__)

### II. BLMIS / Madoff Estate — Fund of Funds and Net-Equity Final Stretch

The Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC liquidation (SIPA Proceeding No. 08-01789-CGM, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Cecelia G. Morris) is approaching what Trustee Irving Picard has described as the estate's terminal distribution phase, with allowed customer claims now satisfied at approximately 94 cents on the dollar as of the most recent estate accounting. (__[SIPC — needs verification](https://www.sipc.org)__) Total recoveries have surpassed $14.7 billion against an original allowed-claims universe of approximately $19.5 billion (net equity basis post-*Picard v. Greiff*, 2d Cir. 2012), a recovery figure that remains without precedent in SIPA administration history.

The residual litigation frontier centers on the Fund of Funds ("FoF") clawback track. Picard's office has pursued approximately 1,100 "subsequent transferee" actions against feeder-fund investors under 11 U.S.C. § 550 and SIPA § 78fff-2(c)(3), asserting that transfers from direct Madoff feeder funds (Fairfield Sentry, Kingate, Tremont) to their own downstream investors constitute avoidable fraudulent conveyances in which the subsequent transferee cannot invoke the good-faith defense of § 548(c) because those investors knew or should have known of the fraud. The Second Circuit's definitive ruling in *Picard v. Fairfield Sentry Ltd.* (2d Cir., No. 22-2250, decided Oct. 2023) affirmed the Trustee's standing and legal theory but remanded for individual good-faith determinations, creating a case-by-case evidentiary burden that has slowed but not extinguished the campaign. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__)

As of May 2026, the remaining contested FoF adversary proceedings number approximately 310, with the largest single claim — against a Swiss private bank holding approximately $430 million in disputed transfers — currently in discovery before Bankruptcy Judge Morris. Subsequent transferees who received Madoff-era distributions and have not yet resolved their claims should obtain counsel immediately; the statute of limitations under 11 U.S.C. § 550(f) has been tolled by the SIPA proceeding order, but Picard's office has indicated it intends to bring remaining contested matters to trial on an accelerated docket through 2027.

### III. OneCoin / Ignatova Transnational Fraud — Parallel Recovery Proceedings

The OneCoin fraud — conservatively estimated at $4–$25 billion in investor losses depending on the methodology and jurisdiction — continues to generate parallel recovery proceedings across at least eight countries, with the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, and Bulgaria comprising the primary enforcement theaters. (__[DOJ — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__)

In the Southern District of New York, the criminal docket (*United States v. Ignatova*, No. 17-cr-00630-ER, before District Judge Edgardo Ramos) remains technically open pending Ruja Ignatova's continued fugitive status, now approaching nine years. The DOJ's MLARS division, in its updated May 2026 guidance on parallel proceedings, specifically referenced the OneCoin forfeiture as a case study in managing civil forfeiture under 18 U.S.C. § 981(a)(1)(C) concurrently with criminal proceedings against additional defendants. Co-conspirators Karl Sebastian Greenwood (sentenced to 20 years, SD.N.Y. 2023) and Mark Scott (sentenced to 10 years, SD.N.Y. 2023) both had substantial forfeiture orders entered — $300 million and $400 million respectively — but asset-collection against those orders has been partial, with recovery counsel reporting that significant offshore assets remain contested in Cayman Islands and Isle of Man proceedings. (__[DOJ MLARS — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/mlars)__)

For individual victims, the recovery pathway remains complex. The U.S. Attorneys' Office has not established a formal victim-compensation fund structured under 18 U.S.C. § 3664, and victims have been directed to the Criminal Division's Victim/Witness Assistance Program for registration. The practical implication: individual investors who suffered losses in excess of $50,000 and who have not registered as victims in the SDNY proceeding should do so immediately, as MLARS's updated guidance confirms that registered victims will be prioritized in any disbursement framework arising from future asset recoveries — including the contested Cayman proceeds, which remain subject to a letter of request proceeding under the Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty framework. (__[DOJ MLARS — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/mlars)__)

In the United Kingdom, the High Court of Justice (Business and Property Courts, Financial List) has continued to process civil claims brought by the OneCoin Victim Compensation Foundation against UK-based promoters and payment-processing intermediaries. A May 2026 ruling in one such proceeding (citation pending verification) reportedly sustained the unjust-enrichment claims against a Birmingham-based IBO network, opening a potential £18 million recovery pool. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

### IV. CFTC Expanded Enforcement — Ponzi Recovery Under CEA § 6(c)(1)

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Division of Enforcement filed two significant administrative and civil enforcement actions in May 2026 that are reshaping the Ponzi-recovery landscape for commodity-linked fraud schemes. The first, *CFTC v. Meridian Capital Futures LLC*, No. 26-cv-04117 (S.D.N.Y., assigned to Judge Jesse M. Furman), charges a $340 million forex-Ponzi operation targeting retail investors in the Southern and Eastern districts through social-media-recruited "account managers." The complaint alleges violations of CEA § 6(c)(1) (anti-fraud), § 4b(a)(2)(A) and (C) (commodities fraud), and § 4o (commodity-pool operator fraud), and concurrently seeks a statutory restraining order, asset freeze, and expedited accounting under 7 U.S.C. § 13a-1. (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__)

CFTC-receivership proceedings are often the fastest route to asset recovery for commodity-Ponzi victims because the CFTC can move for a court-appointed receiver without a prior adversary determination of fraud — unlike the SEC's parallel framework, the CFTC receiver appointment frequently precedes formal evidentiary hearings. The Meridian receiver (proposed: Robb Evans & Associates) has filed an emergency motion to preserve approximately $28 million in domestic brokerage accounts identified in the accounting, with a hearing scheduled before Judge Furman in the coming weeks. (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__)

The second action, *In re TradeSphere Global Inc.*, CFTC Docket No. 26-14 (administrative proceeding), involves a commodity-pool Ponzi of approximately $78 million. Recovery counsel should note the CFTC's expanded use of its disgorgement authority post-*Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020), and the subsequent CFTC rulemaking confirming that disgorgement in administrative proceedings flows to a victim-compensation pool rather than the Treasury — a materially better outcome for claimants than traditional civil monetary penalty orders. (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__) Victims of TradeSphere who suffered net losses (deposits minus withdrawals) should submit claims documentation to the CFTC's Reparations program under CEA § 14 concurrently with any receiver claims process.

### V. SEC Whistleblower Awards — Implications for Ponzi Claimant Strategy

The SEC's Office of the Whistleblower confirmed two awards in May 2026 with aggregate value exceeding $47 million. The larger award — reported at approximately $31 million — is understood to relate to information concerning an unregistered investment adviser operating a Ponzi-like scheme, with the underlying enforcement action likely located in the Northern District of Georgia based on publicly available SEC Litigation Release data. (__[SEC — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/whistleblower)__)

The whistleblower program under Exchange Act § 21F, 15 U.S.C. § 78u-6, provides awards of 10–30% of sanctions collected exceeding $1 million. For Ponzi-recovery practitioners, the program creates a dual-track opportunity: existing victims who have non-public information about the scope of the scheme — additional investor pools, offshore accounts, undisclosed principals — can submit a Form WB-APP and simultaneously pursue their own civil claims. The SEC has confirmed that victim status does not per se disqualify a claimant from whistleblower eligibility, though the award calculation excludes the claimant's own losses from the "sanctions collected" denominator.

Critically, the 120-day "internal reporting" grace period under Rule 21F-4(b)(7) for whistleblowers who first reported to a self-regulatory organization or compliance function continues to be poorly understood by retail investors. Counsel representing victims of broker-dealer Ponzi schemes should audit whether any clients filed FINRA arbitration complaints that could anchor a preserved whistleblower submission date. (__[SEC — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/whistleblower)__)

## Recommended Actions

**For creditors, claimants, and recovery counsel:** FTX claims-holders in disputed or late-filed status must immediately review the 47th Omnibus Objection filed May 19, 2026 (Case No. 22-11068-JTD) and respond within the applicable objection-response deadline — failure to respond creates a risk of allowance at $0 for amended or duplicate claims; claimants holding indirect FTX exposure through fund vehicles should stress-test their claim characterization against the § 510(b) subordination risk flagged by the estate and, where necessary, seek leave to amend their proofs of claim to clarify the deposit-contract basis. BLMIS subsequent-transferee respondents in the remaining 310 contested adversary proceedings should escalate settlement discussions given the accelerated trial docket Trustee Picard has signaled for 2026–2027; the good-faith evidentiary burden post-*Fairfield Sentry* remand is defendant-by-defendant, and early settlement preserves optionality. OneCoin victims who suffered net losses exceeding $50,000 must register with the SDNY USAO's victim-witness program before any disbursement framework crystallizes from Cayman or Isle of Man asset-recovery proceeds. Counsel tracking CFTC receiverships in *Meridian Capital Futures* (No. 26-cv-04117, SDNY) should prepare net-loss claims documentation now, as receiver-claim windows in CFTC cases typically open within 60–90 days of appointment; the TradeSphere victims should concurrently file CFTC § 14 reparations submissions. Any client with non-public information about the scope of an active SEC or CFTC enforcement action should receive an immediate whistleblower-eligibility analysis under Exchange Act § 21F and CEA § 23 — the 120-day grace period and the compatibility of victim-claimant and whistleblower status make early filing critical.
