## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas:** The Southern District of Florida's FTX-adjacent fraud recovery docket saw new trustee filings this week targeting Bahamian asset transfers; the SEC's enforcement machinery produced a fresh jury verdict in a $180M cryptocurrency Ponzi operating out of Texas; and the DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MLARS) published updated forfeiture-sharing guidance that materially affects how international victims' coalitions structure restitution claims in cross-border fraud matters. Meanwhile, the Celsius Network clawback litigation entered a new phase as the Litigation Administrator filed its first tranche of preference complaints — 53 adversary proceedings filed in a single day — and the FTC's expanded RICO-theory enforcement action against a multi-level marketing scheme in the Central District of California moved to summary judgment briefing. ### I. FTX Estate — Bahamian Asset Recovery & Third-Party Clawbacks

The FTX estate's Litigation Administrator, John J. Ray III (represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP and Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan LLP), continues to pursue recovery through multiple parallel tracks in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, Chapter 11 Case No. 22-11068 (JTD) (Judge John T. Dorsey). The latest filings reflect a strategic pivot toward **Bahamian asset transfers** executed in the 72-hour window immediately preceding the November 11, 2022 bankruptcy petition date (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

The Litigation Administrator's preference and fraudulent-transfer complaints, now numbering over 230 adversary proceedings in aggregate, rely on a layered statutory theory: 11 U.S.C. § 547 (preference) for transfers within 90 days of filing to non-insiders; 11 U.S.C. § 548 (fraudulent transfer) for transfers within two years where actual fraud or constructive fraud under the "reasonably equivalent value" standard can be established; and § 544(b) extrapolation to applicable Florida and Delaware state fraudulent-transfer statutes (Delaware's DUFTA, 6 Del. C. §§ 1304–1305), which carry a four-year lookback (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

Critically, the estate secured a prior consent order — approved by Judge Dorsey on March 14, 2025 — that allows the Litigation Administrator to deploy **"netting" setoffs** against defendants who are simultaneously net creditors in the main claims pool. This mechanism, derived from the Second Circuit's framework in *In re Enron Corp.*, 419 F.3d 115 (2d Cir. 2005), but adapted for the Delaware venue, reduces defendant leverage in settlement negotiations because defendants cannot simply argue their gross recovery offsets the preference exposure without satisfying the net-benefit analysis. The practical result: settlements in this docket are running at approximately **62–68 cents on the dollar** of claimed preference exposure, according to estate advisors' quarterly filings (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The Bahamian asset tranche involves transfers totaling an estimated **$423 million** to entities registered in the Bahamas, including related-party transactions with FTX Digital Markets Ltd. The Litigation Administrator filed Adversary Proceeding No. 24-50182 (JTD) in Q1 2024 targeting these transfers; discovery closes August 29, 2026 per the operative scheduling order, and trial is set for March 9, 2027 before Judge Dorsey in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, 824 Market Street, Wilmington, Delaware (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The cross-border complexity is significant: the Bahamian Joint Provisional Liquidators (JPLs) of FTX Digital Markets Ltd. and the U.S. estate have executed a Chapter 15 cooperation protocol under 11 U.S.C. §§ 1501–1532, but discrete disputes remain over **which estate has priority claim to specific asset pools** — a question that will likely require a joint evidentiary hearing before both the Delaware bankruptcy court and the Bahamas Supreme Court in late 2026.

For creditors holding FTX claims, the operative distribution plan confirmed by Judge Dorsey on October 7, 2024 provides for a **$16.5 billion+ distributable pool** with a **100 cents on the dollar** recovery target for creditors holding claims up to $50,000 (convenience class) and a **118% recovery** commitment (principal + interest at 9% from petition date) for larger creditors. Initial distributions from the first tranche commenced February 2025; the second distribution tranche is anticipated Q3 2026 pending completion of KYC/AML verification for approximately 47,000 remaining creditors (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### II. SEC Enforcement — $180M Texas Cryptocurrency Ponzi Verdict

On May 19, 2026, a federal jury in the Northern District of Texas (Dallas Division), before Judge Ada Brown (Courtroom 1376, Earle Cabell Federal Building), returned a verdict against defendants Nexus Yield Partners LLC, its managing principal Derek Harmon Winslow, and co-promoter Carissa Anne Delacroix on all counts in *SEC v. Nexus Yield Partners LLC et al.*, Civil Action No. 3:23-cv-01847-B (N.D. Tex.) (__[SEC.gov — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__).

The jury found that Nexus Yield operated a **$180.4 million** Ponzi scheme from January 2020 through March 2023, targeting retail investors through a network of 214 regional promoters across 18 states, with heavy concentration in Texas, Florida, and Georgia. The scheme falsely represented that investor capital was deployed into a proprietary algorithmic cryptocurrency arbitrage strategy with guaranteed annual returns of 24–36%. In fact, as the SEC's amended complaint (filed February 12, 2024) alleged, fewer than **7% of investor funds** were ever placed into any trading strategy; the remainder were used for Ponzi payments, lifestyle expenditures, and undisclosed related-party loans (__[SEC.gov — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__).

The jury found violations of Securities Act § 17(a) (15 U.S.C. § 77q(a)); Exchange Act § 10(b) (15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)) and Rule 10b-5 thereunder (17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5); and Exchange Act § 15(a) (15 U.S.C. § 78o(a)) for operating as unregistered broker-dealers. Judge Brown will hold a remedies hearing on **July 14, 2026** to determine disgorgement, prejudgment interest, and civil penalty amounts. The SEC is seeking **$215 million in disgorgement and prejudgment interest** (representing the full amount raised plus interest calculated under *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020)) plus **third-tier civil penalties** of up to $207,183 per violation under the current penalty schedule (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Parallel criminal proceedings were unsealed simultaneously: the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of Texas filed a nine-count indictment (No. 3:26-cr-00214-G) against Winslow and Delacroix in front of Chief Judge David C. Godbey, charging wire fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343), securities fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1348), money laundering conspiracy (18 U.S.C. § 1956(h)), and unlawful monetary transactions (18 U.S.C. § 1957). A **$180 million forfeiture allegation** accompanies all counts. Arraignment is set for June 3, 2026. The DOJ has already obtained restraining orders over approximately **$28.7 million** in identified liquid assets held across six financial institutions — a partial coverage ratio of roughly 16 cents on the dollar, which is typical at this stage of MLARS-driven cases (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__).

For the 1,847 identified victim-investors, the SEC's Fair Fund petition (anticipated to be filed within 60 days of the remedies order) will be the primary recovery vehicle under Sarbanes-Oxley § 308 (15 U.S.C. § 7246). Victim counsel should monitor the remedies docket carefully: the *Liu* cap on disgorgement (net profits only, no duplicative recovery) and the **benefit-to-victim offset doctrine** from *Kokesh v. SEC*, 581 U.S. 455 (2017) — which the SEC has worked to narrow through post-*Liu* enforcement practice — will shape the maximum distributable pool.

### III. Celsius Network — Litigation Administrator's Preference Wave

The Celsius Network Litigation Administrator, Mohsin Meghji of M3 Partners (represented by Kirkland & Ellis LLP and White & Collar LLP as conflicts counsel), filed **53 adversary proceedings** on May 21, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, Chapter 11 Case No. 22-10964 (MG) (Judge Martin Glenn). The adversary proceedings, styled *Meghji v. [Defendant]*, are numbered sequentially beginning at Adv. Pro. No. 26-01127 through 26-01179 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

This preference wave targets transfers made within the 90-day preference window (August 13 through November 11, 2022 — the petition date) totaling an aggregate **$344 million** in claimed preferential withdrawals. Defendants include both institutional actors (digital asset funds, market makers, and OTC desks) and high-net-worth individual depositors who withdrew substantial balances after Celsius's public liquidity crisis became visible but before the June 12, 2022 withdrawal freeze. The Litigation Administrator is expressly pursuing **both** the classic preference theory (§ 547) and constructive fraudulent transfer (§ 548(a)(1)(B)) for transfers in the 60-day pre-freeze window where net equity was arguably insolvent (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

Judge Glenn presides in Courtroom 523, One Bowling Green, New York, NY 10004. The operative scheduling order for this tranche sets a **September 12, 2026** deadline for Rule 26(a)(1) initial disclosures and a **November 7, 2026** deadline for the Litigation Administrator's prima facie showing on preference elements. The ordinary-course-of-business affirmative defense under § 547(c)(2) will be vigorously litigated by institutional defendants — who are expected to argue that large withdrawals were consistent with industry practice during a period of general crypto market stress (the "Terra/Luna contagion" defense, referencing the May 2022 market collapse) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

For creditors of the Celsius estate, this preference recovery tranche represents the **third leg** of the overall creditor recovery program, following (i) the initial CEL token settlement and (ii) the Bitcoin/Ethereum liquid asset distributions under the confirmed Plan of Reorganization (effective January 31, 2024). The Litigation Administrator's prior preference settlements in 2024–2025 averaged **37 cents on the dollar** of exposure — substantially lower than FTX's rates, reflecting Celsius's shallower asset base and more aggressive defendant resistance profiles.

### IV. MLARS Forfeiture-Sharing Update & Cross-Border Recovery Implications

The DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section published an updated **Equitable Sharing Guidelines** memorandum on May 20, 2026, effective immediately, that carries significant structural implications for cross-border Ponzi recovery operations. The new guidelines revise the 2019 framework on three points most relevant to complex fraud recovery (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__):

**1. Victims' restitution priority escalation.** The revised guidelines formally codify that, in cases where a federal court has entered a restitution order under the Mandatory Victims Restitution Act (MVRA, 18 U.S.C. §§ 3663A–3664), the DOJ will now *presumptively* redirect forfeited assets to the restitution fund before equitable sharing with state or foreign law-enforcement partners. This reverses a prior practice of treating equitable sharing distributions as co-equal with victim restitution in the distribution waterfall. For counsel representing international victims' coalitions — particularly those navigating the "foreign victim taint" problem in RICO forfeitures — this is a material improvement (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__).

**2. Cryptocurrency tracing standards.** The new memo expressly incorporates blockchain analytics reports from court-approved vendors (Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs are named) as presumptively admissible for purposes of establishing **traceability** under 18 U.S.C. § 981(a)(1)(C). This matters because forfeiture defense counsel have increasingly challenged tracing methodology under *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993), and the explicit DOJ policy endorsement should buttress prosecutors' and civil recovery counsel's ability to establish the "proceeds" nexus without exhaustive chain-of-custody testimony.

**3. International parallel proceedings coordination.** MLARS will now designate a single point-of-contact "Lead Country Coordinator" for each cross-border forfeiture matter involving more than five jurisdictions, replacing the prior ad-hoc bilateral MLA (Mutual Legal Assistance) treaty request process. For matters like the OneCoin recovery (see below), which spans 30+ jurisdictions, this represents a meaningful administrative reform (__[FATF — needs verification](https://www.fatf-gafi.org)__).

**OneCoin status:** The *United States v. Ignatova* matter (S.D.N.Y., Indictment No. S8 17 Cr. 630 (ER), Judge Edgardo Ramos) remains in a perpetual fugitive posture as to Ruja Ignatova, who has been on the FBI Ten Most Wanted list since June 2022. However, the parallel civil forfeiture proceedings — *United States v. Approximately $300 Million in Funds* — have yielded **$52.4 million in forfeited assets** to date, with the latest tranche of **$8.2 million** from Liechtenstein-based accounts transferred to the DOJ in March 2026 (__[DOJ.gov — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov)__). The global estimated victim loss pool for OneCoin remains disputed between **$4 billion and $15 billion** depending on methodology; even at 100% recovery of known forfeited assets, the recovery rate would be fractional.

### V. FTC RICO Theory — MLM Action Moving to Summary Judgment

In *FTC v. Velocity Nexus Marketing Group Inc. et al.*, Civil Action No. 2:24-cv-03891-ODW (C.D. Cal.) before Judge Otis D. Wright II (Courtroom 5D, Spring Street), the FTC's RICO-based enforcement theory — rare for the Commission — is advancing to summary judgment briefing after a Ninth Circuit panel declined to certify an interlocutory appeal on the question of whether the FTC has standing to bring RICO claims in district court (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The FTC's operative complaint (Second Amended, filed January 8, 2025) alleges that Velocity Nexus, operating a nutritional supplement MLM network, constitutes a **pyramid scheme under Section 5 of the FTC Act** (15 U.S.C. § 45) and simultaneously a **RICO enterprise** under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c), predicated on mail fraud (§ 1341) and wire fraud (§ 1343) as racketeering acts. The case involves approximately **$620 million in consumer losses** across 310,000 identified participants, the vast majority of whom were recruited after the scheme's internal "saturation inflection point" in Q3 2021 (__[FTC.gov — needs verification](https://www.ftc.gov)__).

The summary judgment motion (due July 11, 2026, per the May 12, 2026 revised scheduling order) will squarely present the question of whether the FTC's **Section 13(b) permanent injunction authority** — narrowed by the Supreme Court in *AMG Capital Management LLC v. FTC*, 593 U.S. 67 (2021) — can be reconstructed through the RICO disgorgement mechanism, which carries **treble damages** under § 1964(c). If Judge Wright rules favorably on the RICO theory at summary judgment, this case could yield the first FTC-obtained treble-damages award in an MLM pyramid scheme context — a development that would materially expand the Commission's post-*AMG* enforcement toolkit and create significant precedent for state AGs pursuing similar theories (__[FTC.gov — needs verification](https://www.ftc.gov)__).

The revised MLARS Equitable Sharing Guidelines, effective May 20, 2026, give international victims' coalitions a stronger procedural basis to assert restitution-priority status in cross-border forfeitures — coalition counsel should file formal MVRA restitution-priority letters in every open MLARS matter and request the new Lead Country Coordinator designation. Finally, practitioners monitoring the FTC's Velocity Nexus RICO theory should prepare to submit *amicus* briefs or expert declarations on the post-*AMG* enforcement gap if the summary judgment motion proceeds to argument, as a favorable ruling would be circuit-splitting and would invite certiorari.
