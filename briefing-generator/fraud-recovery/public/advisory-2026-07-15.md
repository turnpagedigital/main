# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Wednesday, July 15, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's primary deltas: the SEC's newly constituted Retail Fraud Working Group (announced July 7) signals a structural reorientation of enforcement toward retail-investor Ponzi and offering-fraud cases, with direct implications for near-term receiver appointments; the Stanford receivership's Final Distribution Plan entered active disbursement phase in April, with $339 million in payments to over 14,000 claim groups now moving — but a disputed $8.1 million Swiss tranche and claimant confusion over payment logistics remain unresolved; and Power Block Coin, LLC (*In re Power Block Coin, LLC*, Bankr. D. Utah, Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) entered a terminal fee-application phase as of June 9, signaling that confirmation and/or liquidating trust activation is near-term. Against that backdrop, the Madoff SIPA estate continues its post-17th-distribution steady state — $15.456 billion recovered as of June 26, 2026, with no new distribution motion on file — making the Stanford and Power Block Coin dockets the more action-rich vectors for the next 60 days.

### SEC Retail Fraud Working Group: Pipeline Signal for New Receiver Appointments


On July 7, 2026, the SEC announced the creation of the Retail Fraud Working Group, designed to strengthen the Division of Enforcement's efforts to identify and combat fraud targeting everyday investors, including offering frauds, pump-and-dump schemes, market manipulation, and breaches of duties by investment advisers and broker dealers.
 
The Working Group is led by Kate Zoladz, Deputy Director (West), and Kim Frederick, Assistant Director of the Asset Management Unit.
 
It will serve as a dedicated resource for proactive case generation, coordinate with regulatory partners and foreign counterparts, and conduct investor outreach through the SEC's Office of Investor Education and Assistance.


For claims investors, the practical read is straightforward: a focused enforcement unit with dedicated case-generation resources historically precedes a clustering of new receiver appointments within 6–18 months. The Asset Management Unit's involvement is the tell — that unit targets investment adviser fraud, which is the structural predicate for the largest modern Ponzi schemes (Stanford, TCA Fund Management, and their successors). 
In federal court cases, the SEC may request the appointment of a receiver who works as an officer of the court to recover and protect money and other assets obtained by a defendant in connection with an alleged securities law violation.
 New working group → new enforcement priorities → receiver appointments before year-end is the pattern. Watch the AMU's docket at S.D. Fla., C.D. Cal., and N.D. Tex. for the first wave of complaints.

Clawback posture is relevant here as well. Under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A), and parallel state UVTA provisions, net winners in any scheme the working group targets will face fraudulent transfer exposure the moment a receiver steps into the case. The Ponzi presumption — recognized in circuits including the Second, Fifth, and Eleventh — treats every payment out of a Ponzi fund as constructively fraudulent, because the enterprise was insolvent ab initio. Net-winner defendants have limited good-faith defenses under § 548(c), and those defenses are heavily fact-specific. Early identification and proactive engagement with incoming receivers typically produces better recovery economics than waiting for a complaint.

### Stanford Receivership: Final Distribution Underway — Swiss Tranche and Payment-Method Disputes Outstanding

The Stanford receivership (*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd. et al.*, N.D. Tex., Dallas Div., Receivership Court, Hon. David C. Godbey) is in its terminal distribution phase after 17 years. 
On March 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph S. Janvey filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving a Joint Motion to Amend (together, the "Final Distribution Orders").
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 
According to the Receiver's update posted on April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.


Two issues remain live for claim-position holders. First, a payment-method dispute: 
COViSAL and numerous other claimants have expressed concern about the lack of clear information regarding how payments under the Final Distribution Plan are being issued; the Receiver has not specified publicly whether payments are being sent via physical check or wire transfer.
 For institutional claim buyers holding positions on behalf of multiple beneficial claimants, this is operationally material — wire-transfer delivery timelines differ substantially from check-clearing, and address/account data validation requirements differ accordingly.

Second, the Swiss tranche remains unresolved. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026 regarding the status of $8.1 million in Swiss funds (released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025), and as of the April 2026 update, the Receiver had not responded; the outstanding question is whether those funds will be included in the current Final Distribution or a separate future distribution.
 Eight million dollars is a rounding error at the portfolio level, but the legal mechanism matters: if the Swiss funds flow through a separate court order rather than the Final Distribution Schedule, claimants who filed timely claims under the Final Distribution Plan will need to confirm their participation in any supplemental distribution.

Background: 
Receiver Ralph Janvey and international liquidators Marcus Wide and Hugh Dickinson of Stanford International Bank agreed to distribute up to $300 million of the Bank's assets that had been frozen since regulators uncovered Stanford's decade-long Ponzi scheme, which defrauded investors of more than $7 billion.
 
The Examiner, John Little, filed a brief as amicus curiae in support of the Receivership Court's Order in June 2026, with the brief filed on June 9, 2026.
 That amicus activity suggests continued appellate litigation is active in parallel with the distribution — a risk factor for final resolution timing.

### BLMIS / Madoff (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789): 17th Distribution Closed; Recovery at $15.456 Billion; No New Motion Filed

The BLMIS estate entered a quiet interstitial period following the February 27, 2026 commencement of the Seventeenth Pro Rata Interim Distribution. 
The SIPA Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims; including the seventeenth distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 
The seventeenth interim distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and is paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.



As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The Fiftieth Fee Application (referenced in the madofftrustee.com statement log as of February 13, 2026) was noted on the record without detail. Fee applications at BakerHostetler in the BLMIS proceeding have historically been contested and are a reliable signal event for secondary-market claim pricing — fee creep compresses net recovery per dollar of claim face value, and the gap between gross recovery and net distributable cash widens with each fee-order cycle. No Eighteenth Distribution motion has been filed as of this advisory's publication. Typical cadence since 2021 has been one distribution per calendar year, suggesting a motion in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 is probable, though not certain.

For claims investors holding BLMIS positions at or near full satisfaction: the 27.152% of claims not yet recovered represents the remaining upside, against an $20.3 billion admitted claim pool. Residual clawback litigation from the Trustee's adversary docket (still pending against offshore feeder-fund defendants who have not settled) is the primary remaining recovery vector.

### Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-bk-23041-CDP): Final Fee Applications Signal Case Approaching Confirmation or Conversion


Power Block Coin LLC filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024; the case was assigned No. 24-23041 and is pending before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah, Salt Lake City division.
 (Note: early docket entries reflect Judge Joel T. Marker; the tracked-case ground truth confirms current assignment to Judge Cathleen D. Parker, and the May 2026 docket entry at ECF 507 confirms the Parker assignment as No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.) The case operates under a full Chapter 11 track after the debtor's attempt to proceed under Subchapter V was successfully contested by the Celsius-related creditor bloc.


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah, proposing to wind down the company under Chapter 11 (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), with the plan filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million, and involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage assets and pursue legal claims.


The June 9, 2026 hearing before Judge Parker addressed the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for Allowance of Compensation. 
The Honorable Cathleen D. Parker set a hearing for June 9, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. (Prevailing Utah Time) on the Amplēo Fee Application; Amplēo, financial advisor and accountant to the Debtor, requested final-basis compensation of $10,436.50 for services from April 1, 2025, through April 22, 2026, in addition to fees previously allowed on an interim basis.
 A "third and final" fee application from the debtor's financial advisor is a strong procedural indicator that professional engagement wind-down is underway — consistent with a case approaching plan confirmation, plan effective date, or conversion.


The committee's filing raised concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, which the committee flagged as unsecured and subject to recent encumbrances.
 That loan is a potential fraudulent transfer target under § 548 and/or Utah's UVTA — if the Liquidating Trustee activates adversary proceedings on the Solara loan, that becomes a material recovery variable for unsecured creditors. The most recent docket entry in the tracked-case data is ECF 531 (July 6, 2026), with no new filings in the subsequent nine days; the ground-truth data reports zero new entries in the 72-hour window before the July 13 sync. The docket is momentarily quiet, but the case trajectory points toward imminent confirmatory action.

Fla., C.D. Cal., and N.D. Tex., within the next 6–18 months, creating early claim-acquisition opportunities before secondary markets price in recovery trajectory.
