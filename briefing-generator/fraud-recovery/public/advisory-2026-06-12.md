### I. United States v. John Sterling Myers — U.S. District Court N.D. Ill., No. 1:26-cr-00278 (arraignment today, June 12, 2026)

A Chicago resident operating as an unregistered investment advisor has been indicted on four counts of federal wire fraud after allegedly running a Ponzi scheme that raised approximately $4 million from at least 28 investors across five states — and left more than $3.6 million of their money gone. The indictment was filed June 4, 2026, in Case No. 1:26-cr-00278, before U.S. District Judge Edmond E. Chang and Magistrate Judge Heather K. McShain. Myers, 41, of Chicago, is charged with four counts of wire fraud; arraignment in federal court in Chicago was set for June 12, 2026, at 10:00 a.m., before Judge Chang.

The parallel SEC civil action is equally detailed. According to the SEC's complaint, from January 2022 through at least July 2025, the defendants misappropriated investor money, falsified investor account statements, and engaged in other misconduct while acting as investment advisers to a pooled investment vehicle, Sterling Capital Investments, LLC. The complaint alleges that Myers began operating this so-called "premier" and "exclusive investment pool" in 2022, through Sterling Capital and SCM, and raised approximately $4 million from approximately 28 investors over several years.

The mechanics of concealment are central to the charging theory. Myers sent investors fabricated quarterly account statements depicting accumulated net gains and positive performance beyond that of the S&P 500, and he further concealed the Fund's actual results by failing to issue tax forms to investors to inform them of their distributive share of the Fund's losses. Myers reportedly touted his prior investment banking experience on Wall Street — having worked as an analyst and associate at a New York investment bank from 2007 to 2013 before being laid off — and falsely listed two siblings in executive roles. He distributed fabricated quarterly account statements depicting annual returns ranging from 16% to 54%, depending on the investor.

On the misappropriation count, the SEC further alleged that Myers misappropriated at least $1.8 million by diverting fund assets into personal accounts to pay for expenses including rent and personal credit card debt. As of the end of 2025, the defendants had only repaid investors approximately $398,000, and over $3.6 million of investors' money was gone.

The SEC civil complaint was filed as Case No. 1:26-cv-06696 in the Northern District of Illinois on June 5, 2026. (__[SEC Litigation Release LR-26562](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26562)__) Each of the four wire fraud counts carries a maximum penalty of 20 years in federal prison. The statutory vehicle on the criminal side is 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (wire fraud); (__[DOJ USAO-NDIL Indictment](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndil/media/1444771/dl?inline=)__) the SEC's civil action invokes the antifraud provisions of the Securities Act of 1933, Securities Exchange Act of 1934, and the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 — the same statutory triad the SEC deployed in the 2014 Chicago-area Blue Horizon / Caldera matter and virtually every parallel-track advisory fraud since.

**Recovery posture for victim-investors:** The SEC complaint seeks disgorgement of ill-gotten gains, prejudgment interest, civil penalties, and permanent injunctions. The criminal indictment creates restitution exposure tied to the § 1343 counts. With only $398,000 returned of the approximately $4 million raised, and at least $1.8 million confirmed diverted to personal accounts, net victim losses are structurally large relative to the asset pool likely available. Claimants in this matter should monitor the civil action docket — No. 1:26-cv-06696 N.D. Ill. — for any asset freeze or receivership motion, which, if granted, would govern the priority waterfall for investor distributions.

### II. United States v. Camarda — E.D.N.Y. No. 26-CR-51 — Sentencing Approaching (August 2026)

The A.G. Morgan / Camarda matter remains the most consequential pending fraud-recovery proceeding on the docket for Long Island retail investors. Vincent J. Camarda, 62, the chairman and CEO of the Massapequa, New York advisory firm A.G. Morgan Financial Advisors, pleaded guilty in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York to two federal counts: securities fraud and investment adviser fraud. He had run the scheme for seven years, from January 2017 through December 2024, systematically misappropriating the retirement savings of clients who trusted him with their financial futures.

The same morning as the plea, the Securities and Exchange Commission filed a civil complaint naming Camarda, his firm A.G. Morgan Financial Advisors, LLC, and company president James E. McArthur as defendants in an offering fraud that raised at least $138 million from 431 investors, a majority of whom were elderly and financially unsophisticated. The criminal plea alone carries restitution of at least $160,022,836, forfeiture of $6,639,498, and up to 20 years in federal prison.

The mechanics of the fraud ran in two overlapping phases. In Phase 1 (2017–2020), A.G. Morgan, through Camarda and CCO James McArthur, raised more than $75 million from over 200 investors through Par Funding's unregistered securities offering. Par Funding (Complete Business Solutions Group Inc.) was later determined to be a Ponzi scheme — its CEO Joseph LaForte was sentenced to 15.5 years in federal prison in March 2025. The SEC charged A.G. Morgan, Camarda, and McArthur in June 2022 for their role in these sales. In Phase 2 (2020–2024), beginning around June 2020 — as Par Funding's collapse became unavoidable — Camarda and McArthur launched what would become the larger and more damaging phase of the fraud, creating five private equity funds, issuing promissory notes against them, and selling those notes to their advisory clients. The sales pitch was uniform and specific: the investments were "safe," "low-risk," and "conservative," and the money would be spread across diversified sectors. In practice, four of the five funds invested entirely in a single high-risk mining venture.

The SEC estimates that investors lost approximately $123 million in unreturned principal, with some losing their entire life savings. The asset recovery picture is grim: both Camarda's office building in Massapequa and his personal residence in Amityville are in foreclosure proceedings. The court has ordered restitution of at least $160,022,836 and forfeiture of $6,639,498. Neither figure guarantees that defrauded investors will recover meaningful amounts; schemes of this scale rarely yield full recovery for victims.

Co-defendant McArthur — who served as A.G. Morgan's chief compliance officer — has not entered a guilty plea. McArthur, who served as the firm's president and chief compliance officer throughout the scheme, remains a defendant in the SEC's civil case. He has not entered a criminal guilty plea. His litigation continues in the Eastern District of New York. Sentencing is scheduled for August 2026. (__[Holland & Knight Client Alert](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/fraud-enforcement-is-alive-and-well)__) The August sentencing hearing will also open the formal victim restitution record; investors in the five Camarda Funds and in the Par Funding distribution tranche who have not already submitted victim impact statements through the EDNY Victims' Rights Unit should do so immediately.

### III. Power Block Coin, LLC — Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041 (Judge Cathleen D. Parker) — Docket Entry 519, June 9, 2026

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. has filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah under Chapter 11 protection (Case No. 24-bk-23041). The tracked-case ground truth confirms Judge Cathleen D. Parker is presiding; docket entry 519 was filed June 9, 2026, as the sole new filing in the 72-hour monitoring window, with content description not yet publicly detailed on CourtListener as of this run.

The background posture of the case matters enormously for creditors. The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million. Filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, the plan outlines a strategy to maximize value for creditors through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee. The company operated under the name SmartFi. (__[Chapter11Cases.com — Power Block Coin](https://chapter11cases.com/blogs/news/creditors-committee-proposes-liquidation-plan-for-utah-crypto-firm-power-block-coin)__)

The Committee's plan filing has flagged significant insider-transaction concerns that are directly relevant to fraud recovery under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 (preferences) and 548 (fraudulent transfers). The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates. A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah. The committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.

Creditors holding general unsecured claims in the Power Block Coin estate should be aware that the Celsius Network claims (exceeding $133 million on account of preference exposure in Celsius's own Chapter 11 in the Southern District of New York, No. 22-10964) will significantly dilute any pro-rata distribution if allowed in full. The $17.4 million Solara loan and any other pre-petition insider transfers remain live avoidance targets that could materially improve unsecured creditor recoveries if pursued by the Liquidating Trustee. The significance of docket entry 519 — filed June 9, 2026 — will need to be confirmed once its description populates on CourtListener. ### IV. Federal Enforcement Infrastructure — DOJ National Fraud Division & FY2027 Budget Signal

The macro context for all fraud-recovery proceedings is shaped by a decisive institutional signal. The White House's fiscal year 2027 budget request seeks $30 million for the DOJ's newly established National Fraud Division, which would support 140 positions including 100 attorneys, with resources intended to "equip the department to investigate and prosecute fraudsters and provide much needed relief to those harmed by their schemes." The SEC is seeking a similar budget enhancement to its Enforcement Division, and the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is seeking $25 million more in 2027 to bolster its Bank Secrecy Act enforcement program.

The DOJ's Criminal Division announced in early 2026 that its Fraud Section had brought white collar charges against more than 200 individuals and secured 140 criminal convictions. The Myers indictment — a sub-$5 million retail scheme — is a direct expression of this expanded institutional appetite: the parallel-track model (simultaneous SEC civil + DOJ criminal) that the Camarda matter deployed at scale is now being applied to community-level advisory fraud. The practical implication for fraud-recovery practitioners: investors in small unregistered advisory vehicles who believe they may be victims of falsified account statements should file SEC whistleblower submissions under Exchange Act § 21F immediately rather than awaiting the scheme's natural collapse, as DOJ/SEC coordination is now faster and more systematized than at any prior point in the enforcement cycle.

No. 26-CR-51) must submit victim impact statements before the August 2026 sentencing hearing before Judge Nusrat Choudhury; counsel should coordinate with the EDNY Victims' Rights Unit on restitution sequencing and simultaneously pursue the parallel SEC civil action against McArthur (who has not pleaded), which could yield additional disgorgement recovery beyond Camarda's individually ordered $160,022,836. **Power Block Coin** general unsecured creditors (Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) should closely monitor docket entry 519 (June 9, 2026) once its content is publicly available, evaluate whether the Liquidating Trustee will pursue the $17.4 million Solara Communities loan as a fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. § 548 or as a preference under § 547, and submit claims before any bar date set in connection with the Greenberg Traurig-filed liquidation plan; the Celsius Network's $133 million-plus preference claim against the estate warrants objection or at minimum close scrutiny of the underlying preference theory from the perspective of maximizing the unsecured creditor pool. Finally, all practitioners in this vertical should track the DOJ National Fraud Division's FY2027 staffing build — 100 additional attorneys — as an indicator that parallel-track criminal enforcement of small advisory Ponzi cases will intensify materially through 2027.
