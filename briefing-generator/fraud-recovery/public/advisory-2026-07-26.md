### I. Stanford Financial Receivership (SEC v. Stanford Int'l Bank, No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Judge David C. Godbey) — Final Distribution: Check Void Deadline Today

This is a same-day action item. 
Claimants who received final distribution checks dated April 24, 2026 were required to cash them by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash those checks by today, July 26, 2026 — checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void.
 
On July 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph S. Janvey submitted to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 That status report is the most recent public marker of where the distribution stands.

The practical risk is non-trivial. 
The Receiver has contacted by email certain claimants whose Final Distribution checks remain uncashed because those checks are approaching the void deadline — uncashed funds revert to the receivership.
 
Distribution payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026, though the Receiver has not specified publicly whether those payments are being made via physical check or wire transfer
 — a disclosure gap that has generated friction with victim advocacy groups in Latin America. For claims buyers who purchased Stanford receivership interests, confirm with the claims agent (Verita Global, successor to Gilardi & Co.) that negotiation has occurred before today's close. Any uncashed check reverts to the estate and the claim-level right to receive that payment may be extinguished.

The recovery arc is substantial: 
Receiver Janvey collected $1.305 billion in settlement payments since June 20, 2024, bringing total receivership recoveries to more than $2.5 billion
 as of that date, with the Société Générale Private Banking (Suisse) settlement of $157 million adding a further tranche. 
On February 25, 2025, the District Court entered an order granting the joint motion filed by the Receiver, OSIC, and SG Suisse, with the court stating that all proceedings with respect to the finality of the Bar Order are closed and further objections are barred — once those dismissals are final, SG Suisse will be obligated to fund the $157 million settlement.
 Claim pricing in the Stanford estate should now reflect a near-terminal recovery posture: distribution infrastructure is winding down, the SG Suisse tranche remains in a procedural queue, and uncashed-check reversion risk is live today.

### II. Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041-CDP, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) — ECF 532 Filed July 23; Chapter 7 Conversion Posture

The tracked-case ground truth confirms a new filing at ECF 532 on July 23, 2026, the most recent entry in this docket as of the July 25 sync. The substance of ECF 532 is not confirmed in the sources available to this run; it is noted as new-in-72h and should be pulled directly from CourtListener docket ID 68873409 before trading on the position.

What is confirmed from prior docket record: 
Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, requested to convert its Chapter 11 reorganization case to Chapter 7 liquidation, citing administrative insolvency and failed reorganization efforts — the case is being handled by Judge Cathleen D. Parker under Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.
 That conversion motion creates a fork in the road for claim pricing. A Chapter 7 conversion would subordinate the debtor's counsel (Parsons Behle & Latimer) and remove the estate's control over asset disposition, placing it in the hands of the Chapter 7 trustee (Joli A. Lofstedt has been the trustee of record in prior calendar entries).

The estate's claim stack is structurally dominated by one creditor. 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 Celsius's claims against Power Block Coin are styled as preference claims under 11 U.S.C. § 547, arising from the now-confirmed Celsius Chapter 11 plan (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 22-10964). 
A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah; the committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 Whether that $17.4 million receivable is recoverable — or whether Solara Communities encumbrances have rendered it effectively subordinated — is the single largest open question for recovery in this estate outside the Celsius claim.

Greenberg Traurig (Annette W. Jarvis, Carson Heninger) represents both the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors and the Litigation Administrator/Complex Asset Recovery Manager. 
Greenberg Traurig filed the Committee's liquidation plan on January 30, 2025, outlining a strategy to maximize value through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 A June 9, 2026 hearing was noticed on the Amplēo Fee Application (ECF 506) — a signal that professional fee accrual continues while the conversion/confirmation question remains unresolved. Fee creep in a $192 million claim estate with a dominant $133 million creditor is a pricing drag that claims buyers must model explicitly.

The prior judge of record was Joel T. Marker; the tracked-case ground truth designates Judge Cathleen D. Parker as current presiding judge, consistent with court calendar entries from November 2025 onwards. The reassignment itself warrants attention — contested matters that were briefed before one judge occasionally require recalibration when transferred.

### III. BLMIS / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789, Judge Lashann Hall) — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Fee Application on File; Recovery at $15.38B

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution closed in late February. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced the commencement of the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution; the Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims — bringing aggregate distributions to nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.


The fee pipeline is active and is the correct watch item for claims pricing at this stage. 
On February 13, 2026, Picard and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for work performed from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025; the application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
No funds recovered in the Madoff Recovery Initiative are used to pay costs associated with the recovery — all trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 That structural protection is materially favorable for claims holders: fee accrual does not compress the recovery pool.


As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation
 in SIPC advances. The commitment figure has been flat since February, suggesting that the cohort of litigated claims being resolved into allowed status has slowed — consistent with the estate's entry into its terminal recovery phase. The remaining upside is litigation-dependent: any settlements or judgments in the still-pending feeder-fund avoidance actions will fund an eighteenth distribution.

### IV. Cross-Cutting Theme: Avoidance Litigation and Digital Asset Valuation — Emerging Framework

One analytical item emerged this week from the claims-trading and restructuring commentary ecosystem that is directly relevant to the Power Block Coin estate and any cryptocurrency-based receivership. 
A July 19, 2026 article addressed "Pricing the Transfer: A First-Impression Framework for Valuing Appreciating Digital Assets in Avoidance Actions,"
 a question that sits at the intersection of 11 U.S.C. § 548 fraudulent transfer doctrine and the valuation of crypto assets held at the time of transfer. In the Power Block Coin context, where the estate includes SmartFi token positions, crypto-backed loans, and a $133 million Celsius preference claim, the correct valuation date for any § 547 or § 548 avoidance action is non-obvious — whether courts use petition date, transfer date, or fair-market-value-at-distribution creates meaningful variance in recovery outcomes. Claims buyers holding general unsecured positions in crypto insolvency estates must track this developing body of first-impression authority closely, as a single district or circuit ruling could reset the baseline valuation methodology across all open avoidance proceedings.
