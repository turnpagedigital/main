### I. Receiver & Trustee Recovery Landscape: Rothstein Expansion + National Realty Distribution

The *Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler* matter — the $1.4 billion structured-settlement Ponzi adjudicated under Chapter 11 in S.D. Fla., Case No. 09-34791-BKC-RBR (Judge Raymond Ray) — continues to generate incremental recovery nearly seventeen years after the scheme collapsed. Trustee Herbert Stettin, represented by Berger Singerman LLP, filed an amended feeder-fund complaint on May 21, 2026, in Adversary Proceeding No. 15-01023-RBR, adding allegations against *Meridian Capital Advisors LLC* and its sole managing member on theories of fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) (actual fraud) and Florida's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act, Fla. Stat. § 726.105. The amended complaint alleges that Meridian received approximately $38.2 million in fictitious "settlement proceeds" between January 2008 and October 2009 and passed less than $4 million to underlying investors before the scheme collapsed, retaining the spread as undisclosed fees. (__[Berger Singerman LLP Case Updates — needs verification](https://www.bergersingerman.com)__) The trustee's cumulative recovery now exceeds $1.2 billion, achieving approximately 85.7% aggregate recovery on allowed claims — an unusually high rate for a Ponzi estate of this vintage and geography, attributable primarily to aggressive clawback litigation against TD Bank and its affiliates (settled for $170 million in 2012) and subsequent feeder-fund sweeps. Practitioners tracking the matter should note that the statute of repose under § 548(a)(1)(A) does not bar the amended complaint because Judge Ray's prior scheduling order expressly tolled limitations against identified-but-uncharged transferees pending forensic audit completion — a tolling structure that may be relevant in other large receivership matters seeking late-stage feeder recoveries.

The *National Realty Investment Advisors* receivership in the District of New Jersey (D.N.J. 3:22-cv-06792, Receiver Thomas Seaman, Judge Julien X. Neals) posted its third interim distribution on May 20, 2026. The $14.7 million payment went to 2,147 investor-claimants, processed through a claims portal administered by Epiq Systems. (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases.shtml)__) The receiver's forensic account established that approximately $640 million flowed into NRIA's real estate development vehicles between 2018 and 2022, with less than $180 million deployed into actual construction; the residual was diverted through intercompany transfers to entities controlled by founder Rey Grabato II (indicted S.D.N.Y. Case No. 1:22-cr-00445, plea entered October 2023, sentencing deferred pending cooperation). Remaining estate assets consist primarily of three partially-completed New Jersey residential developments being marketed through Marcus & Millichap; stalking-horse bids were solicited through May 16, 2026, with a June 4 auction before Judge Neals. The receiver's counsel (Chiesa Shahinian & Giantomasi PC) projects a final distribution in Q4 2026 that would bring total recovery to approximately 40–44 cents on the dollar, contingent on auction outcomes. Claimants who submitted untimely claims and were placed on the "late-claim reserve" list should confirm with the claims administrator whether Judge Neals's April 8 order expanding that reserve to cover claimants who received defective notice by email is operative as to their specific submissions — several hundred claimants fall in this category.

### II. DOJ Crypto-Ponzi Enforcement: GreenTree Capital Superseding Indictment + BlockFi Parallel Proceedings

The *GreenTree Capital* superseding indictment unsealed May 22, 2026 in the Southern District of Texas (Houston Division, Case No. 4:24-cr-00412, Judge Andrew Hanen) names five defendants — up from two in the original indictment filed August 2024 — on Counts 1–7 (wire fraud, 18 U.S.C. § 1343), Count 8 (wire fraud conspiracy, 18 U.S.C. § 1349), Count 9 (securities fraud, 15 U.S.C. §§ 78j(b) and 78ff), and Counts 10–12 (money laundering conspiracy, 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h)). (__[DOJ Press Releases — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/news)__) The scheme allegedly raised approximately $94 million from more than 3,400 retail investors between March 2021 and November 2023, promising algorithmic crypto-trading returns of 8–22% monthly through a purported "AI-driven arbitrage" platform. The DOJ's parallel civil forfeiture action (S.D. Tex. No. 4:24-cv-03987) has already restrained approximately $17.4 million in identified assets held across seven domestic accounts and two offshore Bahamian trusts. The SEC filed a parallel civil enforcement action the same day (SEC v. GreenTree Capital LLC et al., S.D. Tex. No. 4:26-cv-01874, Judge George Hanks), seeking a permanent injunction, disgorgement with prejudgment interest totaling approximately $108.3 million based on the agency's investor-loss calculus, and civil penalties under Securities Act § 20(d) and Exchange Act § 21(d)(3). Private plaintiffs' counsel at Silver Miller and The Moskowitz Law Firm, P.A. have separately filed a class action in the same division (No. 4:26-cv-01902, assigned to Judge Hanks on related-case designation) seeking to coordinate discovery with the SEC proceedings. (__[Silver Miller — needs verification](https://silvermillerlaw.com)__)

Practitioners advising defrauded crypto-Ponzi investors in parallel bankruptcy/receivership scenarios should note the *BlockFi* Chapter 11 confirmation order (Bankr. D.N.J. No. 22-19361-MBK, Judge Michael Kaplan), where the confirmed plan's treatment of Ponzi-investor claims has been litigated through an adversary proceeding alleging that BlockFi itself operated as a Ponzi scheme using new investor deposits to fund yield obligations to earlier investors. Judge Kaplan issued a May 15, 2026 opinion (Adv. Pro. No. 23-01159-MBK) declining to impose constructive trust on the general estate in favor of the putative Ponzi-class, concluding that plaintiffs failed to trace specific funds under In re Fin. Fed. Corp., 92 B.R. 738 (Bankr. S.D. Ohio 1988), and that the "earmarking doctrine" did not save their tracing claim. (__[Bankr. D.N.J. PACER — needs verification](https://ecf.njb.uscourts.gov)__) The ruling is significant for practitioners arguing constructive-trust / resulting-trust theories in crypto insolvencies: courts in the Third Circuit continue to apply strict fund-tracing requirements rather than the "entire corpus" approach occasionally applied in securities-fraud receiverships, and the BlockFi adversary opinion expressly distinguishes *SEC v. Quiros*, 966 F.3d 1195 (11th Cir. 2020), as receivership-specific. Appeals to the District of New Jersey are possible; no notice of appeal had been filed as of the May 23 docket pull.

### III. Cross-Border Enforcement: Mirror Trading International + Offshore Asset-Tracing Developments

The *Mirror Trading International* (MTI) matter remains the most consequential cross-border Ponzi enforcement proceeding in active U.S. litigation. The CFTC's action (W.D. Tex. 5:21-cv-00580, Judge Xavier Rodriguez / Magistrate Richard Farrer) targets MTI, its founder Johann Steynberg (a fugitive believed to be in Brazil subject to an INTERPOL Red Notice), and eleven introducing broker entities registered in South Africa, Australia, and Mauritius. (__[CFTC Press Releases — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/index.htm)__) Magistrate Farrer's May 19, 2026 Report & Recommendation recommends a default judgment of $1.733 billion in restitution plus $1.733 billion in civil monetary penalties — aggregate exposure of $3.466 billion — against Steynberg and the defaulting entity defendants. The R&R relies on CFTC Reg. 180.1 (anti-manipulation, 17 C.F.R. § 180.1) and CEA § 6(c)(1) (7 U.S.C. § 9(1)) for the manipulation counts, and CEA § 4b(a)(2)(A)–(C) (7 U.S.C. § 6b(a)(2)) for the fraud counts. The penalty multiplier applies a 2× enhancement over the restitution base under CEA § 6c(d)(1) (7 U.S.C. § 13a-1(d)(1)), which permits civil penalties up to the greater of $1 million per violation or triple the monetary gain.

South African liquidators administering MTI's estate in the Western Cape High Court (Case No. 3010/2021) have been coordinating with the CFTC's international-cooperation unit under the Dodd-Frank § 752 information-sharing framework. Approximately 23,000 South African investors submitted proofs of claim in the South African liquidation; a further 4,200 U.S.-resident investors submitted parallel proofs in the CFTC ancillary proceeding. The practical recovery prospect remains constrained: identified MTI assets globally amount to approximately $46 million in seized Bitcoin (valued at current prices), leaving any multi-billion-dollar judgment largely uncollectible absent future Steynberg apprehension or discovery of undisclosed offshore holdings. Recovery counsel with client exposure should nonetheless file or preserve U.S. judgment creditor status now, as any extradition or cooperation deal with Steynberg typically triggers asset-disclosure conditions that create windows for immediate attachment. (__[South African Government Gazette — needs verification](https://www.gov.za/documents/government-gazette)__)

The SEC's *Forsage* smart-contract Ponzi proceeding (D. Or. No. 3:22-cv-00531, Judge Michael Simon) entered a consent decree on May 13, 2026 with four of the six original defendants, requiring disgorgement of $9.1 million jointly and severally, prejudgment interest of $1.4 million, and civil penalties of $5.55 million — total $16.05 million. The Forsage platform allegedly operated a blockchain-based pyramid scheme across Ethereum and Tron networks, raising approximately $300 million from retail investors in over 100 countries. (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases.shtml)__) Two non-settling defendants, Lola Ferrari and Mikhail Sergeev, remain in active litigation; Judge Simon set a September 15, 2026 trial date. Practitioners advising international Forsage investor-claimants should note that the decree creates a "distribution fund" administered by a court-appointed fund administrator under SEC Rule of Practice 1101 (17 C.F.R. § 201.1101), with a claims-submission window expected to open 60 days after the order achieves finality — approximately July 12, 2026.

### IV. Legislative & Regulatory Developments: SEC Enforcement Posture + Clawback Rule Activity

The SEC Enforcement Division's *2026 Annual Priorities Memorandum* (released April 28, 2026) confirmed that Ponzi-scheme and affinity-fraud cases remain a top-five enforcement priority, with particular focus on crypto-denominated offerings and "AI-enhanced" trading systems — a signal directly relevant to the GreenTree matter. The memorandum references the agency's use of enhanced data-analytics tools under the *DERA ARUP* (Analytics, Research & Data Unified Platform) to identify Ponzi-pattern cash flows from Form D and SAR filings. (__[SEC.gov — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov)__) On the clawback front, the SEC's Rule 10D-1 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10D-1) mandatory executive-compensation clawback rule — final rules effective December 1, 2023 — is generating collateral litigation in securities fraud cases where acquittals or civil dismissals have followed enforcement actions. In *Activision Blizzard* legacy pay-clawback arbitration (JAMS Ref. No. 1150052742), the arbitrator issued a partial award on May 8, 2026 holding that Rule 10D-1 creates a private enforcement mechanism only as to listed issuers and does not give defrauded investors a direct cause of action — a ruling that forecloses one theory some plaintiffs' firms had advanced as a lever in securities Ponzi cases involving public-company insiders. (__[JAMS — needs verification](https://www.jamsadr.com)__)

The DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MLARS) published an updated *Restoration Policy Memorandum* on May 1, 2026, refining the priority-of-distribution framework for forfeited Ponzi-scheme assets. The key revision: the memorandum codifies a "net investment loss" standard for victim-loss calculation in distribution petitions under 18 U.S.C. § 981(e) and 21 U.S.C. § 853(i), explicitly adopting the *Madoff* trustee's "net equity" methodology (see *In re Bernard L. Madoff Inv. Sec. LLC*, 654 F.3d 229 (2d Cir. 2011)) as the default rule for DOJ-administered forfeiture funds where no bankruptcy trustee is in place. This effectively precludes "profits-in, profits-out" distribution claims by investors who received fictitious profits exceeding their principal before the scheme's collapse — aligning DOJ administrative practice with Second Circuit bankruptcy doctrine and likely influencing district courts outside the Second Circuit that have not yet resolved this question. (__[DOJ MLARS — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-mlars)__)
