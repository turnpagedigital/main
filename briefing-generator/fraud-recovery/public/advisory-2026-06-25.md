### OneCoin Remission — Petition Deadline June 30, 2026


In April 2026, the Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin international investment scheme. Between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-founded OneCoin Ltd., which began operations in 2014 in Sofia, Bulgaria, and marketed a fraudulent cryptocurrency through a global multi-level-marketing network. Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.



Through the remission process, victims who purchased the fraudulent OneCoin cryptocurrency between 2014 and 2019 may be eligible for compensation. Individuals who believe they may be victims may obtain a petition form at www.onecoinremission.com. The deadline for filing a petition is June 30.
 That date falls this Monday. 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator.


The remission fund draws on criminal forfeiture recoveries secured through the SDNY prosecutions. 
The Department of Justice filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York, and several key figures involved in the scheme have been sentenced, with the Department pursuing criminal forfeiture of property derived from the proceeds.
 Ignatova herself remains a fugitive. 
She is listed on the FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, and U.S. authorities have publicly announced a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to her arrest and/or conviction.


The European enforcement picture has moved in parallel. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, found in German legal proceedings to be proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct. The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies — Abbots House Penthouse Limited (AHPL) and Abbots Property Limited (APL) — which Ignatova used to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 
The seized funds are to be paid into Guernsey's seized assets fund and then returned to the German authorities for victim compensation.
 The Guernsey forfeiture proceeded under non-conviction-based asset forfeiture authority, a mechanism that will be important to watch as parallel proceedings advance in other jurisdictions.

**Claims-desk implication:** The DOJ remission process is a direct-payment mechanism — not a claims-trading vehicle — but the deadline is hard. Any fund LP or portfolio holding a potential victim position needs to confirm petition status by Friday, June 27, to have buffer time before the June 30 cutoff. Once the window closes, forfeited funds that would have been allocated to that victim position revert to general DOJ asset forfeiture fund disposition. No cure mechanism has been announced.

### BLMIS / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789) — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Eighteenth in View


On February 27, 2026, Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims. Including that distribution, aggregate payments to eligible BLMIS customers total nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances. The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts. Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions.



As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion.
 The delta between total recoveries ($15.411B) and total distributions ($15.38B) reflects residual cash in the Customer Fund plus ongoing litigation-recovery activity. The eighteenth distribution will require a new allocation motion before Judge Loretta G. Birnbaum (who now presides under case citation 08-01789 (LGB)). 
An earlier hearing on the seventeenth distribution motion had been scheduled for January 28, 2026. Chief Counsel David J. Sheehan stated that, with the next anticipated distribution, 1,531 accounts will be made whole.


Fee dynamics remain a signal metric for claim pricing. 
No funds recovered in the Madoff Recovery Initiative are used to pay costs associated with the recovery — all trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 That fee structure is unusual in the universe of Ponzi receiverships and distinguishes BLMIS claim economics sharply from, e.g., Stanford, where professional fees are an estate cost. 
As of March 2026, the Trustee had recovered $15.3 billion toward claims through legal action against Madoff associates, feeder funds, and beneficiaries, and had made seventeen distributions to investors. DOJ action recovered an additional $4.3 billion.


**Claims-desk implication:** At 72.848% of net equity paid and with ~$578M in SIPC advances outstanding against the Customer Fund, the marginal recovery rate on the remaining fraction is increasingly sensitive to litigation outcomes rather than settlement timing. Secondary-market pricing on BLMIS claims should reflect the effective cost of carry against a multi-year tail. The next distribution trigger will be a new allocation motion; watch for a filing in the September–November 2026 window, consistent with recent inter-distribution intervals.

### Stanford Receivership (N.D. Tex., Dallas Div.) — Final Distribution Execution; $112M Paid as of May 4


On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.



Under the Final Distribution Plan, the Receiver will be making payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claimant groups. On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.



As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups totaling more than $112 million.
 The Swiss ancillary proceeding has now closed. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank concluded, and transferred $8.1 million to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol. The Joint Liquidators indicated no further distributions of funds are expected.


A claimants' group, COViSAL, has sought clarification from the court on the disposition of the Swiss $8.1 million tranche. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026, asking whether the $8.1 million in Swiss assets will be distributed and whether those funds will be included in a future distribution separate from the current Final Distribution Plan. As of April 2026, the Receiver had not responded to that request.



Payees must cash checks issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Orders no later than 90 days after the date of issuance. If a payee does not cash the check or request reissuance within 90 days, that payee shall not be eligible to receive any other payment under the Final Distribution Plan.
 This is a hard forfeit — no cure — making check-tracking operationally critical for any fund holding Stanford claims on behalf of beneficiaries in jurisdictions with unreliable mail service (Venezuela courier service was briefly disrupted and restored as of March 11, 2026).

**Claims-desk implication:** Stanford is now in execution-only mode. The Final Distribution Plan has court approval and is being paid down on a rolling basis. No new negotiated recoveries are anticipated. The open question is the $8.1M Swiss tranche and whether it triggers a supplemental distribution or simply folds into the final plan. Claims buyers who acquired positions expecting a specific recovery percentage should reconcile their pro rata share against the May 4 payment progress data.

### Power Block Coin / SmartFi (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041-CDP) — Conversion Track; Celsius Preference Claims Loom


Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, has requested to convert its Chapter 11 reorganization case to a Chapter 7 liquidation, citing administrative insolvency and failed reorganization efforts. The case is before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah under Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.
 The tracked-case ground truth confirms the most recent docket activity at Entry No. 519, filed June 9, 2026.


The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million. A key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions. The committee's filing raised concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates. A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, flagged for its unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property.


The preference exposure is material. 
The Celsius-related Objecting Creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending bankruptcy case of Celsius Network LLC et al. (Case No. 22-10964), pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 If conversion to Chapter 7 is approved, a Chapter 7 trustee would succeed to avoidance claims under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 and 548 that the debtor's management has had limited incentive to pursue aggressively. The Celsius preference position is the dominant creditor claim; its resolution will determine whether unsecured creditors outside the Celsius block see any recovery at all.

**Claims-desk implication:** This case is structurally adverse for general unsecured creditors absent a successful recovery on the Solara loan and any § 548 avoidance actions. The conversion motion is the watch item — once Chapter 7 is granted, the new trustee's first-day priorities will be the Solara collateral and adversary standing against insiders. Claim prices should reflect near-zero recovery unless Solara real property can be liquidated above the encumbrances.

### SEC Enforcement Macro: Fraud-First Pivot Shapes Receivership Pipeline


Consistent with the priorities articulated in the SEC's FY 2025 results release, the mix of cases brought during the first half of FY 2026 reflects a clear emphasis on traditional fraud schemes that directly harm investors. Twenty cases concerning securities offerings represented the single largest enforcement category, accounting for approximately one-third of all actions — typically involving Ponzi-like schemes, misrepresentations to investors, and/or frauds targeting specific groups such as veterans, seniors, or members of a particular religious group.



In September 2025, the Commission formed the Cross-Border Task Force to help address the serious threat that fraudsters located abroad pose to U.S. investors and markets.
 That task force directly intersects with the OneCoin and Stanford cross-border tracing patterns tracked here. From a receivership-pipeline perspective, the current administration's posture — 
prioritizing fraud enforcement, retail investor protection, individual accountability, and clearcut violations
 — means the primary SEC-driven receivership funnel for 2026–2027 is likely to be retail-facing offering frauds and crypto-adjacent schemes rather than institutional securities violations. That shapes where new claim-buying opportunities will originate.


The Enforcement Division lost 18% of its workforce during FY 2025, including many senior, experienced attorneys and accountants.
 Reduced investigative capacity against a stated priority of higher-quality cases suggests longer lag times between scheme collapse and formal receiver appointment — an important variable when modeling time-to-distribution on any new receivership.
