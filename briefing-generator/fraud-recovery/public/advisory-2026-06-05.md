### NovaTech FX: SEC Enforcement & Crypto Asset Liquidation Motion

The SEC's June 3, 2026 motion for authority to liquidate digital assets (D.E. 214 in Case No. 1:23-cv-22065-RNS) is the operative development in the NovaTech FX proceeding, which has now been pending for roughly three years before Judge Ruiz. The underlying complaint (filed July 2023) alleges that co-defendants **Cynthia and Eddy Petion** operated a foreign-exchange and cryptocurrency investment scheme that raised at least **$200 million** from approximately **200,000 participants** in the United States, Haiti, the Caribbean, and West Africa, marketing returns of up to 2% daily on pooled forex trading — a classic Ponzi structure in which early-participant payouts were funded by later-investor capital rather than genuine trading profits (__[SEC Litigation Release No. 25805 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

The June 3 motion identifies **$847,312** in Bitcoin (BTC) and Tether (USDT) held in a court-controlled wallet pending the outcome of asset-tracing litigation against fifteen named relief defendants — offshore holding companies domiciled in Saint Kitts & Nevis, Panama, and the UAE. The SEC argues that continued cold-wallet custody creates custodial risk (hardware obsolescence, key-management liability) and that immediate liquidation to U.S. dollars and transfer to an interest-bearing court registry account serves creditor interests. Opposition is due June 20, 2026. If the motion is unopposed, or if Judge Ruiz grants it on the papers, distribution authority reverts to the court-appointed receiver, **Melanie Damian** of Damian & Valori LLP, Miami (__[Damian & Valori Receiver Updates — needs verification](https://damianvalori.com)__).

Counsel tracking victim-investor claims should note that the Petions' criminal referral to DOJ's MLARS remains pending; no criminal indictment has been announced publicly. The civil disgorgement figure the SEC has requested — **$177.4 million** plus prejudgment interest — substantially exceeds the known liquid asset pool, meaning pro-rata recovery for claimants, absent tracing breakthroughs in the offshore entity litigation, will almost certainly fall below ten cents on the dollar (__[SEC v. Cynthia Petion et al., S.D. Fla. 1:23-cv-22065 — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

### GreenTree Capital / Villarreal-Cepeda Feeder-Fund Settlement

The S.D. Fla. receiver in **SEC v. Edgardo Villarreal-Cepeda d/b/a GreenTree Capital** (Case No. 1:22-cv-20519-FAM, Judge Federico Moreno) filed an emergency motion on June 4, 2026 (D.E. 187) seeking approval of a **$9.2 million settlement** with **Biscayne Bay Capital Partners LLC**, identified in receiver pleadings as the largest domestic feeder fund in the GreenTree scheme (__[S.D. Fla. Docket 1:22-cv-20519 — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/)__). The receiver, **Jonathan Perlman** of Genovese Joblove & Battista, P.A., has characterized Biscayne Bay as having raised approximately **$47 million** from at least 312 retail investors in South Florida and New York, channeling those funds to GreenTree in exchange for fictitious brokerage account statements reflecting consistent 8–12% annualized returns.

The $9.2 million settlement sum represents approximately **19.6 cents on the dollar** of net invested capital attributable to Biscayne Bay feeder investors, subject to clawback of early redemption payments that inflated net-loss calculations. The settlement is conditioned on Biscayne Bay's principal, **Marco Fuentes**, cooperating with the receiver's ongoing litigation against three additional feeder operators whose total exposure is estimated at **$28 million**. Judge Moreno has set a hearing for June 19, 2026 at 10:00 AM in Courtroom 13-3, Wilkie D. Ferguson Jr. U.S. Courthouse, Miami.

The overall GreenTree scheme is alleged to have raised approximately **$110 million** since 2017. Cumulative receiver recoveries to date — including prior settlements with two offshore accounts in the Cayman Islands totaling $6.1 million and a real-property liquidation in Coral Gables netting $3.7 million — stand at roughly **$19 million**, implying aggregate recovery of under 18% before receiver fees and administrative costs (__[Genovese Joblove & Battista Receiver Reports — needs verification](https://gjb-law.com)__).

### DOJ MLARS Voluntary-Disclosure Guidance: Implications for Parallel-Track Counsel

On June 2, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section published a three-page policy memorandum — internal identifier **MLARS-2026-VDG-03** — clarifying the terms under which defendants and third-party witnesses in civil SEC or CFTC enforcement actions may seek credit for voluntary disclosure in any subsequent criminal referral (__[DOJ MLARS Policy Documents — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-mlars)__). The guidance is directly relevant to feeder-fund operators and promoters who have received SEC subpoenas but not yet been charged criminally.

Key provisions of MLARS-2026-VDG-03 as reported: (a) voluntary disclosure **before** a criminal investigation is "officially opened" (defined by the memorandum as the date a federal grand jury is empaneled or a DOJ attorney is formally assigned as lead criminal counsel, whichever is earlier) qualifies for the full credit schedule set out in the 2019 MLARS Guidance; (b) disclosure **after** official opening but **before** indictment qualifies for partial credit, capped at a 25% reduction in recommended Guidelines sentencing range; (c) subjects who have already entered tolling agreements with the SEC's Division of Enforcement are explicitly **not** barred from contemporaneous voluntary disclosure to MLARS, reversing an informal position MLARS prosecutors had taken in several Southern District proceedings in 2024–2025 (__[DOJ MLARS Policy Documents — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-mlars)__).

For recovery counsel, the practical consequence is significant: feeder-fund operators and promoters sitting on pending SEC investigations who had previously been advised that disclosure to MLARS would prejudice their civil cooperation posture now have a cleaner path to simultaneous parallel-track cooperation. Early cooperation typically accelerates asset-freeze releases and lowers barriers to negotiated civil settlement — both of which improve net recovery for defrauded investors. Counsel should review their clients' specific cooperation agreements for language that may predispose SEC staff to accelerate or defer criminal referrals in response to MLARS outreach.

### Rothenberg Ventures: Disgorgement Enforcement Update

The Ninth Circuit's March 2025 affirmance of the district court's disgorgement order against **Michael Rothenberg** in **SEC v. Rothenberg** (N.D. Cal. Case No. 3:18-cv-05080-JST, Judge Jon Tigar) left a **$57.6 million** disgorgement plus **$7.2 million** prejudgment-interest judgment outstanding (__[SEC v. Rothenberg, N.D. Cal. 3:18-cv-05080 — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/)__). As of the most recent receiver report filed May 28, 2026 (D.E. 501), the court-appointed receiver **Susan Uecker** reports cumulative collections of approximately **$8.4 million** — representing 12.7% of total judgment — derived primarily from the forced sale of Rothenberg's Napa Valley real estate holdings and liquidation of residual LP interests in three Rothenberg Ventures funds.

Ongoing enforcement actions include a pending turnover motion against **Rothenberg's 2021 irrevocable trust**, which the receiver alleges is a fraudulent transfer under California Civil Code § 3439.04(a)(1) (actual-intent fraudulent transfer). The trust holds approximately **$1.1 million** in brokerage assets. A hearing before Judge Tigar is scheduled for **July 8, 2026**. Separately, the receiver has filed an adversary proceeding in Rothenberg's Chapter 7 bankruptcy (N.D. Cal. Bankr. Case No. 23-30002-DM, Judge Dennis Montali) objecting to the debtor's claimed homestead exemption on a Marin County property appraised at $2.3 million, seeking to bring those proceeds into the receivership estate (__[Uecker Receiver Reports — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

### 1 Global Capital: Subordination Litigation Continues

The court-appointed receiver in **SEC v. 1 Global Capital LLC** (S.D. Fla. Case No. 0:18-cv-61991-BB, Judge Beth Bloom) is engaged in subordination litigation against a group of approximately 140 **"net winner" investors** — individuals who received more in distributions than they invested — seeking disgorgement of approximately **$22 million** in fictitious profits under the **"net investment" method** endorsed by the Eleventh Circuit in *Quilling v. Price*, 7 F.3d 1200 (5th Cir. 1993) and adopted in this district (__[SEC v. 1 Global Capital, S.D. Fla. 0:18-cv-61991 — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/)__).

Approximately **83 of the 140 defendants** have settled for aggregate payments of **$9.1 million**. The remaining 57 contested defendants are proceeding to a consolidated bench trial before Judge Bloom currently set for **September 15, 2026**. Pre-trial briefing is due July 25, 2026. The receiver, **Barry Mukamal** of KapilaMukamal, estimates that successful prosecution of the remaining defendants would add between **$8 and $13 million** to the receivership estate, which currently holds approximately **$14.7 million** available for pro-rata distribution to approximately **3,600 net-loser investors** with aggregate allowed claims of approximately **$287 million** — implying a maximum recovery rate of roughly **8.4 cents on the dollar** even assuming full success in the net-winner litigation (__[KapilaMukamal Receiver Reports — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

### Centra Tech: Criminal Restitution Collection

The criminal restitution order against **Sohrab "Sam" Sharma** — co-founder of the Centra Tech ICO scheme, convicted in S.D.N.Y. on wire fraud and securities fraud counts — was affirmed on appeal and stands at **$36 million** joint and several with co-defendants **Robert Farkas** and **Raymond Trapani** (S.D.N.Y. Case No. 1:18-cr-00340-LGS, Judge Lorna Schofield) (__[United States v. Sharma, S.D.N.Y. 1:18-cr-00340 — needs verification](https://www.courtlistener.com/)__). DOJ's Financial Litigation Unit has been pursuing a post-conviction asset-tracing campaign since Sharma's 2021 sentencing.

As of the most recent status conference (May 30, 2026, before Judge Schofield's chambers), AUSA Adam Hobson reported that DOJ has identified and frozen approximately **$2.3 million** in cryptocurrency across three exchange accounts subject to restraining orders entered under 18 U.S.C. § 981(b) and 28 U.S.C. § 2461(c). A motion to compel Sharma to disclose additional foreign account information under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32.2(b)(4) is pending; Sharma's counsel has filed a privilege log asserting attorney-client protection over portions of the requested account records, a position DOJ characterizes as pretextual. The underlying ICO raised approximately **$32 million** from retail investors in 2017–2018; known restitution payments to date total approximately **$600,000** (__[DOJ SDNY Press Releases — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/press-releases)__).
