### I. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Cycle & Reserves Mechanics (In re FTX Trading Ltd., Bankr. D. Del., No. 22-11068 (JTD))

The FTX Recovery Trust's fourth distribution — launched March 31, 2026 — marked a structural milestone in the estate's wind-down. The Trust distributed approximately $2.2 billion to creditors on March 31 in its fourth payout under the exchange's Chapter 11 plan, with funds arriving via BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer; the latest distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes — including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B — reached 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent. In this fourth distribution, those eligible for distribution classed as "Class 5A Dotcom" received an additional 18%, bringing their total recovery to 96%.

Aggregate distributions across all rounds now confirm the estate's extraordinary recovery trajectory. Distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion last September, and a fourth round launched on March 31. The fourth distribution follows three prior rounds that together returned billions to creditors since FTX filed for bankruptcy in November 2022; the total estate has recovered an estimated $14 billion to $16 billion, according to prior court filings.

Concurrent with the fifth distribution cycle now taking shape, the estate has been actively rationalizing its reserves. As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the entity filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion. Payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders are scheduled for July 31, 2026, and holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims under the confirmed Chapter 11 Plan will become eligible to initiate the NFT distribution process beginning June 30, 2026. Recovery counsel with non-U.S. clients should note that the July 31 distribution window carries its own eligibility cut-offs distinct from today's record date; onboarding failures are the single most common reason creditors miss payouts.

For the clawback litigation docket, the estate continues to pursue pre-collapse preferential and fraudulent-transfer claims. The estate is still pursuing litigation aimed at recovering assets it says were improperly transferred before the exchange collapsed in November 2022; Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion, according to Bloomberg Law.

### II. BLMIS / Madoff Liquidation — Seventeenth Distribution Underway (SIPC v. BLMIS, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB); *In re* Bernard L. Madoff, No. 08-01789-LGB)

The Madoff SIPA liquidation remains the benchmark against which every Ponzi-scheme recovery program is measured, and it reached two notable milestones in the first quarter of 2026. On February 27, 2026, the Seventeenth Pro Rata Interim Distribution of Recovered Funds to Madoff claims holders commenced, totaling more than $253 million. The U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York had approved the allocation of new recoveries to the BLMIS Customer Fund and authorized the Seventeenth Pro Rata Interim Distribution to BLMIS customers with allowed claims on January 21, 2026.

On the aggregate recovery side: as of February 27, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.366 billion — a recovery that far exceeds any prior restitution effort related to Ponzi schemes both in terms of dollars and percentage of stolen funds. As of March 2026, the Trustee had made seventeen distributions to investors; action by the Department of Justice recovered an additional $4.3 billion.

SIPC's structural role in the liquidation warrants attention: SIPC has made cash advances — up to a maximum of $500,000 per allowed claim — available to the court-appointed SIPA Trustee to distribute to eligible customers; as of May 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation for this purpose. According to the provisions of SIPA, SIPC is reimbursed for its advances to customers once each respective customer claim is fully satisfied; SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.

Ongoing clawback litigation targeting offshore feeder-fund redemptions continues to press the extraterritoriality issues resolved by the Second Circuit and denied *certiorari* by the Supreme Court. The $124 million clawback suit against HSBC Suisse, litigated by Brown Rudnick on behalf of the Fairfield Sentry liquidators, remains a live proceeding. In August 2021 — a year after the U.S. Supreme Court refused to hear a bid by major banks, including HSBC, to prevent the Trustee from clawing back proceeds transferred overseas — the liquidators filed an amended complaint seeking recovery of more than $124 million in redemption payments made to HSBC Suisse; the complaint alleges that investors received payments based on a highly inflated net asset value, and that "HSBC Suisse had knowledge of the Madoff fraud."

### III. Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Enters Payment Phase (SEC v. Stanford Int'l Bank Ltd., N.D. Tex., No. 3:09-cv-298-N (Godbey, J.))

The Stanford receivership is now in its terminal disbursement phase after more than fifteen years of litigation. On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend; payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule — which total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups — will be issued on a rolling basis beginning 14 days after the filing of the Schedule. As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups pursuant to the court-approved Final Distribution Plan (as amended).

The Stanford matter stands as a parallel instructive case to FTX on the role of third-party financial institutions in Ponzi scheme liability. After excluding "deemed satisfied" amounts and the judgments against Robert Allen Stanford and other defendants in the Commission's long-running litigation concerning their $8 billion Ponzi scheme, the monetary relief obtained in fiscal year 2025 totaled $1.4 billion in disgorgement and prejudgment interest and $1.3 billion in civil penalties. The SEC obtained final judgments against Robert Allen Stanford and seven other defendants in No. 3:09-cv-298-N (N.D. Tex., filed Feb. 16, 2009) on January 29, 2025.

### IV. Power Block Coin, LLC — Chapter 11 Liquidation (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041 (Parker, J.))

The authoritative tracked-case ground truth confirms the case is active before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah, with the last docketed filing at Entry No. 519 (June 9, 2026). Note: early case records show the matter was initially assigned to Judge Joel T. Marker before reassignment to Judge Parker per the tracked-case ground truth, which is the controlling reference.

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company under Chapter 11 protection through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.

The proposal came after Power Block Coin, which operated under the name SmartFi, filed for bankruptcy protection on June 20, 2024; the plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million; a key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors.

The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates; a significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, which the committee expressed concern about due to its unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.

The Celsius Network connection is structurally significant: the Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending bankruptcy case of Celsius Network LLC et al. (case #22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. On October 9, 2024, the bankruptcy court sustained Celsius's objection and converted Power Block Coin's Subchapter V case to a Chapter 11 case. Recovery counsel representing unsecured creditors beyond the Celsius estate — particularly international creditors reflected on the April 2026 notice registry (Entry 490) — should monitor whether the Liquidating Trustee has been formally appointed, which would trigger the commencement of avoidance-action statutes of limitations under 11 U.S.C. § 546.

### V. SEC Enforcement Posture — Fiscal Year 2025 Data & 2026 Forward Trajectory

During fiscal year 2025, the Commission filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions and 69 "follow-on" administrative proceedings seeking to bar or suspend individuals from certain functions in the securities markets based on criminal convictions, civil injunctions, or other orders, obtaining orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.

Notable Ponzi-scheme actions from the FY 2025 enforcement cycle include: Paramount Management Group, LLC, Prestige Investment Group, LLC, and their founder Daryl F. Heller, in connection with a Ponzi scheme that allegedly defrauded approximately 2,700 investors — many of whom were retail investors — resulting in $400 million in investor losses; First Liberty Building & Loan, LLC and its owner Edwin Brant Frost IV, in connection with an alleged Ponzi scheme that defrauded approximately 300 investors of more than $140 million. Looking forward into 2026, enforcement observers expect continued emphasis on traditional fraud focusing on investor harm and renewed attention to insider trading, accounting fraud, offering fraud, and Ponzi schemes.
