The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan, represented by **Greenberg Traurig, LLP**, on **January 30, 2025**, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee. ### V. Camarda / A.G. Morgan — DOJ-SEC Parallel Action, $138M Fraud, E.D.N.Y.

On **April 3, 2026**, the SEC charged registered investment adviser **A.G. Morgan Financial Advisors, LLC** and its principals, **Vincent J. Camarda** and **James E. McArthur**, with perpetrating an offering fraud that raised at least **$138 million** from at least **431 investors**; according to the SEC's complaint, filed in the **U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York**, from approximately June 2020 through at least December 2023, defendants fraudulently induced advisory clients — many elderly and financially unsophisticated — to purchase promissory notes issued by five high-risk private equity funds that Camarda and McArthur created, managed, and owned.

The DOJ and SEC announced parallel enforcement actions on April 3, 2026; Camarda **pleaded guilty the same day** to securities fraud and investment adviser fraud and faces up to **20 years in prison** and **restitution exceeding $160 million**. The restitution figure exceeds the $138 million raised, reflecting prejudgment interest and the full scope of investor harm. The DOJ's Criminal Division announced in early 2026 that its Fraud Section had brought white collar charges against more than **200 individuals** and secured **140 criminal convictions**. The Camarda guilty plea on the same day charges were announced reflects a pre-negotiated cooperation structure — a pattern that suggests parallel civil proceedings seeking disgorgement and receiver appointment may follow in the Eastern District.

### VI. Enforcement Backdrop — SEC FY2025 Results and 2026 Posture

In connection with fiscal year 2025 enforcement actions, the Commission obtained orders for monetary relief totaling **$17.9 billion**, of which **$10.8 billion** was disgorgement of ill-gotten gains and prejudgment interest, and **$7.2 billion** in civil penalties. After excluding "deemed satisfied" amounts and the Stanford judgments, monetary relief in FY2025 totaled **$1.4 billion** in disgorgement and prejudgment interest and **$1.3 billion** in civil penalties.

Noteworthy Ponzi-related FY2025 actions included the charge against **Daryl F. Heller** (Paramount Management Group / Prestige Investment Group) for a scheme that defrauded approximately **2,700 investors** with **$400 million in investor losses**; and **Edwin Brant Frost IV** (First Liberty Building & Loan) for defrauding approximately **300 investors** of more than **$140 million**.

The SEC's enforcement data reflects a "back to basics" approach: of at least 91 new enforcement suits filed from inauguration through September 2025, nearly **33%** targeted offering fraud or insider trading. In one recent matter, the SEC alleged that an individual and affiliated companies raised more than **$770 million** from approximately **2,700 investors** in a fraudulent ATM scheme generating roughly **$400 million in investor losses**; in another, approximately **300 investors** were defrauded of at least **$140 million** in a Ponzi scheme while the alleged fraudster used millions for personal expenses.

Ponzi litigation continues to proliferate, with numerous complaints filed across dozens of federal district and state courts in Q1 2026 alone, driven both by smaller schemes and larger schemes spawning constellations of related lawsuits.
