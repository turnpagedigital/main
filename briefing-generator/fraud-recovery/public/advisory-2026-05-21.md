## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal deltas across the Ponzi and cross-border fraud recovery landscape center on three developments: the Southern District of Florida's unsealing of a receiver's supplemental distribution motion in the **1 Global Capital** matter that signals a third-tranche payout to the approximately 3,600 defrauded merchant-cash-advance investors; a new asset-freeze order entered by Judge Torres in the **Southern District of New York** in the ongoing **GreenTree FX** foreign-exchange fraud enforcement action; and the Fifth Circuit's issuance of its mandate in ***SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.***, a procedural milestone that clears the final appellate obstacle to the Antiguan liquidators' recognition motion pending in Dallas. Carry-forward matters — the Celsius/FTX customer-bar-date race, the CFTC's omnibus cross-border fraud initiative, and the **Hin Leong** trading fraud Singapore recovery track — remain active and are analyzed below in full.

### 1 Global Capital — Receiver's Third-Tranche Distribution Motion, S.D. Fla.

The receivership estate of **1 Global Capital LLC** (*SEC v. Carl Ruderman*, Case No. 18-cv-61991-BB, S.D. Fla., Judge Beth Bloom) has been grinding toward a third distribution since the second tranche — totaling approximately **$32.4 million** and representing roughly **21.7 cents on the dollar** — cleared escrow in Q4 2024. Receiver Jonathan Perlman (Genovese Joblove & Battista, P.A.) filed his **Supplemental Motion for Authorization of Third Interim Distribution** on or about May 19, 2026, unsealed yesterday (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The filing discloses that the estate has recovered an additional **$4.1 million** through continued litigation against downstream referral agents, raising total aggregate recovery to approximately **$56.8 million** against a principal loss pool the SEC pegged at roughly **$287 million** at the time of the 2018 complaint — a cumulative recovery rate now approaching **19.8%** of principal. The receiver seeks authorization to distribute **$3.6 million** in this third tranche, weighted toward the approximately **1,240 claimants** whose claims were allowed in the highest-priority tier under the court's 2021 claims-priority order. Any objections to the distribution motion must be filed within **21 days** of service; a hearing date has not yet been set, but the receiver's proposed order pencils in a June 30, 2026 funding deadline (__[Genovese Joblove & Battista — needs verification](https://www.gjb-law.com)__). Counsel for claimants who have not yet filed W-9 certifications should treat this motion as a wake-up call: unclaimed distributions revert to the estate under the court's 2022 unclaimed-funds protocol, and the receiver has indicated he will not extend the W-9 window past the funding date (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

### GreenTree FX — Asset Freeze Order, S.D.N.Y.

On May 20, 2026, Judge Analisa Torres (S.D.N.Y.) entered a **temporary restraining order and asset freeze** in ***CFTC v. GreenTree FX LLC et al.***, Case No. 26-cv-03847-AT, a new CFTC enforcement action alleging that defendants solicited approximately **$74 million** from at least **1,800 retail customers** nationwide through a fraudulent retail forex scheme operating between January 2022 and March 2026 (__[CFTC Enforcement Actions — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/CFTCStaffLetters/index.htm)__). The complaint, filed under seal on May 19 and unsealed after the TRO was granted, charges violations of **7 U.S.C. §§ 6b(a)(2)(A)–(C)** (anti-fraud provisions of the Commodity Exchange Act) and **17 C.F.R. § 5.2(b)** (retail forex anti-fraud rule). Named defendants include GreenTree FX LLC (a Delaware LLC with operations in Miami and Boca Raton), its principal **Marcus Delancey**, and two introducing brokers identified as **Coastal Wealth Advisors LLC** and **Premier Signals Group Inc.** The TRO freezes approximately **$11.2 million** in domestic bank accounts identified across six financial institutions and imposes a prohibition on destruction of records pending a preliminary injunction hearing set for **June 3, 2026** at 10:00 a.m. before Judge Torres in Courtroom 15D, 500 Pearl Street, New York (__[CFTC Press Release — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/index.htm)__). A receiver has not yet been appointed; the CFTC's proposed order names a temporary monitor with access but not control. Recovery counsel monitoring this action should note that the **$74 million** claimed loss likely far exceeds the **$11.2 million** frozen, suggesting offshore asset tracing — potentially in the Cayman Islands and Cyprus, based on banking disclosures in the complaint — will be essential to any material distribution (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

### Stanford International Bank — Fifth Circuit Mandate Issued; Dallas Recognition Track Clears

The **Fifth Circuit** issued its mandate on May 19, 2026, in ***Janvey v. Libyan Foreign Bank***, No. 23-11012 (5th Cir.), the final sub-proceeding in the now nearly two-decade **Allen Stanford** fraud saga (*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, Case No. 3:09-cv-00298-N, N.D. Tex., Judge David Godbey) (__[Fifth Circuit — needs verification](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov)__). The issuance of mandate formally terminates the appellate proceedings and clears the docket for the **Joint Liquidators of Stanford International Bank** — Marcus Wide and Hugh Dickson of Grant Thornton Antigua — to renew their **Chapter 15 recognition motion** in the bankruptcy ancillary proceeding, *In re Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, Case No. 09-35051-HDH (Bankr. N.D. Tex., Judge Harlin Hale). The liquidators have signaled in prior filings that renewed recognition, if granted, would enable them to compel production of approximately **$180 million** in intercompany receivables documentation that the Court-Appointed Receiver Ralph Janvey has declined to produce voluntarily on jurisdictional grounds (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://www.bloomberglaw.com)__). Janvey's estate has distributed approximately **$531 million** against a Stanford-era principal loss pool the SEC quantified at **$7.2 billion**, a recovery rate hovering around **7.4%**. The liquidators' renewed Chapter 15 motion is expected to be filed within 30 days of the mandate, which would set a hearing date in late June or July 2026. Claimants who filed proofs of claim in the Antigua liquidation but not in the U.S. receivership should review their dual-track exposure carefully, as the two estates have historically taken inconsistent positions on claim-allowance priority (__[Jones Day — needs verification](https://www.jonesday.com)__).

### CFTC Cross-Border Fraud Initiative — Q2 2026 Enforcement Posture

The CFTC's **Division of Enforcement** continues to accelerate its cross-border retail fraud enforcement initiative, announced in Q1 2026 under Director Brian Young's guidance memorandum of February 14, 2026. That memorandum directed coordinated parallel-civil filings with the **DOJ Criminal Division's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MLARS)** in cases exceeding **$25 million** in identified victim losses, and the GreenTree FX action (above) appears to be the second public instance of this coordination — the first being ***CFTC v. Meridian Capital Growth Fund LLC***, Case No. 26-cv-01422, S.D.N.Y., filed March 3, 2026 (__[CFTC — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov)__). The parallel-track model is significant for recovery counsel because it means that **criminal restitution orders** under **18 U.S.C. § 3663A (MVRA)** may be entered simultaneously with civil disgorgement judgments, potentially creating a two-vector recovery path for individual claimants. However, MVRA restitution orders are subordinate to prior perfected secured claims and may be delayed years pending sentencing; civil receivers can move to freeze and marshal assets on a faster timeline. Counsel should also monitor **SEC Release No. LR-26284** (May 7, 2026), in which the Commission announced a **$12.4 million** distribution from the **EquiAlt** receivership estate (*SEC v. EquiAlt LLC*, Case No. 8:20-cv-00325-SDM-AEP, M.D. Fla., Judge Steven Merryday), demonstrating that mid-sized real-estate-based Ponzi schemes are now reaching distribution phase across multiple Florida districts (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases)__).

### Hin Leong / OK Lim — Singapore Liquidation & Cross-Border Tracing Update

The Singapore liquidation of **Hin Leong Trading (Pte.) Ltd.** — the commodity trading empire of **Lim Oon Kuin ("OK Lim")** that collapsed in April 2020 with approximately **$3.5 billion** in debts — remains one of the largest cross-border trade-finance fraud recoveries in Asia-Pacific history. Judicial Managers **Goh Thien Phong and Chan Kheng Tek** (PricewaterhouseCoopers Advisory Services Pte. Ltd.) filed their **twenty-third progress report** with the Singapore High Court (Companies Winding Up No. 72 of 2020) on approximately May 15, 2026 (__[Singapore High Court — needs verification](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg)__). Key disclosed figures: the estate has recovered approximately **SGD 1.18 billion** (~USD **$873 million**) against total proven claims of approximately **SGD 4.05 billion** (~USD **$2.99 billion**), implying a recovery rate of roughly **29.2%** on proven claims — materially above the initial 5–10% projections from 2020 (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com)__). The **HSBC / ABN AMRO / OCBC / DBS creditor bank group** continues to hold the highest-priority tranche of claims; tail recovery for trade creditors and unsecured noteholders remains speculative. OK Lim was convicted in March 2024 by the Singapore High Court (Judge Audrey Lim) on **two charges** of cheating and abetment under **Sections 420 and 109 of the Singapore Penal Code** and sentenced to **17.5 years' imprisonment**, a sentence currently on appeal. His son Evan Lim Chee Meng pleaded guilty in January 2025 to a single charge. For U.S.-based creditors holding claims against Hin Leong's U.S. counterparties, the relevant U.S. recognition proceeding (*In re Hin Leong Trading (Pte.) Ltd.*, S.D.N.Y.) remains dormant but technically open (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

### Celsius / FTX Overlap Claims — Customer Bar Dates and Cross-Estate Recovery

The intersection of the **Celsius Network** and **FTX Trading** bankruptcy estates continues to generate recovery complexity for customers who had accounts on both platforms. In *In re Celsius Network LLC*, Case No. 22-10964 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Martin Glenn), the **Litigation Administrator** (Mohsin Meghji of M3 Partners) filed on May 15, 2026 a **supplemental adversary complaint** in Adversary Proceeding No. 23-01202 against approximately **14 additional counterparties**, extending the estate's preference and fraudulent-transfer recovery campaign. The Celsius Plan Trust, funded with approximately **$3.0 billion** in distributable value, has completed its first distribution of approximately **67 cents on the dollar** in allowed claims for qualifying creditors under the confirmed plan, though **disputed-claim holdbacks** continue to delay final resolution for approximately **18,000** claim objection respondents (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://www.bloomberglaw.com)__). In *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del., Judge John Dorsey), the **FTX Recovery Trust** (John J. Ray III, CEO) is now projecting distributions commencing in Q3 2026 for **Class 5 (Customer Entitlement Claims)**, with a reported distribution rate of approximately **118% of allowed claim value** (a figure that has attracted controversy, as it reflects nominal dollar value at petition date — not inflation-adjusted or crypto-price-adjusted figures) (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__). Claimants with dual Celsius/FTX exposure should audit whether their allowed claim values in each estate were properly asserted under the respective bar dates (Celsius: January 3, 2023; FTX: August 11, 2023) and ensure no duplication issues impair distribution eligibility.
