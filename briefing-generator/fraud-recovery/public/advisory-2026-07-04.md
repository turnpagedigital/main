### OneCoin / DOJ Remission — Claims Window Now Closed; Allocation Phase Begins

The Department of Justice's remission compensation process for OneCoin victims, announced in mid-April 2026, reached its statutory deadline on June 30, 2026. 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud could apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the U.S. Department of Justice.
 
OneCoin was co-founded by fugitive Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.



The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission process.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 
The DOJ filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York.
 The operative case caption is *United States v. Ignatova et al.*, S.D.N.Y. Ignatova herself remains at large. 
The FBI continues its investigative pursuit of Ignatova, listed as an FBI Top Ten Fugitive, alongside IRS-CI and the Southern District of New York.


The $40 million fund is strikingly small relative to the $4+ billion in victim losses — a ratio below 1%. That gap is structurally important for claims investors: the remission pool is a DOJ-administered forfeiture proceeding under 18 U.S.C. § 981 / 28 U.S.C. § 2461, not a civil receivership. It distributes available seized assets pro rata among eligible petitioners; there is no ongoing litigation mandate to chase additional assets on behalf of petitioners, unlike BLMIS or Stanford. Recovery here will be a single (or at most a small number of) distributions. Additional asset forfeiture from Ignatova herself — if she is ever apprehended — would flow through a separate proceeding. The Guernsey / Germany track provides some additional texture on what may yet be reachable: 
following international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany, on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The seized funds will be paid into Guernsey's seized assets fund and then returned to the German authorities for use in compensating victims in that matter.
 Those funds flow through the German proceeding, not the DOJ remission pool — parallel tracks that do not consolidate.

### BLMIS / Picard — 17th Distribution Complete; 50th Fee Application Filed; Recovery Now at $15.456 Billion

The BLMIS liquidation proceeding (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789, Judge Loretta G. Preska) remains in active post-distribution posture following the February 2026 seventeenth distribution. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, with more than $253 million being distributed on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including the seventeenth distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances; the seventeenth distribution represented 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.



Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid across the seventeen interim distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


The recovery total has since moved. 
As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 That figure reflects approximately $90 million in incremental recoveries or settlement agreements since the February 27 distribution date. The gap between $15.456 billion in recoveries and $15.38 billion distributed is the current balance being held or pending allocation — the pipeline for the eighteenth distribution, which has not yet been publicly scheduled.

The fee application track is signal-relevant for claims pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, covering work on the global liquidation of BLMIS from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025.
 
The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
None of the money recovered is used to pay administrative costs; all Trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 This is structurally distinct from a commercial receivership: SIPC absorbs the fee drag, not the customer fund. That is a meaningful structural advantage for BLMIS claim holders and distinguishes the BLMIS track sharply from most SEC-driven receiverships, where receiver and counsel fees directly erode the distribution pool.

At 72.848% recovery, the marginal value of a BLMIS claim in the secondary market reflects (a) residual upside from additional recoveries against unresolved defendants and (b) the time value of waiting for the eighteenth distribution. With no public scheduling of the eighteenth distribution and approximately $76 million in incremental recoveries above the last distribution amount, a new motion is likely in the next two to three quarters — consistent with the annual-to-biannual cadence of prior distributions.

### Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution Executing; $339 Million Rolling Schedule; Swiss Tail Being Absorbed

The Stanford Financial Group receivership (SEC v. Stanford International Bank Ltd. et al., N.D. Tex., Dallas Div., presided over by Judge David C. Godbey) has moved into terminal distribution phase. 
On March 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.


Execution commenced on schedule. 
On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan, totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 Pace of execution has accelerated since then. 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver had issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups.
 That pace — from 2,000+ to 5,600+ claimant groups in roughly one month — suggests the rolling schedule is proceeding without material friction, though total dollar amounts disbursed beyond the initial $47.5 million have not been publicly stated with precision.

The Swiss ancillary track has effectively closed. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, resulting in a transfer of $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol.
 
The JLs further informed the Receiver that no further distributions of funds are expected from the Swiss proceeding.
 The COViSAL claimant coalition had made a formal written request as of March 11, 2026, inquiring whether the $8.1 million in Swiss assets would be separately distributed or folded into the Final Distribution Plan — a question that, as of the date of that letter, remained unanswered from the Receiver. That ambiguity is worth monitoring: if those funds are not separately disclosed in the Final Distribution Schedule, they may simply be pooled into the plan's total, which would not affect per-claim economics but complicates independent verification.

For claims investors, the Stanford receivership is at end-stage. The Final Distribution Plan schedule covers the recoverable universe. No further distributions beyond those contemplated in the plan are expected. Duration risk is now principally execution risk — delays in delivering checks to Latin American jurisdictions (Venezuela logistics were disrupted between December 11, 2025, and March 11, 2026, before courier delivery resumed) rather than recovery risk.

### Power Block Coin, LLC — Bankr. D. Utah, Case No. 24-23041-CDP, Judge Cathleen D. Parker — Fee Finalization in Progress; Celsius Preference Exposure Remains Central

The Power Block Coin proceeding (formerly SmartFi) is in late-stage wind-down mode. 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker set a hearing for June 9, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. prevailing Utah time, to consider the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for Allowance of Compensation (ECF 506), by which Amplēo — financial advisor and accountant to the Debtor — requested allowance and final award of fees in the amount of $10,436.50 for the period from April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026.
 The June 9 hearing was the most recent docket entry flagged in the tracked-case ground truth (docket entry 519, filed June 9, 2026). Whether the Amplēo fee application was approved at that hearing is unconfirmed on the current record.

The structural context: 
the creditors' committee's liquidation plan, filed by Greenberg Traurig on January 30, 2025, proposes a Liquidating Trust structure for a debtor that filed for Chapter 11 on June 20, 2024, with claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 
The committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, whose unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property have drawn scrutiny.


The Celsius preference exposure remains the dominant variable. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending Celsius bankruptcy, Case No. 22-10964, in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The resolution of those claims will determine whether there is anything meaningful left for non-Celsius unsecured creditors. For claims investors, the practical question is how Celsius's allowed claim — if eventually liquidated at a fraction of face value through the Celsius plan recovery — maps onto Power Block Coin's distributable estate. The Solara loan ($17.4 million, potentially encumbered) is the estate's largest identifiable asset beyond any digital assets still in custody. Clawback risk under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and applicable Utah UFTA against pre-petition transfers to insiders remains a live lever but is unquantified on the current public record.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and fund LPs tracking the OneCoin remission should confirm whether their petitions were submitted through www.onecoinremission.com before the June 30 deadline and preserve documentation of submission; any petition filed after June 30 is presumptively barred from the current $40 million pool, though a future DOJ forfeiture proceeding keyed to additional Ignatova asset recoveries (including the Guernsey/Germany GBP 8.59 million track and any assets seized upon arrest) would open a fresh window. On BLMIS, the eighteenth distribution has not been publicly scheduled; hold positions and monitor madofftrustee.com for the filing of a new Customer Fund Allocation and Distribution Motion — the incremental recovery gap ($15.456B recovered vs. $15.38B distributed) suggests it is accumulating but has not yet crossed the Trustee's internal threshold for a new motion. On Stanford, the rolling Final Distribution Schedule is executing; if any claimant group in the schedule has not yet received payment, contact the Receiver through stanfordfinancialreceivership.com immediately, as execution is ongoing and the receiver has flagged a 90-day check-cashing window that runs from issuance. On Power Block Coin (Bankr. D. Utah 24-23041-CDP), the docket is in final professional fee stage; watch for plan confirmation hearings and Liquidating Trustee appointment, which will set the formal distribution timeline and clarify whether clawback actions under 11 U.S.C. § 548 against the Solara loan and other insider transactions will be pursued before distributions are made.
