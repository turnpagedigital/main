### Stanford Financial Group Receivership — Final Distribution: Check-Cashing Deadlines This Week

The Stanford receivership (SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd., No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Dallas Div., Receiver Ralph Janvey) has reached what may be its most administratively consequential week for individual claimants. 
Checks dated April 24, 2026 must be cashed by July 23, 2026; checks dated April 27, 2026 must be cashed by July 26, 2026.
 
Checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline.
 This is not a routine administrative notice — uncashed checks represent a permanent forfeiture of distribution proceeds, with no right of reissuance after the deadline lapses.


On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 The substance of that filing has not been publicly summarized as of this run, but its timing — one week before the check-cashing deadlines — suggests it addresses payment progress and potentially unresolved claimant contact issues.


Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 The scale of the distribution underscores why the check-cashing deadline carries systemic significance: even a small percentage of uncashed instruments represents millions of dollars reverting to the receivership estate rather than claimants.

A parallel fee dispute is working through the Fifth Circuit. 
The Court entered an order on October 8, 2025 approving the Receiver's proposed final distribution and awarding the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed that Order to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The Receiver and certain professional firms filed their brief on appeal on March 23, 2026.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the Court's Order on May 23, 2026.
 The Fifth Circuit appeal (fee reduction from $43M to $14.9M) is a significant pending event: if the appellate panel restores any portion of the requested fees, those amounts come off the distributable estate. For claim buyers who have priced positions assuming the $14.9M figure, a reversal would be adverse.

A separate unresolved item flagged by the COViSAL claimant group involves approximately $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026, asking whether and when the $8.1 million in Swiss funds will be distributed; as of the date of that filing, the Receiver had not responded.
 Whether those funds are reflected in the $339M distribution schedule or held pending further proceedings is unverified.

**Claims-buyer takeaway:** For positions in Stanford claims, the July 23–26 check-cashing window is the immediate operational deadline. Any claim acquired from a holder who hasn't cashed — or who has moved and may not have received the check — is a live risk. Verify check receipt and negotiation status with counterparties immediately.

### BLMIS / Madoff — 17th Distribution Settled; 50th Fee Application Filed; Next Distribution Timeline Open

The BLMIS liquidation (SIPA proceeding, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, presided over by Judge Liability B. Bernstein) is in post-distribution-17 steady state. 
The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced February 27, 2026, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The seventeenth distribution represented 1.302% of each allowed claim and, when combined with the prior sixteen distributions, brought the aggregate paid percentage to approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim.



As of June 26, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
As of the same date, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation in the form of cash advances of up to $500,000 per allowed claim.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The fee dimension warrants attention for claim pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, covering work performed from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025.
 
The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest-discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 At that rate, the four-month period generated approximately $37.6 million in fee requests — a figure that, across 50 applications, reflects the cumulative fee drag on the estate. Because SIPC funds all administrative costs rather than the Customer Fund, fees do not directly reduce claimant recoveries in BLMIS; this is structurally distinct from a typical receivership.

The Ponzi-presumption doctrine that underpins all BLMIS clawback litigation — established through years of adversary proceedings before the same court — continues to apply under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) and the "net equity" methodology. Net winners who received fictitious profits above their principal remain exposed to avoidance actions; the trustee's global settlements have resolved the largest of these. The remaining litigation pool is smaller but still active.

No 18th distribution motion has been filed as of this run. Historically, distributions have been filed in Q4 of a given year for Q1 commencement the following year. The absence of a motion through mid-July 2026 is consistent with that cadence; claimants should not expect an 18th distribution announcement before Q4 2026 at the earliest.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Remission Process Closed; Guernsey Forfeiture Confirmed; Asset Hunt Continues

The DOJ remission process for OneCoin victims — 
which the Department of Justice announced in April 2026 as the beginning of the compensation process for victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd., orchestrated by Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood between 2014 and 2019
 — has now passed its June 30, 2026 petition deadline. 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator; the FBI and IRS-CI conducted the criminal fraud investigation.
 
The DOJ launched the remission process with over $40 million in forfeited assets available for distribution.
 Against a scheme that 
resulted in victims investing over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency
, the initial $40 million pool represents recovery of approximately 1% of claimed losses — a figure that underscores how early this recovery arc remains.

The most significant recent primary-source development on asset recovery predates the remission window but materially advances the cross-border tracing picture. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
Ignatova used these criminal proceeds to purchase two luxury Kensington properties and relied on professional enablers across Europe to launder funds, acquire the real estate, and register the properties in Guernsey trusts — structures deliberately chosen to obscure her involvement.
 
The total forfeited from Guernsey-registered property structures in early 2026 was £8,590,200.92; those funds will be returned to Germany to compensate global victims.


The mechanism used is analytically important: 
German authorities initiated "independent confiscation proceedings," which led to the overseas forfeiture order — a non-conviction-based asset forfeiture route taken because criminal proceedings against Ignatova could not conclude given her unknown whereabouts.
 This is the civil NCBAF model (analogous in structure to the UK's Proceeds of Crime Act Part 5 and § 423 of the Insolvency Act 1986 for cross-border enforcement). It signals that multi-jurisdictional recovery can proceed without a conviction — a posture that should encourage victim groups and claim investors tracking the broader asset base.


The U.S. Department of State reward for information leading to Ignatova's arrest currently stands at up to $5 million under the Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program.
 The prosecutions in the S.D.N.Y. continue for associated defendants. 
The DOJ filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York; several key figures have been sentenced, and the Department pursued criminal forfeiture of property derived from proceeds of the scheme.


For claims investors: the $40M DOJ pool is materially inadequate relative to aggregate losses. The Guernsey £8.5M recovery and the ongoing European NCBAF proceedings represent the next tranche of recoverable assets — but each forfeiture requires a separate multi-year mutual legal assistance cycle before funds reach victims. Petition filing by June 30, 2026 was a threshold eligibility event; any claimant who missed that deadline is unverified as to remediation options.

### Power Block Coin (SmartFi) — Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041 — Late Wind-Down, Fee Applications Closing Out

Power Block Coin, LLC (operating under the name SmartFi) remains in active Chapter 11 before Judge Cathleen D. Parker, Bankr. D. Utah, Case No. 24-23041. 
Power Block Coin filed for bankruptcy on June 20, 2024; the plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.



Greenberg Traurig, LLP filed the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors' liquidation plan on January 30, 2025, outlining a strategy to maximize value through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
A key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors.


The case is now in professional-fee-closeout posture. 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker set a hearing on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for allowance of compensation (ECF 506) for June 9, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. prevailing Utah time.
 
By the Amplēo Fee Application, Amplēo — financial advisor and accountant to the Debtor — requested final compensation of $10,436.50 for the period April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026, plus fees previously allowed on an interim basis.
 Final fee applications from debtor's professionals are a reliable signal that administration is closing toward plan confirmation or trust establishment.

The largest structural overhang on creditor recovery remains the Celsius preference-claim exposure. 
Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy (Case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.).
 
The committee's filing raised concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC — an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah — about which concerns were raised regarding its unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property.


The most recent tracked docket entry is ECF 531, dated July 6, 2026, with no description available in the ground-truth record. Zero new filings in the 72-hour window ending July 13. The case is not dormant — the July 6 entry confirms recent activity — but the nature of that filing is unspecified. Claim buyers in this case should treat the Solara Communities loan ($17.4M) and the Celsius preference exposure ($133M) as unresolved variables that materially affect ultimate recovery percentages for general unsecured creditors.
