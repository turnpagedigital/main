### I. BLMIS / Madoff — Picard SIPA Trustee (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 08-01789)

The seventeenth distribution is now closed. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced commencement of the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and, combined with the prior sixteen distributions, has now satisfied approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim.


The cumulative recovery picture is stronger than any comparable Ponzi estate in U.S. history. 
As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation in advances, capped at $500,000 per allowed claim.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


For claims-buyers and net losers pricing positions: the gap between 72.848% recovered and 100% is the residual theoretical upside, offset by the time value of continued litigation. 
As of January 31, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has amassed approximately $15.366 billion as a result of recoveries and settlement agreements.
 The gap between $15.366 billion amassed and $15.456 billion in agreements-plus-recoveries as of June 26 implies ongoing but modest settlement accretion. The admitted claims pool is approximately $20.3 billion at net equity, meaning roughly $5 billion in principal remains outstanding even if every recovery agreement is monetized at face. 
The Trustee continues to move forward to resolve litigation and appeals delaying further distributions.
 The 50th fee application — filed in February 2026 — is a recurring signal for claims-pricing models; the fee waterfall is entirely SIPC-borne per the statutory framework, so fee orders do not erode the Customer Fund directly.

The next distribution motion has not yet appeared on the docket as of this writing. The seventeen distributions have been patterned approximately annually since 2011, typically launched in February. If that cadence holds, the eighteenth motion would be expected in late 2026 or early 2027. Claims still subject to litigation — primarily offshore feeder-fund adversary proceedings — remain the structural constraint on distribution acceleration.

### II. Stanford Financial Receivership (N.D. Tex. 3:09-CV-0298-N, Judge David Godbey) — Check-Void Deadline This Week

This is the most operationally urgent matter in the portfolio today. 
Claimants who received Stanford Final Distribution checks dated April 24, 2026, must cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026, must cash those checks by July 26, 2026.
 
The Receiver recently contacted certain claimants whose checks remain uncashed — those checks will become void and the funds will revert to the Receivership if not cashed timely.
 Funds reverting to the estate on uncashed checks are a net transfer away from victim claimants and into the general estate. Any claims-buyer holding positions linked to claimants with uncashed checks should treat this as a direct recovery risk requiring immediate client outreach.


On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division.
 The July status report is the most recent public accounting of distribution progress; its content was not publicly reproduced in detail, but the filing confirms the Receiver's active reporting posture to Judge Godbey through the close-out phase.

The macro distribution picture: 
payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups, to be issued on a rolling basis.
 
The Schedule was filed March 9, 2026, pursuant to the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.


The Receiver's fee appeal remains live before the Fifth Circuit and is now fully briefed. 
The Court's October 8, 2025 order approved approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The Receiver filed his appeal brief on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting filed a separate supporting brief.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 
The Examiner filed his amicus brief in support of the court's order on June 9, 2026.
 
The Receiver and his professionals filed their reply brief on June 12, 2026, making the appeal fully briefed.
 
It is not yet certain whether the Fifth Circuit will schedule oral argument, nor when.


The fee appeal is significant for the claims-pricing calculus: if the Fifth Circuit partially reverses and awards fees above the $14.9 million threshold, that excess comes from the estate — not from SIPC as in BLMIS — and directly compresses distributable funds. The delta between $14.9 million awarded and $43 million sought is the upper-bound exposure.

Cross-border dimension: 
on December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss ancillary bankruptcy proceeding for Stanford International Bank has concluded, with $8.1 million in Swiss funds transferred to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol; no further distributions from Switzerland are expected.
 The $8.1 million Swiss tranche closes out the last major offshore pipeline into the U.S. receivership.

### III. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Restitution Process + Guernsey Forfeiture

The OneCoin asset-recovery track moved on two fronts in early 2026. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, found in German legal proceedings to be proceeds of Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies; Ignatova had used the funds to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 The Guernsey action is an example of non-conviction-based asset forfeiture operationalized through German-Guernsey mutual legal assistance — the structure most likely to be replicated in other offshore jurisdictions where OneCoin proceeds were parked.


In April 2026, the DOJ announced a formal claim process for defrauded OneCoin investors, funded by a $40 million pool of seized assets; officials stated they are committed to identifying and seizing further criminal proceeds to expand the compensation pool.
 The $40 million pool against an estimated $4 billion in investor losses implies a recovery rate of roughly 1% at the current fund level — deep in speculative territory for claims buyers, though the DOJ's commitment to ongoing seizure activity suggests upward revision is possible.


The U.S. Department of State's Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program offers up to $5 million for information leading to Ignatova's arrest.
 Ignatova remains a fugitive; her whereabouts are unverified as of this writing. 
Karl Sebastian Greenwood, the co-founder of OneCoin, was sentenced to 20 years in prison for his role in orchestrating the fraud.
 Greenwood's cooperation provides a documentary foundation for tracing further asset flows, which may expand the seizure pool beyond the current $40 million.


Authorities are using advanced forensic accounting and blockchain analysis to track where fiat currency and any converted crypto assets moved after Ignatova vanished.
 The blockchain-tracing component is particularly relevant: if OneCoin proceeds were converted into BTC or stablecoins, those wallets may be seizable without an extradition predicate.

### IV. SEC Enforcement Landscape — Retail Fraud Priority + Mining Automatic (July 2026)

Fresh enforcement activity confirms the Atkins-era SEC's "back to basics" posture is yielding new Ponzi-adjacent charges directly relevant to this desk. The SEC filed charges this week against Mining Automatic and its principal, Zan Shaikh, operating as Bright Vision Distribution LLC (Massachusetts). 
The SEC contends the defendants orchestrated a deceptive crypto mining venture that collected $22 million from over 380 investors.
 
The company raised funds from June 2023 to May 2025 but spent only 13% on actual mining operations; the structure displayed hallmarks of a Ponzi scheme, with returns to earlier investors financed through capital from later investors.
 
By March 2025, Mining Automatic ceased payments entirely; not a single investor has received their initial capital back, with more than $20 million in principal outstanding.


On the structural enforcement side: 
on March 11, 2026, SEC Chairman Paul Atkins and CFTC Chairman Michael Selig signed a Memorandum of Understanding on regulatory harmonization, creating a Joint Harmonization Initiative and committing both agencies to coordinate examinations, surveillance, and enforcement — including conferring on potential charges before bringing parallel actions.
 
The SEC reported it filed 456 enforcement actions in FY 2025, including 303 standalone actions and 69 follow-on administrative proceedings, with monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.
 
Forward enforcement priorities explicitly include retail fraud — including Ponzi schemes — as a named category alongside insider trading, market manipulation, and AML/KYC willful violations.


The MOU matters for claimants and receivers: coordinated SEC-CFTC parallel actions increase the probability that criminal DOJ proceedings follow quickly, which in turn accelerates asset-freeze and forfeiture mechanics under 18 U.S.C. § 981 and § 1963.
