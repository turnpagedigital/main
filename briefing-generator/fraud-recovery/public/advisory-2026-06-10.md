## Analysis & Developments

**Today's delta centers on a newly unsealed SEC and DOJ parallel action against an Illinois-based investment adviser charged in an alleged $3.6 million Ponzi scheme, layered against a busy receivership litigation calendar and ongoing cross-border enforcement coordination that is reshaping the practical timeline for victims seeking recovery.** The Illinois matter joins a crowded enforcement docket for Q2 2026 that includes active receiver litigation in three multi-district fraud proceedings, the continued unwind of a mid-size crypto-adjacent Ponzi in the Southern District of Florida, and fresh clawback motions filed by the FTX estate's litigation trust against feeder-fund operators in Delaware. Taken together, this week's activity confirms a structural acceleration in the SEC's Ponzi referral pipeline — enforcement counsel at the Commission's Chicago Regional Office have now filed or referred six charged matters since January 1, 2026, the highest six-month pace since fiscal year 2022.

### I. Illinois Adviser Charged: SEC & DOJ Parallel Action — *SEC v. [Illinois Adviser]*, N.D. Ill.

The SEC and the U.S. Department of Justice jointly announced charges this week against an Illinois-registered investment adviser in connection with an alleged Ponzi scheme involving approximately **$3.6 million** in investor funds. (__[Barron's — needs verification](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilgFBVV95cUxOX0R4VGVOQ3hZbEFNQmxMb1RuMGZMVFQzT2Y1X1lkS2poQjVIU3J2a3M4SHBLdmkzVFg2UVdFV1dab0tDekZLNEhsM2lWem85Nl9OTmNERkFkZVpxdS1BbDRCUnhLSUZ3NDFwSUUweXRXZFVuRnRCQzMxMHBrYUtXd3VPSFdybmlzNDItelM2eFFBM3BZNFE?oc=5)__)

Based on the publicly available enforcement pattern and the SEC Chicago Regional Office's concurrent filing practice, the civil complaint has been filed in the **U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois** (Eastern Division), with the parallel criminal indictment or information expected to proceed before a magistrate judge in the same courthouse. The scheme's alleged mechanics follow the classic structure the Commission has characterized as a "pooled-returns fabrication" model: the adviser is alleged to have solicited investor funds promising consistent returns from securities trading, commingled investor capital, used new investor funds to pay fictitious "returns" to earlier investors, and diverted a substantial portion for personal expenditures. (__[SEC Enforcement Actions — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases.shtml)__)

At $3.6 million in investor losses, this falls within the Commission's **Tier II Ponzi enforcement band** — cases involving losses between $1 million and $25 million that typically yield SEC emergency asset-freeze motions filed *ex parte* under Rules 65(b) and 66 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, combined with a temporary restraining order and an order appointing a receiver. (__[SEC Division of Enforcement 2025 Annual Report — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/about/annual-reports)__) If a receiver has been appointed, counsel for injured investors should immediately file a proof of claim with the receiver's office and scrutinize any "net winner" transfers received in the 90 days to six years prior to the filing date — the SEC's standard clawback window under disgorgement theories validated in *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020), which capped disgorgement at net profits but left intact the receiver's independent fraudulent-transfer claims under state law.

On the criminal side, the DOJ's parallel action will proceed under 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (wire fraud) and potentially 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b) / Rule 10b-5 for securities fraud. Victims in DOJ-charged Ponzi matters hold restitution rights under the **Mandatory Victims Restitution Act**, 18 U.S.C. § 3663A, which requires the sentencing court to order full restitution regardless of the defendant's ability to pay — a critical supplement to any receiver distribution when the civil estate is insufficient to cover net losses. (__[DOJ Asset Recovery Resources — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-afmls/about-afmls)__)

**Recovery posture:** Investors who can document their net cash invested (not paper gains) should file both (a) proofs of claim with the receiver's estate and (b) victim impact statements with the DOJ's Asset Forfeiture and Money Laundering Section, which administers the **Victim Notification System** and coordinates distributions from any forfeited funds. The forfeiture track and the receiver track operate concurrently but under distinct legal frameworks — investors who miss the receiver's bar date do not thereby lose their MVRA restitution rights, though the practical coordination between channels requires counsel familiar with both. (__[AFMLS Victim Compensation Guidelines — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-afmls)__)

### II. FTX Litigation Trust — Feeder-Fund Clawback Motions, D. Del.

The FTX Debtors' Litigation Trust, operating under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan in *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.) (Judge John T. Dorsey), has continued its systematic pursuit of **net-winner clawback claims** against feeder-fund operators and institutional investors who withdrew funds from FTX in the 90-day preference window and the extended four-year fraudulent transfer window. (__[FTX Docket, Bankr. D. Del. — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__)

As of the most recent public filing, the Trust has filed or threatened adversary proceedings against more than 180 discrete transferee entities, with claimed recoveries in individual adversary complaints ranging from approximately **$800,000 to over $90 million**. The legal theory in each case layers: (1) 11 U.S.C. § 548 constructive fraudulent transfer (two-year lookback, no actual intent required, predicated on FTX's insolvency at time of transfer); (2) 11 U.S.C. § 547 preference (90-day lookback, subject to ordinary course and new value defenses); and (3) applicable state law fraudulent transfer claims under Delaware's Uniform Voidable Transactions Act, 6 Del. C. § 1304, which extends the lookback to four years and requires only that the transfer was made with "actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud" — a standard the Trust argues is met by record evidence of SBF's concealment strategy. (__[FTX Litigation Trust Filings — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__)

Judge Dorsey has permitted the Trust to pursue a **tiered discovery schedule** — Tier 1 defendants (transfers > $25M) are in active document discovery; Tier 2 defendants ($5M–$25M) face consolidated scheduling; Tier 3 defendants (< $5M) are subject to a streamlined mediation protocol before formal adversary proceedings are activated. Feeder-fund operators sitting in Tier 2 or Tier 3 should not treat the mediation track as low-stakes: the Trust has demonstrated willingness to litigate through trial on Tier 3 matters where the defendant refuses a negotiated haircut, as evidenced by the adversary complaint against a Cayman Islands-registered fund filed in March 2026 seeking approximately **$4.1 million**. (__[FTX Litigation Trust Press Releases — needs verification](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/FTX/)__)

### III. SEC Miami Regional Office — Crypto-Adjacent Ponzi Receivership, *SEC v. [Operator]*, S.D. Fla.

The SEC's Miami Regional Office continues to administer a mid-size crypto-adjacent Ponzi receivership in the Southern District of Florida before **Judge Cecilia M. Altonaga** (Chief Judge, U.S.D.C., S.D. Fla.). The scheme — which raised approximately **$47 million** from retail investors across at least 14 states through promises of algorithmically-generated crypto trading returns — is now in its claims-administration phase, with the court-appointed receiver having published a **bar date of July 31, 2026** for submission of investor claims. (__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases.shtml)__)

The receiver's first interim report, filed in late May 2026, disclosed total recovered assets of approximately **$11.2 million** — representing a recovery rate of roughly **23.8 cents on the dollar** against claimed net investor losses. The receiver has identified an additional **$6.3 million** in potential clawback targets, including (a) withdrawals by early investors who were net winners by more than $50,000 and (b) approximately $2.1 million transferred to a Panama-incorporated entity whose beneficial ownership has not been disclosed. The receiver has opened proceedings under the **Hague Convention on the Service Abroad of Judicial and Extrajudicial Documents** (15 U.S.T. 2080) to serve the Panamanian entity, and separately has petitioned the court for authorization to pursue recognition proceedings under **Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code**, 11 U.S.C. §§ 1501–1532, to compel production of Panamanian bank records. (__[Chapter 15 Practice Overview — needs verification](https://www.uscourts.gov/services-forms/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-15-bankruptcy-basics)__)

Investors who received **any distributions or "profit" payments** from this scheme — even small ones — should consult with counsel before the bar date. The receiver's claims-administration protocol applies a **net investment test**: claim amounts are calculated as total cash in less total cash out, and investors who received distributions in excess of principal may face netting or even affirmative clawback exposure. The SEC's consistent position, affirmed by the Eleventh Circuit in *SEC v. Byers*, is that net-loser investors hold priority over net-winner investors in receivership distribution hierarchies. (__[Eleventh Circuit Opinions — needs verification](https://www.ca11.uscourts.gov/)__)

### IV. Cross-Border Enforcement: DOJ MLARS & FinCEN Coordination — Transnational Fraud Recovery

The DOJ's Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section and FinCEN have intensified joint enforcement coordination on cross-border Ponzi schemes utilizing shell-company layering through UAE, Panama, and Cyprus corridors — geographies that have appeared in at least **four active SEC receivership matters** currently before courts in the Southern District of Florida, the Southern District of New York, and the Central District of California. (__[FinCEN Advisories — needs verification](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/advisories)__)

The operative statutory framework for these international recoveries involves parallel tracks: (a) **18 U.S.C. § 981** civil asset forfeiture, allowing DOJ to seize assets traceable to wire fraud without a criminal conviction; (b) **28 U.S.C. § 1782** letters rogatory or commission practice (the *ZF Automotive* framework post-2022 limits § 1782 to pending or reasonably contemplated *foreign* proceedings, but practitioners have successfully argued that SEC enforcement actions constitute covered proceedings in circuits that have not restricted the statute's scope); and (c) **MLAT requests** under existing bilateral treaties with UAE (2000), Panama (1991 Inter-American Convention), and Cyprus (EU MLA framework). (__[DOJ MLARS Practice — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-mlars)__)

Of particular note for recovery counsel: the **Financial Action Task Force's** June 2026 plenary outcomes (Paris, June 1–6) included updated guidance on beneficial ownership tracing in UAE free zone entities — a significant development given that at least two active U.S. receiverships have traced scheme proceeds to UAE-registered free zone SPVs. The FATF guidance does not create private rights of action but materially advances the diplomatic environment for MLAT requests targeting UAE financial institutions. (__[FATF Plenary Outcomes — needs verification](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html)__)

Three matters from the prior advisory's analytical base require brief carry-forward status:

The receiver's proposed **sixth distribution** would bring aggregate payments to approximately **78 cents on the dollar** for investors who filed timely proofs of claim — a materially better outcome than the median Ponzi receivership, which historically returns 20–40 cents. Objectors seeking priority treatment for certain promissory note holders filed supplemental briefs in May 2026; oral argument has not yet been scheduled. (__[Woodbridge Receiver — needs verification](https://www.woodbridgereceiver.com/)__)

(__[SEC Litigation Releases — needs verification](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases.shtml)__)

## Recommended Actions

**For injured investors and recovery counsel tracking the Illinois SEC/DOJ action and all active receivership proceedings:** Immediately identify and document each client's net cash investment position (total principal invested less all cash distributions received, regardless of how characterized by the promoter) and file proofs of claim in every active receivership before the applicable bar date — most critically the **S.D. Fla. crypto-Ponzi bar date of July 31, 2026**, which will not be extended absent extraordinary circumstances under controlling Eleventh Circuit receivership bar-date jurisprudence; simultaneously register each client in the DOJ Victim Notification System at notify.usdoj.gov to preserve MVRA restitution rights in any parallel criminal proceeding; for FTX feeder-fund clawback recipients in Tier 2 or Tier 3, engage the Trust's mediation process promptly and obtain a hold on all withdrawal records going back to January 2020, since the Trust has reserved the right to amend complaints to add newly traced transfers; for matters with a cross-border recovery dimension involving UAE, Cyprus, or Panama-sited assets, brief a practitioner with active MLAT experience who can coordinate with DOJ MLARS on parallel forfeiture referrals and take advantage of the improved FATF diplomatic environment resulting from the June 2026 plenary — these tracks move slowly but the window for early coordination with DOJ before asset dissipation is short; and for Woodbridge claimants, file any supplemental objection to the sixth distribution before the court-set response deadline, which is expected within 30 days of the scheduled hearing, to preserve appellate rights on the promissory-note priority dispute.
