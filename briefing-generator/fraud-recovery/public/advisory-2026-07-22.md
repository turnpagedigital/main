### I. Stanford Receivership (N.D. Tex. No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, Judge David C. Godbey) — Fee Appeal Now Fully Briefed; Check Deadline Urgent

The Stanford receivership has entered what Receiver Ralph Janvey characterizes as the closing phase of a 17-year administration, but two active disputes continue to cloud the distribution timeline.

**Fee Appeal — Fifth Circuit.** 
The N.D. Tex. entered an order on October 8, 2025 approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and, among other matters, awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought. The Receiver and certain professional firms appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The Receiver's opening brief was filed March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting filed a separate supporting brief.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 
The Receiver's reply brief was filed June 12, 2026, and the appeal is now fully briefed.
 
It is not yet certain whether the Fifth Circuit will schedule oral argument, nor when such argument might be scheduled.


Claims-pricing implication: if the Fifth Circuit reverses and awards the full $43 million sought, the delta — roughly $28 million — comes off recoveries available for distribution. At current pool sizes, the impact per claimant is modest but nonzero, and the appeal's resolution will be a necessary precondition to any final accounting.

**Check Expiration — Immediate.** 
Claimants who received Final Distribution checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash those checks by July 23, 2026 — tomorrow.
 
Claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash those checks by July 26, 2026. Checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline.
 Recovery counsel with Venezuelan or other hard-to-reach claimants should note the operational risk here — 
COViSAL raised a parallel concern in March 2026, flagging $8.1 million in Swiss funds released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025 and requesting clarification on whether those funds will be included in the Final Distribution. The Receiver had not responded publicly as of the COViSAL inquiry date.


**Final Distribution — Scale.** 
The Final Distribution Schedule, filed with the N.D. Tex. on March 9, 2026, lists payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claim groups.
 
On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan to Judge Godbey.
 The content of that July 9 status report is not yet publicly available in detail; claimants and counsel should pull it from the PACER docket at No. 3:09-CV-0298-N.

### II. BLMIS / Madoff (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789-LGB, Judge Loretta G. Beaumont) — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Eighteenth Motion Not Yet Filed


On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund had commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including the seventeenth distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.



As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
As of January 31, 2026, and since his appointment in December 2008, the Trustee had amassed approximately $15.366 billion as a result of recoveries and settlement agreements.
 No eighteenth distribution motion appears on the public docket as of this run. The pace of incremental distributions — $253 million in the seventeenth versus $35.8 million in the sixteenth — reflects ongoing litigation settlements flowing into the Customer Fund rather than a predictable quarterly cadence. Claims traders should not underwrite a fixed distribution schedule.


The February 13, 2026 statement from Picard's office references the Trustee's Fiftieth Fee Application.
 As a structural matter, all Trustee, legal, and accounting fees are borne by SIPC rather than the Customer Fund — 
subject to court approval, 100 percent of the Trustee's recoveries are allocated to the Customer Fund; none of the recovered money is used to pay administrative costs, with all Trustee, legal, and accounting fees paid by SIPC.
 That structural protection remains intact and is a meaningful differentiator from receiver-managed cases where fee orders directly erode the distribution pool.

### III. OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova (S.D.N.Y. Nos. 17 Cr. 556 (ER), 17 Cr. 630 (ER), 18 Cr. 746 (KPF), 18 Cr. 793 (ER)) — DOJ Remission Program Filing Deadline Closed; Kroll Processing Phase Begins


The U.S. Department of Justice opened a compensation process for victims of the OneCoin crypto Ponzi scheme, with victims able to apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets.
 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 
The filing deadline was June 30, 2026.
 That deadline has now passed, and the case moves into Kroll's administrative processing phase.


Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator; the FBI and IRS-CI conducted the criminal fraud investigation; and DOJ's Office of International Affairs provided significant assistance.
 
Eligible victims include those who purchased OneCoin or invested in the system between 2014 and 2019 and suffered a net loss; the program has international scope, with non-U.S. investors including those in Europe eligible if they meet DOJ criteria.


The recovery arithmetic is stark: $40 million against $4 billion in alleged investor losses represents a 1% recovery rate before Kroll's administrative costs are accounted for. The structural lesson for claims buyers is that DOJ remission programs — unlike SIPA trusteeships or equity receiverships — pay pro rata from whatever the government has seized to date, with no ongoing litigation mandate. 
The DOJ said it will keep seizing assets tied to the $4 billion crypto scam
, but there is no appointed trustee pursuing additional recoveries in parallel with the remission process. The fugitive principal, Ignatova, remains at large.

**Cross-border asset recovery.** 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order — the product of international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany — forfeiting funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92 found in German proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies; Ignatova had commissioned the purchase of shares in both companies to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 Whether those Guernsey proceeds will flow into the DOJ remission pool or remain in UK/German sovereign accounts has not been publicly resolved; counsel relying on the $40 million figure should treat it as a floor, not a ceiling, pending clarification.

### IV. SEC Enforcement Direction Under Woodcock — Ponzi Pipeline Signal

For claimants and receivership counsel, the composition of the SEC's enforcement pipeline is a leading indicator: new enforcement actions filed today become receivership estates distributing in 3-7 years. 
On April 8, 2026, the SEC announced the appointment of David Woodcock as Director of the Division of Enforcement; Woodcock joined from Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP, where he served as a partner and chair of the firm's securities enforcement practice group.



The enforcement results for FY 2025 reflect the Commission's prioritization of actions involving alleged fraud and investor harm, with the Commission filing 456 enforcement actions in FY 2025 — a significant decline from the 583 actions brought in FY 2024.
 Volume is down, but the target selection is relevant: 
notable 2025 actions included charges in connection with several large-scale Ponzi schemes, including one involving approximately 2,700 investors and an alleged $400 million in losses, and another involving approximately 300 investors and more than $140 million in alleged losses.



In his first public remarks at the MFA Legal & Compliance 2026 Conference, Director Woodcock outlined an enforcement agenda focused on high-impact cases involving investor harm, market integrity, and core fraud priorities, with specific areas of focus including Offering Fraud, Accounting and Disclosure Fraud, Market Manipulation, Insider Trading, Private Funds, and Cross-Border Fraud.
 "Cross-Border Fraud" as an explicit priority is the most operationally significant item for recovery counsel — it signals continued resource allocation toward the offshore-asset-tracing work (§ 1782 applications, MLATs, Mareva injunctions) that ultimately determines distribution recovery rates in crypto and multi-jurisdictional cases.
