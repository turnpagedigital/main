### Stanford Financial Receivership — N.D. Tex. · July 9 Status Report Is the Operative Filing


On July 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey submitted to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, a Status Report regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 This filing is the primary document to track this week; the Receiver's website links to the full report but has not yet published a headline summary of how many additional claimant groups have been paid since the April 3 commencement.

The distribution mechanics that set the context: 
on April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 The gap between the $47.5 million April opening tranche and the $339 million scheduled total represents the distribution that remains to be processed, and the July 9 status report is the court's first structured look at pace. Claim buyers and fund LPs holding Stanford CD claims should parse this filing carefully — payment velocity determines effective yield on any secondary purchase.

The cross-border asset thread continues to complicate the picture. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and transferred $8.1 million in Swiss funds to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol; the JLs further noted that no further distributions of funds are expected from that channel.
 Whether those $8.1 million have been incorporated into the Final Distribution Plan or will be administered separately remains an open question — one that claimant advocacy group COViSAL formally raised with the Receiver in March 2026 and has not received a written answer on. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026 titled "SECOND REQUEST: Status of $8.1 Million Swiss Funds," asking whether and when the $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025 will be distributed and whether those funds will be included in a future distribution separate from the current Final Distribution Plan; as of the filing date the Receiver and his legal team had not responded to that request.


Fee appeal: the Receiver's professionals are simultaneously litigating a fee appeal in the Fifth Circuit. 
The Court entered an order on October 8, 2025, approving the Receiver's proposed final distribution and addressing other matters, including awarding the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought; the Receiver and certain of his professional firms then appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The Receiver and his professional firms filed their opening brief on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting filed a separate brief in support; the SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 This appeal is a signal event for claims pricing: if the Fifth Circuit restores any portion of the fee cut, the distributable pool shrinks correspondingly. The delta between $14.9 million awarded and $43 million sought — a $28 million gap — is material against a $339 million scheduled distribution.

### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Eighteenth Cycle Not Yet Announced

The seventeenth interim distribution, which commenced February 27, 2026, is the controlling benchmark for BLMIS claims pricing. 
Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee for the liquidation of Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC (BLMIS) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789), announced on February 27, 2026, that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced; the Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis, bringing the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers to nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.



The seventeenth interim distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will now have been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.
 For secondary claims trading purposes, the 72.848% cumulative figure is the floor recovery-to-date; all subsequent distributions come from the residual pool.

The Trustee's gross recovery position as of late June 2026: 
as of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation, with SIPC-committed advances continuing to increase as claims in litigation are allowed through settlements or adjudication.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The fee picture merits attention in its own right. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for work performed from August 1 through November 30, 2025; the application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 10% public interest discount notwithstanding, at 63,774 hours over four months the run-rate cost of maintaining this liquidation estate remains substantial. No eighteenth distribution motion has been publicly announced as of today; the historical cadence suggests the next motion will file in late 2026 or early 2027.

Clawback posture: Picard's § 548 and state UFTA/UVTA avoidance actions against net winners remain in varying stages. The Ponzi-presumption doctrine — establishing constructive fraud without proof of actual fraud under § 548(a)(1)(B) by treating every BLMIS payment as made while insolvent — continues to govern, and the Second Circuit's net-equity framework (cash in / cash out, no fictitious profits) controls allowed claim amounts. Net winners holding unresolved settlement demands should factor residual clawback exposure into any secondary transaction in BLMIS claims.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — Cross-Border Asset Forfeiture Proceeds; DOJ Restitution Fund Active


In April 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice opened a formal claim process for defrauded OneCoin investors to seek restitution from a $40 million fund consisting of seized assets; officials stated they are committed to identifying and seizing further criminal proceeds to expand the compensation pool, though the $40 million represents only a fraction of the total estimated losses.
 At an estimated $4 billion in aggregate investor losses globally, the current restitution pool is roughly 1 cent on the dollar — a meaningful data point for secondary claim pricing.

The most recent cross-border forfeiture: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct; the money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies through which Ignatova commissioned the purchase of shares to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 The enforcement mechanism here is instructive: Germany initiated Independent Confiscation Proceedings against the assets — not Ignatova personally, since her whereabouts remain unknown — and Guernsey registered and enforced the resulting order under its domestic framework. This is the cross-border § 1782 / MLAT-adjacent playbook executed through civil forfeiture channels rather than mutual legal assistance treaty, and it demonstrates that asset recovery against Ignatova's estate continues to progress even in the absence of criminal jurisdiction over the principal.


Ignatova remains one of the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives and is best known as the founder of the fraudulent OneCoin cryptocurrency scheme, described by The Times as "one of the biggest scams in history."
 
Despite being on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list for several years, she has successfully evaded capture since her disappearance in October 2017; international law enforcement agencies including INTERPOL and the German Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) continue to pursue leads across multiple continents.
 The DOJ's $5 million reward for information leading to her arrest remains outstanding. 
Karl Sebastian Greenwood, the co-founder of OneCoin, was sentenced to 20 years in prison.


Recovery trajectory for claims investors: the $40 million DOJ restitution fund is the only structured distribution vehicle currently operating. The GBP 8.59 million Guernsey forfeiture adds to the pool in principle, though routing of forfeited proceeds into victim compensation — as opposed to the UK Treasury — is not publicly confirmed. Recovery counsel should monitor whether DOJ expands the restitution fund as additional seized assets clear legal challenges.

### SEC Enforcement Context — Enforcement Decline; Cross-Border Fraud Remains Priority Carve-Out


The SEC reported filing 456 enforcement actions in FY 2025, a significant decline from the 583 actions brought in FY 2024; according to the release, this reduction was driven by a deliberate refocus away from cases that do not meaningfully advance investor protection or market integrity, including actions based primarily on technical or strict-liability violations.
 For Ponzi and fraud-recovery watchers, the operative carve-out is cross-border fraud. 
The SEC underscored its continued objective to return funds to harmed investors and combat fraud, citing in particular its Cross-Border Task Force dedicated to addressing the threat that fraudsters located abroad pose to U.S. investors.


The structural implication: new SEC receivership appointments — the earliest signal in any fraud-recovery timeline — will track Chairman Atkins' stated "first principles" agenda, concentrating on offering frauds with clear investor harm rather than technical disclosure failures. 
Chairman Atkins promised that the SEC's enforcement program would return to first principles by focusing on fraud and investor harm, specifically cases involving offering frauds, insider trading, accounting and financial frauds, and investment advisers' breaches of fiduciary duties.
 That scope maps well to traditional Ponzi enforcement. The appointment of David Woodcock, formerly of Gibson Dunn, as Director of the Division of Enforcement as of April 8, 2026, represents the institutional personnel change most relevant to new receivership pipeline. 
The SEC announced the appointment of David Woodcock as Director of the Division of Enforcement on April 8; Woodcock joined from Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP where he served as a partner and chair of the firm's securities enforcement practice group.
