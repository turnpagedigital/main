# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Thursday, July 02, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's deltas:** The OneCoin DOJ remission petition window closed yesterday, June 30, 2026 — the single most consequential near-term procedural gate for claims holders in that matter. The Stanford receivership final distribution continues to roll out, with over $112 million paid to more than 5,600 claimant groups as of May 4, crossing a meaningful threshold. The BLMIS estate has been quiet since the seventeenth distribution commenced February 27; the trustee's stated pipeline remains active but no eighteenth distribution motion has been publicly filed as of this writing. Power Block Coin (SmartFi), *In re Power Block Coin, LLC*, No. 24-bk-23041-CDP (Bankr. D. Utah, Judge Cathleen D. All four matters carry distinct pricing implications for claims investors.

### OneCoin — DOJ Remission Window Has Closed; Next Gate Is Administrator Adjudication


The DOJ announced in April 2026 the opening of a remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin international investment scheme involving co-founders Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood.
 
Victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud became eligible to apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the DOJ.
 
The petition deadline was June 30.
 That window has now closed.

The recovery math here is stark. 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 
OneCoin, co-founded by fugitive Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 A $40 million fund against $4 billion in acknowledged investor losses implies a recovery rate under 1% on any proportional basis — though actual pro rata payments will depend entirely on the volume and validity of petitions filed before the June 30 cutoff. 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 The next procedural gate is Kroll's claims adjudication and the DOJ's remission order, neither of which has a publicly scheduled deadline.

The cross-border picture adds a parallel recovery thread. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds to the sum of GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The seized funds will be paid into Guernsey's seized assets fund and then returned to German authorities, where they will be used to compensate victims.
 That German/Guernsey stream is entirely separate from the U.S. remission process — there is no confirmed mechanism for U.S. petition filers to access it — but it confirms that non-conviction-based asset forfeiture under cooperative MLAT frameworks continues to yield incremental recovery on this matter.

The primary fugitive constraint remains unresolved. 
The FBI continues its investigative pursuit of Ruja Ignatova, an FBI Top Ten Fugitive, alongside IRS-CI and the Southern District of New York.
 For claims investors: recovery in this matter is DOJ-administered remission, not a judicial distribution — no proof-of-claim mechanism, no priority waterfall, no trustee. The $40M pool is fixed unless additional forfeitures generate supplemental funds. Pricing any OneCoin claim above nominal value requires a view on both petition volume (denominator) and additional DOJ forfeitures (numerator), neither of which is publicly quantified at this stage.

### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; No Eighteenth Motion on File


The SIPA Trustee commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution on February 27, 2026, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, aggregate payments to eligible BLMIS customers reached nearly $15.38 billion; the seventeenth distribution represented 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


The SIPC advance position is material for subordination and reimbursement analysis. 
As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
As of the seventeenth distribution, SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the BLMIS Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied claims, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.
 That $578.5 million subordinated reimbursement claim absorbs first from any incremental Customer Fund recovery before unsatisfied net equity claims receive further distribution — a critical structural point for anyone pricing partially-satisfied claims above the 72.848% mark.


As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion.
 That figure represents a modest increase from the February 27 baseline of $15.366 billion — approximately $45 million in incremental recoveries since the seventeenth distribution commenced, consistent with the residual litigation and smaller-balance settlement pipeline that characterizes the estate at this stage. No eighteenth distribution motion has been publicly filed or announced as of today. The next triggering event will be when Picard accumulates sufficient settled recoveries to warrant another Customer Fund allocation motion before Judge Lewis Liman (who succeeded Judge Stuart Bernstein) in Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789.

The ongoing fee structure deserves attention for claim-pricing purposes. 
No funds recovered in the Madoff Recovery Initiative are used to pay costs associated with the recovery; all trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 This is a meaningful structural advantage relative to most receiverships — the fee burden does not erode the Customer Fund — and is a primary driver of the BLMIS estate's industry-leading recovery rate.

### Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Active; $339M Authorized, $112M Paid as of May 4

The Stanford matter has crossed into final distribution execution, with the pace of payments now the operative variable for claim-pricing and liquidity decisions. 
On November 25, 2025, the court approved an amendment to the Final Distribution Plan originally approved October 8, 2025; under the Final Distribution Plan, the receiver will be making payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claimant groups.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups totaling more than $112 million.


The mechanics are straightforward but operationally consequential. 
On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule — totaling more than $339 million and including payments to over 14,000 claim groups — are being issued on a rolling basis beginning 14 days after the filing of the Schedule.
 The rolling disbursement model means that as of May 4, approximately 40% of claimant groups had been paid, with the remaining 60% still awaiting payment. No updated payment count was published after May 4 in the confirmed sources available to this briefing.

A discrete cross-border asset question remains open. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and transferred $8.1 million in Swiss bankruptcy proceeds to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol.
 
As of early April, COViSAL — a Venezuelan claimant group — had sent a formal written inquiry to the Receiver on March 11, 2026 asking whether and when the $8.1 million in Swiss assets would be distributed, and whether those funds would be included in a future distribution separate from the Final Distribution Plan; the Receiver and his legal team had not responded.
 That gap matters: if the $8.1 million is distributed under an amended plan rather than a separate motion, the allocation mechanism and pro rata treatment could affect specific claimant cohorts.

Check-cashing logistics impose a hard deadline. 
Payees must cash checks issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Orders no later than 90 days after the date of issuance; payees may request reissuance once, by submitting a written request within 90 days; if a payee does not cash the check or request reissuance within 90 days from issuance, that payee shall not be eligible to receive any other payment under the Final Distribution Plan.
 
All funds represented by void checks not subject to a timely request for check reissuance revert to the Receivership Estate.
 For international claimants, the 90-day window against a physical-check default payment method is a genuine forfeiture risk.

### Power Block Coin (SmartFi) — Conversion Proceedings; Judge Parker; Amplēo Fee Application Resolved


Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, requested to convert its Chapter 11 reorganization case to a Chapter 7 liquidation, citing administrative insolvency and failed reorganization efforts in the wake of the 2022 cryptocurrency market collapse.
 The case now sits before 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah under Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.
 The tracked-case ground truth confirms the case is active and that docket entry 519 was filed June 9, 2026 — the same date as the scheduled Amplēo hearing.


Judge Parker set a hearing on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for allowance of compensation for June 9, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. (Prevailing Utah Time); by that application, Amplēo requested final allowance of fees in the amount of $10,436.50 for the period April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026, plus fees previously allowed on an interim basis.
 The June 9 docket entry (no. 519) is consistent with the court entering an order resolving that fee application, though the specific content of that entry was not available from CourtListener at last sync (June 11). The Celsius Network LLC preference claim overhang established in the case's early record — 
the Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the Celsius bankruptcy, Case No. 22-10964 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.)
 — remains the dominant unresolved variable for any recovery-oriented analysis of general unsecured claim value here. Conversion to Chapter 7 would trigger a trustee appointment and a fresh § 341 examination of asset disposition; the administrative insolvency representation, if accurate, implies general unsecured creditors face near-zero recovery absent preference recoveries flowing back into the estate.

### SEC Enforcement Posture — Structural Shift Relevant to Future Receiver Appointments

The institutional context for new fraud enforcement (and the receiver appointments that follow) has shifted materially under the current commission. 
The current Commission intends to prioritize the policing of alleged fraud, market manipulation, insider trading, and breaches of fiduciary duty while moving away from cases that it views as regulation by enforcement or as lacking a direct nexus to investor harm.
 
David Woodcock was appointed to serve as Director of the Division of Enforcement on April 8, 2026.
 
Notable FY 2025 actions included charges in connection with several large-scale Ponzi schemes, including one involving approximately 2,700 investors and an alleged $400 million in losses and another involving approximately 300 investors and more than $140 million in alleged losses.


The enforcement pipeline has a structural implication for claims investors: 
during FY 2025, the Commission returned approximately $262 million to harmed investors and awarded approximately $60 million to 48 individual whistleblowers.
 
The SEC received a record 53,753 tips, complaints, and referrals in fiscal year 2025, nearly 19% more than in the prior fiscal year.
 Record whistleblower intake is a leading indicator of the next wave of enforcement actions — and receiver appointments — typically with a 12-to-24-month lag from tip to charging document. The current administration's stated "first principles" focus on intentional fraud rather than technical violations should, if followed, produce a smaller number of higher-severity actions with larger disgorgement targets — a favorable environment for recovery-oriented claim buyers if the disgorgement pool remains intact.

The pending Supreme Court decision in *Sripetch v. SEC* adds a structural overhang. 
The Supreme Court is set to decide *SEC v. Sripetch* on whether the SEC has the ability to seek disgorgement as a penalty without showing investors suffered financial harm.
 
The SEC in at least one April 2026 enforcement action chose not to seek disgorgement — potentially a strategic decision given that the Court recently heard oral argument in *Sripetch* to resolve the circuit split on whether equitable disgorgement is available without proof of actual pecuniary harm.
 A ruling limiting disgorgement availability would shrink the pool of recoverable funds available for Fair Fund distributions in future SEC-enforcement-driven receiverships — the most direct downstream effect on claim recovery rates.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should treat July 2 as the day-after assessment point for the OneCoin remission deadline: confirm with any U.S. claimant that a petition was filed before June 30 (late petitions are not accepted under the DOJ remission process), then monitor the Kroll Settlement Administration docket at onecoinremission.com for the administrator's claims-adjudication schedule, which will govern when pro rata payment calculations become computable; given the $40M fund against $4B+ in losses, expect the per-victim recovery rate to be deeply discounted and price any transfer of OneCoin remission rights accordingly. On Stanford, watch for a June/July receiver update that either advances the May 4 payment count toward the 14,000-group finish line or discloses a separate distribution motion for the $8.1 million Swiss tranche — the Swiss funds' allocation method will affect priority treatment and may be material for Antiguan JL coordination claims. On BLMIS, no immediate action is required — the next trigger is an eighteenth distribution motion in Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789; SIPC's $578.5M outstanding advance balance acts as a waterfall layer that constrains the marginal benefit of any incremental Customer Fund recovery to existing unsatisfied net equity claims, so secondary-market pricing should discount accordingly. On Power Block Coin (24-bk-23041-CDP), monitor the post-June 9 docket for a conversion order or confirmation schedule; if conversion to Chapter 7 is entered, initiate a § 341 notice analysis and assess whether the Celsius $133M preference claim exposure (Case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) generates any estate asset once adjudicated. Finally, track the *Sripetch v. SEC* Supreme Court decision — expected before the Court's term ends in late June or early July 2026 — as a potential ceiling on disgorgement availability in all future SEC enforcement-driven receiverships.
