# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Thursday, July 30, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's picture is consolidation without resolution.** The two anchor cases in this desk's portfolio — *In re Goliath Ventures, Inc.* and *In re Power Block Coin, LLC* — each sit at inflection points that matter for claim pricing: Goliath is in a claims-bar-deadline sprint toward an October sentencing that will anchor the forfeiture pool, while Power Block Coin appears to have crossed from the debtor-controlled Chapter 11 track onto a creditor-committee-driven liquidation path. No new docket activity was confirmed in the 72-hour scan for either case, so the analysis below carries forward the substantive posture from prior coverage and flags the live pressure points.

### Goliath Ventures — Claims Bar Deadline, Forfeiture Pool, and the JPMorgan Third-Party Track


The receiver filed a Chapter 11 bankruptcy petition in the Southern District of Florida on March 16, 2026 — *In re Goliath Ventures, Inc.*, No. 26-13174-RAM (Bankr. S.D. Fla. 2026)
, before 
U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Robert A. Mark.


The case carries a hard structural deadline: 
claims can be filed electronically through the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Florida, and the deadline for filing claims is September 30, 2026.
 For claims investors and institutional claimants, that date is the operative pricing anchor. Rights that are not perfected by September 30 are extinguished; secondary-market transfers after that date carry the full execution risk of an unverified or disallowed claim.

On the criminal side, 
Christopher Alexander Delgado (34, Apopka) pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, and money laundering, and faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in federal prison for each fraud count and up to 10 years' imprisonment for the money laundering count. His sentencing hearing is scheduled for October 8, 2026.
 The sentencing proceeding is the next material event: forfeiture amounts will be formally entered at or shortly before sentencing, and that number is the ceiling for criminal restitution available to victims. 
Delgado admitted in his plea agreement to causing at least $250 million in investor losses; investor money was used to pay earlier investors, fund withdrawals, and cover luxury spending.


The spread between the admitted-loss figure ($250 million) and the gross-raise figure matters for recovery math. 
Goliath drew at least $400 million from investors,
 so roughly $150 million of the gross intake was recycled as fictitious returns or withdrawals before collapse — the classic Ponzi-presumption fact pattern that triggers net-winner clawback exposure under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A). Any investor who received distributions that exceeded principal invested is a net winner and faces clawback liability to the estate. The Ponzi presumption means the estate need not demonstrate actual fraudulent intent for each transfer — the scheme's existence supplies the requisite intent as a matter of law.

The forfeiture package Delgado agreed to is substantial and constitutes the primary hard-asset pool for recovery: 
Delgado bought at least six residential properties worth between $1.15 million and $8.5 million each, plus Lamborghinis, Rolls-Royces, and luxury goods; he agreed to forfeit eight properties, eleven vehicles, thirty watches, more than fifty luxury bags and wallets, at least twenty-nine pieces of jewelry, and several seized bank and crypto accounts as part of the agreement.



A federal judge has accepted the guilty plea and plea agreement for Christopher Delgado.
 That judicial acceptance, reported July 22, 2026, has a practical consequence: it forecloses withdrawal of the plea and locks in the factual basis for the forfeiture order. The estate and creditor counsel can now build a plan around a known (if not yet formally quantified) asset pool.

The third-party litigation track is where incremental recovery will be contested. 
A proposed federal class-action lawsuit accused JPMorgan Chase of processing about $253 million in transactions linked to Goliath while allegedly failing
 to act on red flags — a know-your-customer and Bank Secrecy Act negligence theory. That action, filed in the Middle District of Florida (Case No. 26-cv-21612, per the docket record), represents the secondary recovery layer that claims traders should price. Bank-defendant cases of this type routinely settle for 3–8% of alleged transaction volume under aiding-and-abetting negligence theories; at $253 million notional, a settlement in the $7–$20 million range is plausible but unverified and could take 24–36 months to resolve. The automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 that attached on March 16 does not shield non-debtor defendants, so the bank-liability track runs concurrently. 
New Zealand-based investigative journalist Danny de Hek had published whistleblowing exposures detailing structural and personnel overlaps between Goliath Ventures and a separate crypto scheme that collapsed in 2022; Goliath filed a defamation suit against de Hek in late 2025 and then quickly dropped it after Delgado's initial arrest in February 2026.

### Power Block Coin — Creditor Committee Liquidation Plan, Judge Transfer, and the $133 Million Celsius Overhang


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing the plan on January 30, 2025.
 The plan proposes winding down the company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee, with the trustee empowered to pursue avoidance and other claims.

The tracked-case ground truth identifies the current presiding judge as 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker
 — consistent with the case designation 24-bk-23041-CDP visible in later docket entries — rather than Judge Joel T. Marker, who presided over the early Subchapter V eligibility fight. The judge reassignment is itself a procedural marker: Subchapter V cases convert to standard Chapter 11 or Chapter 7 when the debtor's eligibility is successfully challenged, and the CDP designation signals that transition has occurred.


The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 The Celsius claim is the structural complexity that has slowed this case. 
Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against Power Block Coin in connection with the Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy case (No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The allowance and priority treatment of those claims is the single largest variable in the distribution waterfall.


Power Block Coin LLC filed a voluntary petition under Chapter 11 of the Bankruptcy Code on June 20, 2024, represented by Parsons Behle & Latimer.
 
With less than three hours to spare before the Subchapter V debt limit dropped from $7,500,000 to $3,024,725, the debtor filed its Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024, electing to proceed under Subchapter V as an entity with aggregate noncontingent liquidated secured and unsecured debts of less than $7,500,000.
 The committee's challenge to Subchapter V eligibility succeeded, removing debtor-favorable plan exclusivity and enabling the committee's own plan.


The chapter11cases.com publication notes a July 19, 2026 analysis on "Pricing the Transfer: A First-Impression Framework for Valuing Appreciating Digital Assets in Avoidance Actions,"
 which is directly relevant to this estate's posture: the liquidating trustee will need to pursue avoidance actions, and the valuation of crypto assets transferred pre-petition at potentially depressed prices (versus current values) is a live legal question. The last confirmed docket filing was July 23, 2026 (Dkt. 532, described as a "Complaint" — likely an adversary proceeding initiation) per the live data feed. That filing warrants attention: adversary complaint filings at this stage are typically the trustee or committee initiating preference, fraudulent transfer, or other avoidance litigation.

### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Eighteenth Cycle Underway

The Madoff estate provides the baseline benchmark against which all other Ponzi recovery timelines are measured. 
On February 27, 2026, Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee for the liquidation of Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC (BLMIS), announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.



As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation, and SIPC-committed advances will continue to increase as claims currently in litigation are allowed as a result of settlements or the conclusion of litigation.
 The estate is now in its eighteenth recovery cycle — no new distribution has been announced, which is consistent with the pattern of 12–18 month intervals between distributions as litigation settlements accumulate. Active clawback adversary proceedings continue in Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB). Net winners who received fictitious profits remain exposed; the Ponzi presumption under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) has been affirmed through 17 years of litigation and is not being challenged in any pending appeal of significance this desk has confirmed.

### Cross-Cutting: Clawback Exposure and the Ponzi Presumption

Two structural themes warrant standing attention across all active receiverships and bankruptcies in this desk's portfolio:

**Net-winner exposure.** In every case above — Goliath, Power Block Coin, BLMIS — the Ponzi presumption converts ordinary distributions into constructively fraudulent transfers under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) (or state UFTA/UVTA equivalents). The two-year look-back under § 548 and the longer state-law look-back periods (up to six years under New York DCL, four years under Utah UFTA) create layered exposure. Net winners — defined as investors whose lifetime withdrawals exceed lifetime principal deposits — should treat their "profits" as contingent liabilities, not confirmed income. The recent Goliath plea acceptance (July 22) is a trigger event: it confirms the scheme's existence as a matter of judicial record, which satisfies the factual predicate for Ponzi-presumption clawback without further litigation.

**Cross-border tracing in crypto schemes.** Goliath's crypto-wallet discovery demands (Dkt. 61, issued April 9, 2026, seeking all documents concerning "cryptocurrency wallets belonging to Goliath") signal the estate is pursuing on-chain tracing. The mechanics — subpoenas to Coinbase and Uniswap Labs (both named in the related civil complaint record), combined with blockchain analytics — are consistent with how crypto Ponzi estates have operated in post-FTX cases. Mareva-style injunctions under CPR 25 or § 1782 discovery orders directed at offshore exchanges are available if assets migrated offshore, though no such application has been confirmed this cycle.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and counsel with exposure to the Goliath estate should treat the **September 30, 2026** bar date as an immovable deadline and file or ensure transfer agreements include express representations of timely-filed proofs of claim; the claims register is accessible through the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Florida at flsb.uscourts.gov. For Power Block Coin, monitor Dkt. 532 and any subsequent adversary proceeding filings in Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041-CDP before Judge Parker — if the July 23 filing initiates preference litigation, buyers of claims in that estate will need to assess net-winner exposure before pricing or transferring positions. Net winners in either estate should engage restructuring counsel immediately to evaluate whether their distribution history triggers § 548 or state UVTA exposure; the Ponzi-presumption Ponzi determination in Goliath is now judicially established following the July 22 plea acceptance. For BLMIS secondary-market participants, the seventeenth distribution is complete and no eighteenth distribution notice has been issued; pricing should reflect the next distribution is 12–18 months out at minimum, discounting the remaining pool at the appropriate time-value rate.
