### BLMIS / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, Judge Stuart M. Bernstein): Distribution Pace and Fee-Cycle Signal

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution — the most recent — commenced February 27, 2026. 
The SIPA Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 
Eligible BLMIS customers have now received approximately 72.848 percent of their allowed claims across the seventeen interim distributions, plus SIPC advances of up to $500,000 per allowed claim.


The fee-cycle signal matters for claim pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with Bankr. S.D.N.Y. for work on the global liquidation of BLMIS from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025.
 
The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 Fee applications at this cadence — now at application No. 50 across seventeen-plus years — are the clearest available indicator of ongoing litigation activity, and by extension of the marginal recovery pipeline still available to push aggregate recoveries beyond the current $15.38 billion. The remaining gap to 100 percent recovery against allowed claims (currently sitting at ~27 percent of the estimated $17.5 billion in investor losses against filed claims) still represents a meaningful residual for the active clawback docket. Picard's continued language referencing "future significant recoveries" signals the litigation runway has not closed. Claims-desk takeaway: the 50th fee application, at a blended rate below $590/hour, reflects a mature but still-active mandate. Secondary market discount to NAV should reflect continued distribution cadence (roughly annual) rather than terminal wind-down.

### Stanford Receivership (N.D. Tex. — Dallas Div., Receiver Ralph S. Janvey, Baker Botts counsel Kevin Sadler): Final Distribution Actively Disbursing

The Stanford receivership has entered its terminal distribution phase. 
On March 9, 2026, Receiver Janvey filed with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million.



According to the Receiver's update posted on April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 Active distribution is therefore in progress.

Two live issues create post-distribution risk. First, claimants via the COViSAL investor group raised formal concerns about whether distributions were sent by physical check or wire — a practical administration gap with real consequences for international claimants who may not receive or cash checks promptly. Second, 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver on March 11, 2026, asking whether and when the $8.1 million in Swiss assets — released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025 — will be distributed, and whether those funds will be included in a future distribution separate from the current Final Distribution Plan.
 
As of that filing, the Receiver and his legal team had not responded to that request.
 The $8.1 million Swiss tranche remains unallocated to the current schedule.

Additionally, 
in May 2026, claimant Jaime Escalona submitted materials to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, requesting relief regarding the Receiver's distribution process; on June 1, 2026, the Receiver responded, with that response including an update on the status of ongoing distribution efforts.
 
On May 12, 2026, the Court granted the Receiver's motion for authorization to dispose of certain Stanford records and equipment.
 The records-disposal authorization is a late-stage wind-down signal. Claims-desk takeaway: the Final Distribution Schedule is live and disbursing against $339 million in scheduled payments. The undistributed $8.1 million Swiss tranche and the unanswered COViSAL inquiry are the only known open items. This case is approaching terminal closure; secondary-market claim liquidity should be assessed accordingly.

### OneCoin / DOJ Remission (S.D.N.Y.): June 30 Petition Deadline — Hard Cutoff in 13 Days


The U.S. Department of Justice has opened a compensation process for victims of the OneCoin crypto Ponzi scheme, with victims able to apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by DOJ.
 
Between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-founded OneCoin Ltd., based in Sofia, Bulgaria, and marketed a fraudulent cryptocurrency through a global multi-level-marketing network, with victims investing over $4 billion worldwide.
 
Victim petition forms are available at www.onecoinremission.com.
 
The filing deadline is June 30, 2026.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process.


The arithmetic is sobering for claimants: $40 million in recovered assets against a declared $4 billion fraud universe means the current remission fund represents approximately one cent on the dollar against aggregate investor losses. 
OneCoin defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 Ignatova remains a fugitive. 
The State Department reward for information leading to her arrest stands at up to $5 million under the Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program.
 The marginal recovery case here is entirely dependent on further asset seizures — a function of whether international law-enforcement coordination can close the gap between seized proceeds and fraud proceeds. Cross-border tracing tools remain active: Guernsey authorities are the most recent jurisdiction to execute seizures. 
Authorities in Guernsey announced that GBP £8.59 million linked to Ignatova will be returned to Germany for victim compensation efforts
 — a positive but small additive to the global tracing picture.

Claims-desk takeaway: submit petitions at www.onecoinremission.com before June 30. Pro-rata recovery at the current $40 million fund level against 3.4 million victims means per-victim recoveries will be negligible for most retail claimants unless further seizures materially expand the fund. For claims-buying purposes, the residual value thesis here depends entirely on the still-fugitive Ignatova asset trail and ongoing international coordination — not a near-term distribution thesis.

### Power Block Coin, LLC / SmartFi (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker): Ch. 11 Conversion to Ch. 7 — Liquidating Trust Trajectory

This case has been reassigned from Judge Joel T. Marker to Judge Cathleen D. Parker per the tracked-case ground truth. 
Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, requested conversion of its Chapter 11 reorganization case to a Chapter 7 liquidation, citing administrative insolvency and failed reorganization efforts in the wake of the 2022 cryptocurrency market collapse.


The creditor committee's competing posture is to pursue a Chapter 11 liquidating trust rather than a Chapter 7 trustee. 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041), filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down SmartFi through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million, with a key component involving the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage assets and pursue potential legal claims.



The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, with concerns expressed about that loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 The Solara loan is the primary fraudulent-transfer / § 548 exposure vector in this case for clawback purposes.

The most recent CourtListener docket entry — No. 519, dated June 9, 2026 — carried no description in the sync snapshot fetched June 11. That entry number represents active docket movement and should be pulled directly via PACER to determine whether it reflects plan confirmation, a conversion order, an adversary proceeding filing, or a disclosure statement objection. 
Docket entry 502, filed May 7, 2026, reflected a distribution notice addressed to creditors including Celsius Network LLC and its affiliated debtors, Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney PC, and Jones, Waldo, Holbrook & McDonough P.C.
 — confirming that the case remains in active administrative proceedings as of the prior month.

Claims-desk takeaway: the $133 million Celsius claim dominates the creditor waterfall and will drive recovery percentages for all other unsecured creditors. The Solara loan ($17.4 million, potentially unsecured, and encumbered post-petition) is the highest-value adversary proceeding target in this estate if a liquidating trustee is empowered. Until docket entry 519 is verified, conversion versus liquidating-trust structure remains uncertain — and that structural question determines whether there will be a trustee with avoidance-action authority under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547–548, or a plan administrator with narrower mandate.
