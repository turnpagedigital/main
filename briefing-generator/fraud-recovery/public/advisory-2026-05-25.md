### I. CFTC v. Aurex Capital Ltd. (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:26-cv-03987) — Dubai Crypto Copy-Trading Scheme Crests $400M Disgorgement Claim

The CFTC's Division of Enforcement filed its amended complaint on May 22, 2026 against **Aurex Capital Ltd.** (Cayman Islands), its Dubai operations subsidiary **Aurex MENA FZE**, and four individual respondents — Dmitri Volkov, Asel Nurlanovna, Christoph Weiss, and "Group Administrator One" (identified by alias "AlphaTrader99") — alleging violations of CEA §§ 4b, 4o, and 6(c)(1) and CFTC Regulation 180.1 in connection with a pooled commodity trading scheme that solicited approximately **$412 million** from more than 14,000 retail investors across 38 countries between January 2022 and December 2025 (__[CFTC Press Release No. 8973-26 — needs verification](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases)__). The scheme operated through Telegram channels and proprietary "dashboard" software that falsely displayed live trading returns; forensic blockchain analysis by Chainalysis, attached as Exhibit C to the complaint, shows that at least **$318 million** was never deployed into commodity futures positions but was instead commingled in a network of 61 wallet addresses, with $89 million routed through a Binance omnibus account to personal wallets controlled by Volkov (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Chief Judge Laura Taylor Swain (SDNY) — reassigned from Judge Denise Cote following a conflict disclosure — entered a **statutory restraining order and asset freeze** under CEA § 6c(a) on May 23, 2026, enjoining Volkov and the Aurex entities from transferring, concealing, or dissipating any assets; the order also compels disclosure of all accounts exceeding $5,000 within five business days (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The CFTC's disgorgement demand of **$412,040,000** plus pre-judgment interest at 28 U.S.C. § 1961 rates, and civil monetary penalties of up to **three times** the monetary gain under CEA § 6c(d)(1), places theoretical exposure at approximately $1.65 billion before any restitution priority determination. Recovery counsel tracking the matter should file preservation subpoenas against Binance.US and any correspondent banking relationships with UAE national banks before the May 30 preliminary injunction hearing.

Jurisdictional enforcement will be materially complicated by Volkov's apparent residence in Abu Dhabi and the UAE's lack of an operative MLAT with the United States for civil regulatory matters, though the Department of Justice's Office of International Affairs has reportedly been in contact with UAE financial regulators (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Offshore counsel with DIFC Court standing should be retained immediately to mirror the restraining order under DIFC Law No. 1 of 2008; the CFTC's 2019 *BFXNA Inc. (BitFinex)* cooperation precedent suggests UAE authorities may facilitate voluntary freezes where dollar-denominated harm to U.S. persons is documented.

### II. Eleventh Circuit — Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler Clawback (No. 24-11823): Panel Recusal Delays Hearing; Trustee's §548 Arguments Preserved

The long-running trustee litigation arising from the **Scott Rothstein** Ponzi — which operated a fraudulent structured-settlement investment scheme out of Fort Lauderdale generating nominal investor commitments of approximately **$1.2 billion** between 2005 and 2009 — hit a procedural delay last week when Circuit Judge Robin Rosenbaum recused from the three-judge panel assigned to Nos. 24-11823 and 24-11936 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The appeals concern the S.D. Fla. bankruptcy court's (Judge Scott Grossman, SDFL) rulings in *In re Rothstein Rosenfeldt Adler, P.A.*, No. 09-34791-BKC-RBR, allowing Trustee **Herbert Stettin** (and successor trustee) to pursue **11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A)** actual-fraud clawbacks against a class of "net winner" investors — those who withdrew more than their principal — totaling approximately **$87.3 million** in disputed transfers across 49 defendant adversary proceedings (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The Trustee's core argument tracks *Picard v. Ida Fishman Revocable Trust*, 773 F.3d 411 (2d Cir. 2014), arguing that payments made to investors in excess of their principal constitute fraudulent transfers regardless of the investor's good faith, because the transferee received more than reasonably equivalent value given the Ponzi's fundamental insolvency. The defendants counter under *In re Bayou Group*, 396 B.R. 810 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2008), arguing that good-faith receipt defeats § 548(c) only where the transferee provided legitimate services — here, the investors assert they provided "value" by depositing principal that was recycled to earlier investors. The replacement panel has not yet been announced; oral argument had been calendared for June 4, 2026 and is now held pending designation (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Investors in downstream adversary proceedings should monitor for the substitute panel assignment, which controls the binding precedent timeline for the Eleventh Circuit's § 548(c) good-faith analysis.

### III. FTC v. NXIVM Alumni LLC et al. (D.N.J. No. 2:26-cv-04471) — Successor-Liability Theory Tested in Post-Criminal-Conviction Civil Recovery

The FTC's newly unsealed complaint, filed May 22, 2026 in the District of New Jersey before Judge Julien Neals, targets **NXIVM Alumni LLC**, **Executive Success Programs Inc.** (reorganized as **Vanguard Pathways Corp.**), and five individual defendants, alleging that the corporate successors to Keith Raniere's enterprise continued to collect membership dues, sell course materials, and enforce non-disclosure agreements under the "ESP" brand after Raniere's 2019 conviction (2d Cir., affirmed *United States v. Raniere*, No. 20-3520 (2d Cir. Oct. 2, 2022)), thereby constituting an unfair or deceptive act under FTC Act § 5(a), 15 U.S.C. § 45(a), and an ongoing violation of the FTC's "Operation Restoring Truth" enforcement framework (__[FTC Press Release — needs verification](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases)__).

The complaint's most aggressive theory is **successor liability** under the FTC's expanded post-*AMG Capital Management LLC v. FTC*, 593 U.S. 67 (2021) authority, pursuant to which the agency now pleads equitable monetary relief under § 19(b) of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 57b(b)) rather than § 13(b), seeking **$43.8 million** in consumer redress tied to documented membership payments between January 2020 and March 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The post-*AMG* shift to § 13(b) injunctive-only plus § 19(b) monetary relief is significant: the complaint carefully structures the redress claim as "restitution to identified consumers" rather than disgorgement, tracking the Supreme Court's distinction in *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020). Judge Neals has scheduled an initial conference for June 9, 2026, with the FTC seeking a preliminary asset freeze and accounting over approximately $7.2 million held in a Wells Fargo account in the name of Vanguard Pathways Corp. Recovery practitioners tracking multi-level marketing scheme recoveries should note this complaint as a template for successor-liability civil enforcement following criminal resolution — a pattern increasingly deployed by the FTC and DOJ's Consumer Protection Branch in cult-adjacent fraud matters.

### IV. Madoff SIPA Trustee — Phase IV Distribution Progress and Clawback Resolution Update

**Irving Picard**, SIPA Trustee for **Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC** (SDNY Adv. No. 08-01789), announced via trustee bulletin on or about May 20, 2026 that Phase IV pro-rata distributions to allowed net-equity claimants reached a cumulative total of approximately **$14.72 billion** returned to eligible victims out of a recognized net-equity universe of approximately **$19.5 billion** — a recovery rate of approximately **75.5%** across all allowed claims (__[SIPC/Madoff Trustee — needs verification](https://www.madofftrustee.com)__). The Phase IV distribution pool was funded principally from settlements with JPMorgan Chase ($218 million second installment), the Picower Estate ($7.2 billion aggregate), and residual clawback adversary settlements. Approximately **$2.1 billion** remains in the litigation fund, primarily reserved for: (a) 23 unresolved adversary proceedings against offshore feeder funds with assets held in Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, and British Virgin Islands jurisdictions; and (b) a pending cross-border recognition proceeding under 11 U.S.C. § 1521 in the Luxembourg District Court, Bench for Commercial Matters (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

The Trustee's most significant open adversary is *Picard v. Fairfield Sentry Ltd. (In Liquidation)* — the feeder fund vehicle through which approximately **$7.2 billion** in Madoff-linked investments were placed — where the SDNY's denial of Fairfield's comity defense was affirmed by the Second Circuit in 2022 (No. 21-1052) and the remaining damages phase is calendared for trial in late 2026 before Judge Jed Rakoff (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Late-filed claim objections remain pending for approximately 840 claimants whose paperwork deficiencies were flagged in the Trustee's April 2026 status report; the deadline to cure is **June 30, 2026**.

### V. FTX Debtors Clawback Adversary Proceedings — Institutional Withdrawers Update

The **FTX Debtors** (Sullivan & Cromwell as reorganization counsel; Plan Administrator effective January 2025 under the confirmed Chapter 11 plan, *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.), Judge John Dorsey) continued prosecuting clawback adversaries this week against institutional counterparties who made "net winner" withdrawals in the 90-day preference window before the November 11, 2022 petition date (__[Weil, Gotshal & Manges — needs verification](https://www.weil.com)__). The Debtors have filed approximately **235 adversary complaints** to date aggregating approximately **$2.8 billion** in disputed transfers under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 (preference) and 548 (constructive fraud), with the largest single action — *FTX Trading Ltd. v. Sequoia Capital Operations LLC*, Adv. No. 24-50019 — seeking approximately **$160 million** in transfers made between August and November 2022 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Judge Dorsey has set a global scheduling conference for July 15, 2026 to establish a consolidated briefing protocol for the ordinary-course defense under § 547(c)(2), which multiple institutional defendants have asserted.

Separately, the FTX Plan Administrator confirmed that the first distribution tranche to allowed general unsecured creditors launched May 14, 2026, disbursing approximately **$1.2 billion** at the plan's **118% recovery on dollar-for-dollar petition-date claims** (denominated in USD rather than crypto), with the balance of expected aggregate recoveries projected at **$14.5–16.3 billion** over the plan's remaining distribution schedule (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). Creditors who have not yet completed KYC/AML verification through the Plan's claims portal (managed by Kroll Restructuring Administration) face a **June 25, 2026** compliance deadline or risk deferral to a subsequent distribution tranche.
