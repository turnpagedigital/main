$415M stalking horse. $1.3B in liabilities. The math is the story.

Sleep Number filed Chapter 11 on June 12 in the Southern District of New York (Case No. 26-11399, Judge Paek). Sleep Country Canada holds the stalking horse at $415M cash plus assumed liabilities. Bid deadline: July 8. Auction: July 13. Target close: July 31.

The DIP structure is where unsecured creditors should focus first. The company is financing the case with up to $65M in new money and a $195M roll-up of prepetition debt — converting that prepetition exposure to postpetition priority status. Before any dollar reaches unsecured creditors, the estate must clear roughly $260M in DIP obligations, cure costs across 572 retail stores, and senior secured claims. At $415M gross, the residual waterfall is thin.

A 14-week prepetition process — 53 parties solicited, 19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals, zero actionable recapitalization alternatives — supports the compressed sale timeline but also signals this price may be the ceiling, not the floor.

Two items to track beyond the obvious: (1) Canadian regulatory approval for Sleep Country could slip the July 31 close independent of Judge Paek's schedule. (2) Travis Kelce holds a 5% equity stake via a sponsorship agreement — treatment under Section 365 and potential preference exposure are unresolved.

Bidding procedures hearing: July 2, 10:00 a.m. ET. Objections not raised there are waived on procedural points.

**Practical takeaway:** Unsecured creditors and landlords should file notices of appearance now and calendar July 2 — that is the leverage point on DIP terms, bid protections, and overbid increments.
