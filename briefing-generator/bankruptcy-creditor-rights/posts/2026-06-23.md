September 11, 2026 is the date that matters for ~$145M in BlockFills unsecured claims.

Judge Horan approved the $3.25M Keyrock sale on June 16. The case is now in the confirmation stretch — and the disclosure statement, expected within the next two to four weeks, will be the first document that actually quantifies the recovery split.

The core issue isn't the sale price. It's the customer-property question that's been live since the first-day filing in March. If deposited digital assets are estate property, certain customers sit in a property-recovery position ahead of general unsecured creditors. If they're not, those customers pool into the same class as trade creditors — against a $145M unsecured pool with a $3.25M sale-proceeds floor.

Case markers:
• In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.
• Adversary Proceeding No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners LLC — still live; outcome affects total distributable value
• Celsius holds ~$4.8M in secured promissory notes with first-priority liens — senior to all unsecured recoveries
• No confirmed Rule 3001 claim transfer notices as of June 22

Practical takeaway: Read the disclosure statement the moment it hits the docket. Classification of customer property claims, treatment of the Celsius secured position, and any proposed releases will determine whether you hold a senior property claim or a general unsecured one. That document sets the terms before the September 11 hearing locks them in.
