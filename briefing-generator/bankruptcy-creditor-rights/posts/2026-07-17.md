## LinkedIn

⚖️ Chief Judge Glenn's July 14 opinion in *In re NFE Global Holdings Limited* (Case No. 26-11268, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) is the most consequential cross-border ruling of the year so far.

The court recognized two English Part 26A restructuring plans as foreign main proceedings under Chapter 15, giving full U.S. force and effect to the English sanction order — including plan releases. The plans cut New Fortress Energy's external funded debt from roughly $5.7 billion to under $1 billion, split the group into BrazilCo and CoreCo, and left existing shareholders with 35% of CoreCo before MIP and convert dilution. Creditors voted 99% in favor at the June 15 plan meetings.

The opinion did not wave the case through quietly. Glenn drew a precise COMI-tourism warning: late-stage UK incorporation alone is not a per se bar to recognition, but courts will scrutinize proceedings where one creditor class is favored, recoveries trail what Chapter 11 would produce, or insider manipulation is evident. NFE Brazil was incorporated in April 2026 — roughly two months before the English proceedings commenced. The court found no abuse on these facts: no objections, near-unanimous creditor support, and A&M projections showing superior recoveries versus the relevant alternative.

What that warning telegraphs for future cases: any U.S.-domiciled group contemplating a London plan should expect contested Chapter 15 recognition if the UK entity was formed late, creditor classes are treated disparately, or projected recoveries underperform a Chapter 11 waterfall.

Practitioners advising on cross-border capital structures should read this opinion before the next mandate comes in.
