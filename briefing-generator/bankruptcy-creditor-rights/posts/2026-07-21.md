## LinkedIn

⚖️ $701M. That's where the Sleep Number § 363 sale landed after the auction — $286M above the $415M stalking-horse floor Sleep Country Canada set on June 12.

Judge Kyu Young Paek approved the sale yesterday in the S.D.N.Y. (Case No. 26-11399). Closing is expected July 31.

Why it matters for unsecured creditors: the petition-date balance sheet showed roughly $642M in assets against $1.281B in debt. The stalking-horse bid covered the funded debt at roughly 62 cents on the dollar. The $529.5M cash component of the approved bid changes that calculus materially — though the final waterfall still depends on assumed vs. rejected executory contracts and DIP payoff amounts. Sleep Number operates 572 retail stores; landlords for rejected locations face § 502(b)(6) caps (lesser of one year's rent or 15% of remaining term). At that portfolio size, aggregate landlord rejection claims are a meaningful unsecured-pool variable.

Two dates to hold: July 24 § 341 meeting (Zoom, 1:00 p.m. ET) and the August 20 stay-relief hearing (10:00 a.m. ET). The § 363(m) good-faith finding, if entered, substantially forecloses post-closing appellate challenge.

Practical takeaway: contract counterparties and landlords should confirm their treatment in the assumed-liability schedule before July 31 — if you're in the rejected bucket, file a protective proof of claim now. The window to seek an appellate stay closes at closing.
