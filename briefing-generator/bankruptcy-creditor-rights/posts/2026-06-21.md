$415M stalking horse. $1.28B in debt. The math on Sleep Number's unsecured creditors is not favorable.

Sleep Number filed chapter 11 in the S.D.N.Y. on June 12 (Case No. 26-11399, Judge Kyu Young Paek). The structure is a pure §363 sale — no reorganization. Here is what the waterfall looks like before a dollar reaches general unsecured creditors:

— Up to $260M in DIP financing (including a $195M roll-up of prepetition secured debt into post-petition superpriority)
— ~$672.5M in prepetition secured debt
— Stalking horse price from Sleep Country Canada's subsidiary SNBR, Inc.: $415M cash

Even before transaction costs and administrative claims, the numbers don't reach. The debtor's own filing notes funds "available" for unsecured creditors — that statement should be stress-tested against the actual waterfall, not taken at face value.

Key dates creditors need on their calendars now:
• July 2 — Bidding procedures hearing
• July 8 — Bid deadline
• July 13 — Auction
• July 15 — Sale hearing
• July 31 — Target closing

The company has 572 retail locations and has already moved to reject leases at 44 non-operational sites. A&G Real Estate Partners is managing the footprint review.

Practical takeaway: Landlords holding unrejected Sleep Number leases should file protective proofs of claim immediately. The July 31 closing target compresses every post-sale window.
