⚖️ Today is Sleep Number's July 31 closing deadline — and it matters more than the headline number.

Sleep Country Canada's winning bid came in at ~$701M total consideration, up $286.8M from the $415M stalking-horse floor. That uplift is real estate value. But the residual-estate phase starts the moment the sale closes, and that's where unsecured creditor recoveries actually get determined.

Key mechanics to track now:

• **Sleep Number** (No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Paek) — Sale approved July 21. July 31 target close. Confirm via Kroll portal (restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber). A slip past today extends the DIP period and compresses the reserve available for unsecured distributions. Watch the APA schedules for assumed vs. excluded liabilities and the scope of any tariff-refund rights transferred prepetition — those disclosures are incomplete in the public record and affect the distributable pool.

• **BlockFills / Reliz** (No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan) — $3.25M Keyrock sale closed. Confirmation hearing ran July 13; order not yet independently verified. FCA approval for the U.K. entity transfer remains outstanding. Failure to obtain it could trigger APA purchase-price adjustments with downstream consequences for the liquidating trust.

The customer-asset segregation question in BlockFills is still the sharpest live issue: if the onboarding agreement language supports a constructive trust, affected customers may recover ahead of the general unsecured class. That theory needs to be preserved — not waived — in any confirmation injunction.

**Practical takeaway:** If you hold unsecured claims in either estate, today's action item is docket verification, not waiting for counsel updates. Check the Kroll portal for Sleep Number and Docket Nos. 539/541 on the BlockFills docket.
