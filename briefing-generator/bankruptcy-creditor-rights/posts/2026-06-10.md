# Bankruptcy Creditor Rights — Social Posts | June 10, 2026

## LinkedIn

Two rulings have quietly rewritten where creditor leverage lives in Chapter 11 — and the common thread is that drafting and consent now beat structural cleverness.

Serta Simmons (5th Cir.): The court held the 2020 uptier wasn't a permissible "open market purchase" under the credit agreement, refused to let equitable mootness shield the confirmation order, and excised the indemnification protecting participating lenders. Translation: a confirmed plan doesn't automatically extinguish breach claims from a coercive liability-management deal. But New York's Mitel reached the opposite result — so for now, the same uptier can be lawful or actionable depending on your forum and your covenant language.

Purdue (post-Harrington): The $7.4B Sackler settlement went effective May 1. After the Supreme Court killed nonconsensual third-party releases, this is the proof that a consent-based, opt-in structure can still deliver a global mass-tort resolution — at a higher price, and with more leverage for organized claimants.

The takeaway for creditors: audit your "open market purchase," pro-rata, and sacred-rights provisions now. Object to release and indemnification overreach before substantial consummation — not after. Text and timing are everything.
