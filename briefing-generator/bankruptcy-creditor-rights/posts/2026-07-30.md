## LinkedIn

$3.1 billion in debt. 60 days to get rid of it.

ASP Unifrax Holdings (Alkegen) filed a prepackaged Chapter 11 in the Northern District of Texas on July 26. The court cleared interim DIP financing — $315 million — on July 28 over opposition to the size of the interim draw. The plan already carries 99% of first-lien holders and 80% of second-lien holders. Target emergence: late September 2026.

Why now? The company's PIK-toggle options on its Senior Facilities and Second Lien Notes expire September 30. Once they lapsed, projected cash interest would have jumped roughly $110 million annually — pushing 2026 debt service to approximately $320 million. The October cliff forced the hand. The prepack is the fix: equitize substantially all funded debt, emerge with ~$200 million of liquidity, leave trade creditors unimpaired.

The structure is clean. The timeline is tight. Two watchpoints remain:

• Recovery, if any, for unsecured creditors below the trade line — the RSA terms don't resolve this
• Third-party release mechanics — the U.S. Trustee is already contesting opt-out releases next door in *Harvest Sherwood* (N.D. Tex. No. 25-80109, Aug. 5 confirmation hearing), and Texas courts are an active front in the post-*Purdue* release fight

If Alkegen confirms without a release dispute inside 60 days, that outcome will be a meaningful data point for how prepackaged Texas filings price confirmation risk going forward.

Practical takeaway: If you hold any unsecured claim against Alkegen outside the funded-debt stack, map your position against the RSA waterfall now — the confirmation window is short and your recovery class may not be obvious from public documents.
