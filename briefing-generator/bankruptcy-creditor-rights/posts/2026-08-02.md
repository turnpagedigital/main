# Chapter 11 — Social Posts | Sunday, August 02, 2026

## LinkedIn

$1.5 billion note. $102 million in cash. August 1 came and went without payment.

Hughes Network Systems — the EchoStar-controlled satellite broadband unit operating as HughesNet — missed its debt maturity yesterday and is preparing to file Chapter 11 within days. White & Case is bankruptcy counsel; FTI Consulting is financial adviser. Major noteholders retained Jones Day in early July, which points toward a negotiated restructuring support agreement rather than a free-fall, though no RSA has been confirmed publicly.

The operating picture is difficult. Subscribers fell from 1.56 million at end of 2020 to 681,000 by March 2026 — a collapse driven by Starlink's LEO buildout. That subscriber base does not generate the free cash flow needed to service any restructured debt load at meaningful scale.

Two creditor-rights issues to track closely:

• The $23 billion AT&T spectrum-sale proceeds closed July 28 at the EchoStar parent level. Hughes debt is ring-fenced at the subsidiary. Noteholders cannot reach those proceeds without substantive consolidation or fraudulent-transfer arguments.

• Hughes pays EchoStar $190 million annually to lease capacity on the Jupiter 3 satellite, which EchoStar owns. That intercompany lease is a live § 365 executory-contract issue and a likely focus of any creditor committee formed in the case.

Venue to watch: Southern District of Texas (Houston), consistent with the Dish Wireless prepackaged filing from June 30.

Practical takeaway: If you hold Hughes notes or have executory contracts with the debtor, the Jupiter 3 lease dynamic and the structural subordination to EchoStar parent proceeds are the two issues to pressure-test before the petition drops.
