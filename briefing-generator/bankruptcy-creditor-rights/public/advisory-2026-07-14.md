### LEDE — Sleep Number: Auction Closed Yesterday; Sale Hearing Is This Morning

The Sleep Number sale process crossed its most consequential threshold as of this morning's briefing. 
The July 13, 2026 auction — scheduled for 10:00 a.m. ET at debtor's counsel's offices — has passed, and the post-auction objection deadline falls today, July 14, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, with the sale hearing before Judge Kyu Young Paek in the Bankr. S.D.N.Y. set for tomorrow, July 15, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The auction result is not yet confirmed in whitelisted sources as of this writing; monitor PACER docket No. 26-11399 and the Kroll administrator page at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber for a notice of successful bidder.

The structural stakes are significant. 
The debtors ran a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period anchored to a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, generating 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals.
 
To displace stalking horse Sleep Country Canada as the winning bidder, any competing bidder had to clear an effective floor of $441.45 million — covering the $415 million base price, $16.45 million in bid protections payable in cash, and a $10 million minimum overbid.
 
The stalking horse receives a credit equal to the full bid-protections amount in each auction round, and a good faith deposit of $41.5 million was required; in the event of a buyer breach, forfeiture of that deposit is the estate's sole remedy absent fraud or willful breach.


The capital structure context is critical for unsecured creditors watching recovery prospects. 
Sleep Number Corporation reported $642.3 million in assets against $1.3 billion in liabilities, and the filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 
The $260 million DIP facility — comprising up to $65 million of new-money superpriority term loans and up to $195 million of roll-up loans — was provided by existing prepetition lenders, who filed on June 12, 2026.
 
The loans mature September 16, 2026 at SOFR + 8.00% or base rate + 7.00%, secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral, subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 The roll-up structure is the dominant creditor-rights issue here: prepetition secured lenders have effectively converted the bulk of their pre-petition exposure into DIP priority, narrowing the challenge window for any unsecured committee.


Parties in interest may investigate — but not prosecute — the prepetition liens and obligations within a $100,000 budget, with the Challenge Deadline running to the earlier of 60 days after committee formation (or 75 days after the Interim Order if no committee is appointed) and the commencement of the Sale Hearing.
 That window closes tomorrow morning when the sale hearing convenes — meaning the challenge period for any committee is effectively over unless the hearing is adjourned. Unsecured creditors should note that 
the company's purchase-card program terminates effective today, July 14, 2026, requiring a transition to a replacement program during the same week as the auction.
 Operationally, the business is in a critical transition period regardless of who wins the auction.


The company plans to sell its assets under the $415 million buyout offer from Sleep Country Canada Inc. and has 572 retail stores in the U.S.
 
The company attributes its distress to a confluence of headwinds it describes as a historic mattress-industry recession — a secular consumer shift toward e-commerce, compressed sector margins, reduced discretionary spending, elevated inflation and interest rates, and supply chain disruption.
 Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP represents the debtors, with Brian M. Resnick listed as lead counsel on court filings. (__[Kroll Restructuring Administration — Sleep Number Case Page](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber/)__)

The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.

The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested

The answer drives recovery percentages dramatically: if customer assets are estate property, general unsecured claimants share in a diluted pool; if they are segregated customer property held in trust, the $145 million in GUC exposure contracts materially. ### Power Block Coin (SmartFi) — Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041: Liquidating Trust Path Active, Celsius Claims Dominate the Stack

Power Block Coin, LLC — operating as SmartFi and pending before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in Bankr. D. Utah as Case No. 24-23041 — shows no new docket activity in the past 72 hours. The case entered its current posture in January–May 2026 following the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors' liquidation plan filing.

The creditor-rights picture here is dominated by a single claim concentration risk. 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.


Celsius's $133 million-plus claim — representing approximately 69% of total claims — creates a structural dynamic in which recovery distributions to the general unsecured creditor pool are largely a function of whether Celsius's claims are allowed, subordinated, or offset by counterclaims arising from pre-petition transactions between the two estates. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the debtor in connection with the Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy case (Case No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.


The committee's plan also surfaces insider-transaction issues material to any avoidance-action recovery. 
The committee raised concerns about pre-bankruptcy management citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC — an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah — which raised concerns about the loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 Avoidance claims against Solara and related insiders represent the most realistic source of incremental recovery for non-Celsius GUC holders. The case originated on June 20, 2024 and remains in the Subchapter V eligibility dispute shadow that was the case's opening procedural battle — but that issue has been resolved and the case is proceeding on a conventional Chapter 11 confirmation track. ### Broader Market Observations: Compressed § 363 Timelines and the Self-Priming DIP Problem

The Sleep Number structure illustrates a pattern that has become more pronounced in 2026 retail and consumer bankruptcies: the self-priming, roll-up DIP, where a prepetition lender group provides both new money and converts existing secured exposure into administrative priority, effectively locking in their recovery trajectory while the challenge window — typically 60–75 days — is compressed by a sub-45-day sale process. 
The Sleep Number sale presents a $257.5 million shortfall between the $415 million stalking horse bid and the $672.5 million in secured debt.
 That gap means the lender DIP group is absorbing a negotiated haircut, but the DIP roll-up structure ensures they control the distribution waterfall throughout. For unsecured creditors, the critical question in any comparable structure is whether the Creditors' Committee had sufficient time and budget to mount a meaningful challenge — and in Sleep Number's case, the $100,000 investigation budget and the Challenge Deadline's hard stop at commencement of the Sale Hearing make clear the answer is constrained.


Sleep Number preserved roughly $75.9 million in federal and state net operating losses and interest carryforwards; in a Section 363 asset sale, those attributes typically stay with the selling entity rather than transferring to the buyer, so their near-term utility is limited.
 For creditors in the unsecured class, the NOL value is a potential residual asset of the liquidating estate post-close — but only to the extent the selling entity has future income against which to offset them, which is unlikely in a liquidation context.
