### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Confirmation on the Doorstep


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11. The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371, before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The case has moved efficiently from freefall filing to confirmation in approximately four months — a compressed runway driven by the pre-negotiated structure and the Keyrock sale that closed the § 363 track.


The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.



The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt. The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


On the § 363 track, that sale is now behind the case. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property. Keyrock SA was declared the Successful Bidder for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors as of May 26, 2026.
 The sale hearing was set for June 16, and on the Verita case-administration site the Keyrock transaction appears to have cleared that hurdle; confirmation of the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan is what now sits on Judge Horan's docket for tomorrow.

The plan solicitation process reached its terminal milestones in July. 
The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. The Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. The Confirmation Hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the Confirmation Hearing, and establishing deadlines and procedures for objections, claim objections, and temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes [Docket No. 388].


The central question that has animated this case from day one — whether customer digital assets constitute estate property or are held in trust outside the estate — remains the most consequential unresolved creditor-rights issue in the proceedings. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive § 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 How the confirmed plan resolves that issue will set recovery watermarks for the ~$145 million general unsecured creditor pool. The filing history includes 15 new docket entries in the 72-hour window ending July 10, and the docket last moved on July 10 (Dkt. 515, 510), suggesting active pre-confirmation briefing and supplemental filings were still being lodged as recently as Thursday.

Distressed-investor note: 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 The $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price against $145 million of general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate on the order of 2–3 cents on the dollar from the § 363 proceeds alone, with any further recovery dependent on the liquidating trust and whatever residual estate value the plan preserves for the general unsecured class. Claims trading, if any, will be visible via Rule 3001(e) transfer notices on the Verita docket. Watch Dkt. 515 (filed July 10) and the July 9 Confirmation Hearing administrative filings for the final vote tabulation and any filed objections that survived the July 1 deadline.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Auction Day, Then Sale Hearing


On June 12, 2026, Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 of the Bankruptcy Code in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. The Bankruptcy Court granted joint administration under the caption *In re: Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399.
 
The cases are administered by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.


The capital structure is deeply impaired. 
Sleep Number Corporation reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 
The company enters bankruptcy with $672 million in debt, blaming tariffs and inflation for the filing.
 Prepetition counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick). 
The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — though that representation should be read with the lender-DIP structure in mind.

The DIP is aggressive. 
A group of Sleep Number's existing lenders will fund up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via a roll-up.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans mature on September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR + 8.00% or a base rate plus 7.00%. The loans are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral and are subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 
The sale is described as an expedited asset sale anchored by a $415 million stalking horse bid, financed by a self-priming lender DIP, drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt, with a same-day objection from the U.S. Trustee already on the docket.
 The U.S. Trustee's evidentiary challenge to the vendor relief and the priming/roll-up structure is a live issue that goes directly to the economics of creditor recovery.

On the sale process: 
the filing aims to effectuate a value-maximizing, going-concern sale of substantially all of the Debtors' assets under § 363, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., and supported by the lenders and agent under the Prepetition Secured Credit Facilities. Pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the Stalking Horse Bidder has committed — subject to Court approval — to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.



Building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties (19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals), the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, a July 15, 2026 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.


Tomorrow's auction (July 13, 10:00 a.m. ET) will determine whether any qualified bid surpassed the $415 million floor. The sale objection deadline was July 10 — the docket reflects 20 new filings in the 72-hour window through July 10, including a timely objection from Trailside Liberty Way, LLC (Dkt. 250, Jul. 10, filed by Jared Riley Clark). Landlord objections of this type typically contest the assignment or rejection economics on commercial leases; 
Sleep Number has 572 retail stores in the U.S.
, making the lease treatment a material value variable for the unsecured estate.


Near-term inflection points include whether a creditors' committee forms and how it engages the Challenge Deadline given the budget and the calendar, and whether the auction draws any qualified bid beyond the stalking horse, knowing a premium will not reach the unsecured class.
 
The company discloses significant risks related to its Chapter 11 process, including the likely cancellation of its common shares, and explicitly warns of the likely cancellation of its common shares.
 
Management explicitly warns that common shares are "significantly out of the money," expects a complete or significant loss for shareholders, and anticipates a Nasdaq delisting.


The §341 meeting has been continued to July 14, 2026 at 1:30 p.m. ET by Zoom — the day after the auction but the day before the sale hearing. The sequencing creates a narrow window for any committee or creditor group to surface issues before the July 15 approval proceeding.

## Recommended Actions

**BlockFills creditors** holding general unsecured claims should confirm their ballot was submitted prior to the July 1 deadline and review the Verita docket for the final vote tabulation, which should appear as a pre-confirmation filing in the July 9–12 docket window; if any confirmation objection was timely filed on or before July 1 and remains unresolved, watch Dkt. 515 (Jul. 10) for supplemental briefing or stipulated resolutions before tomorrow's 10:00 a.m. ET hearing before Judge Horan. Creditors with disputed or unliquidated claims should confirm their temporary allowance status under the May 28 solicitation order (Dkt. 388) before confirmation is entered. **Sleep Number creditors** — particularly trade creditors and landlords with 572 store leases in the mix — should review whether a formal unsecured creditors' committee has been appointed and, if so, whether any committee professional has lodged a Challenge to the lender roll-up and priming structure before the Challenge Deadline. The auction at 10:00 a.m. ET tomorrow is the single most consequential event for recovery economics: if the stalking horse bid of $415 million clears without a higher offer, the $257.5 million shortfall against $672.5 million in secured debt leaves minimal distributable value for the general unsecured class, and the U.S. Trustee's pending objection to the DIP priming structure should be monitored through the July 15 sale hearing before Judge Paek. The §341 meeting on July 14 at 1:30 p.m. ET provides a further window to surface factual issues. **Power Block Coin** claimants have no action items this cycle; resume monitoring on next new docket activity.
