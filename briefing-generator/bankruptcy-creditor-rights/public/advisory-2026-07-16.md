## Analysis & Developments

**The Sleep Number § 363 Sale Hearing Has Slipped — Again. The Adjourned Hearing Is Tomorrow, July 20.**

Yesterday's session carried the most consequential development on this beat: the sale hearing in *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, No. 26-11399 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), originally calendared for July 15 at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Kyu Young Paek, did not go forward as scheduled. 
The July 15 sale hearing was adjourned, and the court's docket — maintained by Kroll as claims administrator — reflects a consolidated adjournment of (i) the final DIP hearing, (ii) the hearing on the sale order, and (iii) related relief, all now rescheduled for **July 20, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET**.
 The adjournment is significant on two levels. First, it extends the debtors' DIP exposure: 
the case is funded by a DIP financing package of up to $260 million from the company's existing lenders, comprising $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via roll-up of prepetition debt.
 Every day the sale order is not entered pushes the debtors closer to the DIP milestone default trigger. Second, the adjournment raises the question of whether any unresolved sale objections — or ongoing APA negotiations — are responsible for the delay; the docket description does not specify the cause, and the court has not publicly explained the postponement. Interested parties should monitor for any amended APA or modified stalking horse terms filed ahead of tomorrow's hearing.

The structural backdrop for the July 20 proceeding remains unchanged from what we have been tracking. 
The case is characterized by a $257.5 million structural shortfall — a $415 million stalking horse bid financed by a self-priming lender DIP, set against $672.5 million in secured debt.
 
Pursuant to the stalking horse APA dated June 12, 2026, SNBR Inc. — the Sleep Country Canada affiliate — committed to acquire substantially all of the company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 Debtors' lead restructuring counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP. 
The Debtors cannot enter into, modify, or terminate any lease with annual rent exceeding $50,000 without the buyer's prior written consent, and no additional store closures are permitted outside the pre-agreed list during the sale period.

**Sleep Number — Creditor Recovery Mechanics and the Unsecured Stack**

The recovery picture for general unsecured creditors remains bleak. 
The petition showed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion against liabilities in excess of $1 billion.
 More precisely, 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors filed reporting $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities as of April 30, 2026.
 The gap between the $415 million stalking horse price and $672.5 million in prepetition secured debt means secured creditors will not be made whole at the stalking horse floor, leaving nothing for unsecured creditors unless the auction produces a materially higher bid — and the auction deadline of July 13 has already passed. 
The filing did indicate there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors,
 though the basis for that representation, given the capital structure math, is not evident from publicly available documents and should be verified against the waterfall analysis in the proposed sale order ahead of the July 20 hearing.

The APA's employee-acceptance closing condition is a distinct risk vector. 
The asset purchase agreement conditions closing on the buyer securing acceptances from 85% of business employees — roughly 2,482 of approximately 2,920 in the workforce.
 
That threshold turns the wages motion into a closing condition by another name; any wave of departures driven by uncertainty, delayed compensation, or morale could put the condition out of reach.


For landlords with rejected leases, the recovery cap under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(6) applies immediately. 
The debtors filed a rejection schedule covering 44 locations — all under the Select Comfort Retail Corporation entity — with rejection effective as of the June 12, 2026 petition date, representing approximately 8% of the total store portfolio. The landlords of these locations are immediately left with capped rejection-damage claims limited to the rent reserved for the greater of (i) one year or (ii) 15% of the remaining lease term (not to exceed three years' rent), plus any unpaid pre-rejection rent.
 
With closing targeted by July 31, 2026, the Designation Rights Period for surviving leases could extend into late September 2026, meaning the full picture of which locations survive under Sleep Country's ownership will not be known until months after sale closing.


The compressed creditor investigation window has been a persistent concern. 
Any official committee confronted a Challenge Deadline that effectively terminated when the sale hearing began on July 15 — only 33 days into the case — with a $100,000 challenge budget set against more than $672.5 million in prepetition secured obligations, sharply limiting practical ability to investigate lender conduct or DIP terms before sale approval.
 The one-day adjournment to July 20 provides nominal additional time, but does not meaningfully extend that investigative window.

**BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Post-Confirmation Posture, Docket Active**

The BlockFills case has moved decisively past its sale and is now in the post-confirmation phase. To recap the arc: 
On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities filed Chapter 11 petitions in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan.
 
Less than four weeks after the petition date, the debtors filed a Joint Chapter 11 Plan, Disclosure Statement, and Solicitation Procedures Motion on April 7, 2026, establishing a compressed timeline. The Plan featured a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a § 363 competitive sale process.


The sale track resolved first. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property. Keyrock confirmed that the official bankruptcy court document filed on May 26, 2026 declared it the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the Disclosure Statement and scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing the voting and objection deadlines [Docket No. 388].



The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. The Confirmation Objection Deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. The Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 Per the tracked-case ground truth, the docket reflected five new filings between July 13 and July 14, 2026 (Dkt. Entries 528–529 visible, filed July 14). The nature of those filings is not described in the available docket metadata, but the timing — one day after the confirmation hearing — suggests they may be confirmation order entries or post-hearing supplemental submissions. This is unverified and should be confirmed against the full CourtListener docket at No. 72494188 before making recovery assumptions.

The central unresolved creditor-rights question in BlockFills remains the customer property issue. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
The debtors also raised a procedural deficiency: the objectors had not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and had not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 The plan's treatment of this issue — whether customer claims receive enhanced recovery as "customer property" or are simply channeled into the general unsecured pool — will be the determinative factor for recovery rates on the approximately 
$145 million in general unsecured obligations reported by the debtors.


The recovery gap is severe. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that, 
the Keyrock purchase price is $3.25 million
 — a figure that, absent significant plan consideration from the NewCo track or estate causes of action, implies deeply distressed recoveries for general unsecured creditors. 
The Office of the United States Trustee for the District of Delaware appointed an official committee of unsecured creditors on March 27, 2026 pursuant to § 1102(a)(1) of the Bankruptcy Code [Docket No. 104].
 The committee's prosecution of any preference, fraudulent transfer, or fiduciary-duty claims against former management — including allegations that the former CEO urged certain clients to withdraw assets before the platform froze — could be the primary driver of any supplemental unsecured recovery.

**Contextual Notes: Crypto Insolvency Trend and Customer Property Doctrine**

The BlockFills matter continues to illustrate the unresolved tension in crypto insolvency between the exchange/broker's "property of the estate" position and customer-property segregation arguments that succeeded (in part) in *In re Celsius Network LLC* and *In re Voyager Digital Ltd.* In BlockFills, the commingling was contemporaneously disclosed and confirmed on the record. 
The operative customer agreement explicitly authorized BlockFills to transfer margin to exchanges and clearing houses at its sole discretion, and customers "rank only as a general creditor" upon third-party default.
 
Moreover, on January 16, 2026, Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours.
 Those facts — contractual subordination of customer claims combined with near-instantaneous commingling — are materially less favorable to customer-property arguments than the fact patterns in Celsius or Voyager. Distressed investors evaluating claim purchases should price accordingly.


The shortfall was reported as $77 million as of the end of 2025,
 which, combined with the $3.25 million sale price to Keyrock, implies that any material recovery above single-digit cents on the dollar for general unsecured creditors depends almost entirely on litigation recoveries — a binary, multi-year outcome.

The claims administrator for BlockFills is Veritaglobal, with documents available at [https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills](https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills). The claims bar date and any post-confirmation administrative deadlines should be confirmed directly on that portal.
