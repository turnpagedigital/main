# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Sunday, July 26, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The dominant development this week is the closure of the Sleep Number § 363 sale process.** On July 20, 2026, Judge Kyu Young Paek of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York approved the sale of substantially all assets of *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), to SNBR Inc. — a wholly owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc. — 
following a competitive auction, with Sleep Country making "significant improvements to its prior proposal."
 
Court documents place Sleep Country's winning proposal at approximately US$701 million in total value, including US$529.5 million in cash and the assumption of selected obligations.
 That represents a 69% premium over the original stalking-horse floor: 
the final proposal was substantially higher than Sleep Country's original US$415 million stalking-horse bid, with Reuters reporting the Canadian retailer agreed to increase its offer by approximately US$286.8 million through the auction process.
 
Brooklyn Bedding was selected as the backup bidder.
 Closing is expected by July 31, 2026 — watch for the § 363 sale order to be docketed and for any post-closing objections on cure amounts or assumed liabilities. Unsecured creditors should note that the Kroll restructuring site confirms the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before Judge Paek; 
the company reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities, and the filing indicates that funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 The gap between the $672.5 million secured debt load and the $701 million sale price suggests a thin but non-zero residual for the unsecured estate — the precise waterfall will depend on cure costs, professional fees, and the resolution of any remaining administrative claims. Post-sale plan or conversion proceedings remain the next procedural phase.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. — Confirmation Horizon


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for Chapter 11 relief; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371, before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The structural complexity of this case remains the central issue for distressed investors tracking it. 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


The Verita Global claims portal confirms the confirmation timeline has shifted materially. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the Confirmation Hearing, and establishing deadlines for objections, claim objections, temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes, and related relief [Docket No. 388].
 What was originally targeted as a June 22, 2026 confirmation hearing has migrated: 
a new date of September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET now appears on the Verita key-dates calendar,
 suggesting either a contested confirmation requiring additional briefing or ongoing negotiation among plan constituencies. The docket reflects six new entries in the 72-hour window ending July 24 (Docket Entries 533–538), though descriptions are not publicly accessible without PACER access.

The economic and legal architecture of the plan bears careful attention. 
The plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a § 363 competitive sale process.
 
Customer claims will be satisfied via a pro rata share of trust interests and liquid assets, with participating customers afforded an election to equitize their liquid recoveries into NewCo; the capitalization strategy includes a $15 million new money equity investment opportunity for accredited participating customers.
 The threshold legal question — whether customer digital assets pooled in commingled wallets constitute estate property or must be returned to individual customers — remains contested and unresolved on the public record. 
On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer,"
 which maps this case onto the same § 541 property-of-the-estate dispute that defined *In re Celsius Network* (Case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) and *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* (Case No. 22-11068, Bankr. D. Del.). Judge Horan's treatment of that issue at confirmation — or any pre-confirmation ruling on temporary claim allowance — will be the key substantive development to watch between now and September 11.


The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The Celsius secured position is a wrinkle that could affect NewCo asset transfer mechanics — a clean § 363 free-and-clear transfer requires either full satisfaction or consensual release of that lien. No public filing confirms resolution of the Celsius secured claim as of the search date.


Two prepetition customer actions remain in the background: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.
 — both asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims. Both are stayed by the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 pending confirmation. If the plan does not fully resolve these claimants' exposure through the liquidating trust, stay-lift motions or adversary proceedings in the Delaware court remain a plausible next step post-confirmation.

### Power Block Coin, LLC — Case No. 24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah — Committee Plan, Long Tail


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM); the plan was filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, and proposes to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company under Chapter 11 protection.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million; a key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors.


The case carries several features of interest to distressed investors. First, the Subchapter V eligibility dispute — litigated before Judge Joel T. Marker and decided in October 2024 — established that Power Block Coin had filed just hours before the Subchapter V debt limit reverted from $7.5 million to $3,024,725; 
with less than three hours to spare before the debt limit dropped, the Debtor filed electing Subchapter V, but then on July 4 filed schedules listing the vast majority of its hundreds of debts as contingent, unliquidated, or both, and with unknown amounts.
 Judge Marker's eligibility ruling (Bankr. D. Utah, Oct. 9, 2024) converted that procedural gambit into a contested legal issue, and the case was subsequently converted out of Subchapter V — producing the committee-driven plan now in confirmation proceedings. Second, the Celsius cross-exposure ($133 million in preference claims) means distributions in this case are partially dependent on the ongoing Celsius estate's treatment of those claims (Case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), adding binary risk to any recovery estimate. Third, docket activity confirms a new notice entry at Docket 532 (July 23, 2026) — content unverified without PACER — suggesting the case remains administratively active even at this late stage.

The present judge of record is the Honorable Cathleen D. Parker (the ground-truth file reflects the reassignment from Judge Marker). The claims administrator is not separately listed in the case file, and Greenberg Traurig serves as counsel for the committee. Creditors holding claims should monitor whether the Liquidating Trustee appointment has been finalized and whether any preference or avoidance actions have been commenced — the chapter11cases.com docket tracker flags July 19, 2026 as the publication date for a piece entitled "Pricing the Transfer: A First-Impression Framework for Valuing Appreciating Digital Assets in Avoidance Actions," suggesting that the framework for pursuing digital-asset avoidance claims in Utah and similar cases is actively developing in the practitioner literature.

### Broader Watch: § 363 Auction Dynamics and the Stalking-Horse Premium Signal

The Sleep Number outcome — where the stalking-horse bid functioned as genuine price discovery rather than a ceiling — is worth tracking as a data point for distressed retail and consumer-facing assets. 
An expedited asset sale anchored by a $415 million stalking-horse bid, financed by a self-priming lender DIP, drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt
 produced a winning bid of $701 million, implying the stalking-horse floor understated intrinsic value by a substantial margin. 
Because the debtor lines up a buyer and the outline of a deal before filing, the court process becomes a confirmation and price-discovery exercise rather than an open-ended search, which compresses the timeline and limits operational damage.
 For creditors evaluating claims in cases with similar pre-negotiated stalking-horse structures, the Sleep Number result reinforces that auction competition — even in a 38-day § 363 runway — can materially improve creditor recoveries over the stalking-horse floor, particularly where the stalking-horse is a strategic acquirer motivated by competitive positioning rather than pure financial return.

The DIP structure also merits note. 
The filing aimed to effectuate a going-concern sale under § 363, backstopped by a stalking-horse bid from SNBR Inc. and supported by the lenders and agent under the Company's Prepetition Secured Credit Facilities —
 meaning the DIP lender and the ultimate acquirer's corporate parent were economically aligned. That alignment reduces the probability of DIP lender obstruction but creates structural tension for unsecured creditors, who must assess whether the DIP terms extract value via roll-up protections or superpriority carve-outs before they can reach the residual. The post-sale administrative period — professional fee applications, cure claim resolution, and any remaining unsecured distribution mechanics — will determine whether the initial signal that "funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors" holds.
