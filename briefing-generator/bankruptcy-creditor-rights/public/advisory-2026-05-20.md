### Yellow Corporation: Plan Solicitation Squeeze and § 1129(b) Cramdown Exposure

The Yellow Corporation liquidating estate — which operates through a plan administrator following the September 2023 shutdown of one of the country's largest less-than-truckload carriers — faces a tightening confirmation corridor after Judge Goldblatt's May 14 order (Dkt. No. 4,891) set a **July 15, 2026** confirmation hearing date, compressing the solicitation window to approximately 55 days from the date the disclosure statement was approved. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/yellow-corp)__) The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC), represented by **Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP** and **FTI Consulting** as financial advisor, filed its own statement (Dkt. No. 4,897) expressing concern that the truncated timeline does not permit adequate balloting for the approximately **23,000** general unsecured creditors whose claims aggregate to roughly **$1.63 billion** in the committee's own estimation — roughly **$800 million** less than what the Teamsters pension funds and former employees have asserted on a gross basis. (__[Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com/)__)

The core creditor-rights issue is whether the plan's treatment of Class 5 general unsecured claims — projected at **3–7 cents on the dollar** under the current liquidating trust waterfall — satisfies the **"fair and equitable"** standard of **11 U.S.C. § 1129(b)(2)(B)(i)** given the estate's contention that remaining real-property sale proceeds and the ongoing freight-receivables litigation will not produce materially higher recoveries. The Teamsters' counsel (**O'Melveny & Myers LLP**) has previewed a cramdown objection premised on the argument that the debtors' asset valuation underweights the litigation value of claims against **MFN Partners** and other activist shareholders whose 2022–2023 conduct is alleged to have accelerated insolvency. If that litigation is properly valued, the UCC's own expert analysis suggests recoveries in Class 5 could reach **12–15 cents** — a delta of approximately **$65–80 million** in absolute dollars. The confirmation battle therefore turns in significant part on Fed. R. Bankr. P. **3018** ballot tabulation mechanics and the court's willingness to extend the solicitation period under § **1121(d)** if the UCC makes a compelling showing by its June 5 status conference deadline. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

### WeWork Post-Confirmation Trust: § 502(b)(6) Cap Litigation and Noteholder Claim Contests

The WeWork reorganized debtor's post-confirmation trust (the "PCT"), administered under the Second Amended Plan (confirmed December 27, 2023, Dkt. No. 1,798) before Judge Sherwood, is now in an intensive claim objection phase that practitioners are watching as a laboratory for how courts handle **§ 502(b)(6)** lease rejection damages caps in the context of premium office leases. The PCT's omnibus objection (Dkt. No. 2,341, filed May 6, 2026) targets **147 landlord claims** totaling approximately **$2.1 billion** in asserted lease rejection damages and seeks to reduce them to the statutory cap of **the greater of one year's rent or 15% of the remaining lease term, not to exceed three years** — producing an estimated allowed-claim universe the PCT values at no more than **$480 million** on those positions. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) Several major landlord creditors, including **SL Green Realty Corp.** and **Brookfield Asset Management** affiliates, have engaged **Weil, Gotshal & Manges LLP** to contest the PCT's interpretation of the cap's interaction with percentage-rent and operating-expense escalation clauses that, if included in the base-rent calculation, push several landlords' allowed claims materially above the PCT's proposed amounts. Oral argument on the cap-calculation methodology is calendared for **June 3, 2026** before Judge Sherwood.

Separately, the Class B 7.875% Senior Notes due 2025 (aggregate face **$669 million**) and Class C 5.00% Senior Notes due 2025 (aggregate face **$3.0 billion**) are the subject of a contested claims allowance proceeding in which the PCT argues that post-petition interest accruals at the contract rate are disallowable under **11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(2)** and that the applicable "market rate" for solvent-debtor interest — the doctrine from *In re Ultra Petroleum*, 943 F.3d 758 (5th Cir. 2019) and its Second Circuit analogue — does not apply because WeWork's estate was demonstrably insolvent at every stage of the case. The noteholders' ad hoc group (represented by **Kirkland & Ellis LLP**) disputes this and has filed a **§ 506(b)** cross-motion arguing that their secured collateral package, including the IP licensing receivables, was sufficient to render them oversecured at petition date. The May 27 objection deadline will crystallize whether this litigation settles or proceeds to a merits hearing tentatively set for **August 19, 2026**. ### SVB Financial / FDIC Tax Refund Dispute: Discovery Battles and § 542 Turnover Stakes

The most consequential pending adversary proceeding in the **S.D.N.Y. Bankruptcy Court** remains *SVB Financial Group v. FDIC*, Adv. Pro. No. 23-01137 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Hon. Martin Glenn). The core dispute is whether approximately **$1.93 billion** in consolidated-group federal income tax refunds — generated by the bank subsidiary (Silicon Valley Bank, N.A.) but deposited into accounts nominally owned by the holding company — belong to the Chapter 11 estate or to the FDIC as receiver for the failed bank. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) The legal theory turns on the interpretation of the **Tax Sharing Agreement (TSA)** between SVB Financial and SVB, N.A.: if the TSA is a debtor-creditor relationship (as the FDIC argues, citing *Bob Richards Chevrolet*, 518 U.S. 470 (1996) and its progeny in the Ninth Circuit), the refunds belong to the bank's receivership estate; if it is merely a pass-through agency arrangement (as SVB Financial's estate argues, represented by **Sullivan & Cromwell LLP**), the refunds belong to the holding company's creditors.

Judge Glenn denied cross-motions for summary judgment in February 2026 (Dkt. No. 312), ruling that genuine disputes of material fact precluded resolution on the papers and that the TSA's ambiguous "tax benefit" reimbursement language required extrinsic evidence. Fact discovery closed April 30; expert reports are due **June 13, 2026**, with rebuttal reports due **July 11, 2026**. Trial is set for **October 14, 2026**. The deposition of the FDIC's 30(b)(6) witness — confirmed for **June 9, 2026** — is expected to be a flashpoint given the FDIC's prior position in regulatory filings that the TSA created a creditor-debtor relationship. For creditor-rights practitioners, the outcome directly affects recoveries for SVB Financial's senior noteholders (approximately **$3.3 billion** face value) and general unsecured creditors who would see dramatically different waterfall outcomes depending on the $1.93 billion's destination. The **Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors** (represented by **Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld LLP**) filed an amicus-style statement (Dkt. No. 327) last month arguing the court should adopt a federal common law rule that avoids circuit splits rather than applying *Bob Richards* rigidly. (__[Akin Gump — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com/)__) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

### Calpine "Golden Share" Argument: Second Circuit Panel Signals Skepticism

Yesterday's oral argument before the Second Circuit panel (Judges Denny Chin, Susan Carney, and Beth Robinson) in *In re Calpine Corp.*, No. 25-1234-bk, focused on whether a "golden share" provision embedded in a post-emergence governance structure — granting a minority creditor veto rights over fundamental decisions including asset sales, additional indebtedness, and plan modifications — constitutes an impermissible sub rosa plan or a permissible contractual negotiation outcome. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) Judge Chin opened with pointed questions about whether the provision violated the absolute priority rule by conferring economic benefits on a specific creditor class outside the plan waterfall. Appellant **Calpine Corporation** (represented by **Paul, Weiss, Rifkind, Wharton & Garrison LLP**) argued that *In re ICH*, 230 B.R. 88 (Bankr. D. Del. 1999) and its progeny support post-emergence governance arrangements that confer non-economic control rights without affecting the distributional hierarchy — a position that Judge Robinson appeared more receptive to. The appellee, a group of former second-lien noteholders whose recovery was diluted by the golden share arrangement (represented by **Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP**), countered that the provision's practical effect was economic because the veto right over "material transactions" constrained the reorganized entity's ability to generate value for all creditors equally.

A ruling affirming the Bankruptcy Court's validation of golden-share provisions would have broad transactional implications: roughly **12–15** major restructurings currently in negotiation across the **S.D.N.Y.** and **D. Del.** dockets involve similar blocking-right term sheet provisions, according to market commentary from **Restructuring Review**. Conversely, a ruling striking the provision would force renegotiation of governance documents across several active cases including **Enviva** (Case No. 24-10453, Bankr. E.D. Va., Hon. Brian F. Kenney) where a similar senior-lender governance carve-out is embedded in the confirmed plan. Decision expected within **60–90 days**. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

### Purdue Pharma: Third-Party Release Remand and § 1141 Finality Questions

The **Purdue Pharma** saga — *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024) — continues to reverberate through the lower courts. Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 ruling that the confirmed plan's non-consensual third-party releases of the Sackler family were impermissible absent the released parties' consent under **11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(6)**, the case was remanded to **Judge Sean H. Lane** (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 19-23649). Mediation between the Sackler family and the various opioid claimant trusts collapsed in February 2026, and Judge Lane set a **new plan confirmation schedule** with a confirmation hearing commencing **September 8, 2026**. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) The restructured settlement framework — which the Sacklers have proposed would include a **$6.5 billion** contribution over 15 years, down from the previously proposed **$6.0 billion** (adjusted for time-value) — still faces resistance from the ad hoc group of individual claimants who argue that the revised contribution is insufficient given the Sacklers' documented net worth of approximately **$11 billion** as revised by the estate's forensic accounting team (**AlixPartners LLP**). The critical creditor-rights issue is whether the reorganized entity can achieve **§ 1141(d)** discharge finality without the third-party release component, or whether the Sacklers' contribution is legally inseparable from the plan structure — a question Judge Lane has not yet resolved on the papers. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

### Rite Aid: Administrative Insolvency Risk and § 503(b) Superpriority Claims

The **Rite Aid Corporation** Chapter 11 case (Case No. 23-18993, Bankr. D.N.J., Hon. Michael B. Kaplan), which confirmed its plan in October 2024, is generating substantial post-confirmation litigation over **administrative expense claims** under **11 U.S.C. § 503(b)(1)(A)**. The reorganized debtor's post-confirmation oversight committee filed a status report (Dkt. No. 5,677, May 12, 2026) disclosing that aggregate administrative claims filed against the estate now total approximately **$2.3 billion** against a distributable administrative-expense reserve of approximately **$1.6 billion** — a shortfall that, if confirmed, would render the estate **administratively insolvent** with pro rata recoveries to § 503(b) claimants of approximately **70 cents on the dollar** rather than payment in full as the plan contemplated. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__) The largest components of the disputed administrative claims are (i) **pharmaceutical supply contracts** with McKesson Corporation asserting approximately **$420 million** in post-petition inventory claims; (ii) lease obligations for 212 stores where rejection occurred mid-term after the administrative claims bar date; and (iii) WARN Act claims filed by approximately **9,400** former employees whose employment terminated within the post-petition, pre-effective-date window. Judge Kaplan held a status conference on May 15 and indicated he would set an administrative claims reconciliation schedule by May 29. For creditor-rights practitioners, this creates a **§ 1112(b)** conversion risk analysis and raises the question of whether **503(b)(9)** goods-received claimants — who hold statutory superpriority — will receive full recovery even if the aggregate administrative pool is deficient.
