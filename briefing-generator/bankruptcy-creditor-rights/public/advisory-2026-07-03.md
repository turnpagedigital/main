### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Sale Process Enters Critical Window


Sleep Number and four affiliated debtors initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, jointly administered before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek. The transaction is proceeding under § 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, with Sleep Country Canada serving as the stalking horse bidder.



Sleep Number Corporation reported $642.3 million in assets against $1.3 billion in liabilities. The filing indicates that funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 
The company entered bankruptcy with $672 million in debt, attributing the filing in part to tariffs and inflation.
 Counsel for the debtors is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (lead contact: Brian M. Resnick), per the Kroll restructuring administration docket. (__[Kroll Restructuring Administration — Sleep Number](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber/)__)


An auction is scheduled for July 13, with competing bids due July 8. Under the company's proposed timeline, the sale would close by July 31.
 
Sleep Number operates 572 retail stores in the U.S. under the stalking horse offer from Canadian bedding company Sleep Country Canada Inc., reported at $415 million.
 The petition date was June 12, 2026; 
a final hearing on certain first-day motions is scheduled for July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.



Section 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code authorizes the U.S. Trustee to appoint an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, and the debtors' petitions indicate creditors may be eligible for appointment.
 The U.S. Trustee solicited committee membership shortly after the June 12 petition date. Committee formation — and its composition — will be critical to monitoring whether the $415 million stalking horse price is being pushed up by qualified competing bidders or whether the auction at the July 13 date runs without a serious challenger. 
The company specifically blamed "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 That framing carries legal significance: tariff-driven distress arguments are increasingly appearing in § 1129 feasibility analysis and in DIP negotiations where lenders price in trade-policy risk.


The Southern District of New York bankruptcy court entered the interim DIP order approving DIP financing on an interim basis, through a final DIP hearing currently scheduled for July 9, 2026.
 
Sleep Number's obligations under the DIP loans are guaranteed by each subsidiary. The DIP claims carry superpriority administrative expense status and are secured by first-priority perfected liens on all DIP collateral to the extent unencumbered, plus priming security interests on prepetition collateral.
 For unsecured creditors, the priming structure is the primary recovery risk: the quantum of DIP obligations and professional fees drawn against a $415 million gross headline price will determine the net estate available for GUC distribution.


Sleep Number recently entered into a sponsorship agreement with NFL star Travis Kelce, making him a top shareholder with a 5% equity stake. It was not clear how the sponsorship agreement would be treated in the bankruptcy.
 The treatment of that equity arrangement — whether it constitutes an executory contract subject to § 365 assumption or rejection, or whether the equity interest is simply underwater — is unresolved. Distressed buyers will need to clarify this before the July 8 bid deadline.

The case is assigned case designation (KYP) — indicating Judge Kyu Young Paek's courtroom in SDNY — a relatively recent appointment whose approach to § 363 sale timelines and DIP milestones has not yet generated the volume of published opinions available on established SDNY benches. Watch for how Judge Paek handles any objections to bidding procedures at the July 9 second-day hearing.

### BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Post-Sale, Moving to Confirmation


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities, operating under the trade name BlockFills, filed Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026, in the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), with approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 (__[Chapter11Cases.com — BlockFills](https://chapter11cases.com/blogs/news/blockfills-parent-files-chapter-11-in-delaware-proposes-customer-led-reorganization-into-newco)__)


According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property. Keyrock confirmed that it was declared the "Successful Bidder" in an official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 (__[CoinDesk — Keyrock/BlockFills](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/01/crypto-investment-firm-keyrock-is-acquiring-bankrupt-lender-blockfills)__)

The sale hearing occurred June 16, 2026. Per the Veritaglobal claims administrator site, the next key milestone is September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) — most likely the plan confirmation hearing or a related deadline. (__[Veritaglobal — BlockFills Key Dates](https://veritaglobal.net/BlockFills)__) The docket showed 10 new filings in the 72-hour window through July 1 (docket entry No. 486 filed July 1, per CourtListener docket ID 72494188), though the entry descriptions are not yet populated in the live feed. Given the post-sale posture, those filings likely relate to sale-order implementation, plan-document circulation, or cure/assumption mechanics.


The central unresolved legal question throughout this case has been whether customer digital assets constitute estate property.
 That question is now taking on a different character in the plan phase: if Keyrock's asset purchase agreement characterizes transferred customer lists and trading infrastructure as § 363 "property of the estate," the corollary argument — that the underlying customer obligations flow with that property — will drive the liquidating trust's residual liability and the distribution percentages available to the approximately 2,000 institutional creditors. 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 With $145 million in GUC and $3.25 million in gross sale proceeds, the math on unsecured recovery is stark absent other estate value — the liquidating trust's forensic recovery actions (if any) and the scope of estate causes of action against pre-petition counterparties will determine whether the distributable pool moves meaningfully above the current floor.


In the weeks preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits alleged misappropriation of customer funds — one filed February 27, 2026 in the Southern District of New York, the second March 5, 2026 in the Northern District of Illinois. Both courts granted TROs restricting the debtors' operations.
 Those pre-petition adversary threads — whether pursued in the liquidating trust or settled as part of plan confirmation — remain the material wildcard for creditor recovery above a nominal floor.

### Subchapter V Debt Limit — Legislative Pressure Building, No Resolution Yet


Yesterday — July 2, 2026 — Law360 published an op-ed by Ted Gavin of Gavin Solmonese arguing that Congress must pass legislation to permanently restore the Subchapter V debt limit and clarify key points of law, warning that the tool risks becoming a costly procedural morass.
 (__[Law360 — Congress Must Resolve Growing Subchapter V Uncertainty](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy-authority/mid-cap/articles/2491382)__)

The underlying tension is straightforward. 
In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the Subchapter V debt limit was temporarily raised to $7.5 million, extended twice, before lapsing in June 2024.
 
As of January 1, 2026, the Subchapter V debt limit stands at $3,424,000.
 
On March 3, 2026, six senators — Grassley (R-IA), Durbin (D-IL), Cornyn (R-TX), Whitehouse (D-RI), Graham (R-SC), and Coons (D-DE) — introduced S. 3977, titled the "Bankruptcy Threshold Adjustment Act of 2026."
 
The bill sets the Subchapter V eligibility threshold at $7,500,000 and the Chapter 13 limit at $2,750,000, applying to all cases commenced on or after the date of enactment.
 (__[mediatbankry.com — S. 3977 analysis](https://mediatbankry.com/2026/03/12/a-new-bill-to-set-subchapter-vs-debt-limit-at-7500000-s-3977-with-a-chance-of-passing/)__)


The American Bankruptcy Institute and its Subchapter V Task Force are supporting the bipartisan legislation, introduced in the Senate by Judiciary Committee chair Charles Grassley and Rep. Ben Cline in the House.
 
ABI estimates that between June 22, 2024, and March 15, 2026, approximately 1,475 would-be Subchapter V debtors were rendered ineligible by the lower debt ceiling.
 (__[ABF Journal — ABI Backs Subchapter V Bill](https://www.abfjournal.com/abi-backs-bill-to-expand-subchapter-v-access/)__)


Subchapter V has far less oversight on payment plans after the case exits court, with enforcement often falling to creditors rather than debtors. Despite the lower limit, filings soared — February 2026 saw a 91% increase year-over-year, with 164 more filings than February 2025, per the American Bankruptcy Institute.
 (__[NACM News — Subchapter V debt threshold](https://bcm.nacm.org/lawmakers-propose-raising-debt-threshold-for-subchapter-v-filings/)__)

For distressed investors and claim-recovery counsel, the practical consequence of the current $3.4M ceiling is real: businesses in the $3.5M–$7.5M debt band must run a full Chapter 11 — with committee formation, § 1102 UCC overhead, professional fees, and reporting requirements — where Subchapter V would have permitted a leaner single-trustee reorganization. 
Had the $7.5 million limit been permanent from enactment with standard CPI adjustments, the current limit would stand at over $9 million.
 S. 3977 remains before the Senate Judiciary Committee; no floor vote has been scheduled as of this writing. The Law360 op-ed reflects practitioner frustration that the bipartisan bill has stalled despite near-unanimous notional congressional support.

Creditors in the Sleep Number case (26-11399, S.D.N.Y.) should immediately evaluate whether to submit expressions of interest ahead of the July 8 competing-bid deadline — the stalking horse at $415 million against $1.3 billion in liabilities implies a meaningful GUC haircut, and any upward bid pressure at the July 13 auction directly improves the unsecured distribution pool; landlords and executory-contract counterparties should accelerate § 365 cure-amount analysis before the August asset-transfer date. BlockFills claimants (26-10371, D. Del.) should file proofs of claim before the September 11, 2026 confirmation-adjacent deadline confirmed on the Veritaglobal key-dates page, and engage now with the liquidating trust framework — particularly on the customer-property vs. estate-property characterization — which will govern distribution entitlements; claims trading in this estate warrants attention given the 2,000-creditor base and potential for secondary-market price discovery well below face, as the $3.25 million gross sale price against $145 million GUC produces a recovery floor in the low single digits absent estate-cause-of-action proceeds. On Subchapter V, counsel advising small businesses with debt in the $3.4M–$7.5M band should build the legislative timeline into restructuring plans — if S. 3977 passes, cases filed after enactment become eligible for Subchapter V relief retroactively to the enactment date, which could materially alter the cost and timeline of a planned filing.
