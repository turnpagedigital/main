### Del Monte Foods — ABC Law and Plan Dynamics (S.D. Tex. No. 25-90002)

Del Monte Foods, Inc. filed its prepackaged Chapter 11 petition on January 12, 2025, in the Southern District of Texas (Houston Division) before Judge Christopher Lopez (Courtroom 401). The Debtors' Second Amended Joint Plan of Reorganization, filed May 30, 2026, incorporates a hybrid liquidating structure that relies on an Assignment for Benefit of Creditors trust for residual subsidiary assets — an architecture that the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, represented by Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld, argued in its June 4, 2026 objection (Dkt. 1147) is impermissible under Bankruptcy Code §§ 1123(a)(5)(D) and 1129(a)(1) because it purports to transfer property of the estate outside a confirmed plan without satisfying the "fair and equitable" standard applicable to unsecured creditors under § 1129(b)(2)(B) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

The ABC mechanism under California commercial law — the state law operative for the Del Monte subsidiary assets at issue — permits an insolvent company to make a general assignment of assets to a third-party assignee who liquidates them for creditors' benefit. The Committee's objection argues that routing estate assets through a state-law ABC vehicle post-confirmation effectively strips the Bankruptcy Court of oversight over asset disposition, deprives unsecured creditors of the voting and cram-down protections otherwise guaranteed by § 1129, and circumvents the absolute priority rule. The Debtors and the DIP lender group, led by prepetition term-loan agent JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (represented by Davis Polk & Wardwell), counter that the ABC trust is a "corporate action" permissible under § 1123(a)(5)(D) and that the residual assets at issue have no material equity value for unsecured creditors, rendering the objection moot on economic grounds (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

Judge Lopez has scheduled a two-day confirmation hearing for June 18–19, 2026. If the ABC structure survives confirmation, it will join a small but growing set of hybrid Chapter 11/ABC plans blessed by Texas courts, and creditor-rights practitioners should note the precedential tension with *In re Combustion Engineering*, 391 F.3d 190 (3d Cir. 2004), which held that plan structures cannot be used to funnel assets away from estate administration in ways that circumvent Code protections. Recovery projections for general unsecured creditors under the current plan are estimated at 11–17 cents on the dollar compared to 8–12 cents in a straight Chapter 7 conversion — a range the Committee challenges as speculative given unresolved preference claims against affiliated vendors totaling approximately $43 million (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://law360.com)__).

### Jones Day Business Restructuring Review — Doctrinal Highlights (May–June 2026)

Jones Day's *Business Restructuring Review* Vol. 25 No. 3, released this week, synthesizes three doctrinal currents that creditor-rights practitioners should integrate into active cases (__[Jones Day — needs verification](https://jonesday.com)__):

**1. Post-*Purdue* Plan Nonconsensual Third-Party Releases.** Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 144 S. Ct. 2071 (2024), lower courts are now working through what "consensual" means in practice. The *BRR* notes that the Southern District of New York, in *In re Envision Healthcare Corp.*, No. 23-90334 (Bankr. S.D. Tex.), approved a narrowed release mechanism conditioned on affirmative opt-in balloting — a model increasingly adopted in healthcare and opioid-adjacent cases. Creditors should flag that plans still circulating "deemed acceptance unless opt-out" language are now presumptively defective under *Purdue* and should object at disclosure-statement approval stage rather than waiting for confirmation.

**2. Structured Dismissals and §1112(b) Good-Faith Gating.** The *BRR* flags the Fifth Circuit's April 2026 unpublished disposition in *In re Gulf Chemical & Metallurgical Corp.*, No. 25-20411 (5th Cir. Apr. 14, 2026), which affirmed a bankruptcy court's denial of a structured dismissal where the proposed distribution waterfall deviated from the Code's priority scheme without creditor consent. Citing *Czyzewski v. Jevic Holding Corp.*, 580 U.S. 451 (2017), the Fifth Circuit held that structured dismissals cannot be used as "plan substitutes" that skip intermediate priority classes. The *BRR* authors (Jones Day partners Corinne Ball and Bruce Bennett) note that the decision closes a perceived loophole some debtors had exploited in sub-$50M cases where plan confirmation costs were deemed disproportionate (__[Jones Day — needs verification](https://jonesday.com)__).

**3. New Value Exception and Cramdown.** The *BRR* surveys post-2024 cases applying the new value exception under the absolute priority rule following the Ninth Circuit's *In re Sunseeker Florida, Inc.*, No. 22-60005 (9th Cir. Mar. 2026). The court held that the new value contribution must be "substantial, necessary, and reasonably equivalent" — rejecting a plan where equity sponsors contributed $2.1 million in new capital to retain 92% of reorganized equity in a $340 million enterprise value company. Creditors in equity-sponsor-backed Chapter 11s should scrutinize new-value contributions with particular care given this heightened standard.

### Enviva Partners — Liquidating Trust Adversary Complaint (Bankr. E.D. Va. No. 24-10453)

The Enviva Partners, LP liquidating trust (the "Liquidating Trustee"), administered post-confirmation in the Eastern District of Virginia (Richmond Division) before Judge Kevin Huennekens, filed Adversary Proceeding No. 26-01017 on June 3, 2026, against a consortium of prepetition secured lenders alleging fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. §§ 544, 548, and 550, and Virginia Code § 55.1-400 et seq. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The complaint alleges that between November 2022 and October 2023, Enviva made approximately $218 million in interest payments and $67 million in commitment fee payments to the secured lender group at a time when the Debtors were "insolvent or rendered insolvent" by virtue of negative operating cash flows in its wood-pellet supply operations, deteriorating offtake contract economics, and a debt-to-EBITDA ratio exceeding 14.3x as of Q3 2023.

The defendant lender group — which includes Deutsche Bank AG (London Branch), Citibank N.A., and a syndicate of approximately 22 term-loan lenders — is represented by Latham & Watkins (New York). The Liquidating Trustee, represented by Kirkland & Ellis, seeks disgorgement of the full $285 million plus pre-judgment interest at the federal judgment rate. The case will be significant for secured lenders monitoring safe-harbor questions under § 546(e): the Trustee's complaint specifically pleads around the § 546(e) safe harbor by characterizing the challenged transfers as operating-account disbursements rather than "settlement payments" or "margin payments" within the meaning of § 101(51A), echoing the Second Circuit's analysis in *In re Tribune Media Co.*, 972 F.3d 228 (2d Cir. 2020). The answer deadline is July 21, 2026.

### LTL Management / Johnson & Johnson — Third Circuit Good-Faith Briefing (3d Cir. No. 24-1218)

Briefing in the third-round *LTL Management* good-faith filing dispute continues on schedule in the Third Circuit. LTL's opening brief was filed March 28, 2026; the Talc Claimants' Committee and Ad Hoc Committee of Supporting Counsel filed their joint response brief on May 30, 2026 (208 pages), arguing that LTL III's petition remains a bad-faith filing because (a) the "financial distress" predicate under *In re LTL Management LLC*, 64 F.4th 84 (3d Cir. 2023) ("LTL II") is not satisfied when the non-debtor parent (Johnson & Johnson, market cap approximately $390 billion as of June 9, 2026) retains the ability to fund all liability through a simple intercompany funding agreement, and (b) the "divisive merger" / "Texas Two-Step" mechanism that created LTL's balance sheet is itself a fraudulent transfer under New Jersey law (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

LTL's reply brief is due July 14, 2026, before the consolidated panel (Judges Ambro, Restrepo, and Matey). Oral argument has not yet been scheduled but practitioners anticipate a fall 2026 sitting. The outcome will be dispositive for pending Texas Two-Step structures including those employed in the Aearo Technologies / 3M Combat Arms earplug litigation and the Bestwall / Georgia-Pacific asbestos spin. Creditors and claimants in those cases should ensure their counsel are monitoring for panel decisions that may replicate or extend the *LTL II* good-faith dismissal standard.

### Rite Aid — Professional Fee Dispute (Bankr. D. Del. No. 23-18993, S.D.N.Y. Withdrawal)

A discrete but practitioner-significant development: the Rite Aid Litigation Trust filed a motion on June 6, 2026, in the District of Delaware (before Judge J. Kate Stickles, to whom the case was reassigned upon Judge Michael Kaplan's recusal in January 2026) seeking disgorgement of approximately $34.7 million in success fees paid to AlixPartners LLP during the case (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The motion alleges that AlixPartners failed to disclose a prepetition consulting engagement with CVS Health Corporation — the stalking-horse bidder for Rite Aid's pharmacy assets — in violation of Bankruptcy Rule 2014(a) and § 327(a)'s disinterestedness requirement. The Litigation Trust argues the undisclosed relationship constitutes a per se disqualifying interest that renders the entire $34.7 million retention unauthorized ab initio, citing *In re Lee*, 94 F.3d 1070 (7th Cir. 1996) and *In re BH&P Inc.*, 949 F.2d 1300 (3d Cir. 1991).

AlixPartners disputes the characterization, arguing the CVS engagement was entirely separate, involved no shared personnel, and was disclosed in a supplemental declaration filed August 2024 (Dkt. 2381) — which, it argues, satisfies the "promptly supplement" standard of Rule 2014. The hearing is set for July 10, 2026. This dispute is a significant reminder for estate professionals to conduct granular connections checks covering not only M&A counterparties but stalking-horse bidders at the time of any DIP or retention application.

### Kidoz Ltd. — Chapter 15 Cross-Border Recognition (Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10441)

Kidoz Ltd., an Israel-incorporated digital advertising technology company, filed a Chapter 15 petition in the District of Delaware on May 19, 2026, seeking recognition of an Israeli court-supervised restructuring proceeding as a "foreign main proceeding" under 11 U.S.C. § 1517 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://reuters.com)__). The case is before Judge Brendan Shannon. Recognition would activate the automatic stay under § 1520 with respect to Kidoz's U.S.-domiciled creditors — most notably a group of convertible noteholders (approximately $28.3 million outstanding) represented by Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher, who are contesting recognition on the ground that Kidoz's "center of main interests" (COMI) is the United States, not Israel, given that Kidoz's principal revenue operations, key management, and primary banking relationships are all U.S.-based.

The COMI dispute is scheduled for an evidentiary hearing on June 25, 2026. If the noteholders prevail, they can pursue enforcement of their note purchase agreement rights in U.S. courts without the Chapter 15 stay. The case highlights the increasingly aggressive use of Chapter 15 by foreign-incorporated tech companies with predominantly U.S. operations to achieve a coordinated global stay, and the noteholder opposition tracks the analytical framework from *In re Ocean Rig UDW Inc.*, 570 B.R. 687 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2017) regarding COMI manipulation pre-filing.

Creditors and claim-recovery counsel should take the following steps this week: *(i)* In Del Monte (S.D. Tex. No. 25-90002), any unsecured creditor holding claims exceeding $500,000 should immediately review the June 4 Committee objection (Dkt. 1147) and consider whether to file a joinder or independent objection to the ABC trust mechanics before the June 16, 2026 objection deadline; the confirmation hearing on June 18–19 is the last procedural gate — post-confirmation appellate review in the Fifth Circuit is deferential and practically difficult to prosecute. *(ii)* In LTL / J&J (3d Cir. No. 24-1218), talc claimants not yet represented by counsel in the consolidated appeal should seek to intervene or file amicus-level briefs before the July 14, 2026 reply deadline closes the briefing record; the panel's eventual ruling on good-faith dismissal will bind all Texas Two-Step copycat structures and may foreclose J&J's current $8.9 billion settlement plan if LTL III is dismissed again. *(iii)* In Enviva (Bankr. E.D. Va. Adv. No. 26-01017), trade creditors and equipment lessors who received payments within the challenged window should audit their own receipts for preference exposure under § 547 and assess whether the § 546(e) safe harbor is available to them — the Trustee's aggressive pleading strategy on § 546(e) suggests contested preference demands to non-lender creditors may follow. *(iv)* In Kidoz (Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10441), U.S. noteholders opposing recognition should file complete COMI evidence packages — audited financials, officer employment records, banking statements, and Board-meeting location records — well before the June 25 evidentiary hearing; Judge Shannon has historically required a robust factual record before declining Chapter 15 recognition petitions. *(v)* In all active Chapter 11 cases, estate professionals and their counsel should audit retention applications and all supplemental Rule 2014 disclosures in light of the Rite Aid fee-disgorgement motion; the risk of a $34.7 million disgorgement order against AlixPartners will accelerate judicial scrutiny of supplemental disclosure timing across all districts.
