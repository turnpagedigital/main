### Hughes Network Systems — Imminent Chapter 11 Filing (EchoStar / HughesNet)

**Status as of August 2, 2026 — petition not yet docketed; filing reported as days away.**


Hughes Network Systems, the EchoStar-owned satellite internet provider operating under the HughesNet brand, is preparing to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection to address a $1.5 billion note due August 1, 2026. EchoStar co-founder Charlie Ergen has decided to place the unit into bankruptcy; as of March 31, 2026, Hughes held just $102 million in cash on hand.



The filing comes after Hughes saw its broadband subscriber base collapse from 1.56 million at the end of 2020 to 681,000 by March 2026, driven largely by competition from SpaceX's Starlink LEO constellation.
 The subscriber erosion is the operating story: Hughes earns on geostationary capacity in a market structurally migrating to low-Earth orbit. At 681,000 subscribers there is limited free cash flow to service any restructured debt, so the reorganization plan architecture — and what, if anything, emerges as a going concern — is the open question for creditors.


In early July 2026, major noteholders retained restructuring counsel from Jones Day to explore options ranging from refinancing and asset sales to a potential court-supervised reorganization.
 
White & Case is serving as bankruptcy counsel for Hughes, while FTI Consulting is acting as financial adviser.
 The noteholder / debtor counsel lineup (Jones Day on one side, White & Case on the other) is a conventional pre-arranged posture and suggests a negotiated restructuring support agreement is more likely than a free-fall; that said, no RSA or prepackaged plan has been publicly confirmed.


Hughes's filing would follow the earlier EchoStar subsidiary Dish Wireless's prepackaged Chapter 11 filing on June 30, 2026, which included a restructuring support agreement that facilitated a $23 billion sale of EchoStar's wireless spectrum licenses to AT&T. EchoStar closed the wireless spectrum license sale to AT&T on July 28, 2026.
 The AT&T transaction proceeds accrued at the EchoStar parent level; 
the Hughes debt remains ring-fenced at the subsidiary level, meaning the proceeds from spectrum monetization have not directly resolved the unit's approaching maturity.
 That structural subordination is the creditor-rights crux: Hughes noteholders cannot reach Dish Wireless proceeds absent substantive consolidation arguments or fraudulent-transfer claims based on value flowing from HughesNet's satellite infrastructure to EchoStar.

**What to watch:** (i) petition date and venue — Southern District of Texas (Houston) is the likely forum given the Dish Wireless precedent under the same EchoStar enterprise; (ii) whether an RSA drops simultaneously with the petition; (iii) whether the U.S. Trustee challenges the case as a prepackaged maneuver coordinated by a financially capable parent (EchoStar holds a multi-billion-dollar spectrum portfolio); and (iv) Jupiter 3 / EchoStar 24 lease treatment — 
Hughes pays EchoStar $190 million annually to lease capacity on Jupiter 3, which EchoStar owns
, making the satellite lease a significant § 365 executory-contract issue and a potential value-extraction vehicle the creditor committee will scrutinize.

### Legislative: Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026 — Texas Two-Step Bill

**No floor vote; bill in committee as of this writing.**


Senators Whitehouse (D-RI), Hawley (R-MO), Durbin (D-IL), and Representatives Sykes (D-OH) and Gooden (R-TX) reintroduced the Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026, a bipartisan bill that would deter corporations from using the Texas Two-Step divisive-merger maneuver to avoid mass-tort liability.
 The bill was introduced April 20, 2026 (H.R. 8393 / S. 4346). 
It is endorsed by the National Association of Consumer Bankruptcy Attorneys and National Association of Consumer Advocates.



The bill would amend title 11 of the United States Code to make the filing of a petition for relief under Chapter 11 that is objectively futile or in subjective bad faith a cause for dismissal of the case.
 The operative restructuring mechanic is the bad-faith presumption: courts would presume bad faith where a debtor (i) manufactured venue, (ii) filed to gain a tactical litigation advantage, or (iii) was formed as the product of a divisive merger within the prior four years. 
The legislation would also block stays of litigation against a debtor's non-bankrupt affiliates when the debtor engaged in a Texas Two-Step maneuver within the previous four years.


Reuters carried the story this week — the full article text was not extractable from the scan — and the legislative re-introduction is accurately captured in the Senate press release dated April 21, 2026 (__[Sen. Whitehouse Press Release, Apr. 21, 2026](https://www.whitehouse.senate.gov/news/release/whitehouse-hawley-durbin-sykes-gooden-reintroduce-bipartisan-legislation-to-end-texas-two-step/)__). The bill has not been scheduled for a committee markup. Its practical effect on pending cases (Georgia-Pacific, any new Two-Step filer) remains speculative absent enactment; the 119th Congress's track record on restructuring legislation does not suggest near-term passage.
