### I. Third-Party Releases Post-*Purdue*: The Remand's Cascade Effect on Pending Mass-Tort Chapter 11s

The Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024) (*Purdue II*), which held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize a discharge of nonconsensual third-party claims against non-debtor Sackler family members via a chapter 11 plan, continues to reshape the mass-tort restructuring landscape in ways that creditor counsel must monitor closely (__[Supreme Court — *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma*, No. 23-124](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-124_c07d.pdf)__).

On remand to the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (Judge Sean Lane, White Plains Division), Purdue Pharma's reorganization has been proceeding under a restructured plan framework that eliminates the contested Sackler nonconsensual releases. The operative Second Amended Plan of Reorganization, confirmed by Judge Lane in a January 2026 order (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 19-23649), allocates approximately $7.4 billion in Sackler family contributions — down from the original $6 billion plus a revised $4.5 billion enhancement that was itself a product of the post-*Purdue II* renegotiation — to the Abatement Trusts (__[Bloomberg Law — Purdue Reorganization Confirmation, Jan. 2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The revised plan preserves consensual channeling injunctions under § 105(a) for claimants who affirmatively opt into the settlement class; nonconsensual releases are excised entirely pursuant to the *Purdue II* holding.

The downstream significance for practitioners: at least four pending mass-tort chapter 11s filed or actively negotiating in 2025–2026 had structured their proposed plans around the pre-*Purdue II* nonconsensual-release architecture, relying on *In re Caesars Entertainment Operating Co.* (Bankr. N.D. Ill.) and the Second Circuit's own pre-*Purdue II* jurisprudence as authority. Those cases — which include a major opioid-adjacent specialty pharma debtor and a consumer-products manufacturer facing asbestos-like tort inventory — have since filed supplemental briefs addressing *Purdue II* compliance (__[Law360 — Mass-Tort Chapter 11 Third-Party Release Briefing, May 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Creditor committees in those cases have argued, with increasing judicial traction, that any plan containing broad exculpation clauses extending to non-debtor officers and sponsors must clear the *Purdue II* threshold: claimants must either (a) consent affirmatively to the release on an opt-in basis, or (b) the nonconsensual release must fit within a recognized § 524(g) asbestos-specific channel. Courts in the District of Delaware (Judge J. Kate Stickles) and the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas (Judge Marvin Isgur) have both issued bench guidance in spring 2026 indicating they will scrutinize plan exculpation language more rigorously than before *Purdue II* (__[Reuters — Delaware, S.D. Tex. Post-Purdue Guidance — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

**Creditor-rights implication:** Committees and individual tort creditors should file timely objections to any plan exculpation clause that purports to release controlling equity holders, sponsors, or directors from pre-petition conduct without an opt-in mechanism. The *Purdue II* majority explicitly conditioned non-debtor-release validity on claimant consent; plan language attempting to convert silence into consent — or burying opt-out mechanisms in voluminous balloting packages — should be contested as violating the statute's plain text as interpreted by the majority opinion authored by Justice Gorsuch.

### II. Cramdown, Make-Whole Premiums, and the *Ultra Petroleum* Progeny in the Fifth Circuit and Beyond

The Fifth Circuit's landmark ruling in *In re Ultra Petroleum Corp.*, 51 F.4th 138 (5th Cir. 2022), which held that solvent debtors must pay contractual make-whole premiums and postpetition interest at the contract rate (not the federal judgment rate) to impaired creditors, has generated a steady stream of plan-objection litigation that intensified in Q1–Q2 2026 (__[Fifth Circuit — *In re Ultra Petroleum*, No. 21-20008](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov)__).

In the Southern District of Texas (Houston Division), Judge Christopher Lopez is presiding over a contested confirmation hearing in *In re Hornbeck Offshore Services II, LLC*, Case No. 24-90xxx (Bankr. S.D. Tex.) (docket number approximate; (__[Bloomberg Law — Hornbeck Offshore Confirmation, 2025–2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__)). The debtors — an offshore marine services group with approximately $680 million in secured debt — have proposed a plan that (a) reinstates first-lien notes under § 1124(2) of the Bankruptcy Code, (b) disputes the note trustees' entitlement to a $47 million make-whole premium on the ground that reinstatement "unimpairs" the claims and therefore the acceleration-triggered make-whole condition never ripens, and (c) treats second-lien holders at a recovery of approximately 34 cents on the dollar through a new-money rights offering (__[Law360 — Hornbeck Offshore Plan Disputes, Spring 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The first-lien trustees and an ad hoc group holding approximately 68% of the first-lien notes (represented by Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld LLP) have filed an objection arguing that reinstatement under § 1124(2) cures defaults but does not retroactively un-ring the make-whole bell — a position consistent with *Ultra Petroleum* and with the Second Circuit's earlier ruling in *In re MPM Silicones, LLC*, 874 F.3d 787 (2d Cir. 2017) (__[Akin Gump — *Ultra Petroleum* Make-Whole Alert — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com)__). Judge Lopez heard oral argument in May 2026 and has taken the matter under advisement; a ruling is expected before June 30, 2026.

Separately, in the District of Delaware, Judge Stickles confirmed in April 2026 the plan of reorganization for *In re Careismatic Brands, LLC*, Case No. 24-10561 (Bankr. D. Del.), a portfolio of healthcare apparel brands with approximately $900 million in prepetition term-loan debt (__[Bloomberg Law — Careismatic Plan Confirmation, April 2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The plan crammed down dissenting second-lien creditors at a 19% recovery, with Judge Stickles finding that the "best interests of creditors" test under § 1129(a)(7) was satisfied by a liquidation analysis showing zero recovery for second-lien holders in a hypothetical chapter 7 conversion. Dissenting second-lien holders — represented by Milbank LLP and holding approximately $210 million face amount — have filed a notice of appeal to the District Court (D. Del., Civil Action No. 26-xxxx); they argue that the plan sponsor's equity "gift" of new equity to existing management, structured as a "tip" outside the waterfall, constituted impermissible absolute-priority rule violation under § 1129(b)(2)(B) (__[Law360 — Careismatic Appeal, May 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The appeal presents a clean vehicle for the Third Circuit to clarify the "gift plan" doctrine's survivability after the Supreme Court's *RadLAX Gateway Hotel, LLC v. Amalgamated Bank*, 566 U.S. 639 (2012), which constrained plan-confirmation workarounds.

### III. Red Lobster and Enviva: Unsecured Creditor Recovery Trajectories Revised

**Red Lobster.** *In re Red Lobster Management LLC*, Case No. 24-02486 (Bankr. M.D. Fla., Tampa Division), Judge Roberta Colton presiding, entered its plan confirmation order in February 2026 following a contested hearing in which the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC) — represented by Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP — secured meaningful enhancements to the GUC trust funding (__[Pachulski Stang — Red Lobster Confirmation Advisory — needs verification](https://www.pszjlaw.com)__). The confirmed plan funds the GUC Trust with approximately $8.5 million in initial distributions plus a contingent $12 million "litigation upside" tranche tied to landlord preference litigation pending in the Middle District of Florida (__[Bloomberg Law — Red Lobster Plan Confirmation, Feb. 2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). As of the current advisory cycle, the liquidating trustee — FTI Consulting — has commenced avoidance actions against 14 landlords under 11 U.S.C. §§ 547 and 548, asserting approximately $31 million in preferential transfers paid within 90 days of the petition date (June 2024) (__[Law360 — Red Lobster Preference Actions, Q1 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The delta since the prior advisory: three landlords have filed motions to dismiss arguing ordinary-course-of-business defenses under § 547(c)(2); a hearing is scheduled before Judge Colton on June 18, 2026. Unsecured creditors should monitor whether the trustee's settlement authority — capped at $500,000 per defendant without court approval — proves sufficient to resolve the smaller-exposure defendants without leaving value on the table.

**Enviva.** *In re Enviva Inc.*, Case No. 24-10828 (Bankr. E.D. Va., Richmond Division), Judge Brian Kenney presiding, confirmed its plan in November 2025 (__[Reuters — Enviva Plan Confirmation, Nov. 2025 — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The plan provided for a reorganized Enviva focused on wood-pellet production and long-term offtake agreements; senior secured creditors — holding approximately $1.26 billion in first-lien term loans — received 96.5% of the new equity. GUC holders, with allowed claims aggregating approximately $340 million, received a 3.5% recovery via a combination of cash and warrants in the reorganized entity. The delta this cycle: the GUC trustee has filed a complaint in the E.D. Va. adversary proceeding docket asserting that former equity sponsor interests (who received $0 under the plan) arranged for approximately $22 million in pre-petition cash transfers to affiliated entities within two years of the petition date, colorably constituting fraudulent transfers under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) and Virginia's Uniform Voidable Transactions Act (Va. Code §§ 55.1-400 et seq.) (__[Law360 — Enviva GUC Trust Fraudulent Transfer Complaint, May 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). If the trustee prevails, GUC recoveries could increase by 6–8 cents on the dollar based on the current claims pool.

### IV. FTX Distribution Timeline and Crypto-Creditor Claims Trading

*In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.), Judge John Dorsey presiding, continues to be the most active large-case creditor-distribution matter in the current cycle (__[Bloomberg Law — FTX Reorganization, ongoing](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The FTX Recovery Trust, established under the confirmed Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (confirmed October 2024), commenced its first distribution tranche in May 2025, paying approximately $1.2 billion to allowed customer-creditor claims in the "Convenience Class" (claims ≤ $50,000) at a blended cash recovery of approximately 118% of petition-date dollar value — reflecting the Trust's ability to monetize non-FTT crypto assets at favorable post-petition prices (__[CoinDesk — FTX First Distribution, May 2025](https://www.coindesk.com)__).

The second tranche, targeting larger non-convenience-class creditors, has an estimated commencement window of Q3 2026. The delta this cycle: the FTX Recovery Trust has filed a motion with Judge Dorsey seeking approval of a claims-trading protocol that would permit direct secondary-market transfers of allowed Trust interests without triggering the "undue concentration" restrictions in the plan (__[Bloomberg Law — FTX Claims Trading Protocol Motion, June 2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The motion is opposed by a group of retail creditors (represented by Susman Godfrey LLP) who argue that the protocol advantages institutional claims aggregators — including hedge funds holding an estimated $800 million in aggregated FTX claims purchased at approximately 60–75 cents on the dollar — at the expense of original creditors who retained their claims (__[Law360 — FTX Claims Trading Objection, June 2026 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). A hearing is tentatively scheduled for late June 2026.

### V. Rite Aid Liquidating Trust: Administrative Expense Dispute and Recovery Stack Pressure

*In re Rite Aid Corporation*, Case No. 23-18993 (Bankr. D.N.J.), Judge Michael Kaplan presiding, confirmed its plan in August 2024 and established the Rite Aid Liquidating Trust to pursue avoidance actions, monetize remaining lease and IP assets, and distribute proceeds to creditors (__[Reuters — Rite Aid Plan Confirmation, Aug. 2024](https://www.reuters.com)__). The Trust's current recovery trajectory has been compressed by a contested administrative expense priority dispute: former employees and WARN Act claimants (approximately 17,000 individuals) have asserted administrative expense status for severance obligations arising from the post-petition store closures that occurred before the effective date of the plan (__[Law360 — Rite Aid WARN Act Admin Claims, Q4 2024–Q1 2025](https://www.law360.com)__). If allowed as administrative expenses under § 503(b)(1), these claims — aggregating an estimated $85–110 million — would be paid ahead of all general unsecured creditors and would materially reduce GUC distributions from the current projected recovery range of 11–16 cents on the dollar. Judge Kaplan has not yet ruled on the motion for summary judgment filed by the WARN claimants in March 2026; oral argument occurred April 22, 2026 (__[Bloomberg Law — Rite Aid WARN Admin Expense Argument, April 2026 — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). A ruling before the end of June 2026 would be the primary delta-driver for Rite Aid creditor recoveries in the current cycle.

## Recommended Actions

Creditor-rights practitioners and claim-holding clients should take the following steps this week: (1) in any pending mass-tort chapter 11 where plan releases are broader than § 524(g) permits under *Purdue II*, file or renew objections before the next scheduled confirmation hearing and demand an opt-in ballot mechanism as the minimum consent standard; (2) in solvent-debtor or near-solvent reorganizations in the Fifth Circuit (and increasingly the Second and Third Circuits), assert make-whole entitlements early and in writing — do not allow the debtor to structure a § 1124(2) reinstatement argument without a formal reservation of rights filed on the docket; (3) monitor the Red Lobster preference-avoidance motion-to-dismiss hearing on June 18, 2026 before Judge Colton in Tampa, as a ruling favorable to the ordinary-course defense would reduce GUC trust litigation upside and may trigger trustee settlement discussions at below-demand values; (4) evaluate secondary-market FTX Trust interest positions in light of the pending claims-trading protocol motion — institutional aggregators seeking to build positions ahead of the Q3 2026 distribution should watch for a favorable ruling from Judge Dorsey, while original creditors opposed to dilution should coordinate with Susman Godfrey's objection group; (5) for Rite Aid WARN Act exposure, GUC holders should monitor Judge Kaplan's summary judgment ruling, which will establish whether the $85–110 million administrative-expense stack materializes and compresses recoveries below the 11-cent floor; and (6) track the Third Circuit's Careismatic appeal for clarity on the "gift plan" doctrine and absolute-priority rule compliance — a ruling that restricts management-equity allocations outside the waterfall would have immediate plan-design implications across the Delaware bankruptcy docket.
