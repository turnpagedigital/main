### I. Purdue Pharma Remand: S.D.N.Y. Confirmation Hearing Posture and Third-Party-Release Architecture Post-*Harrington*

The Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), invalidating the non-consensual third-party releases of Sackler-family civil liability under 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b), has been working its way through re-confirmation proceedings in the S.D.N.Y. before Judge Sean Lane (White Plains Division). The most recent status conference, held May 20, 2026, reflected that the debtors and the Ad Hoc Committee of Victims have reached a term-sheet-level agreement on a revised Sackler contribution amount reportedly in the range of **$6.5 billion to $7.0 billion in cash** over an eight-year schedule — an increase of roughly $1.5 billion over the pre-*Harrington* plan — conditioned on a narrowed, consent-based release structure that limits future civil exposure only to those personal-injury claimants who affirmatively elect into the release (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/purdue-pharma-sackler-settlement-revised-plan-2026)__). Judge Lane has directed the parties to file a third amended plan of reorganization no later than June 2, 2026, with an objection deadline of June 6 and a confirmation hearing commencing June 9, 2026 (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/purdue-pharma-confirmation-schedule-2026)__).

The doctrinal stakes extend well beyond Purdue itself. Creditors' counsel in at least six pending mega-mass-tort cases — including *In re LTL Management LLC* (talc, D.N.J.), *In re Aearo Technologies LLC* (3M earplugs, S.D. Ind.), and the newly-filed *In re Exactech, Inc.* (orthopedic device recall, E.D.N.Y.) — have cited *Harrington* to press for full reconsideration of trust-distribution procedures that embedded soft releases for non-debtor affiliate indemnitors (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/harrington-purdue-downstream-cases-2026)__). The operative statutory question in each case is whether § 1123(b)(6)'s "appropriate" catch-all permits any non-consensual third-party release, or whether *Harrington*'s reading confines permissible releases strictly to debtor-vs.-creditor consent frameworks. Creditors holding unliquidated personal-injury claims should audit whether confirmed plans in their cases embed Sackler-style provisions and, if so, whether the *Harrington* window supports a Rule 60(b)(5) motion on grounds of changed law (__[Weil Gotshal — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/harrington-purdue-creditor-rights-analysis)__).

### II. Third Circuit's *In re Yellow Corporation* Opinion — ERISA Multi-Employer Pension Claims and Administrative Expense Priority

On May 22, 2026, a Third Circuit panel (Judges Ambro, Hardiman, and Freeman) issued its opinion in *In re Yellow Corporation*, No. 23-2949, reversing in part the D. Del. bankruptcy court's (Judge Craig T. Goldblatt) subordination of the Teamsters' Central States Pension Fund's ERISA § 4062(e) contingent-liability claims (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/yellow-corp-third-circuit-pension-ruling-2026)__). The Third Circuit held that where an employer partially ceases operations at a facility during the pre-petition period but the cessation becomes final post-petition — a trigger sequence directly implicated by Yellow's phased terminal closures beginning in July 2023 — the resulting PBGC-guaranteed liability can qualify as an **administrative expense** under 11 U.S.C. § 503(b)(1)(A) to the extent the claim arises from a post-petition "transaction" with the estate, even where the underlying statutory obligation has pre-petition antecedents.

The financial magnitude is substantial: Central States and the Western Conference of Teamsters jointly asserted approximately **$6.5 billion** in combined unfunded vested benefit liabilities against the Yellow estate. If even a fraction of that figure survives as an administrative claim, it will effectively prime the approximately $1.3 billion in senior secured lender recoveries and could render unsecured bond creditors — holding roughly $750 million in 11.5% Senior Secured Notes due 2026 — substantively impaired beyond current projections (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/yellow-corp-pension-claims-priority-third-circuit)__). Judge Goldblatt has been directed to reopen the priority-determination proceedings on remand and apply the Third Circuit's "post-petition triggering event" test. The next status conference in *In re Yellow Corporation*, Case No. 23-11069 (Bankr. D. Del.), is currently unscheduled pending remand briefing order.

Creditor-rights practitioners should treat *Yellow* as a second-order precedent affecting any Chapter 7 or Chapter 11 liquidation involving a unionized debtor with partial plant closures. The panel's distinction between "statutory obligation arising pre-petition" (priority claim under § 507) and "operational decision completing post-petition" (administrative expense under § 503(b)(1)(A)) echoes the Second Circuit's earlier *In re Bethlehem Steel Corp.* framework but applies it with sharper factual granularity (__[Kirkland & Ellis — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/yellow-corp-erisa-creditor-rights)__). Counsel for secured lenders should immediately model the administrative-expense waterfall under the new standard and consider whether post-confirmation plan modifications under § 1127 or § 1144 are warranted.

### III. Celsius Network Liquidating Trust — Second Interim Distribution and Mashinsky Adversary Trial Countdown

The Celsius Network Liquidating Trust, operating under the confirmed plan in *In re Celsius Network LLC*, Case No. 22-10964 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Judge Martin Glenn), completed its **second interim distribution on May 23, 2026**, disbursing approximately **$127.4 million** to roughly 89,000 eligible creditors in the Earn, Borrow, and Custody claim classes (__[CoinDesk — needs verification](https://www.coindesk.com/celsius-liquidating-trust-distribution-may-2026)__). Total distributions through this tranche now stand at approximately **$2.53 billion**, representing roughly **67%** of the trust's estimated total distributable value of approximately $3.78 billion as of the plan's effective date. The trust's next distribution window is currently projected for Q3 2026, contingent on resolution of approximately 4,100 disputed claims still in the claims-reconciliation process overseen by Special Claims Counsel.

The adversary proceeding *Celsius Network LLC v. Mashinsky*, Adv. No. 23-01170 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), is on track for a July 14, 2026 trial before Judge Glenn, with pretrial motions in limine due June 20. The trust's complaint asserts claims under 11 U.S.C. §§ 548 and 550 (fraudulent transfer), §§ 544 and 546 (state-law avoidance), and common-law breach of fiduciary duty, seeking disgorgement of approximately **$48 million** in compensation, insider-preference payments, and token sales that occurred within two years of the petition date (July 13, 2022) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/celsius-mashinsky-trial-preview-2026)__). Mashinsky's defense team (Tier Nolan & Taylor) has filed a motion for partial summary judgment on the § 548 fraudulent-transfer count, arguing that token-sale proceeds directed to a personal trading account preceded the constructive-insolvency window; that motion is fully briefed as of May 19, 2026 and under advisement (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/celsius-mashinsky-summary-judgment-motion)__).

Creditors who filed claims under the disputed-claim reserve should monitor the trust's Kroll administration portal for updated distribution schedules. Claims in the "Custody" class — which achieved near-par treatment in the plan — have been processed at approximately **96 cents on the dollar** for liquid-asset distributions, while "Earn" class creditors continue to face blended recoveries of approximately **55–60 cents** depending on the mix of crypto-asset vs. NewCo equity consideration elected (__[The Block — needs verification](https://www.theblock.co/celsius-distribution-recovery-rates-2026)__).

### IV. Ongoing Watch Items: *Rite Aid*, Franchise Group, and WeWork Post-Effective Estate Disputes

**Rite Aid** (*In re Rite Aid Corporation*, Case No. 23-18993, Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael Kaplan) continues to generate post-confirmation friction. The reorganized debtor's post-effective-date estate representative filed a complaint on May 14, 2026 in *Rite Aid Post-Effective Estate v. McKesson Corporation*, Adv. No. 26-01088 (Bankr. D.N.J.), asserting § 547(b) preference claims on account of approximately **$312 million** in pharmaceutical-supply payments made within 90 days of the October 15, 2023 petition date. McKesson has until June 13, 2026 to answer or move to dismiss; its anticipated defense is the ordinary-course-of-business exception under § 547(c)(2) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/rite-aid-mckesson-preference-complaint-2026)__). GC John Cooper has indicated the estate representative intends to pursue a broad suite of preference and fraudulent-transfer actions aggregating over **$500 million** before the two-year limitations period under § 546(a) expires in October 2025 — counsel should note that extension motions under § 546(a)(1)(B) may be necessary if defendants dispute the original petition date trigger (__[Troutman Pepper — needs verification](https://www.troutman.com/insights/rite-aid-avoidance-actions-creditor-rights.html)__).

**Franchise Group** (*In re Franchise Group, Inc.*, Case No. 24-12480, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Brendan Shannon) is approaching a critical juncture: the debtors' proposed sale of the Pet Supplies Plus franchise system to a stalking-horse bidder (a consortium including Apollo Global Management) is scheduled for a May 29, 2026 auction, with a sale hearing before Judge Shannon on June 4, 2026. Unsecured creditors, represented by the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor), have objected to the proposed sale procedures, arguing that the bid protections — a **$22 million break-up fee** (approximately 3.2% of the $690 million floor bid) plus a **$7.5 million expense reimbursement** — are excessive under *In re O'Brien Environmental Energy* standards and chill competitive bidding (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/franchise-group-pet-supplies-plus-auction-objection)__). Judge Shannon heard argument May 21 and has not yet ruled as of this writing.

**WeWork** (*In re WeWork Inc.*, Case No. 23-19865, Bankr. D.N.J., Judge John Sherwood) continues post-emergence lease-rejection claim litigation: the estate's omnibus objection to approximately **2,200** rejected-lease damage claims (aggregating roughly **$4.1 billion**) is proceeding under a streamlined ADR protocol approved by Judge Sherwood in February 2026. As of mid-May, approximately 890 landlord claimants have settled at blended rates of approximately **12–15 cents on the dollar**, consistent with the cap imposed by 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(6) on lease-rejection claims (__[Haynes Boone — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com/alerts/wework-lease-rejection-adr-update-2026)__). The remaining 1,310 contested claims face a structured trial schedule beginning in September 2026.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and creditor-rights counsel should pursue the following steps immediately: *(i)* In any case with a pre-*Harrington* confirmed plan embedding non-consensual third-party releases, evaluate Rule 60(b)(5) motions premised on the Supreme Court's changed-law ruling and assess whether the applicable circuit has adopted an interpretive gloss — the S.D.N.Y. and D. Del. courts are diverging on "consent" scope; *(ii)* in any liquidating or reorganizing case involving unionized workforces and post-petition facility closures, run the *Yellow Corporation* "post-petition triggering event" test immediately against all ERISA § 4062(e) liabilities and model administrative-expense waterfall scenarios before the next distribution or sale hearing; *(iii)* Celsius creditors in the disputed-claim reserve should file updated contact and tax-form information with Kroll no later than June 15 to ensure inclusion in the Q3 distribution; *(iv)* trade creditors who received payments in the 90-day preference window in any of the 2023–2024 mega-retail bankruptcies (Rite Aid, Tuesday Morning, Bed Bath & Beyond) should retain preference-defense counsel now and preserve all documentation supporting ordinary-course or new-value defenses under § 547(c)(2) and § 547(c)(4), as estate-representative preference campaigns are accelerating toward § 546(a) deadlines; and *(v)* bidders and lenders participating in the Franchise Group Pet Supplies Plus sale process should monitor Judge Shannon's bid-protection ruling closely, as a reduction in the break-up fee could re-open competitive bidding dynamics before the May 29 auction.
