### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Hon. Kyu Y. Paek) — Sale Approved, Close Imminent


Sleep Number Corporation filed for Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (Case No. 26-11399), entering the asset purchase agreement with Sleep Country Canada immediately beforehand.
 The case is structured as a pure § 363 sale — no reorganization track. 
The cases are jointly administered before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.


The stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., a wholly-owned Sleep Country Canada subsidiary, opened at $415 million in cash plus assumed liabilities. 
Sleep Country increased its proposal following the competitive auction process; the court-approved bid is valued at approximately $701 million, with $529.5 million payable in cash.
 
After a competitive auction, the U.S. Bankruptcy Court approved the sale to Sleep Country on July 20, 2026.
 
The sale, which remains subject to customary closing conditions, is expected to close by July 31.


The capital structure context matters for creditor recovery analysis. 
The case involves an expedited asset sale anchored by a $415 million stalking horse bid, financed by a self-priming lender DIP, drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt.
 
Sleep Number Corporation reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 The DIP structure is aggressive in its priority positioning: 
DIP lenders receive superpriority administrative expense claims and liens including first-priority on unencumbered DIP collateral, priming senior security interests on prepetition collateral, and junior liens where prior prior senior liens exist.
 At a $529.5 million cash component against a $672.5 million secured stack, the arithmetic for unsecured recovery is tight. 
The filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — but the quantum and waterfall remain to be established through the wind-down and claims reconciliation process.

The prepetition credit agreement, administered by U.S. Bank National Association, accelerated on the petition date. 
The filing of the Chapter 11 cases constituted an event of default that accelerated obligations under the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement, dated February 18, 2018 (as amended through the Forbearance Agreement and Thirteenth Amendment, dated April 27, 2026).
 
A Thirteenth Amendment and Forbearance Agreement was executed, providing $25 million in new term loans maturing June 30, 2026, and suspending the minimum liquidity covenant into early July.
 That short bridge accelerated the § 363 path rather than enabling a standalone reorganization.


The § 341 meeting of creditors was continued and held by Zoom on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET
 — the day before this advisory. There is no indication from the docket as of July 23 of a plan, disclosure statement, or liquidating trust framework. Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick). 
The estate preserves roughly $75.9 million in federal and state net operating losses and interest carryforwards
, though 
in a § 363 asset sale, those attributes typically stay with the selling entity rather than transferring to the buyer, so their near-term utility is limited.


**Creditor posture:** Unsecured claimants — trade vendors, warranty-holders, warranty service counterparties, and current or former employees — are waiting on the wind-down budget and claims bar date. With closing targeted July 31, the next 30–60 days will define the residual estate. Claims filed at Kroll (restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber). 
Sleep Number warns that the Chapter 11 process may be highly adverse for existing shareholders, specifically highlighting the likely cancellation of its common shares.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., Hon. Thomas M. Horan) — Sale Closed, Confirmation Passed, Post-Confirmation Phase


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)). The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


The § 363 sale process resolved in June. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock was declared the Successful Bidder as set out in the official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 That sale hearing was set for June 16 and, per the Law360 report dated June 16, 2026, proceeded.

Plan confirmation followed. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the Confirmation Hearing, establishing objection deadlines, setting the record date, and approving solicitation packages and voting procedures [Docket No. 388].
 
The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The docket shows 6 new entries in the 72 hours ending July 23 (entries 533 and 537, among others filed July 23), suggesting active post-confirmation or plan-implementation filings. The claims administrator is Veritaglobal (veritaglobal.net/blockfills). As of the May 21, 2026 claims register, the estate was processing creditor claims across the four Debtor entities.

The central contested legal question throughout this case — and the one with the broadest precedential implications for the crypto-insolvency sector — is whether customer digital assets constitute estate property. 
The four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursued a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remained contested.
 
A customer objection at the first-day hearing flagged that the onboarding agreement specifically stated all customer deposits would be completely segregated from the general balance sheet of the company.
 That contractual segregation argument, if accepted by Judge Horan, would remove those assets from the estate entirely and deliver them directly to depositors — a result materially different from FTX and Celsius, where customer property was commingled and treated as estate assets.


The company's liquidity crisis stemmed from cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provided the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 The Ad Hoc Group is represented by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP (Sean O'Neal, Jane VanLare, Theodore Leonhardt) and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor (Sean Greecher). 
Debtors are represented by McDermott Will & Emery LLP (Darren Azman, Gregg Steinman, Joseph Evans) and Katten Muchin Rosenman LLP (Peter Siddiqui), with BRG (Mark Renzi) as financial advisor and CRO.


The Committee of Unsecured Creditors, appointed March 27, 2026 [Docket No. 104], retained Morris James (Eric J. Monzo) as local counsel and FTI Consulting as financial advisor, effective April 1, 2026.

### Broader Landscape: § 363 Mechanics and Unsecured Creditor Risk

The Sleep Number outcome illuminates a structural dynamic that recurs in prepackaged or pre-arranged § 363 sales: the DIP lender and stalking horse are often the same prepetition secured creditor group, creating what Stretto's analysis characterized as a "self-priming lender DIP." 
The case is structured as an expedited sale of substantially all assets under § 363 built around a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 The final approved figure of approximately $701 million — up $286 million from the stalking horse — is a meaningful auction premium and signals that competing bidders saw value above the floor. Whether that premium reaches unsecured creditors depends entirely on the wind-down budget, the DIP payoff, and administrative expense claims, none of which are publicly resolved as of July 25.

For distressed investors, the pattern worth tracking: the auction produced a competitive result, the § 363 sale order has been entered, closing is expected by July 31, and the residual estate enters claims reconciliation mode immediately thereafter. The bar date for unsecured claims, the appointment of a wind-down trustee (or continued use of the existing estate professionals), and the ultimate claims recovery rate are the three inflection points to watch in August.

In **Power Block Coin**, watch for any adversary proceeding filings or asset sale motions at Bankr. D. Utah (Case No. 24-23041) and consider whether any Rule 3001(e) claim transfer notices have been filed signaling secondary market activity. Across all three cases, any claim purchasers should calibrate pricing to the unresolved administrative-expense risk — in a § 363 liquidation case like Sleep Number, administrative claims (DIP repayment, professional fees, cure costs) are paid before any unsecured distribution, and the margin between $529.5 million cash and $672.5 million in secured debt is the ceiling, not a floor, for general unsecured recovery.
