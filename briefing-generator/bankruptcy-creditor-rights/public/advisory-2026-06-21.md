### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. | Post-Sale-Hearing Posture

The sale hearing occurred on June 16, 2026 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in Courtroom 5 at 824 Market Street, Wilmington. 
As of May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA had been declared the "Successful Bidder" for substantially all of Reliz Technology Group Holdings' assets at a purchase price of $3.25 million, assuming certain liabilities, customer lists, and BlockFills' proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Final completion of the transaction remains subject to final court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.


The gap between what Keyrock is paying and what creditors are owed is extreme. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The $3.25 million purchase price represents the entirety of the consideration to the estate for substantially all assets.
 That arithmetic — $3.25 million against roughly $145 million in GUC claims — implies a recovery in the low single-digit percent range absent other estate assets or litigation proceeds. Distressed claim buyers should treat face-value pricing as essentially nil and focus instead on whatever residual value litigation against insiders, the Artha Investment Partners adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50224 (TMH)), and any unencumbered cash might generate.


The June 12 agenda showed the sale of substantially all assets going forward at the June 16 hearing, with a protective-order matter adjourned and sale objections remaining from Chicago Blackhawk Hockey Team and Oracle, per Docket No. 424.
 The docket reflects eight new entries in the 72 hours through June 19, including a June 19 filing (Docket No. 446), though the descriptions of those filings are not available from public sources as of this writing. 
The claims bar date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.


The UCC composition is noteworthy for recovery analysis. 
Committee members are SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 — a mixture of institutional crypto counterparties and apparent retail-adjacent claimants. 
FTI Consulting serves as the Committee's financial advisor, effective April 1, 2026.
 The presence of FTI alongside the Committee's counsel indicates an active forensic review of pre-petition transfers; creditors should watch for preference and fraudulent-transfer actions in the adversary proceeding queue beyond the already-opened Artha matter.


In the weeks immediately preceding the chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two customer lawsuits asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026).
 Both actions are effectively stayed by the automatic stay but may inform claims against non-debtor officers and directors — a separate recovery channel creditors' counsel should keep open.


The debtors attribute the liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation to Celsius, with compounding pressure from stranded capital in a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 
The near-term posture is a sale-and-confirmation runway: sale approval at the June 16 hearing, confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 With the sale hearing now concluded, the next material milestone is plan solicitation and the July 13 confirmation hearing — the point at which any disputes over sale proceeds allocation, creditor classification, and the treatment of litigation claims must be resolved.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), Bankr. S.D.N.Y. | New Filing; First Full Advisory Coverage


Sleep Number filed for chapter 11 in the Southern District of New York on Friday, June 12, 2026, docket No. 26-11399.
 
The case is being administered by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 This is a pure Section 363 sale proceeding, not a reorganization. 
The transaction is being undertaken pursuant to Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code.


**Capital structure.** 
The company's total assets stand at $642.3 million against total debt of approximately $1.28 billion, comprising $475 million outstanding under a revolving credit facility, $177.5 million under a term loan entered into in 2021, and a further $20 million term loan drawn in 2026.
 
The bankruptcy petition showed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion with liabilities in excess of $1 billion.
 Equity is wiped out; 
the company explicitly warns that its common shares are "significantly out of the money," expects little or no recovery for equity holders, and anticipates Nasdaq delisting.
 
On June 16, 2026, Nasdaq notified the company of its determination to delist Sleep Number's common stock, with trading suspension effective at the open on June 23, 2026.


**Stalking horse and sale timeline.** 
On June 12, 2026, the company entered into a "stalking horse" Asset Purchase Agreement with SNBR, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc.
 
The stalking horse transaction provides for a purchase price of $415 million in cash, subject to certain adjustments and the assumption of certain liabilities, and carries a 3% break-up fee along with an expense reimbursement cap of $4 million.
 The compressed sale timeline is: 
a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, a July 15, 2026 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.
 
A final hearing on certain first day motions is scheduled for July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET; a bidding procedures hearing is set for July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


**DIP financing.** 
Prepetition lenders are expected to provide up to $260 million in debtor-in-possession financing, including up to $65 million of new-money term loans and up to $195 million of roll-up loans, bearing interest at SOFR plus 8% or base rate plus 7%.
 
The Bankruptcy Court has entered an interim order approving the facility, with a final hearing scheduled for July 9, 2026. The DIP and roll-up loans mature on September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR plus 8.00% or a base rate plus 7.00%; the loans are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral and are subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 The $195 million roll-up is material: it converts prepetition secured debt into post-petition DIP priority, accelerating the waterfall drain on any sale proceeds available to unsecured creditors. 
The debtors canvassed 11 prospective alternative financing sources — none willing to lend on an unsecured or junior basis, and to whose priming proposals the prepetition lenders would not consent — before determining the negotiated DIP offered the optimal path.


**Unsecured creditor position.** 
Sleep Number reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities, and the filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 That statement deserves skepticism given the DIP structure. The $415 million stalking horse price must first satisfy the DIP superpriority claims (up to $260 million), then the approximately $672.5 million in prepetition secured debt. Even on optimistic assumptions about the roll-up mechanics, the residual available to GUCs would be modest. Creditors should model the waterfall carefully before ascribing significant value to unsecured claims. 
The petition lists creditors in the range of 50–99
 — a notably small creditor count for a retailer with 572 stores, suggesting the largest obligations are concentrated in a handful of institutional lenders and trade creditors.

**Advisors.** 
Davis Polk & Wardwell is the company's legal advisor, Guggenheim Securities its investment banker, AP Services (an affiliate of AlixPartners) its interim management, and Joele Frank, Wilkinson Brimmer Katcher its strategic communications advisor.
 
The stalking horse agreement with Sleep Country Canada — which operates 300 stores across Canada — carries a 3% break-up fee and a $4 million expense reimbursement cap.
 Goodwin Procter is advising Sleep Country Canada on the buyer side. 
Kent Percy of AP Services, LLC was appointed Chief Restructuring Officer on June 12, 2026.


**Contextual risk factors.** 
Distress was compounded by the Company's pandemic-era overexpansion of its store footprint, manufacturing capabilities, and debt levels, which heightened leverage and impaired its ability to absorb prolonged headwinds, and by the April 2025 wave of U.S. tariffs.
 
The company filed a motion to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations; A&G Real Estate Partners is assisting with the store footprint review.
 Landlords holding unrejected leases should file protective proofs of claim promptly, given the compressed timeline and the stated intention to maintain stores only where profitable.

### Broader Landscape: Retail Distress, § 363 Mechanics, and the DIP Roll-Up Pressure

Sleep Number joins a queue of large retail chapter 11 cases proceeding as pre-packaged or pre-arranged § 363 sales rather than standalone reorganizations. The structural commonality is a DIP facility that rolls up prepetition secured debt, creating a practical priming waterfall that renders general unsecured creditors dependent on the margin between the auction clearing price and total secured claims. In Sleep Number, the math is tight: $415 million stalking horse against roughly $935 million in DIP-priority and prepetition secured claims (the $260 million DIP plus approximately $672.5 million prepetition debt, net of the roll-up treatment). Any bidder increment above $415 million flows first to secured claims, and only thereafter — if at all — to GUCs.

For distressed investors evaluating secondary claim positions: the bidding procedures hearing on July 2 is the gate. If the court approves the procedures as filed, the July 8 bid deadline leaves 17 days from today. Meaningful overbids would need to be structured and diligenced by then. The 14-week prepetition process (53 parties contacted, 19 NDAs, 5 preliminary proposals) suggests the field has already been broadly canvassed; the probability of a transformative upset bid is lower than in a true free-fall filing with no prior marketing.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed investors in the BlockFills estate should note that the June 16 sale hearing has now passed; absent verified reporting of a successful sale order, the July 13 confirmation target is the next hard deadline, and any creditor with an objection to plan classification, the treatment of litigation claims, or the scope of the release under the Artha adversary proceeding (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH)) should be preparing those objections now — the confirmation timeline will be compressed. In Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), the July 2 bidding procedures hearing is the first critical gate: any party seeking to participate in the July 13 auction should engage restructuring counsel immediately to review the bid procedures motion, qualify as a bidder, and obtain access to the data room. Landlords with leases at Sleep Number's 572 retail locations should file protective proofs of claim and be prepared for a post-sale, pre-rejection objection window that may be extremely short given the July 31 closing target. In Power Block Coin (Case No. 24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah), claimants should monitor the docket directly for any adversary proceedings or asset sale notices that would trigger distribution mechanics. Across all three cases, secondary claim purchasers should focus on litigation upside — particularly D&O actions and avoidance claims — rather than relying on pro rata distribution from asset sale proceeds alone.
