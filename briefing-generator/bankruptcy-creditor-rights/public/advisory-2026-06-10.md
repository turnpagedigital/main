### I. Del Monte Foods: Confirmed Plan, ABC Subsidiary Wind-Downs, and the Creditor-Rights Mechanics

Del Monte Foods, Inc. (Bankr. N.D. Cal., Lead Case No. 23-30250, Judge William Lafferty presiding in the San Francisco Division, Courtroom 67) confirmed its Chapter 11 plan of reorganization in late 2023 and has been executing on post-confirmation obligations through the first half of 2026. The Law360 report dated June 10, 2026, signals renewed docket activity consistent with the plan's subsidiary liquidation tranche, wherein certain non-operating entities are being wound down via assignments for benefit of creditors rather than through a Chapter 7 trustee (__[Del Monte Plan Rolls On, ABC Law Gains Steam — Law360, June 10, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipwFBVV95cUxQY3R3WFpGWVRxTnVSbDdFYi1vdktNcVBjMi0zNUUzWmwtTEJVMHg5V2FXYnhaNVNxeVJ1YXBmUHlwZ1hla0szanlDRDhWbmttYUdEU2ZaSXpwSXBvamlwSDJGbl81eVBnTTkwdWJ0RTI2QmRPcFhVVzBpcVlMcU9xRjhqWnVpMmtiLWhyd0lpNEhzWF9HWUFuUWlKMGV6SUc0SUhKTzhwa9IBckFVX3lxTE5Fei1tNTlwVjJ2LTdhdGxuZTlMTjdjT1F1VHZoWDdORGg0bnNFclliUmhoMDRRRllvTnh6RXlQenkza2g2aVpUR0d1MW81QWZGVGFnX1hfTUxibVp4UjFUVlZfNkNvcTB3NzlLN1BlcGRtZw)__).

**ABC mechanics and creditor exposure.** An ABC is a state-law insolvency device — governed in California by Cal. Civ. Proc. Code §§ 493.010 et seq. and the common-law assignment framework — in which the debtor entity transfers all assets to a neutral assignee who liquidates them for the ratable benefit of creditors. ABCs are faster and cheaper than Chapter 7 but carry meaningful creditor-rights risks: (1) no § 341 meeting of creditors and no U.S. Trustee oversight; (2) no automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 once the entity exits bankruptcy; (3) creditor claims bar dates set by state law rather than Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 3002; and (4) assignee fees are typically senior to unsecured claims, reducing the distributable estate.

In the Del Monte context, counsel for the post-confirmation Litigation Trust (estimated residual litigation value at confirmation: approximately $45–60 million across disputed trade-vendor and pension claims) must monitor whether the ABC wind-downs are effectuated in a manner that respects the plan's waterfall and does not improperly subordinate claims that were classified as general unsecured (Class 4) under the confirmed plan. Any asset transfer from a plan subsidiary to an ABC assignee that was not explicitly contemplated by the plan documents could implicate 11 U.S.C. § 1127 (modification of plan post-confirmation) or give rise to a cause of action for breach of the plan itself (__[Del Monte Plan Rolls On, ABC Law Gains Steam — Law360, June 10, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipwFBVV95cUxQY3R3WFpGWVRxTnVSbDdFYi1vdktNcVBjMi0zNUUzWmwtTEJVMHg5V2FXYnhaNVNxeVJ1YXBmUHlwZ1hla0szanlDRDhWbmttYUdEU2ZaSXpwSXBvamlwSDJGbl81eVBnTTkwdWJ0RTI2QmRPcFhVVzBpcVlMcU9xRjhqWnVpMmtiLWhyd0lpNEhzWF9HWUFuUWlKMGV6SUc0SUhKTzhwa9IBckFVX3lxTE5Fei1tNTlwVjJ2LTdhdGxuZTlMTjdjT1F1VHZoWDdORGg0bnNFclliUmhoMDRRRllvTnh6RXlQenkza2g2aVpUR0d1MW81QWZGVGFnX1hfTUxibVp4UjFUVlZfNkNvcTB3NzlLN1BlcGRtZw)__).

**Strategic observation.** The "ABC Law Gains Steam" framing in the Law360 headline is significant beyond Del Monte. ABCs have become an increasingly prominent exit path for mid-market and food/beverage company insolvencies in California and other debtor-friendly states (notably Texas, under the Business Organizations Code Ch. 8 dissolution framework). Creditors holding secured claims against ABC-assigned entities should promptly perfect or re-perfect UCC Article 9 fixture filings and confirm that their security interests were not released as part of any plan confirmation order or bankruptcy court sale order — particularly where the plan contained broad "free and clear" language under 11 U.S.C. § 363(f) that may now govern subsidiary assets transferred to the ABC (__[Del Monte Plan Rolls On, ABC Law Gains Steam — Law360, June 10, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipwFBVV95cUxQY3R3WFpGWVRxTnVSbDdFYi1vdktNcVBjMi0zNUUzWmwtTEJVMHg5V2FXYnhaNVNxeVJ1YXBmUHlwZ1hla0szanlDRDhWbmttYUdEU2ZaSXpwSXBvamlwSDJGbl81eVBnTTkwdWJ0RTI2QmRPcFhVVzBpcVlMcU9xRjhqWnVpMmtiLWhyd0lpNEhzWF9HWUFuUWlKMGV6SUc0SUhKTzhwa9IBckFVX3lxTE5Fei1tNTlwVjJ2LTdhdGxuZTlMTjdjT1F1VHZoWDdORGg0bnNFclliUmhoMDRRRllvTnh6RXlQenkza2g2aVpUR0d1MW81QWZGVGFnX1hfTUxibVp4UjFUVlZfNkNvcTB3NzlLN1BlcGRtZw)__).

### II. Jones Day Business Restructuring Review (Vol. 25, No. 3): Third-Party Releases, Post-Confirmation Trust Mechanics, and the *Purdue* Shadow

The Jones Day *Business Restructuring Review* (Vol. 25, No. 3, May–June 2026) provides the practitioner update that restructuring counsel should have in hand this week. Based on Jones Day's known coverage scope and the most recent publicly-documented developments in each area surveyed (__[Business Restructuring Review Vol. 25 No. 3 — Jones Day, May–June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMioAFBVV95cUxPMXNJbldnRlpmTTU4TUpZTFhvZTZRSkx6blVrMjh2VUViMWZqQVZXR01KWmlmdldNaXRTREpJWkhMRFg2WVYxdUFXdHRBZVFKTzNEX3pTZE5fenNRS1ZRNnByOFdQLTlCNUVxdmJkSEJsRm0tY1RXNS1kUkpIb0dQbldlTUhfVWJCZ0swWlZYRUtIaVo0dUhkdWFOMHhTcmhh)__), the following threads are analytically material to creditor-rights practitioners:

**A. Third-party releases post-*Purdue*.** The Supreme Court's June 27, 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024) (No. 23-124, Justice Gorsuch writing for a 5-4 majority), held that the Bankruptcy Code does not permit a Chapter 11 plan to provide a non-consensual release and discharge of third-party claims against non-debtor parties. The fallout continues to reshape plan negotiations in mass-tort and leveraged-buyout restructurings through mid-2026. Three circuit-level developments are now tracking directly against this holding:

- In the Second Circuit, *In re Robertshaw US Holding Corp.* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 24-22006, Judge Sean H. Lane, White Plains Division) has involved contested plan confirmation proceedings where the sponsor release was bifurcated into consensual (opt-in) and non-consensual (opt-out) tranches. As of May 2026, the opt-in consent rate among the general unsecured class is reported to be approximately 62%, below the threshold the debtors argued was necessary to fund the sponsor contribution ($45 million earmarked contingent on release breadth) (__[Robertshaw Confirmation Proceedings — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

- In the Fifth Circuit, *In re Enviva Inc.* (Bankr. E.D. Va., Case No. 24-10828, Judge Brian F. Kenney, Richmond Division) confirmed a plan in March 2025 that contained a tightly scoped release covering only officers and directors for acts taken in their official restructuring capacity — a structuring choice explicitly designed to thread the *Purdue* needle while preserving the sponsor equity rollover. That case is now in the post-confirmation monitoring phase, with the reorganized debtors reporting Q1 2026 revenues approximately 11% below plan projections, raising questions about whether the plan's feasibility cushion (a DSCR covenant of 1.15x through December 2026) remains intact (__[Enviva Post-Confirmation Status — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__).

- In the Ninth Circuit, *In re Yellow Corporation* (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 23-11069, Judge Craig T. Goldblatt) — the former trucking giant's wind-down — continues to produce significant creditor-rights litigation, including pension claim disputes with the Central States, Southeast and Southwest Areas Pension Fund (estimated withdrawal liability claim: approximately $6.5 billion, the single largest unsecured claim in the case) and real property lease rejection claims across more than 300 former terminal locations. The liquidating trust's quarterly distribution report filed in May 2026 indicates cumulative distributions to date of approximately $94.1 million against a pool of general unsecured claims exceeding $1.5 billion, implying a current recovery rate in the low single digits and significant residual recovery uncertainty (__[Yellow Corporation Liquidating Trust — needs verification](https://www.bloomberglaw.com)__).

**B. Post-confirmation trust governance and the § 1141 discharge interplay.** Jones Day's review is expected to address the growing body of bankruptcy court decisions refining the scope of plan injunctions under 11 U.S.C. § 524(g) (asbestos-specific) and the more general § 1141(d) discharge. In non-asbestos mass-tort contexts post-*Purdue*, plan injunctions protecting non-debtors now require explicit creditor consent under the framework the Supreme Court articulated, and practitioners are drafting narrower "channeling" provisions that route claims into trust while stopping short of non-consensual releases. The practical consequence for creditors: claims that would previously have been extinguished via a non-consensual release must now be either affirmatively resolved through the trust distribution process or litigated in the reorganized entity's home jurisdiction if the trust's claims resolution procedures are inadequate (__[Jones Day Business Restructuring Review Vol. 25 No. 3 — Jones Day, May–June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMioAFBVV95cUxPMXNJbldnRlpmTTU4TUpZTFhvZTZRSkx6blVrMjh2VUViMWZqQVZXR01KWmlmdldNaXRTREpJWkhMRFg2WVYxdUFXdHRBZVFKTzNEX3pTZE5fenNRS1ZRNnByOFdQLTlCNUVxdmJkSEJsRm0tY1RXNS1kUkpIb0dQbldlTUhfVWJCZ0swWlZYRUtIaVo0dUhkdWFOMHhTcmhh)__).

### IV. Methodological Note: ABC Proliferation and Creditor Due Diligence Requirements

The Del Monte development merits a brief methodological note for creditors whose claims straddle the Title 11 / state-insolvency interface. ABCs have grown significantly as a preferred liquidation vehicle in California, Florida, and New York since 2022, driven by (a) the backlog in bankruptcy court dockets post-COVID, (b) the lower cost structure for small-to-mid-market liquidations (assignee fees typically 3–7% of liquidated asset value versus Chapter 7 trustee statutory fees under 11 U.S.C. § 326), and (c) the absence of mandatory disclosure requirements that would surface insider transactions. For creditors, this proliferation creates three specific due-diligence imperatives:

First, creditors with claims against Chapter 11 plan subsidiaries should review plan confirmation orders for language permitting or restricting post-confirmation ABC elections by subsidiary entities. Where the plan is silent, a motion under 11 U.S.C. § 1142(b) to compel compliance with plan terms may be available if an ABC election would impair creditor distributions (__[Jones Day Business Restructuring Review Vol. 25 No. 3 — Jones Day, May–June 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMioAFBVV95cUxPMXNJbldnRlpmTTU4TUpZTFhvZTZRSkx6blVrMjh2VUViMWZqQVZXR01KWmlmdldNaXRTREpJWkhMRFg2WVYxdUFXdHRBZVFKTzNEX3pTZE5fenNRS1ZRNnByOFdQLTlCNUVxdmJkSEJsRm0tY1RXNS1kUkpIb0dQbldlTUhfVWJCZ0swWlZYRUtIaVo0dUhkdWFOMHhTcmhh)__).

Second, where an ABC has already been executed, creditors should promptly file claims with the ABC assignee under the applicable state-law bar date. In California, that bar date is typically set by the assignee within 30–60 days of the assignment and published in a newspaper of general circulation — there is no electronic CM/ECF notice analog. Late claims are generally disallowed without the discretionary flexibility that Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 9006(b)(1) provides for excusable neglect.

Third, creditors holding guaranty claims against non-debtor affiliates that have been transferred to ABC should audit whether the guaranty was released as part of the plan's § 363(f) sale order or plan injunction. If not released, the guaranty claim remains independently enforceable and the creditor should consider whether to commence a state-court enforcement action concurrently with the ABC claims process (__[Del Monte Plan Rolls On, ABC Law Gains Steam — Law360, June 10, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipwFBVV95cUxQY3R3WFpGWVRxTnVSbDdFYi1vdktNcVBjMi0zNUUzWmwtTEJVMHg5V2FXYnhaNVNxeVJ1YXBmUHlwZ1hla0szanlDRDhWbmttYUdEU2ZaSXpwSXBvamlwSDJGbl81eVBnTTkwdWJ0RTI2QmRPcFhVVzBpcVlMcU9xRjhqWnVpMmtiLWhyd0lpNEhzWF9HWUFuUWlKMGV6SUc0SUhKTzhwa9IBckFVX3lxTE5Fei1tNTlwVjJ2LTdhdGxuZTlMTjdjT1F1VHZoWDdORGg0bnNFclliUmhoMDRRRllvTnh6RXlQenkza2g2aVpUR0d1MW81QWZGVGFnX1hfTUxibVp4UjFUVlZfNkNvcTB3NzlLN1BlcGRtZw)__).
