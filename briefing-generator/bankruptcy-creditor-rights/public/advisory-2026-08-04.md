# 📜 Chapter 11 | Tuesday, August 04, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Two developments dominate this morning's read: the largest new mega-case filing in weeks, and the resolution — after nearly three years — of the most consequential judicial-ethics prosecution in modern bankruptcy history. Harvest Sherwood's confirmation hearing, tracked here since the U.S. Trustee objected in late July, sits on the docket for tomorrow.

### NEW FILING — Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation, et al. | Bankr. S.D. Tex. | Case No. 26-90739 | Judge Alfredo R. Perez

This is the headline of the week. 
Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation and certain of its U.S. subsidiaries, including Hughes Network Systems, LLC, filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division, on August 3, 2026.
 
The lead petition was docketed as Case No. 26-90739 before Judge Alfredo R. Perez in the Houston Division.
 
The company is one of 12 affiliated entities that filed Chapter 11 petitions on the same date and requested joint administration.


**The debt picture.** 
The Germantown, MD-based company owed $750 million in senior secured notes and $750 million in senior unsecured notes that were due to be paid off on August 3, but the debtor lacked the cash necessary to repay the notes and had been unable to secure capital to refinance the debt on acceptable terms, according to a bankruptcy declaration by Chief Restructuring Officer Robert Del Genio, a Senior Managing Director at FTI Consulting Inc.
 
The debtors held approximately $61.2 million in cash and cash equivalents on a bank basis as of the petition date, according to that same Del Genio declaration.
 
The Chapter 11 filing constitutes an event of default under the indentures governing HSSC's 5.25% Senior Secured Notes due 2026 and its 6.625% Senior Notes due 2026, resulting in automatic acceleration of the company's obligations under these debt instruments.


**Operating context.** 
The filing documents a consumer broadband base that fell from 819,000 subscribers to approximately 641,000 in twelve months, and a strategic retreat from households toward enterprise, defense, and in-flight contracts.
 
The filing comes after Hughes saw its broadband subscriber base collapse from 1.56 million at the end of 2020 to 681,000 by March 2026, driven largely by competition from SpaceX's Starlink LEO constellation.


**The EchoStar bankruptcy cascade.** 
This is the second time in roughly a month that an EchoStar subsidiary has sought court protection — Dish Wireless filed a prepackaged Chapter 11 case on June 30, 2026, with a restructuring support agreement that paved the way for a $23 billion sale of EchoStar's wireless spectrum licenses to AT&T.
 
EchoStar closed that spectrum sale on July 28, adding approximately 30 MHz of nationwide 3.45 GHz mid-band spectrum and roughly 20 MHz of nationwide 600 MHz low-band spectrum to AT&T's portfolio.
 The Hughes filing is therefore a free-fall case with no pre-negotiated plan — a materially different posture from the Dish Wireless prepack — landing before the same Houston complex docket.

**Creditor posture and pre-petition activity.** 
In early July, major noteholders representing roughly 80% of Hughes's senior bonds by face value retained restructuring counsel at Jones Day to explore options including refinancing, asset sales, and court-supervised reorganization.
 
On July 21, those creditors sent a formal notice to the company asserting fraudulent transfers.
 That notice — filed just eleven days before the petition — is a signal worth tracking: it preserves standing for avoidance theories that could survive confirmation and complicate inter-debtor and parent-affiliate claims given the Dish/AT&T spectrum proceeds.

**Professionals.** 
White & Case LLP is serving as Hughes's legal counsel in the bankruptcy proceedings, with FTI Consulting — which placed Robert Del Genio in the chief restructuring officer role — serving as financial advisor.


**Scale and creditor count.** 
Hughes Network Systems, LLC reports assets of $1B–$10B and liabilities of $1B–$10B, with a creditor count of 10,001–25,000.
 
Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation reports $1.9 billion in assets and $1.5 billion in liabilities as of March 31, 2026, with the filing indicating that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.


For distressed investors, this is a free-fall, no-plan, five-figure creditor count case with a CRO embedded pre-petition, an organized ad hoc bondholder group with existing counsel, a live fraudulent transfer notice, and a parent that just completed a $23 billion asset sale. The valuation question — what remains in the Hughes enterprise after the LEO competitive collapse — will be the central battle.

### CRITICAL HEARING TOMORROW — Harvest Sherwood Food Distributors, Inc. | Bankr. N.D. Tex. | Case No. 25-80109 | Judge Stacey G.C. Jernigan

Covered here since the July 20 U.S. Trustee objection. 
The Plan confirmation hearing is currently scheduled for August 5, 2026.
 The two open confirmation disputes are structural: 
the U.S. Trustee objected at Docket No. 1063, arguing that the Plan remains unconfirmable because its opt-out third-party release does not establish knowing and voluntary consent, and because it permits residual Liquidating Trust cash to be donated to charity rather than deposited with the Court.


The third-party release issue is a live circuit question post-*Purdue*. Judge Jernigan's ruling tomorrow will add to the growing body of district-level authority applying the Supreme Court's consent standard to opt-out mechanics. The charitable-donation point is narrower but practically significant: residual trust funds directed to charity rather than the Court represent a creditor-recovery question, not just a housekeeping one. Watch for whether Judge Jernigan addresses both issues on the merits or continues the hearing to require further briefing. 
Harvest Sherwood's case centers on monetizing litigation assets — including approximately $1.09 billion in antitrust claims against over 28 defendants across pork, chicken, beef, and turkey producer categories — through a court-supervised wind-down process.

### RESOLUTION — Jackson Walker LLP / U.S. Trustee | Bankr. S.D. Tex. | *In re: Professional Fee Matters Concerning the Jackson Walker Law Firm* | Judge Eduardo V. Rodriguez

The Jones/Freeman scandal closes its principal chapter. 
Jackson Walker LLP will pay $15 million to end litigation with the Justice Department's bankruptcy watchdog over an undisclosed relationship between a former attorney and one of the nation's former top bankruptcy judges.
 
The settlement would end long-running litigation brought by the U.S. Trustee's office accusing Jackson Walker of breaching its ethical duties by failing to disclose the romance its onetime bankruptcy partner Elizabeth Freeman had with ex-bankruptcy judge David R. Jones while the firm was representing clients in cases involving Jones — and the law firm is not admitting wrongdoing as part of the agreement, according to a settlement term sheet filed Sunday in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas.



As part of the deal, Jackson Walker has also agreed to reform its conflicts and disclosure practices.
 
The U.S. Trustee had brought the litigation in November 2023 to vacate orders approving Jackson Walker's retention fees and applications in cases connected to Jones and Freeman; extensive discovery showed that at least two Jackson Walker attorneys exchanged text messages in 2021 indicating they knew about, and discussed the potential fallout of, the undisclosed romance.



The deal comes soon after Houston Judge Eduardo V. Rodriguez recommended approval of nine private settlements that allowed the U.S. Trustee to continue pursuing legal action against Jackson Walker; those settlements, which must still be approved by another judge, would return nearly $4.8 million to several bankruptcy estates.
 
A major point of contention through the litigation had been the U.S. Trustee's efforts to vacate more than $20 million in fees awarded to the firm in 34 cases involving Jones and Freeman.



Jackson Walker is represented by Norton Rose Fulbright US LLP and Rusty Hardin & Associates LLP.


The $15 million figure — against the backdrop of $20 million in contested fees across 34 cases — will be read as a significant discount by critics. 
It remains unclear what the lasting legacy will be: creditors and practitioners may question the integrity of the bankruptcy system or treat the settlement as the cost of doing business, particularly given questions about whether Jackson Walker enjoyed more favorable rulings or had inside information from Jones in large, complex bankruptcies due to his romance with Freeman.
 The settlement requires court approval; its structural significance — mandatory disclosure-practice reform — is the more durable element for practitioners operating in the S.D. Tex. complex docket going forward.
