### 1. BlockFills (*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH)) — Post-Confirmation Administrative Filing

The docket logged eight new entries in the last 72 hours; the most recent, Dkt. No. 552 (filed July 30, 2026 at 4:28 p.m. ET), is a 
Certificate of No Objection — No Order Required
 filed in the normal post-confirmation housekeeping track. No substantive relief is sought or opposed.

The case posture is straightforward. 
On July 13, 2026, Judge Thomas M. Horan confirmed the debtors' Chapter 11 plan, just 122 days after BlockFills' March 15, 2026 filing.
 The claims administrator page at Veritaglobal sets 
September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) as the next key milestone
 — that is the operative effective-date / distribution deadline that creditors should be tracking.

What the CNO at Dkt. 552 does not resolve: the central customer-property question that ran through the case — whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate or belong to counterparties — was settled in principle through the plan rather than litigated to a published decision. That leaves the doctrinal question open for the next crypto insolvency. 
The debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an ad hoc group of the company's largest customers centered on a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 The $3.25 million sale of the operating business to 
Brussels-based digital asset services firm Keyrock
 was approved on June 16; the liquidating trust now holds residual estate assets for distribution to the roughly $145 million unsecured pool.

**Investor note:** The September 11 deadline is the next hard date. Claim traders should be pricing recovery against the trust's realized asset pool, not against the pre-sale enterprise. No further material docket activity is expected before August 25.

### 2. Alkegen (*In re ASP Unifrax Holdings, Inc., et al.*, Bankr. N.D. Tex.) — Interim DIP Approved July 28; Confirmation Hearing Set September 3

This is the live macro-scale proceeding. 
On July 26, 2026, ASP Unifrax Holdings, Inc. and 62 affiliates each filed a voluntary petition for relief under Chapter 11 of the United States Bankruptcy Code in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Texas.
 The filing is before 
Judge Scott W. Everett of the Northern District of Texas, and a hybrid first-day hearing was held on July 28, 2026, in Courtroom 3, Floor 14, Earle Cabell Federal Building, 1100 Commerce Street, Dallas, Texas.


The capital structure the prepack addresses: 
Alkegen's September 2024 out-of-court refinancing extended funded debt maturities to 2028 and 2029 and provided approximately $350 million of incremental liquidity, but did not meaningfully reduce the overall debt burden; with PIK-toggle options on the Senior Facilities and Second Lien Notes set to expire September 30, 2026 — adding approximately $110 million of incremental annual cash interest — forecasted 2026 annual debt service was projected at approximately $320 million, up from $297 million in 2025.
 The ticking clock on the PIK options made an in-court process unavoidable.

Plan mechanics: 
Under the terms of the RSA, Alkegen will eliminate approximately $3.1 billion of total debt and meaningfully improve its capital structure.
 The RSA was announced July 20 and the petition followed six days later, consistent with a pre-negotiated prepack timeline. 
Alkegen enters this process with the committed support of its key financial partners and has secured $315 million in new money debtor-in-possession financing.


The interim DIP hearing, held July 28, produced a partial win for the debtors but not without friction: 
bankrupt specialty materials maker Alkegen received permission from a Texas judge to access part of a $630 million bankruptcy loan from existing lenders after opposition over the size of the interim package was overruled.
 The distinction matters — the $315 million "new money" figure in the BusinessWire release represents the new capital injection; the $630 million total DIP facility appears to incorporate a roll-up of existing prepetition exposure. Opposition to the interim draw size is a signal that at least one creditor constituency is already contesting lender value extraction through the roll-up mechanism. Watch the final DIP order due at the 
August 25, 2026 final hearing on certain first-day motions
 for whether any roll-up carve-back is negotiated.

Confirmation schedule: 
a combined disclosure statement and plan confirmation hearing is set for September 3, 2026 at 10:30 a.m. CT.
 That is an exceptionally compressed timeline — 39 days from petition to confirmation hearing — consistent with a prepack where votes were solicited prepetition, but it leaves virtually no runway for unsecured creditor groups to organize or mount plan objections. 
Operational initiatives, including the A&M-led "Project Horizon" reorganization into two divisions, proved insufficient to service the prepetition debt load absent a comprehensive balance sheet restructuring; the plan contemplates the equitization of substantially all of the company's funded debt, addressing approximately $3.5 billion of prepetition funded debt obligations.


Counsel of record: 
Kirkland & Ellis LLP (Steven Serajeddini, P.C., Nicholas Adzima, Margaret Reiney — Chicago) and Gray Reed (Jason S. Brookner, Lydia R. Webb, Amber M. Carson — Dallas) serve as restructuring counsel.


**Investor note:** With $3.5 billion in funded debt equitizing into reorganized equity, the key valuation question is what multiple the prepetition lenders assigned to the post-emergence operating company. 
PIK-toggle expiry drove the timeline
 and effectively extinguished any out-of-court option — a dynamic that typically leaves trade creditors and lower-priority holders with limited negotiating leverage in the prepack context. The September 3 combined hearing is the only meaningful intervention point before the plan is effectively done.
