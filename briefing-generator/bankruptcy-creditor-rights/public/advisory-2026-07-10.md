## Analysis & Developments

**Sleep Number (26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., J. Paek): Sale objection deadline closes today; landlord friction surfaces.** Today is the last inflection point before next week's auction. 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors filed a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the S.D.N.Y., jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
The stalking horse APA, executed June 12, calls for SNBR Inc. — a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc. — to acquire substantially all assets for $415 million in cash plus assumption of certain liabilities.
 The sale objection deadline is 4:00 p.m. ET today; 
the auction, if necessary, is set for July 13 at 10:00 a.m., and the sale hearing is scheduled for July 15 at 10:00 a.m.
 
The stalking horse bid establishes the floor for competing offers, following a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, yielded 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals, and was compressed into a 26-day postpetition diligence period.


The most material creditor-side friction as of yesterday: the docket reflects an objection filed by certain Kimco landlords (Dkt. 225, July 9) contesting proposed cure costs related to Dkt. 134. 
An auction is scheduled for July 13 and the company remains open to higher and better offers; for landlords the filing raises immediate concerns around rent payments, lease assumption or rejection, and the auction outcome.
 The cure-cost dispute is structurally significant — under § 365(b)(1), the debtor must cure all monetary defaults as a condition of assumption, and any buyer selecting store leases to assume will inherit those cure obligations. Landlords who believe the proposed cure amounts are understated must file by 4:00 p.m. today or risk waiver. 
Sleep Number entered Chapter 11 with $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities, and the filing indicates funds available for unsecured creditor distribution.
 That unsecured waterfall — if any — depends almost entirely on sale proceeds clearing the secured credit facility. 
The Prepetition Credit Agreement, dated February 18, 2018 as amended through the Forbearance and Thirteenth Amendment dated April 27, 2026, accelerated all principal and accrued interest upon the petition date.
 Debtor's counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.

The capital structure context matters for recovery sizing. 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale under § 363, backstopped by the stalking horse bid and supported by the lenders and agent under the Company's prepetition secured credit facilities.
 
These pressures were compounded by pandemic-era overexpansion and April 2025 IEEPA-based tariff levies; cost-cutting under CEO Linda Findley reduced operating costs by $136 million versus 2024, and the Company monetized liquidity through April 2026 prepetition term loans and an IEEPA tariff-refund claim sale, but a recapitalization outreach yielded only a single non-actionable proposal.
 Distressed investors should watch whether competing bids emerge before the July 13 auction and whether the UCC — constituted after the § 341 creditors' meeting continued to July 14 — files any § 363(b) objection or seeks bid procedure modifications before close. 
The § 341 meeting has been continued by Zoom to July 14, 2026 at 1:30 p.m. ET.

All four debtors filed Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026; the cases are jointly administered under No. 26-10371 before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan in the District of Delaware.
 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, acquiring substantially all assets, certain liabilities, customer lists, and proprietary technology; Keyrock confirmed its designation as Successful Bidder in a court document filed May 26, 2026.
 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations; the filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, centering on a NewCo reorganization and liquidating trust.



The general claims bar date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 This is the operative deadline for creditors who have not yet filed proofs of claim. The docket carried 15 new entries in the 72 hours through July 9 (Dkt. 504–509 and adjacent entries), signaling active post-sale administration — likely professional fee applications, plan supplement filings, and liquidating trust documentation. The overarching legal question still coloring recovery expectations is whether customer digital asset balances constitute estate property. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested, and the dual-track restructuring was built around a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive § 363 sale process.
 Resolution of that question in the plan documents determines whether customers recover as secured property claimants or as unsecured creditors sharing pro rata in the $3.25 million sale proceeds plus any liquidating trust assets — an enormous delta on a $145 million unsecured pool.


The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Claim traders should note that Rule 3001(e) transfer notices in the 72-hour window would reflect secondary-market activity on the unsecured pool. None are confirmed filed as of this writing based on available docket data; the September 11 bar date creates the window during which claim pricing typically tightens as the creditor universe becomes knowable. 
Pre-petition customer litigation included Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026) and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. v. **Tehum / YesCare / Corizon (23-90086, Bankr. S.D. Tex., J. Lopez): Insider denies fraud in Texas Two-Step adversary.** Yesterday's Bloomberg Law headline is the week's most consequential Texas Two-Step development. 
Tehum Care Services Inc. director Isaac Lefkowitz disputed allegations by two creditor trusts that he orchestrated a fraudulent bankruptcy scheme related to a divisional merger — the maneuver commonly called the Texas Two-Step.
 
Lefkowitz also argued in his Monday filing that the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas lacks jurisdiction over claims against him as a non-bankrupt individual.
 That jurisdictional challenge is the crux: if the court agrees, the GUC Trustee and PI/WD Trustee must either proceed in district court or face a potentially case-dispositive venue fight.

The adversary itself — No. 26-03138 — was filed April 27, 2026. 
Matthew J. Dundon (GUC Trustee), Michael Zimmerman (PI/WD Trustee), and the Post-Effective Date Debtor filed Adversary Proceeding No. 26-03138 in the Texas Bankruptcy Court; defendants include Lefkowitz, the Lefkowitz Entities (YesCare, CHS TX, Geneva, Perigrove, Perigrove 1018, M2 HoldCo, M2 LoanCo, PharmaCorr), and six named former Corizon officers.
 
The complaint pleads sixteen counts: actual and constructive fraudulent transfer attacking both the divisional merger and approximately $31 million in pre-petition cash transfers; aiding and abetting; multiple breach-of-fiduciary-duty counts; alter ego against Lefkowitz; veil piercing against Perigrove 1018; successor liability against both YesCare and CHS TX; and breach of contract on the Plan and Cure Agreement.


The trigger for the adversary was a payment default. 
The Plan's Estate Party Settlement required a slate of Settlement Parties — YesCare and its wholly-owned subsidiaries, Geneva, Perigrove 1018, Perigrove LLC, M2 HoldCo, M2 LoanCo, and PharmaCorr — to pay $50 million ($2 million on the Effective Date, $48 million over 30 monthly installments at 6.00% per annum); the Channeling Injunction terminated automatically on any payment default not cured within five business days.
 
The settling parties missed five monthly payments of $1.5 million each to the two trusts before a forbearance was negotiated.
 
The PI/WD Trust's first annual report showed the trust had received $6.95 million in contributions from inception through December 31, 2025, had paid only $77,000 on 15 settled claims, and had 323 submitted PI/WD claims in process — with the cure agreement itself subsequently defaulting by March 2026.


Lefkowitz's jurisdictional argument — that a non-bankrupt insider cannot be haled into bankruptcy court under a § 1334 "related to" theory — has genuine appellate traction in the Fifth Circuit, which has applied a narrower "conceivable effect" test in some contexts. 
Plan Article XII.A reserved exclusive jurisdiction in the Texas Bankruptcy Court over all matters arising out of or related to the Plan and the Estate Party Settlement; the Settlement Parties separately and expressly consented to Texas jurisdiction in § 11 of the February 1, 2026 Cure Agreement.
 That consent provision is the trustees' strongest counter: Lefkowitz's own entities signed the Cure Agreement selecting Texas jurisdiction, making the jurisdictional denial a difficult argument to sustain. The successor liability count against YesCare is independently determinative: 
a finding that YesCare is liable as a mere continuation of Corizon could strand every debtor entity under the full pre-merger obligation pool.


For distressed investors tracking incarcerated-creditor recovery, the practical read is: distributions from the PI/WD Trust remain minimal and will stay that way until either (a) the adversary produces a judgment and collection, or (b) Lefkowitz's entities resume installment payments. 
Tehum faced $775 million in personal injury claims; tort claimants argued the $50 million deal was designed to shield company insiders and settle those claims for pennies on the dollar.

**Structural Watch: Texas Two-Step Jurisprudence After Tehum.** The Lefkowitz jurisdictional filing is the latest data point in an ongoing circuit-level question about the permissible reach of divisional mergers as a liability-management tool. The Fifth Circuit's treatment of the Texas Two-Step in Tehum is being watched against the Third Circuit's LTL Management analysis, which the Supreme Court declined to review in 2024. If Lefkowitz prevails on jurisdiction in S.D. Tex., future mass-tort debtors may route Texas Two-Step targets through Article III district courts — a structurally more hostile forum for channeling injunctions and consensual third-party releases. 
The settlement resolved Tehum's estate causes of action against YesCare Parties about the Texas divisional merger and related acts; in exchange, YesCare Parties agreed to pay $50 million, split evenly between the PI/WD Trust and the GUC Trust.
 At $50 million against $775 million in alleged exposure, the recovery rate before litigation costs approaches 6.5 cents on the dollar — a reference point that will inform any plaintiff-side negotiating posture if the adversary reaches mediation.
