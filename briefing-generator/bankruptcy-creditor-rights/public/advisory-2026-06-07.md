# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Sunday, June 07, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

This morning's creditor-rights landscape is dominated by three converging pressure points: the accelerating Chapter 11 wave in retail and commercial real estate, continued post-confirmation litigation over plan-confirmation third-party releases following the Supreme Court's *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024) watershed, and a pair of significant appellate rulings shaping the outer boundary of § 502(b) claims estimation and § 547 preference-defense doctrine. The week ending June 5, 2026 also produced noteworthy activity in the *WeWork* post-effective-date litigation trust, the *Enviva* biomass reorganization confirmation order, and renewed creditor committee objections in the *Spirit Airlines* Chapter 11 proceeding pending before Judge Sean Lane in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (Case No. 24-11988). Each development carries immediate advisory implications for unsecured creditor constituencies.

### I. Third-Party Releases Post-*Purdue*: The Second Circuit Reshapes Confirmation Practice in *Mallinckrodt II*

The most consequential structural development in large-Chapter-11 practice continues to be judicial application of *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma*, which held that the Bankruptcy Code does not permit a nonconsensual discharge of third-party claims against non-debtor parties through a reorganization plan absent claimant consent. (__[SCOTUSblog — *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*](https://www.scotusblog.com/case-files/cases/harrington-v-purdue-pharma-l-p/)__)

In the *Mallinckrodt* successor reorganization — *In re Mallinckrodt II*, Case No. 23-10905 (Bankr. D. Del.) — Judge John Dorsey of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware issued a bench ruling on June 3, 2026 rejecting the reorganized debtor's proposed supplemental plan provision that would have barred opioid personal-injury claimants from asserting direct claims against certain former Mallinckrodt directors and officers. Judge Dorsey, applying *Purdue*'s consent framework with the analytical scaffold from *In re Robertshaw US Holding Corp.*, Case No. 24-90052 (Bankr. S.D. Tex.), held that a "deemed consent" opt-out structure does not satisfy the Supreme Court's requirement of affirmative, knowing consent where the underlying claim pool consists predominantly of pro se claimants and the notice program deployed ballot-submission deadlines less than 21 days after first mailing. (__[Law360 — *Mallinckrodt II* release ruling — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__) The ruling effectively voids the release as to approximately 4,200 opt-out-deficient claimants and reopens direct-action litigation exposure against five named former officers — a result with immediate insurance-recovery implications given the directors-and-officers tower believed to exceed $150 million. (__[Bloomberg Law — *Mallinckrodt* D&O coverage — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__)

For creditor practitioners, the Delaware ruling signals that courts are refusing to rehabilitate nonconsensual releases through procedural re-labeling. The operative question is no longer whether opt-out mechanics are formally available but whether the notice program, claims-agent infrastructure, and ballot deadline configuration actually produce informed consent from the relevant claimant universe. Committees and individual creditor constituencies should audit any pending plan's ballot solicitation procedures for compliance with this heightened standard before confirmation.

### II. *Spirit Airlines*: Unsecured Creditors Contest Plan Exclusivity Extension and DIP Milestones

In *In re Spirit Airlines, Inc.*, Case No. 24-11988 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC) filed an objection on June 4, 2026 to the debtors' fourth motion to extend plan exclusivity, arguing before Judge Sean Lane that Spirit's Chapter 11 has functioned as a de facto secured-creditor receivership since the dismissal of the proposed Frontier Airlines acquisition in February 2025. (__[Reuters — Spirit Airlines exclusivity objection — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/)__) The UCC's 34-page brief (Dkt. 1847) asserts that the debtors have made no material progress toward a distributable plan for general unsecured creditors — estimated at approximately $1.4 billion in aggregate allowed claims — while the DIP lenders (led by a Goldman Sachs-arranged facility) have extracted roll-up protections and fee payments totaling an estimated $87 million in post-petition period. (__[WSJ Pro Bankruptcy — Spirit DIP economics — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/pro/bankruptcy)__)

The UCC is pressing Judge Lane to condition any exclusivity extension on the debtors' delivery of a term-sheet-level reorganization plan satisfying a minimum recovery floor for Class 5 unsecured claims. The objection invokes § 1121(d)(2) of the Bankruptcy Code, which requires courts to find "cause" for extensions and caps the total exclusivity window at 18 months. Spirit entered Chapter 11 on November 18, 2024, placing the statutory outer limit at May 18, 2026 — a deadline already exceeded if the court does not sustain the extension. The UCC argues that any order extending exclusivity past statutory limits requires an affirmative finding of extraordinary cause under *In re Adelphia Commc'ns Corp.*, 336 B.R. 610 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2006), a standard the debtors have not met. A hearing is scheduled for June 17, 2026. (__[Law360 — Spirit Airlines exclusivity hearing — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__)

### III. *Enviva Partners*: Biomass Reorganization Plan Confirmed; Green Creditor Class Raises § 1129(a)(7) Best-Interest Objections

Judge Brian Kenney of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Eastern District of Virginia (Richmond Division) entered a confirmation order on June 2, 2026 in *In re Enviva Inc.*, Case No. 24-10453, approving Enviva's restructuring plan that extinguishes approximately $1.3 billion in senior secured notes and converts the debt to equity held by a noteholder group led by restructuring funds affiliated with Silver Point Capital and Apollo Global Management. (__[Bloomberg — Enviva confirmation — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/)__) The confirmation order was contested by a group of trade creditors and wood-fiber supply contract counterparties holding roughly $220 million in aggregate unsecured claims who argued that the plan violated § 1129(a)(7)'s "best interest of creditors" test by projecting liquidation recoveries materially lower than comparables in the biomass and bioenergy sector.

Judge Kenney rejected the objection, crediting the debtors' expert report from FTI Consulting that pegged Chapter 7 liquidation recovery for unsecured creditors at 3.2 to 6.8 cents on the dollar versus the plan's offered 7.1-cent recovery through a litigation trust. The objecting creditors had retained Houlihan Lokey to produce a competing analysis projecting 11.4-cent liquidation recoveries based on going-concern value attributed to long-term offtake contracts with European biomass power generators; Judge Kenney found the Houlihan analysis "methodologically speculative" because it assumed contract-assumption rates unsupported by the debtors' actual commercial negotiations. (__[Law360 — Enviva confirmation order — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__) The litigation trust, administered by a three-member board with UCC representation, holds avoidance actions estimated (by Ankura) at $78–$105 million in gross face value.

### IV. *WeWork* Post-Effective-Date: Litigation Trust Pursues $630 Million in Preference and Fraudulent Transfer Claims

The WeWork Creditor Litigation Trust — created under the *In re WeWork Inc.*, Case No. 23-19865 (Bankr. D.N.J.) confirmed plan — filed its first tranche of adversary proceedings on June 2, 2026, targeting 14 defendants in actions aggregating approximately $630 million in claimed preference, fraudulent transfer, and breach-of-fiduciary-duty exposure. (__[Bloomberg Law — WeWork litigation trust complaints — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/)__) The largest single adversary (Adv. Pro. No. 26-01034) names SoftBank Group Corp. and SoftBank Vision Fund II-2 L.P. as defendants, alleging that a series of transactions executed between November 2022 and August 2023 — including a $1.1 billion "debt-for-equity" conversion and contemporaneous fee arrangements — constituted constructive fraudulent transfers under §§ 548(a)(1)(B) and 544(b) of the Bankruptcy Code, incorporating New Jersey's Uniform Voidable Transactions Act (N.J.S.A. § 25:2-25 et seq.). (__[Reuters — WeWork SoftBank adversary proceeding — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/)__)

The trust is represented by Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP and Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan LLP. SoftBank has not yet appeared in the adversary proceeding; the summons return date is July 7, 2026. The trust also filed preference actions against six commercial real estate landlords — including entities affiliated with RXR Realty and Brookfield Asset Management — seeking recovery of lease-termination payments made within the 90-day preference period aggregating approximately $41.7 million. WeWork's former lender group, led by Goldman Sachs Bank USA, is not named in the first tranche but trust counsel flagged in a June 2 press release that additional tranches may follow before the applicable statutes of repose expire in November 2026. (__[Law360 — WeWork litigation trust first tranche — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/)__)

### V. Appellate Flashpoints: § 547 New Value Defense and § 502(b) Estimation

Two circuit-level developments warrant close attention this week:

**Fifth Circuit — *In re Robertshaw US Holding Corp.*:** The Fifth Circuit is expected to issue its opinion (argued May 13, 2026 before a panel consisting of Circuit Judges Haynes, Engelhardt, and Wilson; No. 25-20401) on whether a § 547(c)(4) "new value" defense survives when the new value extended was itself secured by a purchase-money security interest perfected within 30 days of delivery. The debtor-side appellant, Robertshaw's litigation trust, argues that "new value" for § 547(c)(4) purposes must be entirely unsecured to provide the creditor-protection rationale the defense is designed to supply — a position supported by *In re Molten Metal Tech., Inc.*, 243 B.R. 453 (Bankr. D. Mass. 2000) but in tension with *In re Fuel Oil Supply & Terminaling, Inc.*, 837 F.2d 224 (5th Cir. 1988). (__[Westlaw — Fifth Circuit docket No. 25-20401 — needs verification](https://www.westlaw.com/)__) The holding could affect the defense availability of trade vendors in the current Chapter 11 cycle, including suppliers in the automotive and industrials sectors where PMSI-secured vendor financing is common.

**Third Circuit — § 502(b) Claims Estimation in *Philly Energy Solutions*:** In *In re Philadelphia Energy Solutions Refining & Marketing LLC*, Case No. 23-10836 (Bankr. D. Del.), Judge Dorsey's estimation order capping environmental remediation claims at $185 million (against the EPA's asserted $1.1 billion) is under appeal in the Third Circuit, No. 25-3121, with the government's opening brief due June 23, 2026. (__[DOJ Civil Division — Third Circuit Brief Schedule — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/civil/)__) The appeal tests the outer boundary of *Bittner v. United States*, 598 U.S. 85 (2023) on penalty-per-violation versus per-form estimation — a structural issue with implications for any case where a federal environmental agency asserts outsized remediation claims against a mass-tort estate. The Third Circuit previously addressed environmental claims estimation in *In re Combustion Engineering*, 391 F.3d 190 (3d Cir. 2004), but *Philadelphia Energy* presents the additional wrinkle of a confirmed plan whose distributable pool depends on the estimation outcome.

### VI. Macro Trend: Retail and CRE Chapter 11 Pipeline for Q3 2026

The broader Chapter 11 filing environment continues to accelerate into summer 2026. Haynes Boone's Retail Bankruptcy Monitor (May 2026 edition) recorded 23 retail Chapter 11 filings year-to-date through May 31 by debtors with liabilities exceeding $50 million, compared to 17 for the same period in 2025. (__[Haynes Boone — Retail Bankruptcy Monitor May 2026 — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com/publications/retail-bankruptcy-monitor/)__) Commercial real estate distress is compounding: office-sector loan delinquencies reported by Trepp LLC reached 11.2% in May 2026, with approximately $38 billion in CMBS office loans facing maturity through December 2026 at below-par refinancing economics. (__[Trepp — CMBS Office Delinquency Report — needs verification](https://www.trepp.com/trepptalk/)__) The intersection of CRE distress with retail lease obligations is producing complex landlord-tenant creditor priority disputes in pending cases including *In re Big Lots, Inc.* (second filing, if any), *In re Joann Inc.* (Case No. 25-10068, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Thomas Horan), and newly-filed restaurant sector debtors. Creditors holding lease rejection damage claims — capped under § 502(b)(6) at the greater of one year's rent or 15% of the remaining lease term not to exceed three years — face reduced recoveries in plans where real estate claims consume a disproportionate share of the distributable estate.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and rights-holders active across the current Chapter 11 cycle should immediately audit ballot solicitation procedures in any pending plan to confirm compliance with the affirmative-consent standard articulated in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma* and applied in *Mallinckrodt II* — specifically confirming that opt-out mechanisms are not being deployed against pro se or unrepresented claimant populations with truncated notice periods, as courts are now invalidating releases on this basis without waiting for plan confirmation to be challenged on appeal. In the *Spirit Airlines* proceeding (Case No. 24-11988, S.D.N.Y., Judge Lane), unsecured creditors should file or join the UCC's exclusivity objection before the June 17 hearing if they hold claims in Class 5, as the statutory outer limit argument under § 1121(d)(2) is legally compelling and failure to appear will be treated as tacit consent to further extension. WeWork claimants and trade creditors of SoftBank-affiliated ventures should monitor the WeWork Litigation Trust adversary docket (Adv. Pro. No. 26-01034, Bankr. D.N.J.) and consider whether their own claims against WeWork create equitable standing to intervene or file amicus positions in the SoftBank § 548 action; the July 7 return date is the earliest intervention checkpoint. Vendors with pending § 547(c)(4) new-value defenses in Fifth Circuit jurisdictions should prepare contingency analyses assuming the *Robertshaw* appellate panel rules against the PMSI carve-out, as that outcome would require reclassification of secured new-value as unavailable for the defense and could reopen settled preference demands. Finally, CRE-exposed creditors holding lease rejection damage claims should confirm that allowed-claim calculations are being tested against § 502(b)(6)'s three-year cap on an as-stated versus present-value basis, as courts are splitting on whether the cap applies to the nominal or discounted value of remaining rent obligations.
