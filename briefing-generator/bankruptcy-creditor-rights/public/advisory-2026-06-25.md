### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — DIP Locked, UCC Formation Imminent, July 8 Bid Deadline Approaching


Sleep Number Corporation filed its Chapter 11 petition on June 12, 2026 in the Southern District of New York, disclosing assets of $500 million to $1 billion against liabilities exceeding $1 billion.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before Judge Kyu Young Paek.
 Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick leading). 
The company listed $642 million in assets and $1.28 billion in debts at the petition date.


The sale mechanics are tightly scripted. 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern Section 363 sale backstopped by a stalking-horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., with SNBR committing — subject to Court approval — to acquire substantially all of the company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, a July 15, 2026 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026, building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties (19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals).


The DIP is now documented. 
On June 16, 2026, Sleep Number entered into the Fourteenth Amendment to the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement, pursuant to which the prepetition lenders committed to provide up to $260 million of debtor-in-possession financing in the form of up to $65 million in new money superpriority senior secured term loan commitments, available in multiple draws.
 
The DIP facility also includes up to $195 million in a roll-up of prepetition obligations converted on a 3:1 basis.
 
The Bankruptcy Court entered an interim order approving the facility, with a final hearing scheduled for July 9, 2026; the DIP loans mature September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR + 8.00% or base rate plus 7.00%.


For unsecured creditors, the capital structure is sobering. 
The commencement of the Chapter 11 cases constituted an event of default under the Prepetition Credit Agreement, resulting in acceleration; substantially all of the Company's debt, approximately $672.5 million in aggregate principal as of the Petition Date, is now in default and accelerated but subject to the automatic stay.
 
The debtor's largest unsecured creditors include Leggett & Platt Inc. (over $10.2 million), Horizon Media (over $7.3 million), Elite Comfort Solutions (over $6.1 million), Flextronics International Europe (over $6 million), Gumotex (over $3.7 million), and NFL Ventures LLP (over $2.6 million).
 Those figures suggest trade creditors face significant compression absent a robust auction premium above the $415 million stalking-horse floor. 
The company discloses significant risks related to its Chapter 11 process, including the likely cancellation of its common shares.


On the UCC front: 
the U.S. Trustee issued creditor committee questionnaires under Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), with a response deadline of June 22, 2026 at 12:00 p.m. ET.
 The UCC, once constituted, will have an immediate and contested role — the DIP roll-up structure (prepetition debt converting 3:1 into DIP priority ahead of unsecured claims) is precisely the mechanism that draws committee scrutiny in cases of this profile. 
A hearing to consider the Bidding Procedures Motion is set for July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, and a final second-day hearing is scheduled for July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.



The first-day declaration attributes distress to the April 2025 wave of U.S. tariffs — including IEEPA-based levies on certain direct-import products — followed by a February 2026 U.S. Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs, leaving the company to navigate continuing regulatory uncertainty.
 
Leading into the filing, the Debtors operated under a Forbearance Agreement and Thirteenth Amendment to the Prepetition Credit Agreement entered April 27, 2026, which provided $25 million in incremental term loans, forbore against specified events of default, suspended the minimum-liquidity covenant through early July 2026, and imposed milestones toward a strategic transaction designed to pay the Prepetition Credit Agreement obligations in full.


The speed of the case — a four-week sprint from petition to bid deadline — reflects the pre-packaged marketing process but compresses any committee investigation window materially. Creditors and counterparties with lease, warranty, or supply claims need to act now.

### BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Post-Sale Hearing; Plan Confirmation Now the Central Risk


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The court filing showed Reliz reporting assets between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The 363 sale process has concluded at the hearing level. 
As confirmed in a court document filed May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors; Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
A hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026; parties continued to collaborate on the administrative process to complete the transaction, with final completion subject to court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.


The sale price relative to the capital structure is the creditor-recovery issue. 
According to BlockFills, trading volume exceeded $60 billion in 2025 — a 28% increase from the prior year — and the firm served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 A $3.25 million asset purchase against $145 million in unsecured customer and trade claims implies a recovery in the low single-digit percentage range absent material plan contributions from litigation recoveries or avoidance actions.

The adversarial landscape adds recovery optionality — but also complexity. 
An adversary proceeding against Artha Investment Partners LLC is pending in the same court under Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH).
 
In the weeks before the filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two pre-petition customer actions — Dominion Capital LLC v. March 5, 2026) — with plaintiffs seeking emergency injunctive relief and courts granting temporary restraining orders.



The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.


The plan track is now the primary creditor battleground. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement and scheduling a confirmation hearing, establishing deadlines for plan objections, claim objections, voting procedures, and ballots (Docket No. 388).
 
Reliz is now operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.

### Structural Observation: The 363-to-Plan Sequencing Risk in Both Cases

Both matters present a version of the same structural problem for unsecured creditors: a 363 sale that absorbs the operating assets and most of the going-concern value, leaving a residual estate to fund plan distributions. In BlockFills, the $3.25 million sale proceeds against $145 million in unsecured claims means recovery depends almost entirely on avoidance action and litigation yields — a highly uncertain binary. In Sleep Number, the $415 million stalking-horse floor must clear $672.5 million in secured debt before unsecured creditors see a dollar; whether a competitive auction moves the needle materially depends on whether the 14-week prepetition process left genuine strategic interest on the table. 
The Debtors canvassed 11 prospective alternative financing sources — none willing to lend on an unsecured or junior basis — before settling on the negotiated DIP.
 That canvass suggests the secured lender group has substantial leverage over process and timeline.

The roll-up mechanic in Sleep Number deserves particular attention from the UCC once constituted. 
The DIP facility includes up to $195 million in a roll-up of prepetition obligations converted on a 3:1 basis.
 Roll-ups of that magnitude — converting prepetition unsecured or undersecured exposure into priming superpriority DIP claims — are consistently challenged by committees as unfair to general unsecured creditors, and Judge Paek's chambers will likely see that briefing as a threshold issue once the committee retains counsel.

D. Del.), the July 13 confirmation hearing is the next hard deadline, and creditors holding claims against the estate should (a) confirm their proofs of claim are on file (the bar date notice requires originally executed paper filings, not electronic submission), (b) review the May 28 disclosure-statement approval order (Dkt. 388) and associated plan solicitation materials, (c) assess voting positions given the likely low single-digit recovery implied by the $3.25 million Keyrock sale price against $145 million in unsecured obligations, and (d) monitor the adversary proceedings — particularly Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners — as the principal upside scenario for unsecured recovery beyond the sale proceeds; and in *Power Block Coin* (24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah), creditors should monitor for Docket Entry 519 details and any subsequent adversary or asset-disposition filings.
