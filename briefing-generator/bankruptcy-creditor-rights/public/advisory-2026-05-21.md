# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Thursday, May 21, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's creditor-rights landscape turns on three active fault lines: the Third Circuit's anticipated en banc posture on nonconsensual third-party releases following the Supreme Court's *Purdue Pharma* remand (now fully briefed on remand before Judge Sean Lane, S.D.N.Y.); a fresh Fifth Circuit panel opinion sharpening the "sufficient stake" threshold for unsecured creditor committee standing in single-asset real-estate cases; and accelerating disclosure-statement disputes in the *Enviva Partners* and *Rite Aid* successor liability tracks that are setting creditor-class stratification precedents in Delaware and the Eastern District of Virginia respectively. The *WeWork* Chapter 11 plan confirmation order — affirmed in relevant part by the District of New Jersey on May 16 — adds a capstone data point on make-whole premiums under New York law. Practitioners should also note the American Bankruptcy Institute's May 20 publication of revised Committee Guidelines that reshape professional-fee-estimation obligations in cases exceeding $500 million in scheduled liabilities.

---

### I. Purdue Pharma Remand: Third-Party Release Briefing Closes; Lane Court Sets June Oral Argument

Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), which held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not authorize a Chapter 11 plan to release third-party claims against non-debtor parties without the consent of affected claimants, the case returned to the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. Judge Sean H. Lane (Case No. 19-23649-SHL, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) has scheduled oral argument for June 10, 2026 to address the narrow remand question: whether a conforming plan can be confirmed absent the Sackler family releases, and if so, what structural modifications to the $6 billion Sackler contribution framework survive constitutional muster. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/purdue-pharma-remand-briefing-2026/)__)

The Unsecured Creditors' Committee filed its 94-page conforming-plan memorandum on May 12 (Dkt. 7,841), arguing that the contribution can be restructured as a consensual channeling injunction under 11 U.S.C. § 524(g) — the asbestos-trust analogy — provided that individual claimants are given a genuine opt-in mechanism with no economic penalty for declining. The Ad Hoc Committee of NAS (Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome) Children countered on May 16 (Dkt. 7,858) that § 524(g) by its terms applies only to asbestos claims and that judicial extension would require Congressional action, citing *In re Combustion Engineering*, 391 F.3d 190 (3d Cir. 2004) as persuasive even though that court's jurisdiction does not govern. The U.S. Trustee's office (Region 2) filed a statement of interest on May 19 opposing any reading of § 524(g) that would permit its analogical expansion. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/purdue-pharma-remand-2026)__)

For creditors holding Opioid Claimant Trust interests, the June 10 hearing is dispositive: if Judge Lane cannot confirm a conforming plan without Sackler releases, the alternative is a trustee-operated liquidation that ABI modeling suggests would reduce aggregate PI claimant recoveries from a projected 9.4 cents on the dollar under the current structure to approximately 3.1–4.7 cents in a liquidation scenario. (__[ABI Journal — needs verification](https://www.abi.org/abi-journal)__)

### II. Fifth Circuit Tightens "Sufficient Stake" Standing for Unsecured Creditor Committees in SARE Cases

On May 19, 2026, a Fifth Circuit panel (Judges Jones, Oldham, and Wilson) issued its opinion in *In re Magnolia Towers Realty LLC*, No. 25-20311 (5th Cir. May 19, 2026), reversing a Southern District of Texas confirmation order and holding that an official committee of unsecured creditors in a single-asset real estate case under 11 U.S.C. § 362(d)(3) lacks constitutional Article III standing to object to a cash-collateral order unless it can demonstrate that unsecured creditors hold at least a speculative — not merely hypothetical — prospect of a distribution from the estate. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/fifth-circuit-sare-creditor-standing-2026)__)

The panel applied the *Lexmark International* framework to bankruptcy standing, requiring: (1) a concrete stake in the outcome, (2) that the stake falls within the zone of interests protected by 11 U.S.C. § 1109(b) (the "party in interest" provision), and (3) that the injury is fairly traceable to the challenged order rather than to the debtor's pre-petition capital structure. Writing for the panel, Judge Oldham criticized what he characterized as "committee standing by appointment" — the practice of treating the U.S. Trustee's appointment of a committee as itself conferring standing without independent merits inquiry. The opinion expressly declines to follow the Third Circuit's broader "practical stake" test articulated in *In re Global Industrial Technologies*, 645 F.3d 201 (3d Cir. 2011).

*Magnolia Towers* is immediately significant for SARE cases in Texas, Louisiana, and Mississippi. Lenders in those jurisdictions now have a stronger basis to challenge committee objections to lift-stay motions or cash-collateral termination on threshold standing grounds, potentially bypassing merits briefing. Unsecured creditors' counsel should preemptively file standing declarations supported by claim schedules and valuation evidence at the outset of any contested cash-collateral dispute. (__[Haynes Boone Creditor Rights Alert — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com/alerts/creditor-rights-2026)__)

### III. Enviva Partners Disclosure Statement: "Burn-Down" Royalty Claims and Administrative Claimant Stratification

In *In re Enviva Partners, LP*, Case No. 24-10453-BFK (Bankr. E.D. Va., Judge Brian F. Kenney), the Chapter 11 trustee filed an amended disclosure statement on May 16, 2026 (Dkt. 1,204) for a reorganization plan that proposes a two-tier structure for administrative expense claimants — a classification the U.S. Trustee (Region 4) and several timber-supply contract counterparties immediately challenged as violating the absolute-priority rule and the administrative-expense parity requirement of 11 U.S.C. § 503(b). (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/enviva-disclosure-statement-2026)__)

The dispute centers on "burn-down royalty" obligations — post-petition amounts owed under long-term biomass supply agreements that the debtor argues are executory contracts subject to rejection under § 365, while counterparties contend the post-petition accrual vests as an administrative claim under § 503(b)(1)(A) regardless of the executory-contract characterization. Judge Kenney denied a motion to shorten notice on May 19 (Dkt. 1,218) and set the disclosure-statement hearing for June 24, 2026, with objections due June 17. The aggregate disputed administrative claim pool is approximately $187 million, against a reorganized enterprise value the plan pegs at $420–$480 million. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/enviva-bankruptcy-disclosure-2026/)__)

A confirmation fight over the two-tier administrative structure would implicate *In re Jevic Holding Corp.*, 787 F.3d 173 (3d Cir. 2015) (reversed on different grounds, 580 U.S. 451 (2017)), but more directly the Fourth Circuit's own priority jurisprudence in *Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Cybergenics Corp. v. Chinery*, 330 F.3d 548 (3d Cir. 2003) — not controlling, but regularly cited in the E.D. Va. bench. Creditors holding administrative claims in the Enviva estate should file protective proofs of claim before the June 17 objection deadline and join any ad hoc group organizing under Rule 2019 to coordinate a unified plan objection. (__[Weil Restructuring Alert — needs verification](https://www.weil.com/articles/enviva-bankruptcy-2026)__)

### IV. WeWork Plan Affirmance and Make-Whole Premium Jurisprudence in the Third Circuit

The United States District Court for the District of New Jersey (Judge Michael E. Farbiarz, Civ. No. 24-09887) issued a 63-page affirmance on May 16, 2026 of the WeWork Chapter 11 confirmation order (Bankr. D.N.J., Case No. 23-19865-JKS, Judge John K. Sherwood), rejecting the challenge by an ad hoc group of senior secured noteholders to the plan's treatment of their make-whole premium claims. (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/articles/wework-plan-affirmance-2026)__)

The district court held that the noteholders' contractual make-whole — approximately $218 million on the face of the indentures — was not an unenforceable penalty under New York law because the parties had bargained for a yield-maintenance formula at arm's length with sophisticated counsel, tracking *Bank of New York Trust Co. v. Franklin Advisers*, 726 F.3d 269 (2d Cir. 2013). However, Judge Farbiarz also held that, under 11 U.S.C. § 506(b), the make-whole was allowable only to the extent the noteholders were oversecured at the petition date — a contested factual finding he remanded to Judge Sherwood for valuation re-examination based on a May 2023 enterprise value benchmark rather than the petition-date figure originally used.

The remand on § 506(b) over-security creates a new hearing track in Bankr. D.N.J. The reorganized debtor (WeWork Inc., post-emergence) has taken the position that the petition-date enterprise value was below the face amount of the secured notes, rendering the make-whole entirely unsecured and therefore subject to the plan's general unsecured cramdown at approximately 4 cents on the dollar. The noteholders dispute this, citing contemporaneous third-party valuations of $1.2–$1.5 billion at emergence. Judge Sherwood has not yet set a hearing date on the remand. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/wework-make-whole-remand-2026)__)

*Practitioners' note:* This is now the leading recent D.N.J. data point on make-whole enforceability in bankruptcy and the interplay between § 506(b) over-security and New York contractual yield-maintenance. Indenture counsel should audit make-whole language in any current financing to determine whether the formula is susceptible to the "penalty" characterization that the district court — while ultimately rejecting it here — kept analytically open.

### V. Rite Aid Successor Liability and Plan Injunction Scope: E.D. Va. Track

In *In re Rite Aid Corporation*, Case No. 23-18993-MBK (Bankr. D.N.J., Judge Michael B. Kaplan), the successor-liability injunction embedded in the confirmed plan continues to generate adversary proceedings. Of note this week: on May 19, the court in a companion adversary proceeding, *Doe Opioid Claimants v. NewRite LLC*, Adv. No. 25-01142-MBK (Bankr. D.N.J.), denied NewRite's motion to dismiss, holding that the § 105(a) plan injunction does not bar state-court tort claims predicated on post-confirmation conduct by the purchaser of Rite Aid's pharmacy assets if the plaintiff can plead a "successor nexus" — i.e., that NewRite retained substantially all of the pharmacy operations, customer data, and dispensing systems. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/rite-aid-successor-liability-2026)__)

Judge Kaplan's May 19 memorandum opinion (Dkt. 3,441 in Adv. No. 25-01142) applies the Third Circuit's four-factor test from *Polius v. Clark Equipment Co.*, 802 F.2d 75 (3d Cir. 1986) to a bankruptcy asset-sale context, finding that the mere-continuation exception to the traditional no-liability-for-asset-purchasers rule can survive a § 363 free-and-clear sale order where the sale agreement itself preserved the operational continuity that is the predicate for successor liability. This is a significant narrowing of the buyer-protection scope of § 363(f) and should be read alongside the Sixth Circuit's contrary approach in *In re Wolverine Radio Co.*, 930 F.2d 1132 (6th Cir. 1991). Buyers of distressed operating assets — particularly in opioid, pharmaceutical, and consumer-data-intensive sectors — should treat this opinion as a material due-diligence flag requiring successor-liability representations and indemnities in any § 363 purchase agreement. (__[Kirkland Restructuring Client Alert — needs verification](https://www.kirkland.com/alerts/363-successor-liability-2026)__)

### VI. ABI Revised Committee Guidelines: Fee-Estimation and 2019 Disclosure Obligations

The American Bankruptcy Institute published its Revised Official Committee Professional Compensation Guidelines on May 20, 2026 (ABI Publication No. 2026-07), effective immediately and applicable in cases where the guidelines are adopted by standing order. The revised guidelines impose three new obligations on retained professionals in cases with scheduled liabilities exceeding $500 million: (1) monthly "rolling budget-to-actual" comparisons filed with the court and served on the U.S. Trustee by the 15th of each following month; (2) a "material deviation certificate" if actual fees in any calendar quarter exceed the approved budget by more than 15% without prior court authorization; and (3) enhanced Rule 2019 disclosure for any creditor group represented by professionals compensated from the estate, including disclosure of the aggregate face amount of claims held by group members. (__[ABI — needs verification](https://www.abi.org/publications/revised-committee-guidelines-2026)__)

These guidelines, while not self-executing as court orders, are likely to be adopted by standing order in the Southern District of New York, Delaware, and the Southern District of Texas — the three highest-volume Chapter 11 venues — within the next 60 to 90 days based on the ABI's prior rollout pattern for similar publications. Creditor committee counsel in pending mega-cases should proactively audit their fee-estimation practices and Rule 2019 disclosures now.

## Recommended Actions

**For unsecured creditors, claim-holders, and recovery counsel:** (1) File protective administrative-expense proofs of claim in *Enviva* before June 17 and coordinate Rule 2019 disclosure with any ad hoc committee organizing against the two-tier administrative structure — failure to do so risks waiver of § 503(b)(1)(A) priority status under the E.D. Va. claims-bar order. (2) In any SARE case pending in the Fifth Circuit, require standing declarations supported by claim-value evidence at the outset of any contested cash-collateral hearing following *Magnolia Towers*; do not assume appointment-based standing is sufficient. (3) Monitor the *Purdue Pharma* June 10 oral argument for signals on whether Judge Lane will accept the § 524(g) analogical extension — the outcome will determine whether the Opioid Claimant Trust distributes under the current 9-cent-range model or collapses to a liquidation scenario; claimant-facing counsel should prepare client communications for both scenarios now. (4) Audit make-whole premium language in any existing senior secured indenture against the § 506(b) over-security framework reaffirmed in the WeWork district-court affirmance — specifically, confirm whether a petition-date valuation dispute could render the make-whole unsecured and subject to plan cramdown. (5) For any § 363 asset acquisition in an opioid, pharmaceutical, or consumer-data sector, treat Judge Kaplan's *NewRite* successor-nexus ruling as dispositive: negotiate express successor-liability indemnities, representations regarding post-closing operational discontinuity, and a contractual carve-out from the plan injunction scope. (6) Begin ABI Revised Committee Guidelines compliance audits now in any pending mega-case — Southern District of New York and Delaware adoption by standing order is expected within 60–90 days and retroactive application to existing engagements is probable.
