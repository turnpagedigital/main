### I. Sleep Number Corp. (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. — Judge Kyu Young Paek) — Sale Order Entered; §341 Meeting Today

The defining event of the week is confirmed. 
Judge Kyu Young Paek of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York approved the Sleep Number sale on July 20, following a competitive auction, with Sleep Country Canada's winning proposal totaling approximately $701 million — comprising approximately $529.5 million in cash plus assumption of selected obligations.
 
The final price was substantially higher than Sleep Country's original $415 million stalking-horse bid; Reuters reported that the Canadian retailer increased its offer by approximately $286.8 million through the auction process.
 
Brooklyn Bedding was selected as the backup bidder.


The sale process itself was compressed and, by the standards of a case this size, orderly. 
Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on June 12, 2026, in the Southern District of New York, jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399.
 
As of the April 30, 2026 balance sheet date, the debtors reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 The DIP structure warrants close attention for those tracking waterfall economics: 
prepetition lenders committed to up to $260 million in debtor-in-possession financing, structured as up to $65 million in new-money superpriority senior secured term loans and up to $195 million in roll-up loans that convert and exchange prepetition credit agreement obligations.
 
Both the DIP Loans and Roll-Up Loans mature September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR plus 8.00% or a base rate plus 7.00%.
 The roll-up ratio — approximately 3:1 in favor of existing lenders converting prepetition exposure — effectively ensures those lenders recover at par before any residual flows downstream.


The buyer's real portfolio decisions arrive during a Designation Rights Period running up to 60 days after closing, in which Sleep Country must formally designate each unassumed lease for assumption and assignment or rejection; any lease not designated by that deadline is automatically rejected without further court order. With closing targeted by July 31, 2026, that window could extend into late September 2026, meaning the full picture of which store locations survive under Sleep Country's ownership will not be known for months post-close.


Two near-term procedural items: 
the Section 341 meeting of creditors — continued from an earlier date — is scheduled today, July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET by Zoom.
 
The transaction remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31.
 For unsecured creditors, 
the filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors,
 though the magnitude of that recovery depends heavily on how the sale proceeds are allocated against the superpriority DIP stack, the roll-up tranche, and any remaining prepetition secured claims. Counsel of record for the debtors is 
Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.


The macro context is not incidental: 
tariffs and inflation were cited as key drivers of the financial distress, with court filings stating that President Trump's tariff policies disrupted the company's international supply chain, increased costs, and hurt profits.
 That framing will likely appear in any future fraudulent transfer or D&O litigation — the line between foreseeable macroeconomic headwinds and management decision-making is where plaintiffs' counsel will probe.

### II. BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. — Judge Thomas M. Horan) — §363 Closed; Plan Confirmation September 11

The asset sale track in BlockFills is functionally complete. 
Keyrock was named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and the U.S. bankruptcy court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.
 
On July 16, 2026, Keyrock announced that it had completed the acquisition of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business assets.
 
According to the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, assuming substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property.


That $3.25 million headline figure against 
approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations
 is the central recovery data point for unsecured claimants. The assets monetized through the §363 sale — the institutional client book, Vision Trader technology, and brokerage infrastructure — represent the enterprise's going-concern value. What remains in the debtors' estate for plan distribution is material recovery questions: litigation claims, causes of action against former insiders, and whatever is left after administrative claims, DIP repayment, and the Celsius secured notes.


The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The Celsius secured tranche sits ahead of the general unsecured pool, further compressing recoveries.

The regulatory dimension of the Keyrock close deserves attention. 
The transaction expands Keyrock's regulatory footprint with the acquisition of a CIMA-registered entity in the Cayman Islands and the proposed acquisition of an FCA-authorized entity in the United Kingdom, with the UK transfer subject to regulatory approval.
 
Final regulatory clearances for the transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026 — in crypto's current regulatory environment, that is not a trivial detail.
 Any regulatory condition that delays or modifies the Keyrock close post-July 16 announcement could affect the sale consideration paid into the estate.

On the plan track, what remains is confirmation. 
The Veritaglobal case portal lists a September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) date as the next material event on the docket.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, and establishing deadlines for objections, claim objections, temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes, and solicitation procedures (Docket No. 388).
 
The confirmation objection deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and the original confirmation hearing was set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The September 11 date on the Veritaglobal portal suggests the July 13 confirmation hearing was adjourned — consistent with the six new docket entries filed on July 23 reflected in the tracked-case ground truth data, though the specific content of those entries was not publicly accessible this run.

The overarching legal question that shaped this entire case — 
whether customer digital assets constitute estate property
 — remains the fulcrum for plan-level distributions. 
On January 16, 2026, counterparty Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; those assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes, the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, the debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 That commingling disclosure is the predicate for the estate-property-vs.-customer-property dispute that drives recovery stratification: if assets are estate property, the general unsecured pool shares ratably; if any class of customer assets is separately traceable, those customers may have priority claims. The plan must resolve this, and confirmation objections filed by July 1 likely pressed this issue directly.


For the restructuring, the company retained Berkeley Research Group as financial advisor in August 2025, with BRG Managing Director Mark Renzi appointed as Chief Restructuring Officer; McDermott Will & Schulte LLP serves as lead bankruptcy counsel.

### III. Broader Landscape — New Filings, Appellate Watch, and Structural Themes

**Rhode Island Jail Operator.** 
On July 13, 2026, a Rhode Island jail operator filed Chapter 11 with $169 million in debt.
 That filing warrants monitoring for creditor-rights practitioners: municipal-contract counterparties in government services insolvencies present unique claim-priority and contract-rejection dynamics under §365, particularly where the debtor's operations are subject to state regulatory oversight.

**Linqto (securities-for-debt).** 
On July 10, 2026, Linqto received court authorization to pay off its Chapter 11 loan with securities.
 The mechanics of that order — securities consideration in satisfaction of a DIP obligation, which implicates §364 priority and the requirement that DIP lenders receive cash or equivalent value on maturity — are worth reviewing if the underlying order is publicly available. Debt-for-equity satisfaction of superpriority administrative claims is structurally unusual and could be litigated if any party challenges the adequacy of the consideration.

**Ambipar / Brazilian Cross-Border Recognition.** 
On July 10, 2026, Ambipar sought U.S. recognition of a Brazilian reorganization.
 Chapter 15 petitions by Latin American debtors have accelerated in 2026 as Brazil's restructuring regime (Lei 11.101) has matured. The threshold questions — center of main interests determination, automatic stay scope, and recognition of plan distributions — remain fact-intensive and circuit-specific.

**Sleep Number's tariff-driven distress as precedent.** The sleep technology case adds to a growing body of Chapter 11 filings where debtors have explicitly attributed financial deterioration to tariff policy disruption. That attribution matters for at least two downstream legal inquiries: (a) whether prepetition lenders who received accelerated paydowns had constructive knowledge of insolvency, and (b) whether the board's decision-making timeline — relative to the tariff announcements — exposes directors to enhanced fiduciary scrutiny. Both questions will surface if any party pursues preference or D&O claims post-confirmation.
