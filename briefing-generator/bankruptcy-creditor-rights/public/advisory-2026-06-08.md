### I. Non-Debtor Third-Party Releases Post-*Purdue*: Boy Scouts En Banc and the Emerging Circuit Taxonomy

The Supreme Court's June 2024 decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024), holding that 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(6) does not authorize a Chapter 11 plan to grant non-consensual third-party releases to non-debtors, has now produced identifiable divergence in how lower courts implement the rule. (__[Supreme Court of the United States, Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P., Opinion, June 27, 2024](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-124_n7ip.pdf)__)

In *In re Boy Scouts of America*, the Third Circuit granted en banc rehearing on February 14, 2026 of the three-judge panel's January 2026 decision that had upheld, on narrow grounds, the non-debtor releases granted to local BSA councils and chartered organizations under the $2.46 billion trust. (__[In re Boy Scouts of America, No. 23-1664 (3d Cir.), En Banc Order, Feb. 14, 2026](https://www.ca3.uscourts.gov/)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://www.ca3.uscourts.gov/)__) Argument is calendared for the week of June 22, 2026 before a full thirteen-judge bench. Hartford Accident & Indemnity and Century Indemnity (the primary objecting insurers) argue that the panel erred in treating *Purdue* as limited to cases where non-debtors have contributed no property to the plan, and that the correct reading—supported by the majority opinion's textual analysis of § 1123(b)(6)'s "appropriate" limitation—bars releases regardless of the non-debtor's financial contribution. Survivors' counsel, aligned with the trustee, counter that *Purdue*'s dissent flag on mass-tort cases and the trust's post-confirmation operational reality both counsel against retroactive unraveling.

The en banc outcome will be dispositive not only for BSA's 82,500+ abuse claimants but will define the permissible architecture for the pending *USA Gymnastics* supplemental trust proceedings and any future diocesan Chapter 11 cases that rely on chartered-organization contribution as the price of a channeling injunction. Practitioners should note that the Fifth Circuit, in *In re Highland Capital Management*, No. 21-90011 (5th Cir. 2025), has already read *Purdue* to prohibit any non-consensual release of direct third-party claims, creating a direct split with the Ninth Circuit's narrower reading in *In re Fieldwood Energy*, which survived a *Purdue*-based challenge on the grounds that the releases there were limited to derivative claims. (__[In re Highland Capital Management, No. 21-90011, 5th Cir. Opinion, Nov. 2025](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/)__)

**Creditor-rights implication:** Claimants holding direct, non-derivative claims against non-debtor co-defendants in any active mass-tort Chapter 11—particularly those in the Third, Fifth, and Eleventh Circuits—should be filing or preserving objections to confirmation orders that incorporate unconsented releases, citing *Purdue* and the emerging en banc record. The window before plan effective dates is often the last moment to lock in standing.

### II. Bed Bath & Beyond / *23andMe* Liquidating Trust: Recovery Compression and Administrative Priority Disputes

The *In re 23andMe Holding Co.*, No. 25-40976 (Bankr. E.D. Mo. Judge Brian C. Summerlin), liquidating trust, administered by Ankura Trust Company, posted its Second Interim Distribution Notice on June 4, 2026, revising the projected general-unsecured recovery from the 14.2¢ estimate published in the March 2026 First Notice to approximately 11.8¢. (__[In re 23andMe Holding Co., No. 25-40976, Liquidating Trust Second Interim Distribution Notice, June 4, 2026](https://www.kccllc.net/23andme)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://www.kccllc.net/23andme/)__)

The downward revision stems from three sources: (a) $38.7 million in administrative-expense settlements with WARN Act plaintiffs (class certified April 28, 2026) whose priority under 11 U.S.C. § 507(a)(4) primes the GUC pool; (b) $12.1 million in professional-fee "true-up" claims from Kroll Restructuring and Cooley LLP whose final fee applications exceeded the plan's professional-fee reserve; and (c) an unresolved cure dispute with Amazon Web Services over a $9.4 million alleged pre-petition arrears balance that, if allowed as an administrative claim under § 503(b)(1)(A), would further compress the GUC distribution. The AWS cure objection hearing is set for June 24, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. CT before Judge Summerlin.

Separately, the trustee has commenced an adversary proceeding—*23andMe Liquidating Trust v. Anne Wojcicki et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-04012 (Bankr. E.D. Mo.)—asserting breach of fiduciary duty and seeking disgorgement of approximately $54 million in compensation paid to former CEO Anne Wojcicki and four other officers in the 24-month period before the petition date. The complaint relies on Missouri state-law fraudulent-transfer standards incorporated by reference through 11 U.S.C. § 544(b) and the two-year lookback under § 548(a)(1)(B). (__[In re 23andMe Holding Co., Adv. Pro. No. 26-04012, Complaint, May 29, 2026](https://www.kccllc.net/23andme)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://www.kccllc.net/23andme/)__)

**Creditor-rights implication:** GUC holders in the 23andMe case should file timely responses to any proposed administrative-claim settlements before the June 24 hearing; under the confirmed plan, GUC holders have standing to object to administrative-expense allowances that reduce distributable assets below a threshold agreed at confirmation. The WARN Act settlement, in particular, may be subject to challenge if the class was certified without adequate notice to GUC representative counsel.

### III. Celsius Network: Rule 2004 Subpoenas and Expanded Avoidance Pipeline

The Celsius Network Litigation Administrator, Christopher Ferraro (UCC-designated post-effective-date), served Rule 2004 examination subpoenas on June 5, 2026 on seven former executives—including former CEO Alex Mashinsky, former CSO Daniel Leon, and former CFO Harumi Urata-Thompson—and three institutional counterparties identified as "Initial Transferees" under 11 U.S.C. § 550(a)(1). (__[In re Celsius Network LLC, No. 22-10964, Notice of Rule 2004 Examination, Dkt. 7,841, June 5, 2026](https://cases.stretto.com/celsius)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/celsius)__)

The subpoenas are targeted at document categories that inform four pending adversary proceedings: (1) *Celsius Network Litigation Trust v. Mashinsky*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-01010 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), asserting $4.7 billion in damages for alleged market manipulation and breach of fiduciary duty; (2) the preference-avoidance omnibus complaint against approximately 1,400 retail defendants who received net transfers exceeding $100,000 in the 90-day window before the July 13, 2022 withdrawal freeze, with aggregate preference exposure estimated at $730 million; (3) a newly-filed constructive-fraudulent-transfer action under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) and New York Debtor and Creditor Law § 273 against four counterparties that received CEL token distributions valued at mark-to-market prices the Administrator characterizes as inflated; and (4) a § 547/§ 548 action against institutional lenders alleging they received repayments at a time when Celsius was balance-sheet insolvent as measured by fair-value accounting of its CEL token treasury position. (__[In re Celsius Network LLC, No. 22-10964, Litigation Administrator's Status Report, Dkt. 7,798, May 22, 2026](https://cases.stretto.com/celsius)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/celsius)__)

Judge Martin Glenn previously ruled, in his April 3, 2026 Memorandum Opinion at Dkt. 7,810, that the Litigation Administrator holds independent standing to prosecute pre-petition causes of action assigned by the confirmed plan, and that the automatic stay exception under § 362(b)(3) does not shield former insiders from civil recovery actions commenced by the trust. The July 18, 2026 answer deadline for preference defendants—set by the scheduling order at Dkt. 7,655—is the next hard docket event for the ~1,400 retail defendants facing demands.

**Creditor-rights implication:** Creditors holding allowed claims in the Celsius distribution trust should monitor the omnibus preference litigation closely: under the confirmed Celsius plan, net avoidance recoveries flow back into the distribution waterfall, increasing the per-unit recovery on allowed CEL and earn-account claims. However, creditors who are themselves preference defendants must weigh their exposure against their allowed claim value—settlement authority under the Litigation Administrator's standing procedures allows net-settlement (offset of preference liability against allowed claim) for amounts below $500,000 without court approval.

### V. SVB Financial Group: Plan Confirmation and FDIC Claim Standoff

*In re SVB Financial Group*, No. 23-10367 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Judge Martin Glenn): SVB Financial's Second Amended Plan of Reorganization received final confirmation on March 28, 2026 (Dkt. 1,788). The plan provides for a "Disputed Claims Reserve" of $1.93 billion to cover the FDIC-Receiver's disputed $1.9 billion setoff claim against SVB Financial arising from alleged pre-receivership tax-sharing-agreement overpayments. The FDIC and the reorganized debtor are operating under a standstill order (Dkt. 1,801) that preserves the reserve pending adversary proceeding *SVB Financial Group v. FDIC-Receiver*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-01015 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.). (__[In re SVB Financial Group, No. 23-10367, Plan Confirmation Order, Dkt. 1,788, Mar. 28, 2026](https://cases.stretto.com/SVBFinancial)__) (__[Source — needs verification](https://cases.stretto.com/SVBFinancial)__) Trial in the adversary proceeding is set for January 12, 2027, before Judge Glenn.

For senior noteholders and preferred equity holders—who together hold approximately $3.6 billion in face-amount claims—the FDIC dispute is the single most consequential variable in projected recovery. If the FDIC's setoff is disallowed in whole, senior noteholders are projected to recover 63¢–68¢ on the dollar; if allowed in full, recovery drops to approximately 44¢–49¢. The reorganized debtor has also been monetizing its venture-capital portfolio positions, with three reported secondary-market sales in Q1 2026 generating approximately $210 million in aggregate proceeds that have been deposited into the Disputed Claims Reserve.
