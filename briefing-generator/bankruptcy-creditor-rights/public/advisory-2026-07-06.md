### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Key Issue on Customer Property Ownership Remains Live


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH).
 The docket's last-dated filing per CourtListener is docket entry 489 on July 2, 2026. No new entries registered in the last 72 hours as of this writing — but this case is categorically not quiet.

The central unresolved issue — and the one that carries the highest recovery variance for creditors — is whether customer digital assets held by BlockFills constitute property of the bankruptcy estate or customer property held in trust. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 That question is not merely academic: 
the Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations
, and if customer digital assets are deemed estate property, that pool expands materially and recovery rates compress across the board. If customer assets are segregated as trust property under applicable state law or commodity account principles, the committee's leverage diminishes and NewCo structure terms become the operative recovery vehicle.

The case has active adversary dynamics. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026) and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026). The plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and the courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 Both actions were stayed by the filing; whether the automatic stay continues to hold against the fraud-based theories will be a pressure point as the plan process approaches. (__[Chapter11Cases.com — BlockFills Special Report](https://chapter11cases.com/blogs/news/blockfills-crypto-brokerage-pursues-dual-track-restructuring-in-delaware)__)

The liquidity collapse narrative is instructive for distressed investors evaluating other crypto intermediaries. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius. Compounding these balance sheet pressures were stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 The UCC is represented by Morris James LLP (Eric J. Monzo) with FTI Consulting as financial advisor; 
UCC members include SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.

### NFE Global Holdings Limited — Chapter 15 Recognition Complete; Implementation Expected Q3 2026

This cross-border matter moved to a materially new posture last week. 
In *Re NFE Global Holdings Limited* [2026] EWHC 1620 (Ch) (Cawson J, sanction judgment) and [2026] EWHC 1223 (Ch) (Hildyard J, convening judgment), the English court sanctioned two restructuring plans under Part 26A of the Companies Act 2006 that extinguished approximately US$9.6 billion of debt across the New Fortress Energy group.
 The sanction order was entered on June 18, 2026. 
Chapter 15 recognition was subsequently granted in the United States on June 26, 2026, although Judge Glenn's detailed reasoning is awaited.


The creditor dynamics were notably frictionless. 
All seven creditor classes voted in favour, six unanimously and one by 99.84%, making cross-class cram-down unnecessary.
 
Total plan creditor consideration comprised new equity and new takeback debt instruments totalling US$971 million. Restructuring benefits were allocated in two stages: first between five different collateral pools by reference to the increase in value each pool derived versus the relevant alternative; then pari passu within each pool.


The forum selection rationale carries significant precedential weight for future cross-border deals. 
The court described the case as an example of "good forum shopping": the English restructuring plan under Part 26A was chosen because it was expected to produce a superior outcome for creditors, a US$1.44 billion uplift versus the relevant alternative.
 U.S. creditors with exposure to multi-jurisdictional LBO capital structures should note the combination: a Part 26A plan sanctioned at the High Court level, followed by Chapter 15 recognition in the S.D.N.Y., is now a confirmed execution pathway for $9+ billion restructurings. 
The transactions contemplated by the UK RP are expected to be implemented by the third quarter of 2026, subject to the satisfaction of customary conditions and regulatory approvals.
 Implementation mechanics and the separation of "BrazilCo" (NFE's Brazilian terminals and power plants) from "New NFE" (the remaining LNG-to-power platform) remain the outstanding operational items. For the 12-month period ending March 31, 2026, bankruptcy filings rose to 591,850, an 11.9% increase from 529,080 during the year ending March 31, 2025. Business filings increased to 25,960, while nonbusiness filings increased to 565,890.
 Chapter 11 activity in the consumer/retail sector continues to account for a disproportionate share of large-case volume. 
High rates on imports from key trading partners have weighed on input costs and disrupted global supply chains, with consumer products and industrials among the hardest-hit sectors.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed investors should treat the next nine days in the Sleep Number § 363 process as a hard deadline regime: the bid deadline falls tomorrow (July 8, 4:00 p.m. ET) with the § 341 meeting running in parallel the same afternoon — any unsecured creditor not yet represented on the UCC or a bidder consortium should engage counsel today, given that the sale objection deadline is July 10, the auction is July 13, and the sale hearing is July 15 before Judge Paek; in a compressed 26-day postpetition process with a 14-week prepetition marketing run already completed, the auction field and committee posture are largely set and latecomer leverage is minimal. In the BlockFills case (26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan), the operative question is whether docket entries 488–489 (filed July 2, 2026) introduce any modification to the dual-track NewCo/363 structure or the customer property framework — investors holding claims should monitor the Veritaglobal portal at veritaglobal.net/blockfills for document access, scrutinize any adversary complaint filings for fraud-based theories that might pierce the automatic stay, and watch for Rule 3001 claim transfer notices that would signal secondary market activity and implied claim valuations; the gap between an estate-property finding and a customer-trust finding represents potentially the entire unsecured recovery differential. On the NFE Chapter 15 matter, investors in the new takeback instruments should monitor Judge Glenn's forthcoming written order for any conditions or carve-outs attached to recognition, and track Q3 2026 implementation milestones given that the BrazilCo separation remains subject to regulatory approvals.
