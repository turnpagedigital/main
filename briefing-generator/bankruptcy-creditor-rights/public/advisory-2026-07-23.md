# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Thursday, July 23, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The dominant development this week is the court-approved, $701 million Section 363 sale of Sleep Number Corporation — a 69% auction premium over the stalking-horse floor that now sets the terms for the wind-down and determines what, if anything, reaches unsecured creditors. In parallel, the BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings) case has crossed its sale-closing milestone, with Keyrock SA completing its $3.25 million acquisition of operating assets on July 16; the residual estate now moves toward a liquidating trust, and the September 11, 2026 governmental bar date is the next hard deadline for creditors who have not yet filed. Together, these two cases illustrate the week's central theme: compressed Section 363 timelines that structurally constrain committee investigation periods and leave general unsecured creditors dependent on auction surplus — which, in Sleep Number's case, arrived, and in BlockFills', did not.**

### Sleep Number Corporation — Case No. 26-11399 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) · Hon. Kyu Young Paek

**Sale approved; closing expected July 31. Unsecured recovery picture remains open but constrained.**


Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on June 12, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
The petition disclosed $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities, with the filing designed to effectuate a going-concern sale of substantially all assets under Section 363, backstopped by a stalking-horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc.


The auction ran on July 13, 2026, and the result was material. 
Sleep Country's original stalking-horse proposal contemplated $415 million in cash plus the assumption of selected liabilities, which established the opening terms for the auction; Sleep Country then increased its proposal following the competitive process, with the court-approved bid valued at approximately $701 million, with $529.5 million payable in cash.
 
Reuters reported that Sleep Country agreed to increase its offer by approximately $286.8 million through the auction process, with Brooklyn Bedding selected as the backup bidder.
 
After the competitive auction, the U.S. Bankruptcy Court approved the sale to Sleep Country on July 20, 2026.
 
The sale remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31.


The structural arithmetic matters for distressed investors. 
The DIP facility, provided by prepetition lenders, committed up to $260 million — comprising $65 million in new-money superpriority senior secured term loans and $195 million in roll-up loans converting prepetition Credit Agreement obligations.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans mature on September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR + 8.00% or base rate plus 7.00%.
 
The estate preserved roughly $75.9 million in federal and state net operating losses and interest carryforwards, though in a Section 363 asset sale, those attributes typically stay with the selling entity rather than transferring to the buyer, limiting near-term utility.
 The $286.8 million auction jump creates theoretical headroom above the secured debt stack, but the precise waterfall — including DIP repayment, roll-up satisfaction, cure costs, and administrative claims — has not yet been publicly resolved. 
The filing indicated that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
, and the sale premium is the mechanism that makes that possible, but the magnitude of any distribution will depend on post-closing reconciliation of assumed and excluded liabilities, working-capital adjustments, and administrative expense levels that are not yet public.


The Section 341 meeting of creditors has been continued and will be held by Zoom conference on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET
 — one day from today. This is the next immediate procedural checkpoint. 
Debtors' counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP
, and Kroll Restructuring Administration serves as claims and noticing agent. 
The DIP milestone schedule ran concurrently with the Challenge Deadline, compressing the effective window for any committee investigation.
 Whether a creditors' committee conducted a meaningful lien challenge before the auction closed has not been publicly confirmed. 
First-quarter sales fell 19% to approximately $319 million and the company posted a loss of roughly $50 million, with management citing tariffs and inflation as key pressures
 — the macro forces that precipitated the filing remain relevant context for any plan of distribution or wind-down that follows closing.


The deal combines Sleep Number's 572 stores across 50 U.S. states with Sleep Country's 307 corporate-owned stores across Canada.
 For trade creditors and warranty-claim holders, the operative question is which liabilities Sleep Country assumed and which remain with the estate. That detail will turn on the asset purchase agreement as filed and the sale order entered by Judge Paek — neither of which is publicly reproduced here.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) · Hon. Thomas M. Horan · Bankr. D. Del.

**Sale closed July 16. Plan confirmation was targeted for July 13. Governmental bar date: September 11, 2026. What remains is the liquidating trust.**


On March 15, 2026, each of the four debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371, before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


The sale track resolved first and at a stark discount to liabilities. 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors per the bankruptcy court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge approved the $3.25 million sale on June 16, 2026, as BlockFills prepared for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 
Keyrock completed its acquisition of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business on July 16, 2026.


The regulatory dimension of the Keyrock transaction is not fully closed. 
The deal folds in a CIMA-registered entity in the Cayman Islands, acquired outright, plus an FCA-authorised entity in the United Kingdom still awaiting regulatory approval.
 
Final regulatory clearances for the transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026, which in crypto's current regulatory environment is not a trivial detail.
 Whether any regulatory condition failure could trigger a post-closing adjustment to the purchase price — or affect asset transfer completeness — is a live issue for the liquidating trust's recovery calculus.

The plan track was running concurrently. 
The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, the confirmation objection deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and the confirmation hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket ground truth shows the last filing date as July 17, 2026, with zero new filings in the 72-hour window before this advisory — consistent with a case that has crossed its major milestones and is now in a quieter post-confirmation or post-hearing posture. 
The next hard public deadline is the governmental bar date: September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.


The economics for general unsecured creditors are stark. 
The debtors reported $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities at filing.
 
The company attributed its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
Despite mounting balance sheet pressures, the company paid approximately $12 million in employee bonuses in 2024 against only $900,000 in adjusted profits
 — a disparity that creditors' representatives and the plan administrator will likely examine in connection with any avoidance actions that flow through the liquidating trust. The $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds, netted against administrative and professional fees, are the primary source of recovery for the trust; the recovery percentage against $145 million in general unsecured obligations will be materially below par.

The core legal question that dogged this case from the outset — 
whether customer digital assets constitute estate property — was central and contested throughout the dual-track restructuring.
 The plan's treatment of that question, and whether customer creditors received a different classification than trade creditors, will determine whether any creditor class exercises appellate rights against the confirmation order.

### Broader Market Context — Distressed Retail and Crypto Insolvency Convergence

The Sleep Number and BlockFills dispositions, both resolved this week, illustrate a bifurcation in recovery outcomes that is increasingly defining the 2026 distressed cycle. In Sleep Number, a competitive auction produced a 69% premium over the stalking-horse floor — a result that at minimum preserves the theoretical possibility of unsecured recovery and reflects genuine strategic value in the going-concern brand and retail footprint. In BlockFills, Keyrock paid $3.25 million for assets against a $145 million unsecured liability pool, a recovery rate that will be measured in low single-digit cents on the dollar at best, with the precise outcome dependent on trust administration costs and any avoidance-action proceeds. 
Keyrock closed its purchase for $3.25 million on July 16, four months after BlockFills filed for Chapter 11 following $75 million in losses; the fire-sale price, next to Citadel Securities' $400 million bet on Crypto.com the same week, shows a crypto market-making industry splitting into clear winners and casualties.


The DIP structure in Sleep Number deserves a note for future monitoring. 
The DIP loans are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral and are subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 
These milestones ran concurrently with the Challenge Deadline, compressing the effective window for any committee investigation.
 The self-priming, roll-up structure — where prepetition lenders effectively convert their prepetition exposure into superpriority DIP debt — is a recurring structural tension in lender-sponsored 363 cases. The U.S. Trustee raised objections to the priming and roll-up mechanics in Sleep Number early in the case; whether those objections were resolved at the final DIP hearing or carried through to the sale hearing is not publicly confirmed in available sources, but the issue is worth tracking as the post-closing distribution process unfolds.

## Recommended Actions

**BlockFills / Reliz creditors:** File your proof of claim before the governmental bar date of September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET if you have not done so; contact claims administrator Veritaglobal at (866) 554-5810 or veritaglobal.net/blockfills. Review the confirmed plan's treatment of your claim class — particularly whether customer creditors were classified separately from trade creditors and what that means for your recovery percentage and distribution timing. Monitor the liquidating trust for any adversary proceedings or avoidance actions, which represent incremental recovery upside against the $3.25 million sale floor. Track the Keyrock FCA regulatory approval; any failure or conditioned approval could affect asset completeness and correspondingly reduce trust assets. **Sleep Number creditors:** The July 24 Section 341 meeting (Zoom, 1:00 p.m. ET) is the immediate checkpoint — attend or review the transcript. The sale closed at approximately $701 million against a secured stack that included $260 million in DIP facilities and approximately $672.5 million in prepetition funded debt; model the waterfall carefully before assuming unsecured recovery is assured. The asset purchase agreement and sale order — both available through Kroll at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber — should be reviewed to confirm which liabilities Sleep Country assumed and which remain with the estate. Closing is expected by July 31; post-closing, the debtor entities will be in a wind-down posture and the administrative bar date will likely be set promptly thereafter. **Distressed investors broadly:** The Sleep Number auction premium is a meaningful data point: when a brand carries genuine going-concern value and the stalking-horse is strategic (not financial), competitive tension can appear even in a compressed 363 timeline. The BlockFills sale illustrates the opposite — when the estate's primary asset is customer relationships in a sector where those relationships are reputation-dependent and the balance sheet is deeply insolvent, a single bidder at a fraction of book liabilities is the realistic outcome.
