# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Tuesday, July 21, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The headline today is Sleep Number.** Judge Kyu Young Paek approved the § 363 sale yesterday — a compressed, 39-day petition-to-approval sprint that produced a meaningful auction premium over the stalking-horse floor. The result is consequential for unsecured creditors who entered the case staring at a $257.5 million shortfall under the original bid. BlockFills carries the other key thread: the asset sale to Keyrock closed in June, the liquidating-trust path is open, and a September 11 confirmation hearing looms — the single date creditors with ~$145 million in general unsecured claims should be tracking.

### Sleep Number — § 363 Sale Approved at $701M; Closing Expected July 31


A U.S. bankruptcy judge approved the Sleep Number sale on July 20 following a competitive auction conducted as part of the company's Chapter 11 proceedings.
 The approval came at the adjourned sale hearing before 
the Honorable Kyu Young Paek, administering the jointly-administered cases under Case No. 26-11399 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.


The economics shifted materially between petition and approval. 
The stalking-horse Asset Purchase Agreement, signed June 12, 2026, between Sleep Number and SNBR Inc. — a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc. — established the opening purchase price at $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
Sleep Country increased its proposal following the competitive process; the court-approved bid is valued at approximately $701 million, with $529.5 million payable in cash.
 
Sleep Country also agreed to assume obligations that include certain employee severance expenses and contractual liabilities.


The bid improvement of roughly $286 million over the stalking-horse floor is the creditor-recovery story here. Recall the structural gap at filing: 
as of April 30, 2026, the debtors reported consolidated assets of roughly $642.3 million against consolidated debts of roughly $1.281 billion, a negative net worth near $639 million.
 
The stalking-horse bid covered roughly 62 cents on the dollar of the funded debt, well short of the full payment the prepetition milestones had contemplated.
 The final $701 million figure, if the sale closes as expected, changes that calculus substantially — though the precise waterfall to general unsecured creditors depends on assumed-versus-rejected executory contracts and final DIP payoff amounts. 
The first-day filing indicated there would be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — a representation that becomes more credible at the $529.5 million cash figure than it was at $415 million.


The sale remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31.
 
The § 341 meeting of creditors has been continued and will be held by Zoom conference on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET.
 Separately, 
a hearing on a motion for relief from the automatic stay is scheduled for August 20, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET
 — a development worth monitoring, as stay-relief motions at this stage of a consummating sale often involve contract counterparties or landlords seeking to accelerate lease-rejection treatment.

For context on the case trajectory: 
a new chief executive arrived in April 2025, the company removed roughly $136 million in operating costs relative to 2024, suspended its 401(k) match in October 2025, launched a turnaround strategy in November 2025, and rolled out a redesigned mattress portfolio in March 2026.
 
Management attributed the distress to a confluence of industry-specific and macroeconomic headwinds — a secular consumer shift toward e-commerce that eroded in-store foot traffic, reduced discretionary spending, elevated inflation and interest rates, and a less dependable global supply chain.
 The prepetition marketing process — which canvassed 53 parties, generated 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals — produced only one binding stalking-horse before petition. The auction generated the premium. That sequencing validates the bid-procedures structure Davis Polk & Wardwell (lead counsel, Brian M. Resnick) negotiated with the secured lenders, who held the prepetition credit facility governed by the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement, with U.S. Bank National Association as Administrative Agent.

Lease-creditor exposure remains an open item. 
Sleep Number operates 572 retail stores in the U.S.
 
Assets acquired by Sleep Country pursuant to the Asset Sale do not include executory leases or contracts that the purchaser chooses to reject or are otherwise rejected in the Chapter 11 Cases.
 Landlords for stores not assumed face rejection-damages claims capped at the lesser of one year's rent or 15% of the remaining lease term under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(6). At a portfolio of this size, the aggregate § 502(b)(6) exposure is a material unsecured-claim category.

### BlockFills — Keyrock Sale Closed; Plan Confirmation Set September 11

The asset-disposition phase of *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-10371 (TMH) (Bankr. D. Del.), is effectively complete. The open question for creditors is what the liquidating trust delivers on approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group on June 16, 2026, as BlockFills prepared for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 The successful bidder is Keyrock SA. 
Per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and assumed "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.


The $3.25 million gross sale price against a general unsecured pool of approximately $145 million is a striking recovery ratio — and reflects the nature of the underlying estate. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc. and three affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 protection on March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 The spread between those petition-date ranges tells most of the recovery story before confirmation briefs are even filed.

The case originated in a liquidity crisis with fraud-litigation overtones. 
On February 6, 2026, BlockFills publicly announced a broader suspension of deposits and withdrawals; in the weeks preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits were filed asserting misappropriation of customer funds and conversion.
 
The courts in both matters granted temporary restraining orders that restricted the debtors' ability to conduct operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 
The automatic stay afforded by Chapter 11 was intended to halt further litigation and prevent individual customers from pursuing assets outside the bankruptcy process.


The two pre-petition customer actions — *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., filed February 27, 2026) and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill., filed March 5, 2026) — remain relevant to the liquidating-trust's claim-allowance process. Whether those plaintiffs' fraud-based claims receive unsecured treatment or assert priority as customer-property claimants under a constructive-trust or § 541 carve-out theory is a threshold confirmation issue. No verified court ruling on that issue is in the record as of today's run.


Final regulatory clearances for the Keyrock transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026.
 That status — court-approved but not yet fully regulatory-cleared — creates a residual closing risk that could affect the timing of trust-asset distributions. The confirmed plan confirmation hearing date is 
September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
, per the Verita claims administrator's key dates calendar. Creditors who purchased claims in the secondary market — or are contemplating doing so — should track whether the pre-petition customer-action plaintiffs' litigation positions are resolved, settled, or subordinated at or before confirmation. The Verita claims portal at veritaglobal.net/blockfills tracks all related filings.

For distressed investors, the post-sale posture in BlockFills is now a pure trust-recovery play. 
The Keyrock deal expands Keyrock's regulatory footprint to include a CIMA-registered entity in the Cayman Islands and a proposed FCA-authorized entity in the U.K., pending regulatory approval
 — but none of those assets remain in the estate. What remains is the liquidating trust's cash, any residual estate claims (including any avoidance actions against insiders or counterparties), and whatever distributions emerge after professional fees and trust expenses. At $3.25 million in gross sale proceeds against $145 million in unsecured claims, the trust's recovery potential depends almost entirely on whether the estate has viable affirmative claims — fraudulent transfer, preference, or breach-of-fiduciary actions — against identifiable defendants.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed-claim holders in the Sleep Number estate should move immediately on two fronts: (1) confirm whether their claims are in the assumed-liability schedule or the rejected-contract bucket before the July 31 closing — executory contract counterparties who are not assumed face a post-sale bar-date environment with capped § 502(b)(6) damages and should file protective proofs of claim if the general bar date has not already run; and (2) assess whether the final sale order entered by Judge Paek contains a § 363(m) good-faith finding and, if so, whether any pending objections survive mootness — the window to seek a stay is functionally closed at July 31 closing. In BlockFills, general unsecured creditors should file claims through the Verita portal (veritaglobal.net/blockfills) and monitor the September 11 confirmation hearing for plan treatment of the pre-petition TRO plaintiffs' claims, which carry a non-trivial risk of asserting customer-property priority ahead of the general unsecured pool; if the trust has viable avoidance claims, secondary-market acquirers of general unsecured paper may see better recovery than the $3.25 million gross-sale figure implies — but only if those avoidance claims are identified and pursued aggressively by trust counsel before any statute of limitations issues arise under 11 U.S.C. § 546.
