## Analysis & Developments

### LEDE — Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.): Sale Approved, Close Imminent, Residual Estate Questions Now Dominate

The central creditor-rights development this week is not the §363 sale itself — that cleared — but what happens to the estate immediately after it closes. 
Sleep Number Corporation announced on July 20, 2026 that it received court approval for the sale of substantially all of the Company's assets and ongoing business operations to SNBR Inc., a wholly owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc.
 
The sale remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31.


The headline figure improved materially through the auction process. 
The original stalking-horse APA, dated June 12, 2026, committed Sleep Country to acquire substantially all assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
Following the court-supervised auction, on July 18, 2026, the parties entered into an Amended and Restated Asset Purchase Agreement that increased the base cash purchase price from $415,000,000 to $529,500,000 — a $114.5 million lift — while reducing the adjustment escrow from $25 million to $10 million.
 
All-in, the total consideration is approximately $702 million, including the cash component of nearly $530 million.


The structural backdrop matters for recovery analysis. 
Sleep Number filed carrying approximately $672.5 million in funded debt against roughly $600 million in total assets, per EVP and CFO Amy O'Keefe's bankruptcy court declaration.
 
The petition reported assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion with liabilities exceeding $1 billion.
 
The declaration attributed the filing to tariff pressure on imported components, pandemic-era overexpansion of the store footprint and manufacturing capacity, and a failed recapitalization search; Sleep Number contacted 33 parties about refinancing and received no actionable proposals before running a 14-week prepetition marketing process that reached 53 potential buyers.


The case is jointly administered before the Hon. Kyu Young Paek in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. 
Judge Paek is presiding over the jointly administered cases, and the transaction is being undertaken pursuant to Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code with Sleep Country serving as the stalking horse bidder.
 Debtor counsel is Brian M. Resnick at Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP. 
The unsecured creditors' committee has retained a team of lawyers from Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP
 — a significant committee-side engagement that signals the UCC intends to be active on post-sale distribution mechanics and any retained causes of action.

Two creditor-side flashpoints emerged in mid-July that will shape the residual estate. First, 
the U.S. Trustee challenged Sleep Number's staff retention plan on July 15, 2026
 — a KERP objection that, if sustained in whole or in part, could redirect retention dollars toward the unsecured pool. Second, 
a New York bankruptcy judge approved the nearly $702 million sale on Monday following an agreement to postpone potential future severance payments until the court determines their treatment.
 The deferred-severance issue is the one to watch: if the court ultimately characterizes executive severance as administrative claims, they prime general unsecured creditors; if characterized as general unsecured or subordinated, the recovery pool broadens.

For distressed investors evaluating Sleep Number claims: the auction premium of roughly $287 million over stalking-horse suggests meaningful enterprise value, but the funded-debt overhang, administrative expense accrual, and the U.S. Trustee's KERP challenge all bear on the ultimate creditor waterfall. The §363 sale process is largely closed; the residual estate work — claims reconciliation, causes of action, and plan confirmation — is what follows. 
The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors,
 but sizing that recovery will require waiting for the post-close balance sheet and claims bar date mechanics.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Case No. 26-10371-TMH, Bankr. D. Del.): Keyrock Acquisition Closes; Plan Confirmation Hearing Set September 11, 2026

The sale leg of the BlockFills case has concluded. What remains open — and what creditors holding the roughly $145 million in general unsecured obligations need to monitor — is the plan-confirmation track and the liquidating trust.

On the sale: 
Keyrock was named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and the U.S. Bankruptcy Court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.
 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock completed the acquisition of assets of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business on July 16, 2026.


The regulatory dimension is not yet fully resolved. 
The transaction folds in a CIMA-registered Cayman entity acquired outright and seeks to transfer a proposed FCA-authorized U.K. entity to Keyrock — the U.K. transfer still requires FCA approval, which would expand Keyrock's ability to face certain institutional clients in that jurisdiction.
 Regulatory contingencies attached to the sale consideration are a meaningful wrinkle: if FCA approval is conditioned or denied, there may be a purchase price adjustment mechanism that affects proceeds flowing to the liquidating trust.

The recovery math is bleak by any measure. 
The Debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 Against that, 
the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 A $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in unsecured claims implies recovery in the low single-digit cents per dollar range before administrative expenses — though the reorganization plan's liquidating trust structure may incorporate additional causes of action that could supplement distributions.

The case's procedural posture post-sale is confirmation-focused. 
The confirmation hearing is scheduled for September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the District of Delaware. 
The Debtors filed their Joint Chapter 11 Plan and Disclosure Statement at Docket Nos. 128 and 129 respectively on April 7, 2026, and simultaneously moved for approval of the Disclosure Statement, a scheduling order for the confirmation hearing, and related procedures.
 Verita Global (KCC) is acting as claims and noticing agent. 
The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


For claim holders: the ad hoc group's pre-negotiated term sheet likely advantages that constituency's members in plan classification. Investors evaluating secondary claim purchases at this stage need to assess (1) the plan's treatment of customer claims vs. trade claims, (2) the scope and assignability of causes of action being transferred to the liquidating trust, and (3) whether any Rule 3001 transfer notices — which would signal active claim trading — have been filed on the docket.

### Contextual Carry-Forward: Serta Uptier Litigation; US Trustee Retention-Plan Activity; Cross-Border Recognition

**Serta Simmons — Uptier Lenders' $400M Recovery.** 
Lenders left out of the Serta uptier deal won $400 million in Chapter 11 litigation, per Law360 reporting dated July 8, 2026.
 This continues to be the most consequential creditor-rights data point for distressed investors tracking non-pro-rata exchange transactions and liability-management exercises. The Serta outcome reinforces that excluded lenders with claims at the right level of the capital structure have a credible recovery path if they can establish that the uptier violated the credit agreement's sacred-rights provisions or covenant of good faith. The precedent puts pressure on borrowers and majority lenders contemplating similar out-of-court exchange mechanics.

**US Trustee Scrutiny of Retention Plans.** The July 15 KERP challenge in Sleep Number is consistent with a broader Office of the United States Trustee posture we have tracked across multiple large-cap Chapter 11 filings in 2026. The UST has become a structural actor in the administrative-claim allocation fight, particularly in cases where management continuity incentives are structured in ways that compress the general unsecured pool.

**Cross-Border Recognition.** 
Ambipar sought U.S. recognition of a Brazilian reorganization proceeding on July 10, 2026,
 a Chapter 15 petition worth tracking for investors with exposure to Latin American credits. Cross-border recognition disputes continue to be a venue-and-recognition battleground; the adequacy of foreign main proceeding findings under §1516 and the automatic stay's scope post-recognition remain live issues at the circuit level.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and rights-holders should focus their near-term attention on three time-sensitive matters. In Sleep Number (26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Paek), the July 31 closing deadline is imminent — claim holders should confirm they are properly scheduled, contact Kroll Restructuring Administration at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber to verify claim status, and monitor the court's resolution of the deferred-severance and KERP issues because both determinations will directly shape the administrative expense load carried into the residual estate before any unsecured distribution; the UCC's Pachulski Stang engagement signals that committee-side pressure on both fronts is likely. In BlockFills (26-10371-TMH, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan), investors evaluating secondary claim purchases should pull the plan and disclosure statement at Docket Nos. 128 and 129 and the Verita Global claim register at veritaglobal.net/blockfills before the September 11 confirmation hearing, paying particular attention to plan classification of customer vs. trade claims and the scope of causes of action assigned to the liquidating trust, since those causes of action — not the $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds — are the realistic source of any material supplemental distribution. On Serta, claims-trading desks should treat the $400 million excluded-lender recovery as a live comparable when pricing exposure in other uptier situations, and track whether any similar excluded-lender actions are filed in the Second or Fifth Circuit in the next 60 days.
