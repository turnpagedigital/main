# 📜 Chapter 11 | Monday, August 03, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's desk is dominated by one headline filing: Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation's free-fall Chapter 11 — the second EchoStar-family petition in five weeks and the most consequential satellite-sector restructuring since ViaSat's covenant troubles in 2024. The Harvest Sherwood confirmation hearing set for Wednesday presents an imminent creditor-rights flashpoint on opt-out third-party releases.

### NEW MEGA FILING — Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation | Bankr. S.D. Tex. (Houston Div.)


Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation and certain of its U.S. subsidiaries, including Hughes Network Systems, LLC, filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division on August 3, 2026.
 
The debtor group includes EchoStar Orbital L.L.C., Hughes Communications, Inc., and Hughes Network Systems, LLC, among eleven wholly-owned subsidiaries.
 A case number has not yet been confirmed through this run's primary-source search; the docket is expected to appear on PACER under the S.D. Tex. Houston clerk's registry shortly. Judge assignment is unconfirmed at time of publication.

**The debt picture.** 
Hughes cited mounting competition from low-earth-orbit satellite providers and an inability to repay $1.5 billion in bonds that matured August 1.
 
The company reported just $102 million in cash as of March 31, 2026
 — a $1.4 billion liquidity shortfall against the maturity wall. 
The Chapter 11 filing constitutes an event of default under the indentures governing HSSC's 5.25% Senior Secured Notes due 2026 and its 6.625% Senior Notes due 2026, resulting in automatic acceleration of those obligations.
 
Any enforcement efforts are automatically stayed under the Bankruptcy Code.


**Free-fall posture.** 
The filing arrives without a pre-negotiated restructuring plan.
 That is the critical variable for distressed investors: absent a stalking-horse bid, RSA, or DIP commitment disclosed in the first-day papers, the case enters the discovery phase with an open capital structure and no roadmap for plan-side economics. 
HSSC filed a series of first-day motions seeking customary relief to minimize the impact of the bankruptcy on its operations, customers, and employees.


**Operational context.** 
Hughes's customer base includes roughly 641,000 residential subscribers in rural areas of North and South America, as well as defense agencies, airlines, and government clients.
 
The company's broadband subscriber base declined approximately 21.7% in the year ending June 30, 2025.
 
The reorganization is designed to allow the company to address its maturing secured and unsecured debt, strengthen its capital structure, and accelerate its transformation into an enterprise, government, and defense-focused business.
 That strategic pivot — shedding consumer broadband, retaining government and enterprise contracts — is the thesis creditors and plan sponsors will need to underwrite.

**Creditor organization pre-filing.** 
Hughes's bondholders retained Jones Day for restructuring advice on July 7, 2026, after the EchoStar subsidiary admitted it does not have the cash to cover the $1.5 billion debt maturity.
 
A separate creditor group hired Glenn Agre Bergman & Fuentes to scrutinize a satellite-lease arrangement that sends roughly $190 million a year from Hughes to EchoStar.
 That inter-company payment stream is the most obvious target for fraudulent transfer or equitable subordination analysis — watch for an adversary proceeding or committee motion to recharacterize once the UCC is formed.

**EchoStar family context.** 
On June 30, 2026, EchoStar's primary pay-TV broadcast arm, DISH DBS Corporation, filed for prepackaged Chapter 11 restructuring in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas to address $10 billion in subsidiary-level debt.
 
When DISH DBS filed its prepackaged reorganization petition, Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation was explicitly excluded from the court docket and maintained independent cash distributions to maintain operational continuity.
 The failure to fold Hughes into the DISH DBS prepack — despite the shared parent, shared venue, and overlapping creditor communities — has now produced a second, more complex, free-fall proceeding. Whether the two cases will be jointly administered or coordinated at the hearing level is an open procedural question for Day 1.

**Leadership change.** 
Paul Gaske resigned from all director and officer positions at both EchoStar and HSSC on July 28, 2026, and the company has appointed Robert Del Genio, a Senior Managing Director at FTI
 Consulting, as Chief Restructuring Officer — a standard move for a free-fall, signaling the board has ceded operational control to restructuring professionals.

**Watch list for distressed investors.** Four items warrant immediate attention: (1) the DIP financing terms — no commitment has been publicly disclosed, which is unusual for a $1.5B+ filing and may signal lender-side friction; (2) the inter-company satellite-lease payments of ~$190M/year to EchoStar, which the Glenn Agre group is already investigating; (3) whether a 363 sale process for the consumer broadband subscriber base will run in parallel with a plan process; and (4) committee formation, expected within 21 days of the petition date under 11 U.S.C. § 1102.

### UPCOMING CONFIRMATION HEARING — Harvest Sherwood Food Distributors, Inc. | No. 25-80109 | Bankr. N.D. Tex. (Judge Stacey G.C. Jernigan)

Confirmation is scheduled for Wednesday, August 5, 2026 — flagged here because today's procedural posture matters to the outcome. 
On July 20, 2026, the U.S. Trustee objected to confirmation of the Debtors' Third Amended Plan at Docket No. 948, arguing that the plan remains unconfirmable because its opt-out third-party release does not establish knowing and voluntary consent and because it permits residual Liquidating Trust cash to be donated to charity rather than deposited with the Court, at Docket No. 1063.


The opt-out release objection tracks the post-*Harrington v. Purdue Pharma* landscape directly: the U.S. Trustee's position is that even in a liquidating plan, non-debtor releases require affirmative consent, not mere silence. 
Harvest Sherwood filed for Chapter 11 protection on May 5, 2025, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Texas, reporting $1 billion to $10 billion in assets and $500 million to $1 billion in liabilities.
 The estate's primary value is its litigation portfolio: 
$44.6 million in antitrust settlements have already been achieved against an estimated $1.09 billion in claims across 28+ defendants, with additional recoveries pending.
 
Following DIP repayment, general unsecured creditors would be the primary beneficiaries of remaining litigation assets.


The August 3 docket entry (Entry No. 1124, filed 09:57 AM) is a generic notice per the tracked-case ground truth; its substantive content is unconfirmed and does not appear to be a landmark filing. Judge Jernigan's ruling on the release and charitable-residual objections Wednesday will have direct precedential weight in the N.D. Tex. mega-docket court on post-*Purdue* plan-release standards.

### LEGISLATIVE WATCH — Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026 (Texas Two-Step)

No floor vote or committee action occurred in the last 24 hours. The Reuters item flagged in this run's news scan referenced the Texas Two-Step in the restructuring context generally; no new court ruling or statutory development was extractable from that headline. For context: 
Senators Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI), Josh Hawley (R-MO), and Dick Durbin (D-IL), along with Representatives Emilia Sykes (D-OH) and Lance Gooden (R-TX), reintroduced the Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026, a bipartisan bill designed to deter the growing trend of corporations using the "Texas Two-Step" to evade responsibility for injuries they caused and delay justice for claimants.
 The Hughes filing is not a Texas Two-Step; it is a straight free-fall. Coverage continues under the legislative track.
