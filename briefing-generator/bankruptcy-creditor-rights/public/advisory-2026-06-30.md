### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Post-Sale Hearing Posture, Governmental Bar Date on Deck

The BlockFills case is now in the window between the June 16 sale hearing and the September 11 governmental bar date — a period that will shape how much, if anything, general unsecured creditors ultimately recover from the liquidating trust contemplated in the Joint Chapter 11 Plan.


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on March 15, 2026 (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)).
 
The cases are jointly administered before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan.
 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


The Section 363 sale track has now closed at the hearing level. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Brussels-based Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property — and as of May 26, 2026, Keyrock had been declared the "Successful Bidder" in an official Bankruptcy Court document.
 
The omnibus hearing and sale hearing were both calendared for June 16, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket reflects no new entries since June 26 (Dkt. 467), and the tracked-case ground truth confirms zero new filings in the 72-hour window ending June 29. The June 16 hearing has passed; no public reporting has confirmed court approval of the sale or entry of a sale order as of this writing, and the docket through June 26 has not been independently verified as containing a signed order. Creditors should monitor PACER for a sale order or any notice of a busted transaction before drawing conclusions about closing certainty.

The gap between the $3.25 million sale price and the roughly $145 million in general unsecured claims is the central recovery issue. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested
 — a point that directly determines whether customer claims participate in distributions alongside trade creditors or are satisfied outside the estate through direct asset return. 
The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 If the customer-property argument prevails, institutional clients who held digital assets in custody-style accounts could recover outside the general unsecured pool, concentrating the estate's thin proceeds among trade and other non-customer creditors. Conversely, if customer assets are estate property, all unsecured claims share the same recovery waterfall from a $3.25 million gross sale price — a figure that, against $145 million in GUC obligations, implies recovery in the low single-digit cents on the dollar before administrative costs.

The next hard deadline: 
the governmental bar date is set for September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Counsel for any governmental creditor — including the CFTC, which has previously scrutinized BlockFills' derivatives operations for eligible contract participants — should treat this as a drop-dead deadline for claim preservation.

Background on the liquidity collapse is worth keeping in view. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 
On February 2, 2026, Bitcoin dropped below $80,000 for the first time since April 2025, and BlockFills temporarily suspended certain deposit and withdrawal activity that day in response to liquidity pressure and customer withdrawal requests.
 Those suspended withdrawals are the basis for the most legally significant creditor claims in the case.

The Keyrock transaction should also be read as a valuation data point, not just an M&A outcome. 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 A $3.25 million clearing price for that franchise — technology, IP, and ~2,000 institutional relationships — signals that the secondary market assigns near-zero going-concern value to the platform absent the counterparty relationships that collapsed with the filing. For distressed investors evaluating claim purchases, this is the controlling comparable.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Bidding Procedures Hearing Today; $415M Stalking Horse on the Clock

Today is June 30, 2026. The Sleep Number case is moving at an unusually compressed pace, and the next 15 days are the operative window for distressed investors.


Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on June 12, 2026, with the court granting joint administration under Case No. 26-11399.
 
The cases are before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
The petition lists $642 million in assets and $1.28 billion in debts.


The case is structured as a pure Section 363 sale with a negotiated stalking horse. 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale of substantially all of the Debtors' assets under Section 363, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., pursuant to a Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, committing to acquire substantially all of the company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The deal carries a 3% break-up fee along with an expense reimbursement cap of $4 million.


The bidding procedures motion is scheduled for hearing today — 
July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Paek
 — and if approved, the sale timeline becomes binding. 
The Debtors are seeking a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, a July 15 sale approval hearing, with a closing date of July 31.
 That is a 49-day cradle-to-close process from petition date, which is aggressive even for a pre-marketed 363.


The company justified the expedited timeline on the basis of a prepetition 14-week marketing process during which it contacted 53 potential strategic and financial purchasers, of whom 19 executed non-disclosure agreements and five submitted preliminary proposals.
 
In total, Guggenheim contacted 33 parties during the recapitalization phase, 26 of which executed NDAs and received diligence materials; ultimately only one party submitted a written recapitalization proposal, which was deemed not actionable; and while Sleep Number received several proposals for senior bridge financing, the existing lender group would not consent to being primed, and no actionable proposals for unsecured or junior financing were received.
 That pre-petition foreclosure of out-of-court options is the predicate for the compressed in-court timeline and bolsters the Debtors' argument that the July 31 closing deadline is a genuine operational constraint, not an artificial one designed to chill competition.

The capital structure is heavily impaired. 
The company has approximately $672.5 million of funded debt, comprising approximately $475 million outstanding under a revolver, $177.5 million outstanding under a term loan entered into in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered into in 2026.
 Against $642 million in stated assets, the secured debt already exceeds asset value — leaving general unsecured creditors in a structural out-of-the-money position unless the auction surfaces a price materially above $415 million or the stalking horse APA's assumed-liabilities schedule is broader than the face amount suggests.

The DIP facility was approved on an interim basis and goes to a final hearing on July 9. 
The Bankruptcy Court entered an interim DIP order; the DIP and roll-up loans mature September 16, 2026, and bear interest at SOFR + 8.00% or a base rate plus 7.00%, secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral, subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 
The DIP is funded by Sleep Number's existing lenders: up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via a roll-up of prepetition debt.
 The roll-up mechanics — prepetition debt exchanged for DIP priority status — will be the focus of any Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors if one is formed; the U.S. Trustee was soliciting committee members as of June 22. 
The company discloses significant risks tied to its Chapter 11 process, including the likely cancellation of its common shares.



Davis Polk & Wardwell is the company's legal advisor, Guggenheim Securities its investment banker, and AP Services (an affiliate of AlixPartners) its interim management.
 The Kroll restructuring website is the claims administration portal at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber.

For distressed investors: today's bidding procedures ruling (assuming approval) locks the July 13 auction as the inflection point. The $415 million floor and a 3% break-up fee make a competing bid economically meaningful only if a buyer can clear roughly $427 million and demonstrate committed financing on a compressed timeline. The operational store footprint question is also live — 
Sleep Number filed a motion to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations, with A&G Real Estate Partners assisting the effort.
 The ultimate number of stores assumed versus rejected will drive whether the buyer is acquiring a viable retail operating business or a brand and IP package.

### Structural Theme: The Customer-Property Fault Line in Crypto Insolvencies

The BlockFills and Power Block Coin cases both surface the same unresolved legal question that has animated the Celsius, Voyager, and FTX proceedings: whether digital assets held by a brokerage/lender for institutional or retail customers constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or remain customer property subject to direct return outside the estate. In BlockFills, 
the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested
 as of the case's early reporting — and the resolution of that question in Judge Horan's courtroom will directly determine whether the $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds flow to general unsecured trade creditors or must first be applied against claims from the institutional customers whose assets were frozen in February 2026. No court order resolving this question has been identified in public filings through the June 26 docket date. Creditors on both sides of this fault line — institutional clients asserting ownership of specific digital assets and trade/financial creditors asserting pari passu GUC status — should treat the plan confirmation process and any adversary proceedings as the primary battleground.

The broader appellate landscape on this issue remains unsettled. The Second Circuit's Celsius decision on the ownership of cryptocurrency held in Earn accounts and the subsequent Voyager customer property litigation produced outcomes that are fact-pattern-specific and highly dependent on account agreement language. Delaware courts — where BlockFills sits — have not yet issued a definitive ruling analogous to the Second Circuit's Celsius analysis. Judge Horan's ultimate determination, when it comes, will be the Delaware bankruptcy court's most significant contribution to the customer-property debate since the FTX proceedings before Judge Dorsey.

Case No. 26-11399 (KYP)), today's bidding procedures hearing at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Paek is the operative event: parties with a strategic interest in the auction should confirm whether bidding procedures are approved as proposed, whether the July 8 bid deadline and July 13 auction are locked, and whether the UCC (if formed) has raised objections to the $195 million DIP roll-up that could alter the cost structure for competing bidders; the final DIP hearing on July 9 and the sale approval hearing on July 15 are the two subsequent mandatory attendance dates. In both cases, claim buyers should model recovery scenarios against the stated capital structures before committing to secondary market purchases.
