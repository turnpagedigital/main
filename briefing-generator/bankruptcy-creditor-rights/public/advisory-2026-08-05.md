### I. New Mega-Case: Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation, et al. — S.D. Tex. No. 26-90739 (Judge Perez)

**The filing.** 
Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation and certain of its U.S. subsidiaries, including Hughes Network Systems, LLC, filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division.
 
Hughes Network Systems, LLC filed on August 2, 2026; the case is assigned to Judge Alfredo R. Perez.
 
The company is one of 12 affiliated entities that filed Chapter 11 petitions on the same date, requesting joint administration under the Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation case.
 The lead case is No. 26-90739. 
Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation reported $1.9 billion in assets and $1.5 billion in liabilities as of March 31, 2026.


**Creditor profile — scale matters.** 
Court records estimate that Hughes Network Systems, LLC has between 10,001 and 25,000 creditors.
 
The debtor's largest unsecured creditors include U.S. Bank NA, Radisys Corporation, Intuitive Machines LLC, and Qualcomm Technologies Inc.
 This places the case squarely in the category of large, complex consumer-facing reorganizations with a diffuse creditor constituency — a significant practical challenge for plan confirmation.

**The debt structure and why there is no RSA.** 
Hughes Satellite Systems Corporation issued two series of notes under indentures both dated July 27, 2016: approximately $750 million of 5.250% senior secured notes and approximately $750 million of 6.625% senior unsecured notes. Both matured on August 1, 2026.
 
Because that date fell on a Saturday, which the indentures treat as a legal holiday, payment was not required until Monday, August 3.
 The company filed on August 2 — one day early. 
Unlike the QVC and DISH filings in the same district, this one is not prepackaged. No restructuring support agreement is in place. The company entered bankruptcy in open conflict with the creditors who hold most of its debt.
 That is the defining feature for distressed investors: there is no consensual roadmap, valuation will be contested, and inter-creditor dynamics between the $750M secured tranche and the $750M unsecured tranche have not been resolved prepetition.

**Operational backdrop.** 
The filing documents a consumer broadband base that fell from 819,000 subscribers to approximately 641,000 in twelve months, and a strategic retreat from households toward enterprise, defense, and in-flight contracts.
 
The subscriber collapse — from 1.56 million at end of 2020 to 681,000 by March 2026 — was driven largely by competition from SpaceX's Starlink LEO constellation.
 
Hughes Satellite Systems had only $102 million in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable investment securities.
 The pivot to enterprise and government is the reorganization thesis; whether that business is separable and valuable enough to support the secured debt stack is the contested valuation question.

**Advisors.** 
White & Case LLP is legal counsel to the company and other listed U.S. subsidiaries; FTI Consulting, Inc. is serving as financial advisor to the filing entities.
 First-day motions are pending — 
the company will seek court approval for customary first-day motions focused on continuing to pay employees, meeting obligations to vendors, customers, and channel partners, and conducting business in the ordinary course.


**EchoStar context.** 
Hughes Network Systems' bankruptcy filing follows on the heels of another EchoStar subsidiary, Dish Wireless, whose prepackaged Chapter 11 filing on June 30, 2026, included a restructuring support agreement that facilitated a $23 billion sale of parent EchoStar's wireless spectrum licenses to AT&T.
 
Hughes Network Systems and its operations were deliberately kept outside those earlier cases, leaving the satellite broadband unit to confront its own separate debt wall independently.
 The sequencing — DISH DBS first, Hughes second — reflects Ergen's decision to protect the spectrum transaction before exposing the broadband unit to a contested restructuring.

**Distressed investor read.** This is a free-fall case with $1.5B of funded debt, no RSA, an openly adversarial relationship with secured creditors, a shrinking but potentially monetizable government/enterprise contract book, and a 10,001–25,000 creditor universe that guarantees committee complexity and prolonged confirmation. The secured/unsecured parity in face amount ($750M each) sets up a classic inter-creditor fight over enterprise value. Watch the creditors' committee formation, any DIP financing motion (none confirmed at filing), and an early disclosure statement schedule. Claims trading should be expected. Judge Perez has managed several prior large Southern District of Texas complex cases; his scheduling tendencies will set the pace.

### II. Jackson Walker $15M Settlement — S.D. Tex. (Chief Judge Rodriguez / multiple case captions)

**What settled.** 
Jackson Walker LLP will pay $15 million to end litigation with the Justice Department's bankruptcy watchdog over an undisclosed relationship between a former attorney and one of the nation's former top bankruptcy judges. The settlement would end long-running litigation brought by the U.S. Trustee's office accusing Jackson Walker of breaching its ethical duties by failing to disclose the romance its onetime bankruptcy partner Elizabeth Freeman had with ex-bankruptcy judge David R. Jones while the firm was representing clients in cases involving Jones.


**Filing mechanics.** 
Under a settlement term sheet filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Texas on August 2, Jackson Walker agreed to resolve claims brought by the U.S. Trustee. The agreement remains subject to court approval.
 
The law firm is not admitting wrongdoing as part of the agreement.


**Structural terms beyond the cash payment.** 
In settlement documents filed with a federal court in Texas, Jackson Walker stated that it would also fund an independent review of bankruptcy disclosure requirements and compliance with ethical obligations, as well as revise certain conflict-checking procedures.
 Those structural conditions — an independent disclosure review and conflict-screening reforms — are notable; the U.S. Trustee extracted practice-change commitments, not just disgorgement.

**What preceded this.** 
The U.S. Trustee brought the litigation in November 2023 to vacate orders approving Jackson Walker's retention fees and applications in cases connected to Jones and Freeman.
 
Extensive discovery showed that at least two Jackson Walker attorneys exchanged text messages in 2021 indicating they knew about, and discussed the potential fallout of, the undisclosed romance.
 
The deal comes after Chief Judge Eduardo V. Rodriguez recommended the approval of nine private settlements that allowed the U.S. Trustee to continue pursuing legal action against Jackson Walker; those settlements would return nearly $4.8 million to several bankruptcy estates.
 The $15M global settlement is therefore additive to — not a substitute for — the $4.8M estate-level clawback track, both of which remain subject to separate approval.

**Counsel.** 
Jackson Walker is represented by Norton Rose Fulbright US LLP and Rusty Hardin & Associates LLP.


**Why this matters beyond the dollar figure.** The case established that an undisclosed romantic relationship between law firm counsel and a presiding judge constitutes a disqualifying conflict, that knowledge by other firm attorneys triggers firm-level liability, and that the U.S. Trustee will pursue fee disgorgement across every affected case — not just the case in which discovery first surfaces. 
The U.S. Trustee had aimed to disgorge up to $23 million the firm collected in cases connected to Jones and Freeman.
 The $15M outcome is a significant but not full recovery against that ceiling. The independent-review condition sets a structural precedent for U.S. Trustee enforcement going forward.
