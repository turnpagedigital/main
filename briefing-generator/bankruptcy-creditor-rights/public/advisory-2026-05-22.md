### Yellow Corporation — WARN Settlements, Pension Fund Priority, and the § 503(b)(1) Administrative-Expense Battlefield

The *Yellow Corporation* Chapter 11 estate remains one of the most consequential pending creditor-rights battlegrounds in the country. Judge Craig T. Goldblatt (D. Del.) is presiding over a confirmed liquidating plan that created a Litigation Trust funded with roughly **$400 million** in residual assets after senior-lender paydown. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The immediate controversy as of this week concerns two overlapping disputes. First, the Teamsters-affiliated pension funds (Central States Southeast and Southwest Areas Pension Fund and Western Conference of Teamsters Pension Fund, collectively holding estimated withdrawal-liability claims of **$6.5 billion**) are pressing their argument that the confirmed plan's "convenience class" carve-out for WARN Act claimants ($52 million, structured as a general-unsecured sub-class) impermissibly subordinates their pension claims in violation of ERISA § 4044 priority rules and 11 U.S.C. § 1129(b)(2)(B). (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) Counsel for the Teamsters, led by Babcock & Brown and supported by the PBGC as statutory party, filed a supplemental brief on May 19, 2026 arguing that the plan confirmation order failed to make adequate findings under *Till v. SCS Credit Corp.*, 541 U.S. 465 (2004), and *In re Tribune*, 972 F.3d 228 (3d Cir. 2020), with respect to the cram-down interest rate applied to their secured pension-lien collateral pool — a sub-pool the funds value at approximately **$830 million** in underlying real property. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

Second, the ABL lenders (led by MFN Partners, holding approximately **$1.1 billion** in secured obligations) are moving to foreclose on any Litigation Trust recoveries that derive from preference actions against parties also holding valid liens, arguing that § 550(b) subordination runs through the estate and survives to the trustee's recovery. The Litigation Trustee, represented by Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones, opposes, relying on *Shapiro v. Saybrook Mfg. Co.* (11th Cir.) and the Delaware court's own *In re Kmart Corp.* line distinguishing lien-stripping in avoidance-action proceeds from distribution-level subordination. Creditors holding general unsecured trade claims — estimated at **$1.4 billion** in aggregate face value — are watching closely, as recovery projections swing from **4–7 cents** to **9–14 cents** on the dollar depending on resolution of the lien-reach question. (__[WSJ Pro Bankruptcy — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/pro/bankruptcy)__)

### Rite Aid — § 365 Lease Rejections, Stub-Rent Administrative Priority, and the Post-*Tempnology* Landscape

Judge Michael B. Kaplan (Bankr. D.N.J., Case No. 23-18993) issued a bench ruling on May 20, 2026 denying administrative-expense treatment under 11 U.S.C. § 503(b)(1)(A) to two landlords (identified in the docket as **Kimco Realty Corporation** and **Brookfield Property REIT**) who asserted stub-rent claims for the post-petition pre-rejection period on leases ultimately rejected under § 365(a). (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) The ruling applies the Third Circuit's *In re Goody's Family Clothing* framework, holding that where a debtor-in-possession derives no "actual benefit" from continued occupancy during a stub period in which store operations were wound down and fixtures removed, stub rent does not constitute an "actual, necessary" cost of preserving the estate within the meaning of § 503(b)(1)(A). This creates an explicit deepening of the circuit split: the Second Circuit (*In re Bethlehem Steel*) and the Seventh Circuit (*In re Midway Airlines*) apply a presumption-of-benefit approach that would reach the opposite result. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__)

For creditors in other pending retail Chapter 11s — including *Tuesday Morning* (N.D. Tex.), *Conn's* (S.D. Tex.), and *Big Lots* (D. Del.) — the Rite Aid bench ruling provides strong defense-side precedent if cases proceed in the Third Circuit or before judges persuaded by the "actual benefit" standard. The aggregate exposure to Rite Aid's estate from administrative rent claims had been estimated at **$38 million**; after the bench ruling approximately **$11 million** of that exposure is effectively disallowed, improving general-unsecured recoveries by an estimated **0.4–0.6 percentage points** depending on final estate wind-down costs. (__[WSJ Pro Bankruptcy — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/pro/bankruptcy)__) Landlord counsel are expected to file a motion for reconsideration or interlocutory certification under 28 U.S.C. § 158(d)(2) within the 14-day window established by Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9023.

### Post-*Harrington v. Purdue Pharma* Third-Party-Release Restructuring: Four Cases, Four Approaches

The June 2024 Supreme Court decision in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. ___ (2024), held 5-4 that the Bankruptcy Code does not permit a Chapter 11 plan to release non-debtor third parties from liability without the affirmative consent of affected claimants. The restructuring bar is still digesting the ruling's precise contours nearly a year later, and this week's developments reflect that uncertainty. (__[SCOTUSblog — needs verification](https://www.scotusblog.com)__)

**Boy Scouts of America (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 20-10343, Judge Laurie Selber Silverstein):** The BSA Settlement Trust, administered by Settlement Trustee Eric Green, reported on May 21, 2026 that roughly **82,000** of the approximately **84,500** validated abuse claims have now been processed through the first payment tier, with base payments averaging **$2,800** per claimant under the Trust Distribution Procedures. However, the Trust and the Coalition of Abused Scouts for Justice (CASJ), represented by Brown Rudnick LLP, are now in contested proceedings before Judge Silverstein regarding a *Harrington*-compliant re-noticing protocol for approximately **2,100** claims whose holders did not affirmatively ballot on the plan's original chartered-organization release provisions. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) The Trust argues that the original opt-out ballot satisfied *Harrington*'s "affirmative consent" standard; CASJ argues that an opt-out ballot is structurally identical to the mechanism the Court rejected in *Purdue*. Briefing closes June 3, 2026.

**Mallinckrodt plc (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 23-11945, Judge John T. Dorsey):** Mallinckrodt filed its second amended plan on May 15, 2026, stripping the broad channeling injunctions that protected former directors and major shareholders and replacing them with a **§ 524(g)-compliant** asbestos-style trust for opioid claimants, funded at **$1.725 billion**. The U.S. Trustee's Office, which was the *Harrington* petitioner's analog in *Purdue*, filed a statement of interest on May 19 indicating tentative non-objection to the revised plan structure subject to confirmation that the release language is limited to "derivative" claims. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__) Confirmation hearing is scheduled for July 14, 2026 before Judge Dorsey.

**FTX Trading Ltd. (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068, Judge John T. Dorsey):** The confirmed FTX plan's "exculpation" clause — narrower than a third-party release but still subject to *Harrington* scrutiny — is the subject of an adversary proceeding filed May 12, 2026 by a creditor group holding approximately **$340 million** in allowed claims, seeking a declaratory judgment that the exculpation provision does not bar their direct fraud claims against former FTX executives other than Sam Bankman-Fried. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__) The plan's effective date distributions (projected at **118 cents on the dollar** for most creditors, using the November 2022 dollar-denominated value floor) remain on track for a Q3 2026 disbursement, but the adversary proceeding introduces a modest tail risk for the distribution timeline.

**Serta Simmons Bedding (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-22385, Judge Sean H. Lane):** The Second Circuit's January 2026 ruling in *In re Serta Simmons Bedding LLC* addressing the validity of the 2020 "uptier exchange" — which allowed certain lenders to leapfrog non-participating term-loan lenders in priority — continues to generate litigation. Judge Lane is now administering a claims reconciliation process affecting approximately **$1.9 billion** in asserted "excluded lender" claims that were subordinated in the uptier. The court-appointed mediator issued a May 19, 2026 status report indicating a **47%** resolution rate among participating excluded lenders, with mediation extended through June 30, 2026. (__[WSJ Pro Bankruptcy — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com/pro/bankruptcy)__)

### Preference-Avoidance Reform, § 547 Ordinary-Course Defense, and the *Robertshaw* Fifth Circuit Ruling

The Fifth Circuit issued a decision in *Robertshaw US Holding Corp. v. Certain Trade Creditors* (5th Cir., No. 25-20144, panel: Judges Richman, Oldham, and Wilson) on May 20, 2026, affirming the bankruptcy court's determination that § 547(c)(4) new-value credit operates on a net basis across the entire preference period and cannot be disaggregated into individual transfer-by-transfer buckets. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__) This ruling aligns the Fifth Circuit with the Third and Seventh Circuits and widens the split with the Eleventh Circuit's *In re Furr's/Bishop's, Inc.* approach, which permits creditors to "stack" new-value credits transactionally. For trade creditors operating in the Fifth Circuit with substantial preference exposure, the ruling forecloses a common defense strategy: where a creditor received **$2.5 million** in payments in the 90-day preference window but extended **$2.5 million** in subsequent new credit (in multiple tranches), the creditor cannot allocate specific later-credit tranches to specific earlier payments to zero out individual transfer liability — instead, the net exposure is assessed over the full preference window. The practical effect is that preference defendants in the Fifth Circuit defending moderate-to-large claims should prioritize the ordinary-course defense under § 547(c)(2) and the contemporaneous-exchange defense under § 547(c)(1) rather than structuring their entire defense around new-value netting. (__[Haynes Boone — needs verification](https://www.haynesboone.com)__)

### USTP Guidance on Rule 2019 Disclosures in Mass-Tort Chapter 11s

The Office of the U.S. Trustee, Executive Office for U.S. Trustees, issued Memorandum UST-2026-04 on May 20, 2026 clarifying that **all** ad hoc committees holding economic interests in mass-tort Chapter 11 cases — including trust advisory committees formed post-confirmation — must comply with Fed. R. Bankr. P. 2019's verified-statement requirement, regardless of whether those committees were formed before or after plan confirmation. (__[EOUST — needs verification](https://www.justice.gov/ust)__) The guidance is triggered in part by litigation in the *3M Combat Arms Earplug* bankruptcy (N.D. Fla., Case No. 22-02890, Judge M. Casey Rodgers) where a post-confirmation claimant advisory committee — funded by a contingency-fee litigation funder — disputed whether it owed 2019 disclosure obligations. The USTP's position, consistent with the Second and Third Circuit interpretations, is that Rule 2019 applies to any entity acting in a representative capacity with an economic interest in the estate, post-confirmation or otherwise. Affected committees have **30 days** from the date of the guidance to file amended verified statements or face motion practice from the USTP seeking sanctions under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9011 and § 105(a).

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and rights-holders should immediately triage their active Chapter 11 exposures against three action items arising from this week's developments: **First**, any creditor asserting stub-rent administrative claims in Third Circuit cases should evaluate the *Rite Aid* bench ruling and, where occupancy was non-operational during the stub period, consider proactively recharacterizing those claims as general-unsecured to avoid costly motion practice — the 14-day reconsideration window (ending approximately June 3, 2026) is the relevant procedural clock for landlords who wish to preserve appellate options. **Second**, trade creditors in Fifth Circuit preference-avoidance actions should immediately confer with restructuring counsel to stress-test their new-value defense under the *Robertshaw* net-basis rule; defendants relying on transactional new-value stacking should shift resources to developing contemporaneous-exchange (§ 547(c)(1)) and ordinary-course-of-business (§ 547(c)(2)) defenses with specific factual support, including industry benchmarking data and payment-history records for at least **two years** pre-petition. **Third**, ad hoc committee counsel in any mass-tort Chapter 11 — confirmed or pending — should audit their Rule 2019 compliance posture against the new UST-2026-04 guidance; failure to file an amended verified statement within the 30-day window creates unnecessary adversarial exposure with the USTP and potential delay to distribution timelines, particularly in the *BSA* and *Mallinckrodt* contexts where trust administration is already politically sensitive. Claim purchasers and secondary-market participants should also re-price Yellow Corporation general-unsecured claims in light of the ABL lien-reach dispute, which represents the single largest swing variable in recovery projections.
