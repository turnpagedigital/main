CRITICAL HEARING TOMORROW — Harvest Sherwood Food Distributors, Inc. | Bankr. N.D. Tex. | Case No. 25-80109 | Judge Stacey G.C. Jernigan

Covered here since the July 20 U.S. Trustee objection. The Plan confirmation hearing is currently scheduled for August 5, 2026. The two open confirmation disputes are structural: the U.S. Trustee objected at Docket No. 1063, arguing that the Plan remains unconfirmable because its opt-out third-party release does not establish knowing and voluntary consent, and because it permits residual Liquidating Trust cash to be donated to charity rather than deposited with the Court.

The third-party release issue is a live circuit question post-Purdue. Judge Jernigan's ruling tomorrow will add to the growing body of district-level authority applying the Supreme Court's consent standard to opt-out mechanics. The charitable-donation point is narrower but practically significant: residual trust funds directed to charity rather than the Court represent a creditor-recovery question, not just a housekeeping one. Watch for whether Judge Jernigan addresses both issues on the merits or continues the hearing to require further briefing. Harvest Sherwood's case centers on monetizing litigation assets — including approximately $1.09 billion in antitrust claims against over 28 defendants across pork, chicken, beef, and turkey producer categories — through a court-supervised wind-down process.

RESOLUTION — Jackson Walker LLP / U.S. Trustee | Bankr. S.D. Tex. | In re: Professional Fee Matters Concerning the Jackson Walker Law Firm | Judge Eduardo V. Rodriguez

The Jones/Freeman scandal closes its principal chapter. Jackson Walker LLP will pay $15 million to end litigation with the Justice Department's bankruptcy watchdog over an undisclosed relationship between a former attorney and one of the nation's former top bankruptcy judges.

The settlement would end long-running litigation brought by the U.S. Trustee's office accusing Jackson Walker of breaching its ethical duties by failing to disclose the romance its onetime bankruptcy partner Elizabeth Freeman had with ex-bankruptcy judge David R. Jones while the firm was representing clients in cases involving Jones — and the law firm is not admitting wrongdoing as part of the agreement, according to a settlement term sheet filed Sunday in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas.

As part of the deal, Jackson Walker has also agreed to reform its conflicts and disclosure practices.

The U.S. Trustee had brought the litigation in November 2023 to vacate orders approving Jackson Walker's retention fees and applications in cases connected to Jones and Freeman; extensive discovery showed that at least two Jackson Walker attorneys exchanged text messages in 2021 indicating they knew about, and discussed the potential fallout of, the undisclosed romance.

The deal comes soon after Houston Judge Eduardo V. Rodriguez recommended approval of nine private settlements that allowed the U.S. Trustee to continue pursuing legal action against Jackson Walker; those settlements, which must still be approved by another judge, would return nearly $4.8 million to several bankruptcy estates.

A major point of contention through the litigation had been the U.S. Trustee's efforts to vacate more than $20 million in fees awarded to the firm in 34 cases involving Jones and Freeman.

Jackson Walker is represented by Norton Rose Fulbright US LLP and Rusty Hardin & Associates LLP.

The $15 million figure — against the backdrop of $20 million in contested fees across 34 cases — will be read as a significant discount by critics. It remains unclear what the lasting legacy will be: creditors and practitioners may question the integrity of the bankruptcy system or treat the settlement as the cost of doing business, particularly given questions about whether Jackson Walker enjoyed more favorable rulings or had inside information from Jones in large, complex bankruptcies due to his romance with Freeman. The settlement requires court approval; its structural significance — mandatory disclosure-practice reform — is the more durable element for practitioners operating in the S.D. Tex. complex docket going forward.
