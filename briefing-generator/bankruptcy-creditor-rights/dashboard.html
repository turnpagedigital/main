BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — §363 Sale Closed; Plan Confirmation and Regulatory Clearance Remain Open

Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH). The case sits before Judge Thomas M. Horan in Courtroom No. 5, 824 Market Street, 5th Floor, Wilmington, Delaware 19801. The Debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.

The case ran a dual-track from the outset. The Plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a §363 competitive sale process. The 363 track resolved first. Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.

Keyrock was named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and the U.S. bankruptcy court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.

The $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate on the order of 2 cents on the dollar from the sale proceeds alone — a figure that should be the baseline assumption for any holder underwriting unsecured exposure at this point. The plan track, however, carries additional recovery variables. On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement and scheduling the confirmation hearing, with a Voting Deadline of July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET and a Confirmation Objection Deadline of the same date [Docket No. 388].

The Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.

The docket ground truth shows three new filings in the 72 hours through July 27 (docket entries 539 and 541), but their descriptions are not populated in the live data. The confirmation hearing set for July 13 likely proceeded — consistent with the July 10 filing at Docket No. 523 (a service list filing from Verita) — but a confirmed order has not been independently verified in this run. The Veritaglobal key dates page lists a September 11, 2026 date that may represent a plan effective date or administrative claims bar date. That forward date suggests the case has not yet fully closed.

The regulatory dimension is the most exposed variable. The transaction folds in a CIMA-registered Cayman entity acquired outright and seeks to transfer a proposed FCA-authorised U.K. entity to Keyrock. The U.K. transfer still requires FCA approval, which would expand Keyrock's ability to face certain institutional clients in that jurisdiction.

Final regulatory clearances for the transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026. A failure to obtain FCA approval could impair asset value transferred under the APA, potentially triggering a purchase-price adjustment or clawback, with downstream consequences for the liquidating trust's distributable assets.

The Committee of Unsecured Creditors — comprising SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc. — is advised by FTI Consulting (retained April 1, 2026) and Morris James LLP as Delaware co-counsel (per Docket No. 262). The Debtors are represented by McDermott Will & Emery LLP (Darren Azman, Gregg Steinman, Joseph Evans) and Katten Muchin Rosenman LLP (Peter Siddiqui) as legal counsel, with BRG (Mark Renzi) as financial advisor and CRO.

The Ad Hoc Group is represented by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP (Sean O'Neal, Jane VanLare) and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor, LLP (Sean Greecher).

The unresolved customer-property question remains the case's sharpest legal issue for distressed investors. The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested. The onboarding agreement language — which specifically stated that customer deposits would be "completely segregated from the general balance sheet" — was cited at the first-day hearing by at least one creditor as a basis for a constructive trust or segregation argument. Resolution of that issue will govern how the liquidating trust distributes residual value beyond the $3.25 million sale proceeds: if customer assets are estate property, the pool is shared ratably among all unsecured creditors; if they are not, affected customers may recover ahead of the class.

Three items from the Law360 ticker merit brief note for distressed investors monitoring the Chapter 11 ecosystem:

QVC / Qurate ($5B Plan Confirmation — July 16): On July 16, 2026, QVC won approval of its $5 billion Chapter 11 plan. Distressed investors in retail sector claims should note the precedent for administrative efficiency even at multi-billion-dollar scale.
