BlockFills — Keyrock Sale Closed; Plan Confirmation Set September 11

The asset-disposition phase of In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., No. 26-10371 (TMH) (Bankr. D. Del.), is effectively complete. The open question for creditors is what the liquidating trust delivers on approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.

A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group on June 16, 2026, as BlockFills prepared for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing. The successful bidder is Keyrock SA. Per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and assumed "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.

The $3.25 million gross sale price against a general unsecured pool of approximately $145 million is a striking recovery ratio — and reflects the nature of the underlying estate. The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.

Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc. and three affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 protection on March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities. The spread between those petition-date ranges tells most of the recovery story before confirmation briefs are even filed.

The case originated in a liquidity crisis with fraud-litigation overtones. On February 6, 2026, BlockFills publicly announced a broader suspension of deposits and withdrawals; in the weeks preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits were filed asserting misappropriation of customer funds and conversion.

The courts in both matters granted temporary restraining orders that restricted the debtors' ability to conduct operations and exercise control over certain assets.

The automatic stay afforded by Chapter 11 was intended to halt further litigation and prevent individual customers from pursuing assets outside the bankruptcy process.

The two pre-petition customer actions — Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., filed February 27, 2026) and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill., filed March 5, 2026) — remain relevant to the liquidating-trust's claim-allowance process. Whether those plaintiffs' fraud-based claims receive unsecured treatment or assert priority as customer-property claimants under a constructive-trust or § 541 carve-out theory is a threshold confirmation issue. No verified court ruling on that issue is in the record as of today's run.

Final regulatory clearances for the Keyrock transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026. That status — court-approved but not yet fully regulatory-cleared — creates a residual closing risk that could affect the timing of trust-asset distributions. The confirmed plan confirmation hearing date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET , per the Verita claims administrator's key dates calendar. Creditors who purchased claims in the secondary market — or are contemplating doing so — should track whether the pre-petition customer-action plaintiffs' litigation positions are resolved, settled, or subordinated at or before confirmation. The Verita claims portal at veritaglobal.net/blockfills tracks all related filings.

For distressed investors, the post-sale posture in BlockFills is now a pure trust-recovery play. The Keyrock deal expands Keyrock's regulatory footprint to include a CIMA-registered entity in the Cayman Islands and a proposed FCA-authorized entity in the U.K., pending regulatory approval — but none of those assets remain in the estate. What remains is the liquidating trust's cash, any residual estate claims (including any avoidance actions against insiders or counterparties), and whatever distributions emerge after professional fees and trust expenses. At $3.25 million in gross sale proceeds against $145 million in unsecured claims, the trust's recovery potential depends almost entirely on whether the estate has viable affirmative claims — fraudulent transfer, preference, or breach-of-fiduciary actions — against identifiable defendants.

Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed-claim holders in the Sleep Number estate should move immediately on two fronts: (1) confirm whether their claims are in the assumed-liability schedule or the rejected-contract bucket before the July 31 closing — executory contract counterparties who are not assumed face a post-sale bar-date environment with capped § 502(b)(6) damages and should file protective proofs of claim if the general bar date has not already run; and (2) assess whether the final sale order entered by Judge Paek contains a § 363(m) good-faith finding and, if so, whether any pending objections survive mootness — the window to seek a stay is functionally closed at July 31 closing. In BlockFills, general unsecured creditors should file claims through the Verita portal (veritaglobal.net/blockfills) and monitor the September 11 confirmation hearing for plan treatment of the pre-petition TRO plaintiffs' claims, which carry a non-trivial risk of asserting customer-property priority ahead of the general unsecured pool; if the trust has viable avoidance claims, secondary-market acquirers of general unsecured paper may see better recovery than the $3.25 million gross-sale figure implies — but only if those avoidance claims are identified and pursued aggressively by trust counsel before any statute of limitations issues arise under 11 U.S.C. § 546.
