# Tariffs / Trade — Social Posts | June 12, 2026

## LinkedIn

Three days after Judge Eaton looked across his Manhattan courtroom and told DOJ lawyers "my appeal to you is to withdraw your appeal," the IEEPA refund fight is exactly where it was — and that's the story.

Where things stand:

• ~$166B in IEEPA duties collected. ~$85B accepted into the CAPE refund pipeline; ~$23B already sent to Treasury for payout, with another $480M moving this week.
• The dispute is narrower than the headline number suggests: the government now concedes only $10B–$11.4B is actually frozen by its June 2 Federal Circuit appeal. "Even I think that's real money," Eaton said from the bench.
• The appeal targets one category — finally-liquidated entries — on the theory CBP can't reliquidate without an importer-specific court order. The judicial-estoppel record (AGS, Dec. 2025) cuts hard against that.
• Eaton is weighing a Rule 23 class of IEEPA-paying importers, which would let him order class-wide refunds without 4,000 separate suits.

No new appellate order dropped in the last 24 hours. That's not a reason to wait. Protest clocks run 180 days from liquidation no matter what the Federal Circuit does, and once an entry goes "finally liquidated" it falls into the one category the government is fighting over.

If you paid IEEPA duties: file your CAPE declaration, and preserve a protective CIT filing before your entries age into Phase 3.
