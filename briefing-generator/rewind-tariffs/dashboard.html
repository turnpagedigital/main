Recommended Actions

Importers whose IEEPA refund entries were accepted under CAPE Phase 1 should verify issuance status against CBP's ACE portal immediately — the $86.3 billion repaid figure as of July 10 suggests a meaningful number of accepted entries are still awaiting check or ACH disbursement, and the July 13 court filing deadline may have triggered updated CBP reporting that changes that figure. Importers in the 18% of entries not covered by Phase 1 should file 19 U.S.C. § 1514 protests at their servicing port before the 180-day liquidation clock runs — protest rights run independently of the CAPE portal and are unaffected by the pending Federal Circuit appeal; missing this window forfeits refund rights with no cure. Counsel tracking the new Section 301 challenges (Burlap & Barrel v. Greer and Learning Resources v. United States, both filed July 24 at the CIT) should prepare for an early government motion to dismiss or transfer based on the jurisdictional and standing arguments already tested in the IEEPA cycle, and should file protective motions to suspend liquidation of Section 301 entries now — even absent a TRO, a suspended-liquidation order from the CIT preserves the refund corpus if the new challenges ultimately succeed. Importers with Brazil-origin goods face independent analysis given the July 22 Section 232-adjacent 25% Brazil tariff layered on top of the new Section 301 rates; product exclusion petitions filed at USTR now may be the fastest path to rate relief pending litigation outcome. Finally, for any importer not yet enrolled in the class certification proceeding in Atmus Filtration, monitor the CIT's ruling on class certification — a favorable ruling would automatically include eligible importers and may be the only refund path if Phase 2 CAPE coverage is narrower than the full entry universe.
