IEEPA Refund Track — CAPE Phase 1 Active; Phase 2 Scope Unannounced

The IEEPA refund pipeline remains the more mature of the two recovery tracks. On February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court held 6-3 in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump (No. 24-1287) and Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc. (No. 25-250) that IEEPA does not grant the President authority to impose tariffs, and the same day President Trump issued an Executive Order rescinding IEEPA tariffs and directing agency heads to "terminate the collection" of those tariffs "as soon as practicable."

The majority opinion, written by Chief Justice Roberts, was joined by Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson.

The refund process is continuing with respect to approximately $166 billion in duties previously collected under IEEPA; CBP has deployed Phase 1 of the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) functionality within the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) to process IEEPA refund claims.

A substantial number of filings have been submitted since the system opened in late April 2026, with a significant number of entries accepted for refund processing and liquidation and refund issuance under way, although a subset of submissions has been rejected for failing entry-specific validations.

Coverage under Phase 1 is not universal. CAPE Phase 1 covers approximately 82% of IEEPA entries and/or duty deposits — including unliquidated entries; entries with liquidation statuses of "Suspended," "Extended," or "Under Review"; warehouse and warehouse withdrawal entries; and entries liquidated within the preceding 80 days.

CBP has not yet announced the scope of CAPE Phase 2. Only the importer of record is eligible to obtain refunds directly from CBP. The remaining ~18% of entries — those with final liquidations older than 80 days — are not covered by Phase 1. For those entries, the path runs through § 1514 protests or, if time has elapsed, § 1520(d) or duty-drawback under 19 U.S.C. § 1313. Importers who did not timely protest and sit outside the CAPE window face a significantly diminished recovery probability.

Section 122 Refund Mechanics — No CAPE Equivalent Yet

The Section 122 refund infrastructure remains substantially undeveloped relative to the IEEPA track. The refund mechanism is an open question. There does not appear to be a mature administrative framework for Section 122 recoveries in the same way importers expected for other tariff activity. As a result, protests, litigation strategy, and liquidation timing may become more important.

It is possible that the CAPE process will be duplicated for future Section 122 refunds from CBP, but that cannot be assumed at this stage.

If the CIT ruling is ultimately upheld and implemented more broadly, refunds would likely be processed through standard post-entry administrative procedures, which can involve significant delays between a final finding of unlawfulness and the actual issuance of refunds. The § 1514 protest clock runs 180 days from the date of liquidation. For entries made beginning February 24, 2026, liquidation timelines are still unfolding — but importers who let protests lapse on the assumption of an administrative CAPE-style process risk losing their rights entirely if Section 122 relief is ultimately broadened through litigation rather than a CBP-initiated refund program. The CIT's injunction ordered Section 122 surcharges paid by the importer plaintiffs to be "refunded with interest as provided by law," but that decision was reinforced — and then effectively suspended — by the CIT's May 20, 2026 denial of the DOJ's stay motion, with the CAFC's administrative stay suspending the CIT's injunction in any event.

The Section 122 appellate posture now points strongly toward Supreme Court review. The legal status of the Section 122 tariffs remains in flux and subject to ongoing appellate review, and many believe this case will also proceed to the U.S. Supreme Court. The Federal Circuit's stay-grant language — expressly crediting likelihood of government success — does not guarantee reversal, but it meaningfully complicates the importer-recovery thesis for any entity that has not yet filed a § 1514 protest.

---

Section 301 Replacement Tariff Risk — The Structural Overhang

Even a clean, broadened Section 122 victory at the Federal Circuit or Supreme Court does not eliminate tariff exposure. The administration recently initiated wide-ranging Section 301 investigations under the Trade Act of 1974, which authorizes tariffs on goods from countries engaged in unjustifiable, unreasonable, or discriminatory trade practices.

The USTR is conducting two Section 301 investigations involving 76 separate potential tariff determinations, expected to conclude before Section 122 expires July 24, 2026. Unlike IEEPA and Section 122, Section 301 carries no time cap and no balance-of-payments definitional constraint. If Section 301 determinations roll out before July 24, importers may face immediate replacement duties at comparable or higher rates — with the IEEPA refund recovery offset against a new, legally durable cost base. That substitution risk is the central variable CFOs should be stress-testing now, independent of the litigation outcome.

---

Recommended Actions

Importers and refund counsel should bifurcate their docket posture by track. On the IEEPA-CAPE side: verify that all eligible entries have been submitted through the ACE CAPE portal; confirm that any rejected submissions have been corrected and resubmitted, since CBP has been returning entries for entry-specific validation failures; identify Phase 1-excluded entries (liquidated more than 80 days before CAPE opened) and assess whether § 1514 protest windows remain open or whether § 1520(d) and § 1313 drawback provide the only residual paths. On the Section 122 side: importers should review Section 122 duties paid and currently paying to preserve their rights to assert claims and seek refunds in the future — the Federal Circuit's June 11 full stay does not eliminate refund rights, but it means those rights must be preserved through § 1514 protests filed within 180 days of each entry's liquidation date; do not rely on a future CAPE-equivalent being announced before the July 24 expiration. Companies that prepare by organizing their data, preserving refund rights, and assessing cross-functional implications will be better positioned to respond — regardless of whether the appellate process results in refunding the surcharge, or replacement tariff authorities are established before the surcharge's July 24 expiration. Monitor the Section 301 investigation docket through USTR's public register; if determinations are published before July 24, conduct a product-by-product classification review immediately — both to assess the new duty rate and to determine whether previously refunded or refundable IEEPA duties are effectively recaptured by the replacement regime.
