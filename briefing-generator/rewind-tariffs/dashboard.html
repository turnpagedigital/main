2. CAPE Phase 3: The July 17 Eaton Order and What It Requires This Week

The operative Phase 3 instrument is Judge Eaton's order of July 17, 2026, in V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. United States, Court No. 25-00066. On July 17, 2026, Senior Judge Richard Eaton issued an order directing CBP to "reliquidate, without regard to IEEPA duties, any and all of Plaintiffs' entries that have been liquidated for more than 80 days and on which Plaintiffs made estimated deposits pursuant to IEEPA." This followed the transfer to Judge Eaton's docket of more than 3,700 cases seeking IEEPA refunds.

The order provides the government with the legal authority it had said it needed before it would voluntarily reliquidate finally liquidated entries — the government's position had consistently been that a court order directing reliquidation of entries whose liquidation had become final would be necessary before such refunds could flow. The relief is not universal: the order applies only to companies that have filed cases seeking IEEPA tariff refunds at the CIT, and it directs reliquidation of all entries liquidated for more than 80 days, which includes entries that may continue to become final during the CAPE process itself.

To successfully submit a CAPE declaration for finally liquidated entries, plaintiffs must first submit to CBP certain information, including their importer of record identification numbers. CBP will issue separate instructions to plaintiffs' counsel. This is a procedural predicate — firms that have filed but have not yet submitted importer IDs are sitting on a Phase 3 delay of their own making.

The CIT's order further states that the court will issue case-specific orders in each of the approximately 3,700 individual IEEPA cases directing Customs to reliquidate certain finally liquidated entries — and separately confirms that the new lead case is Freestyle World Inc. v. United States, as the plaintiff in Euro-Notions intends to voluntarily dismiss.

On the volume side, the scale of what has already moved through CAPE is significant. As of CBP's July 13 filing, CAPE has accepted 24.4 million entries for IEEPA-duty removal, with approximately $86.3 billion completed, certified, and sent to Treasury. That figure sits against a total pool of roughly $166 billion in collected IEEPA tariffs — meaning approximately 52% of the pool has been certified for refund, with Phase 3 (finally liquidated entries) and the protest-track entries constituting the bulk of the remainder.

There is currently no procedure for requesting a refund of IEEPA tariffs on an entry that has been protested. Expanding CAPE's capabilities to cover protested entries would allow CBP to process a broader range of refund claims through a single administrative platform — CBP indicated at the July 15 webinar that it is evaluating that expansion, but no order has issued and no timeline has been confirmed.

3. August 4–6 Proceedings: The Consequential Cluster

Three successive proceedings next week will define the Phase 3 operational timeline and the class-action path for non-filers:

August 4 — CAPE Progress Report (5:00 p.m. EDT). CBP is required to file a short progress report on CAPE with the court by 5:00 p.m. EDT on August 4, 2026. The report will confirm whether Phase 3 infrastructure is operational following the July 17 reliquidation order and whether CBP has begun accepting importer-ID submissions from the 3,700-case population. This is the first post-Phase-3-order data point.

August 5 — Closed Settlement Conference. A closed settlement conference follows on August 5. No public filings will emerge from this session, but its outcome could affect the government's posture in the Federal Circuit appeal — DOJ's June 2 notices of appeal remain pending, and a negotiated administrative resolution of the finally-liquidated-entry question would moot the appeal's sharpest edge.

August 6 — Class Certification Hearing. The class certification hearing for IEEPA duty refund plaintiffs is set for August 6, 2026, in connection with the pending motion filed by Terry Precision Cycling. Plaintiffs argued at the June 9 hearing that if the government is committed to refunds but worried about CASA (the Supreme Court's universal-injunction decision), it should not oppose class certification — though Judge Eaton expressed reservations about that path. A class certification ruling, even a favorable one, would face immediate government opposition and likely a Federal Circuit interlocutory challenge; its primary value is as leverage in the settlement conference the day before.

4. The Refund Gap for Non-Filers: Holland & Knight "File Now" Alert (July 27)

For importers that have not filed suit, the path to refunds on finally liquidated entries remains unresolved. Potential relief depends on pending proceedings before the Federal Circuit and unresolved class certification motions at the CIT, neither of which presently provides a certain outcome or timeline.

On June 2, 2026, DOJ formally appealed the CIT's order requiring refunds of all IEEPA tariffs. In related filings, the government characterized the refund orders as impermissible universal injunctions and argued that the CIT lacks authority to grant relief extending beyond parties before the court. Although the appeal encompasses the broader refund orders, recent government filings suggest the principal dispute concerns finally liquidated entries, with the government arguing that importers seeking refunds for finally liquidated entries must pursue importer-specific remedies.

The practical consequence: one of the most consequential remaining issues concerns entries that became "finally liquidated" before refunds could be processed — and for those entries, the distinction between importers that filed CIT actions and those that did not has become increasingly important. The July 17 Eaton order resolved that distinction in favor of filers. It does nothing for non-filers.

Importers that have not filed should evaluate promptly whether a protective action is appropriate. The Holland & Knight alert, published July 27, treats the August 6 class certification hearing as a near-term data point but does not treat it as a substitute for individual action — the message is that class relief is uncertain and the filing window is narrowing with each day that more entries pass the 80-day liquidation mark.
