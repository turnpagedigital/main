2. CAPE Phase 3 — "Late July" Deadline Passes; CBP Progress Report Due August 4

The CIT issued a reliquidation order on July 17, 2026, requiring CBP to "reliquidate, without regard to IEEPA duties, any and all of Plaintiffs' entries that have been liquidated for more than 80 days" on which IEEPA deposits were made — and the order reaches both finally liquidated entries and entries that may become final while being processed by CAPE, supplying the legal authority the government had maintained was necessary before it could reliquidate finally liquidated entries. The case citation is In re Tariffs Collected in Reliance on IEEPA, No. 26-00219 (Ct. Int'l Trade July 17, 2026). The CIT's July 17 order applies to the plaintiffs in the more than 3,700 IEEPA cases currently before the CIT — meaning importers who have filed suit will receive individualized relief through those proceedings.

CBP is required to file a short progress report on CAPE with the court by 5:00 p.m. EDT on August 4, 2026. That filing will be the first public confirmation of whether Phase 3 programming is live, how many finally-liquidated entries have been submitted, and whether the government has identified additional categories it contends are outside the reliquidation order's scope.

What remains unresolved. The CIT has issued its first Phase 3 procedure order for litigating plaintiffs with entries liquidated more than 80 days, but broader access and open-protest functionality remain unresolved.

The government has stated that Phase 3 refunds will be processed only for importers who have filed lawsuits at the CIT. Importers without a docketed CIT action who have finally-liquidated entries should treat the August 4 CBP report as a critical data point and should be coordinating with trade counsel now on whether a protective § 1581(i) filing is still possible.

The overall pipeline context: as of July 10, 2026, approximately $121.75 billion in claims had been accepted and roughly $86.3 billion had actually been repaid to importers, including interest.

CBP's latest filings indicate the finally-liquidated pool covers approximately $11.4 billion, or 7 percent of total IEEPA tariff revenue. Interest accrues under 19 CFR 24.36 at 7% for individuals and 6% for corporations, compounded daily — the meter is running on that $11.4 billion tranche every day Phase 3 sits without disbursements.

Warehouse-entry note. Per CSMS #69127837, CBP updated its guidance on how warehouse entries and warehouse withdrawals are handled under CAPE: effective July 7, 2026, Entry Types 21 and 22 (warehouse entries) will no longer be accepted on a CAPE Declaration.

Warehouse withdrawals (Entry Types 31, 32, 34, and 38) will continue to be accepted, with refunds processed upon reliquidation of the associated warehouse entry. Importers who filed Type 21 or 22 entries before July 7 and have not corrected their submissions need to refile using withdrawal entries.

3. Section 232 Pharmaceutical Tariffs Activate Today — 100% Ad Valorem on Patented Drugs and APIs for Annex III Companies

Effective as of 12:01 a.m. EDT today, July 31, 2026, Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs apply to goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, for the companies listed in Annex III of Proclamation 11020.

Proclamation 11020, issued April 2, 2026, determined that imports of certain pharmaceuticals and pharmaceutical ingredients threaten to impair U.S. national security and imposed tariffs under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, setting a default 100 percent tariff rate for covered patented products and associated ingredients — effective July 31, 2026, for certain large companies and September 29, 2026, for small companies — while excluding generic pharmaceuticals and their associated ingredients.

Annex II lists 13 companies that entered into company-specific agreements with the Secretary of Commerce before April 2, 2026, and those companies have individual tariff terms that may differ from the standard tier structure; Annex III names 17 large pharmaceutical companies facing the accelerated July 31 effective date.

Generic pharmaceuticals and their associated ingredients are not subject to Section 232 tariffs at this time.

The tariff operates as a floating rate: where a covered product is also subject to a non-duty-free MFN rate, the Section 232 tariff is reduced so that the cumulative duty equals 100%; if the applicable MFN rate exceeds 100%, only the MFN rate applies.

Refund and drawback interplay. Drawbacks are available for duties imposed by this proclamation. For clients in the pharmaceutical supply chain, duty drawback under 19 U.S.C. § 1313 is an active recovery mechanism from today's effective date, particularly for Annex III companies importing APIs that are subsequently incorporated into exported finished goods. Counsel should begin segregating entries by Annex classification immediately; misclassification between Annex I (covered) and Annex IV (zero-rate) codes carries significant duty exposure.

These Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs were not struck down by the Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling. Section 232 operates under separate legal authority that the court did not touch, and these tariffs are permanent until affirmatively removed. Unlike Section 122, there is no statutory sunset.

4. Section 122 — Expired July 24; Federal Circuit Appeal Preserves Refund Optionality

The Section 122 surcharge was in force from February 24, 2026 to July 24, 2026 — a 150-day statutory life — and expired at its statutory limit; Congress did not extend it.

The July 24 sunset extinguishes the surcharge going forward but does not itself refund duties collected during the 150-day period; the disposition of amounts already paid remains tied to the pending Federal Circuit appeal in State of Oregon v. United States and Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States (Court Nos. 26-01472 & 26-01606).

The procedural sequence: the CIT ruled on May 7, 2026 (Slip Op. 26-47) that the administration exceeded its authority under 19 U.S.C. § 2132 because Proclamation 11012 failed to demonstrate the balance-of-payments conditions the statute requires. On June 11, 2026, the Federal Circuit granted the government's motion to stay the CIT's injunction, finding that three of the four traditional stay factors favored the government, with the fourth neutral — effectively pausing the relief previously granted to the named plaintiffs, meaning the 10% surcharge was collected from all importers through the statutory expiry.

That appeal is still running. If it ultimately upholds the finding that the surcharge was unlawful, refund opportunities may open to importers beyond the original plaintiffs. The refund-preservation action for Section 122 duties paid during the 150-day period is a timely protest under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 or, for importers with standing arguments, a § 1581(i) protective filing at the CIT. Counsel should be evaluating both routes now — the 180-day protest clock is rolling on entries from the February–July window, and the earliest February 24 entries are approaching that deadline.

5. Replacement Tariff Regime — Section 301 and Generic Drug Phased Framework

Section 122's expiration was followed by a Section 301 forced-labour action of 10% or 12.5% on products of approximately 60 economies.

USTR's June 2, 2026 Section 301 determination concerning the forced-labor enforcement practices of 60 trading partners proposed additional duties of 10 to 12.5 percent, with a public hearing on July 7. Final rates and effective dates are unconfirmed as of this writing; Andrew should monitor the Federal Register for the Section 301 final determination, which is the next scheduled milestone directly affecting the duty rate environment for importers whose IEEPA refunds were calculated against the now-expired Section 122 baseline.

On July 21, 2026, President Trump announced a phased plan to impose tariffs on imported generic drugs for companies that have not committed to build U.S. manufacturing facilities; effective August 1, 2026, all generic drugs will continue at 0% for a two-year period, after which the tariff on generic drugs will rise to 100% for one year (August 2028–August 2029) and would be 200% thereafter. This announcement carries no immediate duty impact but directly affects sourcing decisions and supply-chain restructuring timelines for pharmaceutical importers not already covered by the Annex III Section 232 regime that activates today.
