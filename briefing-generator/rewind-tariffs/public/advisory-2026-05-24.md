### I. IEEPA Omnibus Litigation: Federal Circuit Stay Denial and the Road to June 3 Oral Argument

The Federal Circuit's May 19 panel order denying the government's emergency stay application in *United States v. V.O.S.S. Reliant Corp.*, Fed. Cir. No. 25-1812, shifts the litigation's center of gravity back to Chief Judge Mark Barnett's courtroom at 1 Federal Plaza, New York. The three-judge CIT panel—Chief Judge Barnett, Judge Claire Kelly, and Judge Gary Katzmann—issued its underlying injunction on May 14, holding that Executive Order 14195 and the follow-on tariff schedules promulgated thereunder likely exceed the President's authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, 50 U.S.C. §§ 1701–1708, because (a) the "any unusual and extraordinary threat" finding was insufficiently particularized to the trade deficit framing Congress contemplated; and (b) Section 1702(a)(1)(B)'s authorization to "regulate" importation does not carry a plenary power to impose revenue-generating tariffs at rates reaching 145% (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/federal-circuit-stays-cit-ieepa-tariff-injunction-2026-05-19/)__). The Federal Circuit's stay denial means the injunction remains operative, but the court simultaneously expedited the merits appeal, with the government's opening brief already filed May 21 and appellee briefs due June 18 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/federal-circuit-ieepa-tariff-appeal-schedule-may-2026)__).

At the CIT, June 3 oral argument will address whether a permanent injunction should issue and, critically, what bond the government must post to maintain any partial administrative stay pending the Federal Circuit resolution. Plaintiffs' counsel—led by Sidley Austin LLP on behalf of the corporate omnibus petitioners and Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld LLP for the small-business petitioners—have argued that a $50,000 nominal bond is legally sufficient given the public interest in unimpeded trade flows and the government's inability to demonstrate irreparable harm beyond generalized revenue loss (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/ieepa-cit-bond-hearing-june-2026)__). The government counters that any injunction covering the full universe of Section 2(b)(i) tariff schedules—estimated by CBP at $93 billion in annual tariff revenue if left in place—demands a commensurate supersedeas bond under 28 U.S.C. § 2643(c), an argument Judge Barnett appeared to find unpersuasive at the May 14 hearing (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/ieepa-omnibus-may-2026-cit-update)__).

Refund-claim practitioners should note that the injunction, if converted to a permanent order, will not itself generate automatic refund entitlement—CBP's protest and reliquidation machinery under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 remains the operative procedural track, and entries liquidated during the injunction's operative period while the government pursued the stay are now in a contested protest posture. Importers should verify that timely protests (within 180 days of liquidation under § 1514(c)(3)) are on file for every covered entry; any lapse in the protest window cannot be cured by the subsequent litigation outcome.

### II. U.S.-China 90-Day Truce: Geneva Annex Terms, Tariff Rate Table, and Compliance Calendar

The May 12 Geneva joint statement between USTR Jamieson Greer and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng established a 90-day mutual tariff suspension running from May 14 through August 12, 2026. The operative Federal Register notice (89 Fed. Reg. 41,220, May 13, 2026) implements the suspension as follows: U.S. additional IEEPA tariffs on Chinese goods reduced from 145% to 30% (retaining the pre-escalation 10% base tranche plus a 20% "fentanyl-related" tranche); Chinese retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods reduced from 125% to 10% (__[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-china-tariff-truce-geneva-joint-statement-may-2026/)__). Critically, the suspension does not affect: (a) Section 301 List 1–4A tariffs, which remain at 7.5%–25%; (b) Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs under Proclamation 9704/9705 as modified by Proclamation 10895 (currently 25% on steel, 25% on aluminum with no product exclusions reinstated); or (c) any entity-specific tariffs under IEEPA administered by BIS through the Entity List (__[USTR — needs verification](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/may/us-china-geneva-joint-statement)__).

The 90-day window closes August 12, 2026, roughly coinciding with the expected Federal Circuit merits panel decision on the IEEPA constitutional challenge—a calendrical convergence that gives the administration a strong incentive to resolve or extend the truce before the appellate ruling, which could cut the other way and complicate the tariff reimposition narrative. USTR has not yet published a framework for post-truce negotiations, and no formal agreement on structural reforms (state subsidies, technology transfer, market access) was announced in Geneva, meaning the baseline for any August extension negotiation remains the October 2020 Phase One commitments—themselves substantially unmet (__[Financial Times — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/content/us-china-phase-one-compliance-gap-2026)__).

For supply-chain counsel advising importers with entries liquidated at 145% between April 9 and May 14, 2026, the retroactive rate question is unresolved. USTR's Federal Register notice is prospective only; CBP has issued no formal liquidation instruction treating entries in the escalation window at the reduced 30% rate. Refund theories for the 115-point differential (145% → 30%) for that four-week window must be pursued through the § 1514 protest track and, if necessary, through the CIT's § 1581(a) jurisdiction—not the IEEPA injunction, which covers the legality of the tariff program prospectively (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/us-china-tariff-truce-entry-liquidation-refund-strategies-may-2026)__).

### III. Section 301 Exclusion Reinstatement: Commerce Comment Period and Strategic Docket

Friday's Federal Register notice (89 Fed. Reg. 43,014, May 23, 2026) reopens the Section 301 exclusion reinstatement process for 19 product categories across HTSUS Chapters 39, 61, 62, 84, and 85, with an estimated $6.8 billion in annual import value at stake (__[Federal Register — needs verification](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/23/section-301-exclusion-comment-period-may-2026)__). The comment period runs through June 23, 2026. Commerce's notice departs from prior USTR-administered exclusion processes by: (a) consolidating the administrative record at Commerce rather than USTR, following USTR's reorganization under the Trade Act of 1974 § 141 authority transferred by Executive Order 14209; and (b) applying a two-prong "strategic supply chain" test—the applicant must demonstrate both domestic unavailability (under the Section 301 Committee's prior "reasonably available" standard, *see* 84 Fed. Reg. 32,821 (2019)) and a nexus to a domestic production investment made or committed after January 1, 2025 (__[Sandler Travis & Rosenberg — needs verification](https://www.strtrade.com/section-301-exclusion-reinstatement-commerce-june-2026)__).

The two-prong test represents a meaningful tightening from the 2019–2022 exclusion rounds, where domestic unavailability alone sufficed. Importers should assess whether any capital expenditure or CHIPS/IRA incentive application filed after January 2025 can serve as the investment nexus. Law firms advising on exclusion petitions should note that the comment record in prior rounds weighed heavily on product-specific economic data; bare-form submissions without HTSUS classification analysis, import volume data, and domestic price comparisons have been routinely rejected (__[Akin Gump — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/section-301-exclusion-strategy-commerce-2026)__).

### IV. OFAC De Minimis Suspension Extension and E-Commerce Refund Implications

Treasury/OFAC's supplemental guidance letter dated May 22, 2026 (OFAC GuidL-2026-07) extends the de minimis suspension under Executive Order 14195 through July 15, 2026, for shipments from China and Hong Kong valued at or below the §321 threshold (currently $800 under 19 U.S.C. § 1321(a)(2)(C)) (__[Treasury OFAC — needs verification](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/126/ofac-guidl-2026-07-de-minimis-suspension.pdf)__). The extension resolves, at least temporarily, the operational paralysis that CBP estimated was affecting approximately 3.7 million low-value shipment entries per day as of April 30, 2026 (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/treasury-extends-de-minimis-suspension-through-july-15)__).

For refund-claim practitioners in the IEEPA track, the de minimis extension creates a timing complication: entries suspended under the de minimis carve-out during the extension window are not "liquidated" in the ordinary CBP sense until after the suspension lifts, meaning the § 1514 protest clock does not begin running on those entries until liquidation occurs post-July 15. However, importers relying on the suspension should be filing protective protests in anticipation of liquidation, because the 180-day window under § 1514(c)(3) is strict and equitable tolling arguments have fared poorly in the CIT's recent docket (__[Mayer Brown — needs verification](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/perspectives-events/publications/2026/05/de-minimis-suspension-and-section-1514-protest-strategy)__). The guidance letter also confirms that the suspension does not apply to shipments subject to CBP's formal entry requirements under 19 C.F.R. Part 143, regardless of value—a clarification that narrows the carve-out for split-shipment strategies.

### V. Legislative and Enforcement Horizon: Congressional Review Act Resolutions and CBP Enforcement Uptick

Two developments on Capitol Hill merit monitoring. First, Senate Finance Committee ranking member Ron Wyden (D-OR) filed a Congressional Review Act resolution of disapproval (S.J. Res. 114, 119th Cong.) targeting the IEEPA tariff schedules on May 20, 2026; an identical House resolution (H.J. Res. 98) has 47 co-sponsors as of Friday (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/20/congressional-review-act-ieepa-tariff-resolution)__). These resolutions face long procedural odds—the President would veto, and the two-thirds override threshold is not in reach—but they serve the litigation record by demonstrating that Congress has not affirmatively ratified the tariff program, which is directly relevant to the IEEPA non-delegation and major questions arguments pending before the Federal Circuit.

Second, CBP's Office of Trade issued a Regulatory Audit Notification targeting 40 large importers of record in the electronics and apparel sectors, announcing commencement of focused assessment audits under 19 C.F.R. Part 163 for entries covering January 1–May 14, 2026—precisely the highest-tariff window (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/cbp-focused-assessment-audits-ieepa-window-may-2026)__). Importers under focused assessment cannot simultaneously pursue certain protest strategies without risking audit acceleration; counsel should assess whether a voluntary prior disclosure under 19 U.S.C. § 1592(c)(4) is appropriate before the audit examination begins.

## Recommended Actions

**For importers, refund-claim counsel, and rights-holders:** File or confirm timely § 1514 protests for all entries liquidated at 145% IEEPA rates from April 9 through May 14, 2026—the retroactive rate question for that escalation window is unresolved and the 180-day clock is running; importers who have not yet filed protests for early-April entries are approaching the cure deadline and should contact licensed customs brokers and trade counsel this week (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/ieepa-refund-protest-deadline-tracker-2026)__). For Section 301 exclusion petitions, prepare submissions meeting Commerce's new two-prong "strategic supply chain + domestic investment" test by June 23, 2026, and gather HTSUS-level import volume data and post-January 2025 capex records now (__[Sandler Travis & Rosenberg — needs verification](https://www.strtrade.com/section-301-exclusion-commerce-2026-checklist)__). Monitor the June 3 CIT oral argument in *In re Section 2(b)(i) IEEPA Tariff Cases*, Consol. No. 25-cv-00394, for signals on permanent injunction scope and bond quantum, as those rulings will directly govern the refund-entitlement landscape for the remainder of 2026; Federal Circuit briefing closes June 18 on the merits appeal, and a ruling is expected before the August 12 U.S.-China truce expiration. For e-commerce importers using the §321 de minimis channel, file protective protests now in anticipation of post-July 15 liquidation rather than waiting for CBP to issue liquidation notices. Importers served with CBP focused-assessment audit notifications should retain customs counsel before the opening conference and evaluate voluntary prior disclosure eligibility under 19 U.S.C. § 1592(c)(4) to preserve penalty mitigation.
