# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 28, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today marks a critical procedural checkpoint in the IEEPA refund process as the Court of International Trade requires CBP to file its first progress report on CAPE Phase 1 operations by noon Eastern, followed by a closed settlement conference at 2:00 p.m. in *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. U.S. Customs and Border Protection*. The filing arrives one week after CAPE went live in ACE on April 20, and the court will be assessing whether the system is processing the approximately $127 billion in Phase 1-eligible refunds — representing roughly 82% of the $166 billion in total IEEPA duties collected from over 330,000 importers across 53 million entries — at a pace consistent with the 60-to-90-day disbursement timeline CBP committed to. Early reports from broker desks and trade forums indicate that CAPE is functioning more effectively than many anticipated, though the first 72 hours produced portal congestion, duplicate-tax-ID errors tied to ACE backend account conflicts, and rejection cascades for filers who submitted declarations without sufficiently clean entry data. ([CBP IEEPA Duty Refunds](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)) ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/ieepa-refund-cape-portal))

Simultaneously, the administration's effort to construct replacement tariff authority is accelerating on multiple fronts. USTR begins a two-day public hearing today on Section 301 investigations into forced labor enforcement failures across 60 economies, with a parallel hearing on manufacturing overcapacity in 16 economies — including China, the EU, Japan, India, Mexico, and Vietnam — scheduled for May 5. USTR Greer has indicated these investigations will proceed on an "accelerated timeline" targeting action by July 24, 2026 — the same date on which the 10% Section 122 replacement tariffs reach their 150-day statutory maximum and automatically expire absent congressional extension. That convergence is not coincidental: the administration appears to be engineering a seamless handoff from Section 122 authority to Section 301 authority, ensuring continuous tariff coverage even as two lawsuits challenging Section 122 — one brought by 24 state attorneys general led by Oregon, Arizona, California, and New York, and another by the Liberty Justice Center on behalf of Burlap & Barrel and Basic Fun — remain pending at the CIT following oral arguments on April 10. ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/ustr-launches-awaited-section-301-investigations)) ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_section_301_investigations_ieepa_tariff_refund_developments_legal_challenges_section_0426.html))

The tariff landscape continues to expand beyond IEEPA and Section 122. On April 2, the President issued proclamations under Section 232 dramatically restructuring metals tariffs — imposing 50% duties on articles made substantially of steel, aluminum, or copper and 25% on derivative articles, effective April 6 — and, in a novel extension of Section 232 authority, imposing 100% tariffs on patented pharmaceuticals and associated active pharmaceutical ingredients. The pharmaceutical tariffs take effect July 31 for 17 named major drug companies and September 29 for all remaining companies, with reduced rates for strategic partners: 15% for the EU, Japan, South Korea, and Switzerland, and 10% for the United Kingdom with a pathway to zero contingent on a bilateral pharmaceutical pricing agreement. As Lawfare has noted, this expansion of Section 232 well beyond traditional national-security commodities into pharmaceuticals tests the statute's boundaries in ways that may invite further judicial scrutiny. ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/trump-s-new-tariffs-expand-the-boundaries-of-section-232)) ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/d2cd8123/section-232-spotlight-overhauled-metals-tariffs-and-new-pharmaceutical-tariffs))

On the consumer litigation front, the downstream consequences of IEEPA refunds are materializing. At least five putative consumer class actions have been filed in federal district courts across Florida, Georgia, South Carolina, and Tennessee, with claims against Lululemon (alleging approximately $240 million in passed-through tariff costs), Costco, FedEx, UPS, EssilorLuxottica, and Fabletics. Plaintiffs contend that companies which raised consumer prices to absorb IEEPA tariffs are unjustly enriched if they retain government refunds without passing savings through to end customers. These suits are expected to take years to resolve, but they signal that importers filing CAPE declarations face a two-front dynamic: pursuing refunds from CBP while simultaneously defending against restitution claims from consumers who bore the economic burden. ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/))

Internationally, the broader trade environment remains volatile as bilateral negotiations stall and new fronts open. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney told the CBC on April 27 that tariff-relief deals other countries have signed with the United States "weren't really worth the paper they were written on," though he signaled that a resolution between Canada and the U.S. could come within "days" if Washington showed the inclination. President Trump is scheduled to arrive in Beijing on May 14 for a summit with President Xi, with tariff truce extension, fentanyl-related tariff adjustments, and a potential new trade framework on the agenda — though market pricing continues to reflect substantial uncertainty about whether these discussions will produce actionable outcomes before the November 10 expiration of the existing tariff reduction agreement. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/some-us-tariff-relief-deals-are-worthless-canada-s-carney-tells-cbc)) ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/04/27/trump-xi-summit-us-china-trade-deal-taiwan-geopolitics/))

## Recommended Actions

Companies with unliquidated IEEPA entries or entries within 80 days of liquidation should prioritize filing CAPE declarations immediately, ensuring entry data is reconciled against original ACE records before submission to avoid the manual-review routing that has plagued early filers with even minor variances in HTS classification, entered value, or duty calculations. Importers whose entries have already liquidated beyond the 80-day window should monitor CBP guidance on Phase 2 of CAPE, which is expected to address the remaining 18% of eligible refunds through a process that may require protest filings under 19 U.S.C. §1514 or individual CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i). Companies that passed IEEPA tariff costs through to consumers via price increases should consult litigation counsel now to assess exposure to the growing wave of consumer class actions, documenting their pricing decisions, cost-allocation methodology, and any refund pass-through commitments before discovery demands arrive. Given the July 24 convergence of Section 122 expiration and expected Section 301 action, compliance teams should be modeling scenarios in which the current 10% baseline rate is replaced by product-specific and country-specific rates under Section 301, and importers of patented pharmaceuticals should be preparing for the July 31 effective date of 100% Section 232 duties by evaluating supply-chain diversification, strategic-partner rate eligibility, and inventory positioning.
