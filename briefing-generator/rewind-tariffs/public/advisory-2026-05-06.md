# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | May 6, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The IEEPA refund timeline reached its most concrete milestone yet this week, with CBP confirming that the first tranche of actual ACH refund payments will begin processing on May 12 — six days from today and less than three weeks after the CAPE portal's April 20 launch. The pace is notable: as of CBP's April 28 declaration to the CIT, 75,300 CAPE declarations covering 11.2 million entries had been submitted, with 1.74 million entries clearing all validations and entering the refund pipeline. Judge Eaton's most recent status conference confirmed that roughly 21% of submitted entries have been accepted, and approximately 3% have progressed to the refund stage, placing the first disbursements squarely on the May 12 target. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-refunds-ieepa-timeline-may-2026/)) Fortune's May 5 guide for importers confirms that Phase 1 eligibility remains limited to unliquidated entries and entries within the 90-day voluntary reliquidation window, with CBP projecting 60–90 days from CAPE acceptance to payment for validated claims. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/how-can-my-company-get-a-tariff-refund-ieepa/)) The $166 billion total at stake makes this the largest single refund operation in the history of U.S. customs administration, and the question now shifts from whether payments will flow to how quickly the remaining 97% of pipeline entries can be validated and processed.

The Section 301 public hearings opened yesterday at the International Trade Commission, with USTR soliciting testimony on structural manufacturing overcapacity across 16 economies including China, the EU, Japan, India, and Mexico. The hearings, running through May 8, represent the formal acceleration of the administration's tariff replacement strategy — the legal architecture being constructed to impose permanent, uncapped tariffs before the 10% Section 122 global surcharge expires on July 24. ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/may/public-hearings-regarding-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity)) US News reported yesterday that industry groups are deeply split, with domestic steel and aluminum producers urging aggressive action while retail and manufacturing importers warn that new Section 301 tariffs could replicate or exceed the cost burden the Supreme Court just eliminated. ([US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-05-05/us-industries-trade-groups-split-over-trumps-tariff-probe-on-excess-factory-capacity)) Ambassador Greer has committed to an "accelerated timeline" for determinations, a posture that becomes procedurally significant in context: unlike Section 122, Section 301 tariffs carry no rate ceiling and no sunset provision, meaning importers could face a permanent replacement regime with rates exceeding the 10% floor they are currently paying. ([Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/march/trade-policy-plan-b-goodbye-ieepa-tariffs-hello-section-301-investigations))

The Trump-Xi Beijing summit on May 14 adds a geopolitical overlay to the Section 301 timeline, with the visit — the first by a U.S. president to China in nearly a decade — arriving as both countries emerge from tariff escalation that pushed bilateral rates above 100% before the Busan truce. Senior trade officials held a sixth round of talks in Paris last month, and the administration has signaled that China-specific tariff outcomes could be carved out of the broader Section 301 process if the summit produces bilateral commitments on fentanyl enforcement, rare earth exports, and agricultural procurement. ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/new-atlanticist/experts-react/experts-react-what-does-the-trump-xi-meeting-mean-for-trade-technology-security-and-beyond/)) ([Time](https://time.com/7329777/trump-xi-meeting-korea-us-china-trade-deal-tariffs-takeaways/)) For importers, the practical consequence is that the tariff landscape for Chinese goods remains bifurcated: IEEPA refunds flow through CAPE regardless of the bilateral relationship, but the replacement tariff regime — whether Section 301, a negotiated bilateral framework, or some combination — could impose new duties on the same product categories within months.

The consumer pass-through litigation front continues to expand as the May 12 refund date approaches, with Marketplace reporting Sunday that several major retailers have now publicly positioned themselves on the pass-through question. FedEx and Cards Against Humanity have pledged to return refunds to customers; Costco's CEO stated the company will aim to return refunds through "lower prices and better values." ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/05/04/tariff-refunds-are-coming-will-consumers-see-a-slice)) These voluntary commitments exist against a backdrop of at least a dozen consumer class actions — including the $10.2 billion Walmart suit and actions against Costco, Nintendo, Lululemon, and Temu — testing the "double recovery" theory under unjust enrichment and state unfair practices statutes. Morgan Lewis and Troutman Pepper have both published analyses warning that the first refund disbursements on May 12 will trigger a new wave of filings, as plaintiffs' firms will argue that companies have now received government money for costs already passed to consumers. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) The Section 122 challenge litigation — with 24 state attorneys general and the Liberty Justice Center pressing separate constitutional arguments at the CIT — further complicates the forward tariff picture, as a successful challenge could create a second refund cycle for the replacement tariffs themselves.

## Recommended Actions

Companies expecting CAPE refund disbursements beginning May 12 should verify that their ACE-linked bank account information is current and that their customs broker has confirmed acceptance status for all submitted declarations, as CBP has indicated that payment routing errors will require manual resolution and could delay individual refunds by weeks. Importers who have not yet filed CAPE declarations for unliquidated entries should treat the next 30 days as a shrinking window — the 90-day voluntary reliquidation period continues to expire on a rolling basis for the earliest IEEPA entries, and entries that pass this threshold will require either a formal protest under 19 U.S.C. §1514 (within 180 days of liquidation) or an individual CIT action under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i), both of which carry higher costs and longer timelines. Companies with IEEPA refund exposure above $5 million should engage litigation counsel to evaluate consumer pass-through liability before the first payments land on May 12, and should assess whether voluntary consumer rebate programs or pricing adjustments could preempt class action exposure in the jurisdictions where suits have been concentrated. The Section 301 hearings running through May 8 at the ITC warrant monitoring by any company importing from the 16 targeted economies, as the accelerated timeline means preliminary determinations could arrive before the end of Q2 — companies should begin modeling the impact of potential Section 301 tariffs on current sourcing arrangements and consider whether geographic diversification or tariff engineering strategies are viable before replacement duties take effect.
