# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | May 7, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The IEEPA tariff refund process crossed a decisive threshold this week as CBP confirmed through court filings that the first tranche of ACH refund payments will begin processing around May 11, marking the first time actual dollars will flow back to importers since the Supreme Court struck down the tariffs on February 20. As of CBP's most recent reporting, approximately 75,300 CAPE declarations covering more than 11.2 million individual entries have been submitted since the portal opened on April 20, and roughly 1.74 million of those entries have cleared all validations and are queued for Treasury disbursement. The agency has simultaneously released new Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) reporting tools — including an Entry Summary Report for tracking refund status and amounts, an ACH Rejected Refunds Report flagging incomplete enrollment, and a CAPE Details Report for declarations already forwarded to Treasury — all designed to give importers real-time visibility into the pipeline. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-refunds-ieepa-timeline-may-2026/)) ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2026/05/ace-reports-now-available-support-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds))

The filing rate remains a critical concern: CBP reported that only about 21% of eligible Phase 1 claims had been submitted as of April 28, meaning the vast majority of the approximately 53 million entries subject to IEEPA tariffs — of which roughly 63% are eligible for Phase 1 under the unliquidated or within-80-days-of-liquidation criteria — remain unclaimed. Fortune's comprehensive filing guide published May 5 underscored that only importers of record or their licensed customs brokers can submit CAPE declarations, and that ACH refund enrollment is a prerequisite without which no payment can move, creating a bottleneck for companies that have not yet configured their ACE Portal accounts. The 60-to-90-day payment timeline following acceptance may prove optimistic given volume pressures, and importers who delay filing risk having entries liquidate out of Phase 1 eligibility before their declarations are submitted. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/how-can-my-company-get-a-tariff-refund-ieepa/))

On the litigation front, the consumer pass-through class action docket continues to expand rapidly, with putative nationwide actions now targeting at least a dozen major retailers and importers. The foundational legal theory — that companies which passed IEEPA tariff costs through to consumers via higher prices and now recover those tariffs from the government are unjustly enriched — gained additional attention after a March 27 class action against Lululemon USA Inc. in the Eastern District of Michigan alleged approximately $240 million in passed-through tariff costs. Troutman Pepper has flagged this as an emerging exposure category that could materially affect the net recovery calculus for companies receiving CAPE refunds, particularly those in consumer-facing sectors where pricing transparency makes the pass-through demonstrable. The litigation risk creates a strategic tension: importers need to file promptly to secure their refunds within the administrative window, but the refunds themselves may become evidence in the growing class action docket. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ieepa-tariff-refund-process-sparks-consumer-class-actions))

The administration's tariff replacement architecture continues to take shape with urgency as the July 24 Section 122 sunset approaches. The 10% global tariff imposed under Section 122 on February 24 expires after 150 days, and the President cannot extend it unilaterally — only Congress can authorize an extension, and bipartisan appetite remains uncertain given the Reclaim Trade Powers Act moving in the opposite direction. USTR initiated Section 301 investigations on March 11 targeting 16 economies for excess manufacturing capacity and over 60 economies for forced-labor enforcement, and Ambassador Greer has signaled intent to conclude these investigations on an accelerated timeline before the Section 122 expiration. Unlike Section 122, Section 301 carries no statutory rate cap and no time limit, making it the administration's preferred vehicle for a durable replacement tariff regime. The rescheduled Trump-Xi summit, now expected in mid-May, could further complicate the Section 301 trajectory if bilateral commitments carve China out of the broader investigation framework. ([Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/trade-policy-shifts-ieepa-tariffs-end-section-122-begins-and-sections-301-and-232-activity-grows)) ([Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/march/trade-policy-plan-b-goodbye-ieepa-tariffs-hello-section-301-investigations))

The government appeal window on the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling closes in approximately early June, and as of today there has been no filing, no motion for extension of time, and no public signaling of intent from the administration. Market pricing for IEEPA tariff refund claims continues to reflect residual appeal risk and the potential application of tariff setoffs, but the absence of any procedural activity in the appeal window — combined with the substantial institutional investment in building and staffing the CAPE refund system — increasingly suggests that the administration has accepted the ruling as final. ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/court-of-international-trade-orders-nationwide-tariff-refunds))

## Recommended Actions

Companies with IEEPA exposure should treat the next two weeks as a critical filing window: importers who have not yet submitted CAPE declarations should verify their ACE Portal accounts are active, confirm ACH refund enrollment is complete, and compile entry documentation for all shipments between April 5, 2025 and February 24, 2026 that paid IEEPA duty — the new ACE reporting tools announced this week provide entry-level visibility that should accelerate data compilation. Companies in consumer-facing sectors receiving or expecting refunds should engage counsel immediately on pass-through disclosure obligations, as the expanding class action docket makes preemptive documentation of pricing decisions and cost allocation methodology a prudent defensive measure under the unjust enrichment theories being advanced. Importers with entries approaching the 80-day post-liquidation boundary should prioritize those filings to preserve Phase 1 eligibility, and all filers should monitor the ACH Rejected Refunds Report in ACE to ensure declarations are not stalled by enrollment deficiencies. Companies modeling forward tariff exposure should plan for the Section 122 sunset on July 24 and the likely imposition of Section 301 tariffs without a rate cap, building scenario analysis around the USTR's accelerated investigation timeline and the potential outcomes of the mid-May Trump-Xi summit.
