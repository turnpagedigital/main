# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | May 13, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The IEEPA tariff refund program crossed a critical operational milestone this week as CBP reported that $35.5 billion in refunds, including statutory interest, have been finalized as of May 11, covering more than 15.1 million entries across 86,874 validated applications out of 126,237 total submissions received through the CAPE portal. Businesses are now receiving deposits directly — Flexport's clients alone have collected $137 million, and small importers are reporting individual refunds arriving within weeks of acceptance — confirming that the government's processing infrastructure, while imperfect, is functioning at a pace that few anticipated when the portal launched on April 20. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-ieepa-tariff-refunds-businesses/)) ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-05-12/u-s-anticipates-tariff-refund-payments-of-35-5-billion-as-of-may-11)) The remaining gap between the $35.5 billion processed and the estimated $166 billion in total IEEPA duties owed underscores the scale of work ahead, particularly as Phase 2 — covering fully liquidated entries beyond the 80-day reliquidation window, reconciliation entries, drawback entries, and entries subject to open protests — has yet to receive a launch timeline from CBP.

Meanwhile, the administration's fallback tariff architecture suffered a significant judicial setback and then secured a procedural lifeline in rapid succession. On May 7, a divided CIT panel held that President Trump's 10 percent universal tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 exceeded the statutory authority Congress had delegated, finding that the administration failed to demonstrate the balance-of-payments deficit that Section 122 requires as a triggering condition. The injunction, however, applied only to the named plaintiffs — Burlap & Barrel, Basic Fun, and the State of Washington — leaving CBP to continue collecting Section 122 duties from the roughly 170,000 other importers who have paid deposits on an estimated 13 million entries since February 24. ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/us-court-of-international-trade-invalidates-the-administrations)) ([Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/more-tariff-refunds-not-so-fast-court-of-international-trade-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs-but-relief-is-limited-to-plaintiffs/)) The DOJ filed its appeal to the Federal Circuit on May 8, moved for a stay on May 11, and by today, May 13, the Federal Circuit issued a temporary administrative stay pausing enforcement of the CIT's order while it sets a briefing schedule on the government's full stay motion — meaning the 10 percent tariffs remain in effect for all importers during the pendency of the appeal. ([WWD/Sourcing Journal](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/trade/court-international-trade-cit-section-122-tariffs-stay-injunction-1238950938/)) ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/us-federal-court-halts-ruling-against-trump-s-global-tariffs-for-now-126051300083_1.html))

The Section 122 litigation now runs on a parallel track to the broader tariff replacement strategy. With the Section 122 tariffs set to expire on July 24 absent extension, and the administration actively pursuing Section 301 investigations into Chinese industrial overcapacity and forced labor practices, the coming weeks will determine whether the government attempts to layer new tariff authorities before the universal baseline sunsets. The Trump-Xi summit in Beijing this week — where the two leaders are expected to discuss a managed-trade "Board of Trade" mechanism and possible tariff reductions on approximately $30 billion in goods — adds a diplomatic variable that could reshape the Section 301 calculus significantly. ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-05-13/trump-xi-to-weigh-tariff-cuts-on-30-billion-of-imports-in-managed-trade-push)) ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/as-the-trump-xi-summit-draws-closer-trade-uncertainty-still-looms-large/)) Trade counsel at Diaz Trade Law noted in a May 12 analysis that importers who paid Section 122 duties should begin considering whether to file protests under 19 U.S.C. §1514 to preserve refund rights if the Federal Circuit ultimately affirms the CIT's ruling, since the narrow injunction means non-plaintiff importers currently lack an automatic path to recovery. ([Customs & International Trade Law Blog](https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/05/12/title-another-blow-to-the-u-s-tariff-war-can-importers-obtain-refunds-after-section-122-tariffs-were-invalidated/))

Industry-specific guidance continues to proliferate as the refund process matures. AdvaMed published a detailed webinar summary on May 11 walking MedTech importers through CAPE mechanics, emphasizing that Phase 1 captures roughly 60 percent of affected entries, that refunds are consolidated by importer of record and liquidation date rather than processed entry-by-entry, and that statutory interest accrues at approximately 6 percent from the date duties were paid through liquidation. ([AdvaMed](https://www.advamed.org/2026/05/11/ieepa-tariff-refunds-webinar-summary/)) Sidley Austin's April analysis on refund claims from the perspective of lenders, borrowers, and claims purchasers remains relevant as the secondary market for IEEPA refund claims continues to develop, with funders pricing appeal risk, tariff setoff exposure, and Phase 2 timing uncertainty into their recovery models. ([Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers))

## Recommended Actions

Companies with IEEPA refund claims already submitted through CAPE should monitor their ACE portal accounts for status updates and ensure ACH-enabled bank accounts are current, as CBP is now disbursing refunds several days per week on a consolidated basis. Importers who have not yet filed Phase 1 declarations should prioritize submissions of clean, unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within the past 80 days, coordinating closely with customs brokers who may be managing high submission volumes. For Section 122 exposure, importers should consult trade counsel about filing protective protests under 19 U.S.C. §1514 to preserve refund rights in the event the Federal Circuit affirms the CIT's invalidation — the 180-day protest window runs from the date of liquidation, and entries liquidating now without a timely protest may forfeit recovery rights if the tariffs are ultimately struck down on appeal. Companies with complex entries excluded from Phase 1 — including reconciliation entries, drawback entries, and antidumping or countervailing duty entries — should begin compiling entry documentation and monitoring CBP communications for Phase 2 guidance. Finally, importers sourcing from China should watch the Trump-Xi summit outcomes closely, as any agreement on the Board of Trade mechanism or Section 301 scope could materially alter the tariff landscape for the second half of 2026.
