# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 24, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Four days into the CAPE portal's operation, the most telling indicator of systemic confidence is not the volume of declarations filed but the speed at which institutional actors have committed to the process. UPS, FedEx, and DHL have all begun submitting IEEPA tariff refund declarations on behalf of customers where they served as importers of record or customs brokers, a development that represents a meaningful institutional endorsement of the refund mechanism's viability. CBP reported that on the portal's first day alone, over 55,000 parties submitted claims covering more than 4 million import entries, and initial processing data suggests the system is functioning more effectively than many anticipated. That said, the "Unable to calculate duty" error — triggered when even minor variances exist between a CAPE declaration and original entry data in HTS classification, entered value, quantity, or duty calculation — is routing a material number of submissions to manual CBP review, extending processing timelines beyond the stated 60–90 day window for affected filers. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tariff-refund-portal-system-trump-cape-cbp/)) ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-cape-system-and-processing-guidance))

The procedural landscape at the Court of International Trade continues to evolve following the April 6 dismissal of Atmus Filtration, Inc. v. United States, the original lead case selected by Judge Eaton on March 4. Before granting the Atmus dismissal, the CIT designated Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. CBP et al., No. 25-00595, as the new lead case, with Judge Eaton reissuing the refund implementation orders under the Euro-Notions docket. Critically, this reset the government's appeal clock: the deadline for the administration to appeal the CIT's refund orders is now June 8, 2026. The Supreme Court rehearing petition deadline of approximately May 21 also remains live, though neither the Department of Justice nor USTR has filed any motion, extension request, or public statement indicating intent to appeal — a silence that, at 63 days from the February 20 decision, is becoming increasingly difficult to interpret as anything other than acquiescence. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/new-cit-ieepa-tariff-refund-case-selected-after-dismissal-of-lead-case/)) ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/04/insights-april-2026/tariff-refund-mechanism-takes-shape))

The administration's tariff replacement strategy is entering a critical compression window. Section 122's 10% global surcharge, imposed February 24 as a bridge measure following the IEEPA invalidation, expires on July 24 — 91 days from today — and the President lacks unilateral authority to extend it under 19 U.S.C. §1322. No Congressional extension bill has been introduced, and the bipartisan Reclaim Trade Powers Act moves in the opposite direction, seeking to constrain rather than expand presidential tariff authority. USTR's dual Section 301 investigations — forced labor enforcement targeting 60+ economies and manufacturing overcapacity targeting 16 economies — have public hearings beginning April 28 and May 5, respectively, with the administration telegraphing an accelerated timeline to have replacement tariffs in place before the Section 122 sunset. Whether USTR can complete the notice-and-comment process, publish findings, and impose duties within 91 days remains the central question for importers planning around the tariff regime transition. ([FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/section-122-tariff-expiration-countdown-july-2026)) ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2026/insights/the-trump-administration-new-tariff-road-map))

Consumer pass-through litigation is accelerating in parallel with the refund process. Plaintiffs' firms have filed class actions against Temu, Shein, Costco, Lululemon, EssilorLuxottica, UPS, and FedEx alleging unjust enrichment and violations of state consumer protection statutes under theories that companies who passed IEEPA tariff costs to consumers through higher prices cannot retain both the price increases and government refunds. The Temu complaint alleges price increases of up to 377% on certain product categories during the tariff period, and the UPS and FedEx actions target itemized brokerage and clearance fees that plaintiffs contend would never have been charged absent the unlawful tariffs. As NPR reported April 22, consumers who paid higher prices will not receive direct refunds through CAPE — they must rely on voluntary corporate pass-through or litigation to recover. ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/22/nx-s1-5791663/the-tariff-refund-customers))

## Recommended Actions

Companies with pending CAPE declarations should prioritize reconciling their entry data against CBP's records before submission, paying particular attention to HTS classification accuracy, entered values, and duty calculations, as even minor discrepancies will divert claims to manual review and extend the 60–90 day processing timeline. Importers who have not yet registered for ACH disbursement — a prerequisite for receiving refunds — should complete enrollment immediately through the ACE portal, as 56,497 importers representing $127 billion in eligible refunds had completed this step as of April 14. Companies should attend the CBP CAPE support webinar on April 28 at 1:00 PM ET for guidance on declaration formatting and common error resolution. For importers with liquidated entries outside Phase 1's 80-day window, filing protests under 19 U.S.C. §1514 remains the parallel recovery pathway and should not be deferred pending future CAPE phases. Legal counsel should also be evaluating consumer class action exposure: any company that raised prices attributable to IEEPA tariffs and is simultaneously seeking CAPE refunds should assess unjust enrichment risk and consider whether voluntary consumer pass-through programs may mitigate litigation exposure. Companies monitoring the tariff regime transition should calendar the July 24 Section 122 expiration and the USTR Section 301 hearing dates to assess whether replacement tariffs will materially change their landed cost calculations.
