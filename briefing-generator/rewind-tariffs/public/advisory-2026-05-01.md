# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | May 1, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The CAPE refund machine is approaching its most significant milestone yet: CBP confirmed in an April 30 court filing that the first actual refund payments are expected to begin flowing on or around May 11, barely three weeks after the portal's April 20 launch. As of Sunday evening, importers and brokers had submitted more than 75,000 refund claims, of which 47,000 declarations covering 11.22 million individual entries had cleared validation. Approximately 1.74 million entries have now completed the full liquidation recalculation pipeline and are queued for disbursement — a 3% throughput rate that, while modest in absolute terms, represents meaningful institutional progress for a system processing the largest mass refund in U.S. customs history. ([Spectrum News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/business/2026/04/30/tariff-refunds-online-portal-cbp-whiskey-trump-ieepa-section-301-investigations)) The 15% rejection rate identified last week persists, with CBS News reporting that CBP has now rejected approximately 2.124 million entry-level submissions for data-quality failures — incorrect HTS codes, mismatched importer-of-record identifiers, and expired entry numbers. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tariff-refund-portal-trump-cbp/)) Judge Eaton has ordered CBP Executive Director Brandon Lord to file a formal Phase 1 progress report by May 12, which will provide the Court of International Trade with its most comprehensive look yet at processing velocity, rejection categories, and the realistic timeline for clearing the $127 billion in Phase 1-eligible entries.

The logistics carriers have emerged as an unexpectedly constructive force in the refund ecosystem. UPS disclosed that it remitted over $5 billion to the Treasury during the IEEPA tariff period across roughly 16 million entries, and CEO Carol Tomé confirmed the company has already begun filing CAPE declarations — starting with approximately $500 million covering 2.5 million entries where UPS served as the importer of record. ([WWD/Sourcing Journal](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/logistics/ups-5-billion-tariff-refunds-cbp-phase-1-guidance-outlook-fuel-costs-surcharges-iran-war-amazon-volumes-treasury-1238932414/)) FedEx and UPS have both pledged to pass refunds through to their customers — a commitment that Fortune estimates could channel over $5 billion back to small and mid-size businesses that used the carriers as customs intermediaries. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/29/fedex-ups-pledging-tariff-refunds-back-to-customers/)) This pass-through commitment is significant because it partially addresses the structural gap in the CAPE system: only the importer of record can file declarations, leaving millions of downstream businesses dependent on their customs brokers and carriers to act on their behalf. Companies that shipped through third-party logistics providers should confirm now whether their carrier has filed or plans to file on their behalf.

The consumer pass-through litigation wave continues to accelerate, with the Walmart class action filed April 27 in the Northern District of Ohio representing a new order of magnitude. The complaint alleges Walmart stands to recover an estimated $10.2 billion in IEEPA refunds after already passing tariff costs to consumers through higher prices — a "double recovery" theory that mirrors suits already pending against Costco, Lululemon, Nintendo, and FedEx. ([Claim Depot](https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/walmart-class-action-alleges-102b-tariff-refund-windfall-at-consumers-expense)) Sidley Austin published a detailed analysis on April 30 examining how these refund claims interact with existing lending arrangements, securitization structures, and the emerging claims-purchasing market, warning that outright purchasers of tariff refund claims face particular risk under the Anti-Assignment Act because the statutory prerequisites — that the claim be "allowed," the amount "decided," and a "warrant for payment" issued — are likely not met for most IEEPA claims at this stage. ([Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers)) The refund financing market is pricing these risks in real time, and the Sidley analysis suggests that buyers may wish to structure transactions as obligations to pursue payment with rights to proceeds rather than outright claim assignments.

The calendar over the next two weeks is dense with consequential events. The Section 301 overcapacity hearing targeting 16 economies convenes May 5, building the procedural foundation for replacement tariffs before the July 24 Section 122 sunset. The May 12 CAPE progress report will set expectations for Phase 1 completion and Phase 2 parameters. The Trump-Xi Beijing summit beginning May 14 — the first U.S. presidential visit to China in nearly a decade — carries the potential to reshape the trade landscape materially, with rare-earth supply, a proposed "Board of Trade" framework, and the November 10 tariff truce expiration all on the table. ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/04/27/trump-xi-summit-us-china-trade-deal-taiwan-geopolitics/)) And the government's appeal deadlines continue to approach without activity: the Supreme Court rehearing window falls around late May to early June, and the CIT refund order appeal deadline in Euro-Notions remains approximately June 8. Continued DOJ silence is increasingly being priced by market participants as confirmation that the administration has accepted the IEEPA ruling and pivoted its tariff strategy entirely toward Section 301, Section 232, and whatever emerges from the Beijing summit.

## Recommended Actions

Companies that have not yet filed CAPE declarations should do so before the May 12 progress report, as the court-supervised timeline is likely to tighten once Judge Eaton reviews processing data and sets Phase 2 parameters. Importers who used FedEx, UPS, or other carriers as their importer of record should contact those carriers directly to confirm refund filing status and expected pass-through timelines, rather than assuming declarations have been submitted. Companies anticipating refunds exceeding $1 million should consult litigation counsel on class action exposure before receiving payments, particularly given the Walmart complaint's $10.2 billion framing and the Sidley Austin analysis highlighting Anti-Assignment Act risks for entities that have sold or pledged refund claims. Compliance teams should map their current supply chains against the 16 economies under Section 301 overcapacity investigation and the 60 economies under forced labor investigation, as accelerated tariff actions under these authorities could take effect before the Section 122 tariffs expire on July 24. All importers should ensure their ACE Portal bank account information is current and accurate ahead of the May 11 disbursement window.
