# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 20, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today marks the most consequential operational milestone in the IEEPA tariff refund saga since the Supreme Court's February 20 decision in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, 146 S. Ct. 628 (2026): CBP's Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) tool goes live in the Automated Commercial Environment at 8:00 a.m. EST, opening Phase 1 of the refund process to importers of record and licensed customs brokers nationwide. Phase 1 covers unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation, which CBP projects account for approximately 63% of the estimated $166–175 billion in IEEPA duties collected — roughly $104–110 billion in initial refund eligibility. ([CBP IEEPA Duty Refunds](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)) ([CSMS # 68340863](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f)) Importers and brokers will file CAPE Declarations via CSV upload through the ACE Secure Data Portal, with each declaration limited to 9,999 entries though multiple declarations may be submitted. CBP has indicated that valid refunds will generally issue within 60–90 days following acceptance, absent compliance concerns requiring further review. As of April 9, 56,497 importers and notify parties have registered for electronic refunds, enabling CBP to deliver electronic payments covering approximately 82% of all entries that included IEEPA duty payments — an estimated $127 billion in refund-eligible entries. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-to-file-for-tariff-refund/))

The CAPE launch follows significant procedural developments at the Court of International Trade, where Judge Richard Eaton reissued the court's comprehensive IEEPA refund order after selecting Euro-Notions Florida as the plaintiff in the new lead case. This reissuance became necessary after the initial lead plaintiff filed a voluntary notice of dismissal on April 6, briefly creating uncertainty about the order's enforceability. ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/04/trade-court-broadens-ieepa-refund-order-coverage)) The reissued order reportedly broadened the scope of covered entries, reinforcing the court's authority over the refund process under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i). Companies that have not yet verified their ACE Portal accounts, confirmed their electronic payment enrollment, or reconciled their entry records should treat today's launch as an urgent action item — the 80-day liquidation window for earlier entries is already narrowing, and entries that liquidate before a CAPE Declaration is filed may require individual protest filings under 19 U.S.C. §1514, a substantially more burdensome path to recovery.

The broader tariff policy landscape continues to shift rapidly as the administration constructs replacement authorities ahead of the Section 122 surcharge's July 24 expiration. The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative closed its public comment period on April 15 for sweeping Section 301 investigations targeting structural excess capacity in manufacturing across 16 economies — including China, the EU, Japan, India, and Mexico — and forced labor practices in 59 countries. ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_section_301_investigations_ieepa_tariff_refund_developments_legal_challenges_section_0426.html)) Public hearings at the U.S. International Trade Commission are scheduled to begin April 28 and extend into May, with USTR signaling an "accelerated timeline" designed to deliver new tariff authority before Section 122 expires. Meanwhile, the Section 122 surcharge itself faces two active legal challenges at the CIT, where 24 state attorneys general argued at oral arguments on April 10 that the administration has failed to demonstrate the "fundamental international payments problems" required under the statute. ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/alerts/section-122-tariffs-challenged-in-court-of-international-trade/)) ([Liberty Justice Center](https://libertyjusticecenter.org/tariffs/section122-tariffs/))

Downstream litigation continues to proliferate as plaintiffs' firms recruit consumers for putative class actions targeting companies that passed IEEPA tariff costs through to retail prices. A proposed class action filed March 27 against Lululemon USA Inc. alleges the company passed approximately $240 million in IEEPA tariff costs to consumers while simultaneously pursuing government refunds, raising questions about unjust enrichment and disclosure obligations that will resonate across the retail and manufacturing sectors. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) Companies receiving IEEPA refunds should proactively assess their contractual obligations to distributors and customers, document their pricing methodology during the tariff period, and coordinate refund distribution strategies with legal counsel before pass-through claims crystallize.

## Recommended Actions

Companies with IEEPA duty exposure should treat today's CAPE launch as a day-one filing priority: importers of record and their licensed brokers should log in to the ACE Secure Data Portal beginning at 8:00 a.m. EST, verify their electronic refund enrollment status, and prepare CSV-formatted CAPE Declarations covering all eligible unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation. For entries approaching the liquidation window, companies should simultaneously calendar protest deadlines under 19 U.S.C. §1514 as a backstop in case CAPE processing encounters delays. Importers who have not yet registered for electronic refund payments — approximately 18% of entry-holders based on CBP's April 9 figures — should complete registration immediately through ACE to avoid manual check processing, which CBP has warned will be substantially slower. Companies should also begin compiling documentation for Phase 2 entries (liquidated entries beyond the 80-day window), as CBP has indicated subsequent phases will address the remaining 37% of eligible entries on an accelerated schedule. Finally, given the expanding consumer class action landscape, general counsel and compliance teams should coordinate with trade counsel to establish refund allocation frameworks and document pricing decisions made during the IEEPA tariff period, particularly for companies that incorporated tariff costs into consumer-facing prices.
