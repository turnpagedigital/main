# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 22, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The CAPE portal's second full day of operations is confirming what the first day suggested: the institutional machinery for processing $166 billion in IEEPA tariff refunds is moving with surprising speed. UPS, FedEx, and DHL have all confirmed they are filing CAPE Declarations on behalf of customers for shipments where they served as importer of record or customs broker, a development that effectively brings the three largest express consignment operators into the refund pipeline simultaneously. FedEx has stated it will work "expeditiously" to issue refunds to customers once CBP processes its declarations, while DHL has committed to passing refunds through to the party that originally paid the duties — positions that are establishing what may become the de facto industry standard for downstream distribution of IEEPA recoveries. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ups-fedex-dhl-tariff-refund-consumers/)) Phase 1, limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation (January 30 through April 19, 2026), covers an estimated 63% of all eligible entries. CBP has indicated that 56,497 importers had completed ACH registration as of April 14, representing $127 billion of the $166 billion total, meaning the vast majority of refund dollars are positioned to flow once declarations are processed within the estimated 45- to 90-day window. ([Hinshaw & Culbertson](https://www.hinshawlaw.com/en/insights/hinshaw-alert/qanda-how-to-submit-your-ieepa-refund-claim-as-cape-portal-launches-april-20-2026))

The Trump administration's continued silence on an appeal of the Supreme Court's February 20 ruling in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* is becoming one of the most consequential non-events in recent trade law. As Fortune's analysis noted this week, the government has filed no appeal, no motion for extension of time, and no public statement signaling intent to challenge the decision — even as CBP simultaneously builds out the very refund infrastructure that the ruling compels. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/21/why-didnt-trump-administration-appeal-universal-tariff-refund-court-order-cape-rollout/)) The appeal window extends through early June, and market participants continue to price in residual appeal risk alongside concerns about tariff setoffs and Phase 2 timeline uncertainty. Judge Eaton's next closed conference on April 28 in *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. U.S. Customs and Border Protection* will require CBP to report on CAPE submission volumes, error rates, and processing timelines from the portal's first week — data that will be the first real measure of whether the system is functioning at scale or whether the launch-day CSV formatting errors and duplicate Tax ID rejections signal deeper capacity constraints. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cit-endorses-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-ahead-of-cbps-april-20-rollout/))

The administration's parallel tariff strategy is entering a critical phase on multiple fronts. The replacement Section 122 tariffs — the 10% global surcharge imposed on February 25 — face a statutory 150-day expiration on July 24, 2026, with no unilateral presidential authority to extend and a congressional extension requiring 60 Senate votes that the political landscape does not support. The CIT heard oral arguments on April 10 in the two pending Section 122 challenges, with at least two judges suggesting the government's statutory interpretation "proves too much" by effectively granting unlimited presidential tariff authority under a statute designed for narrow, temporary balance-of-payments measures. ([Reason/Volokh](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/10/thoughts-on-todays-oral-argument-in-the-section-122-tariff-cases/)) Meanwhile, USTR's Section 301 investigations — targeting manufacturing overcapacity across 16 economies and forced labor enforcement failures across 60 countries — move to public hearings beginning April 28, with written comments already closed as of April 15. Baker Botts has characterized the Section 301 pivot as the administration's "Plan B" for building durable tariff authority on firmer legal ground, though the investigation-to-tariff timeline under Section 301 historically spans 12 to 18 months. ([Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/march/trade-policy-plan-b-goodbye-ieepa-tariffs-hello-section-301-investigations))

Consumer-side class action litigation is accelerating in tandem with the refund process, creating an emerging second front of legal exposure for importers. Plaintiff firms have now filed putative class actions against Costco, FedEx, UPS, and EssilorLuxottica, among others, alleging unjust enrichment and consumer fraud on the theory that importers passed IEEPA tariff costs through to consumers and are now positioned to receive government refunds without returning the embedded surcharges. Troutman Pepper's analysis warns that these pass-through theories could gain traction in jurisdictions with broad consumer protection statutes, and that the window for importers to assemble contemporaneous documentation of pricing decisions and cost absorption methodologies is closing as refunds begin flowing. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) FedEx's public commitment to passing refunds through to original duty payers may insulate it from the worst of this exposure, but companies that absorbed tariff costs internally and are now receiving refunds face a different calculus — and should be preparing their evidentiary record now.

## Recommended Actions

Companies that have not yet filed Phase 1 CAPE Declarations should do so immediately through the ACE Portal, ensuring CSV files comply with CBP's formatting specifications and that submissions are limited to 9,999 entries each, as the 80-day post-liquidation window for Phase 1 eligibility is time-limited and early filers will be positioned to receive refunds within the 45-day processing estimate. Importers who rely on customs brokers — particularly those using UPS, FedEx, or DHL — should confirm with their broker whether declarations have been filed on their behalf, as the express consignment operators are filing for customers but the importer of record retains ultimate responsibility for ensuring their entries are captured. All companies anticipating CAPE refunds should prioritize assembling contemporaneous documentation of pricing decisions, tariff cost pass-through calculations, and internal communications regarding surcharges applied to customers, as this evidentiary record will be critical to defending against consumer class action claims under state unjust enrichment and consumer protection statutes. Importers should calendar the April 28 CIT status conference for updated Phase 2 timeline guidance and monitor the Section 301 public hearings beginning the same day for signals on the administration's next tariff moves. Companies with entries falling outside Phase 1 eligibility should begin evaluating whether to pursue individual CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i) or to await Phase 2, a decision that should be informed by the April 28 status report on CBP's processing capacity and the government's posture on appeal.
