# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | May 8, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The Court of International Trade delivered a second major blow to the administration's tariff architecture yesterday when a three-judge panel ruled 2-1 in *Burlap & Barrel, Inc. v. Trump* (Slip Op. 26-47) that the Section 122 bridge tariff — the 10-15% global surcharge imposed within days of the Supreme Court's February 20 IEEPA decision — exceeds presidential authority under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974. The panel found that the executive order "does not identify balance-of-payments deficits within the meaning of Section 122 as it was enacted in 1974," rendering the tariffs "unauthorized by law." The ruling consolidated two challenges: the Liberty Justice Center case brought on behalf of importers Burlap & Barrel and Basic Fun!, and a parallel action filed by 24 state attorneys general led by Oregon. While the injunction currently applies only to the named plaintiffs, the reasoning — that the administration failed to demonstrate the statutory predicate of a "large and serious" balance-of-payments deficit or imminent threat thereof — would logically extend to any importer raising the same challenge. ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/05/07/federal-court-trumps-newest-tariffs-are-also-illegal/)) ([Liberty Justice Center](https://libertyjusticecenter.org/pressrelease/liberty-justice-center-victorious-in-lawsuit-challenging-trumps-section-122-tariffs/))

The Section 122 ruling lands at a particularly consequential moment for the IEEPA refund pipeline. CBP's CAPE system has now processed approximately 75,300 declarations covering over 11.2 million entries since the portal's April 20 launch, with roughly 1.74 million entries — approximately 3% of total entries — having cleared all validations and entered the refund stage. Judge Eaton's earlier order indicated that the first tranche of ACH refund payments should begin reaching importers' bank accounts around May 11, representing the first actual dollars flowing back from the estimated $166 billion in IEEPA duty collections. The government's 60-day window to appeal Judge Eaton's refund order to the Federal Circuit closes on approximately June 7, and the continued absence of any appeal filing, motion for extension, or public signaling of intent to challenge the refund mechanism remains one of the most significant quiet signals in this process. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-refunds-ieepa-timeline-may-2026/)) ([CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds))

The administration's tariff replacement strategy now faces compounding legal vulnerability. The Section 122 tariff was designed as a 150-day bridge — expiring July 23 — while USTR completes Section 301 investigations launched March 11 targeting excess manufacturing capacity across 16 economies and forced-labor enforcement in 60-plus economies. Ambassador Greer had signaled intent to conclude these investigations on an "accelerated timeline" before the Section 122 sunset. Yesterday's CIT ruling, if upheld or expanded, could collapse that bridge sooner than planned, leaving a gap in the tariff regime for non-USMCA, non-Section 232 goods. The administration retains durable authority under Section 232 (steel, aluminum, copper, pharmaceuticals already covered as of April 2) and Section 301 once those investigations conclude, but the sequencing risk is now acute. ([Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/trade-policy-shifts-ieepa-tariffs-end-section-122-begins-and-sections-301-and-232-activity-grows)) ([Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/))

Consumer pass-through litigation continues to accelerate alongside the refund process. At least a dozen putative class actions have been filed since late February alleging that retailers and consumer goods companies — including FedEx, UPS, and EssilorLuxottica — face unjust enrichment claims for recovering IEEPA tariff refunds while retaining the higher prices passed to consumers during the tariff period. The Troutman Pepper analysis published this week identifies the strategic tension between filing CAPE declarations promptly and creating documentary evidence that plaintiffs' firms can cite in double-recovery theories. Sidley Austin's April alert to lenders and claims purchasers underscores that refund claims are being traded, securitized, and factored at rates reflecting not only appeal risk but also the emerging class action exposure, making proper documentation and disclosure practices a risk-management imperative for importers seeking to maximize net recovery. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) ([Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers))

The US-China trade framework remains separately governed by the one-year extension reached in November 2025, which reduced the fentanyl-related tariff to 10% and brought the general China rate to 49% (down from 59%), with China suspending retaliatory tariffs and committing to significant agricultural purchases through 2028. That framework expires November 10, 2026, and negotiations for a more durable arrangement continue. The interaction between IEEPA refund processing for China-origin goods and the separate Section 301 tariffs that remain in effect on those same goods adds complexity for importers managing overlapping duty obligations and refund eligibility. ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-United-States-and-China-Negotiate-One-Year-Trade-Deal)) ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/tax/library/pwc-us-reaches-limited-trade-deal-with-china-signs-trade-agreements-with-asean-partners.html))

## Recommended Actions

Companies that have not yet filed CAPE declarations should do so immediately, particularly those with unliquidated entries or entries within the 90-day voluntary reliquidation window — the first payment tranche expected May 11 demonstrates that the system is functioning and delays reduce priority in the processing queue. Importers should simultaneously calendar the June 7 government appeal deadline and prepare for the possibility that refund certainty increases materially if that deadline passes without action. Companies paying Section 122 duties should consult trade counsel about whether the *Burlap & Barrel* ruling creates grounds for their own challenge or protest filing under 19 U.S.C. §1514, recognizing that the current injunction is limited to named plaintiffs. All importers receiving IEEPA refunds should implement contemporaneous documentation of tariff pass-through practices and pricing adjustments, as the consumer class action landscape makes clear that refund recovery without defensible records of how tariff costs were allocated creates significant litigation exposure. Companies with Section 301 exposure on China-origin goods should monitor USTR's accelerated investigation timeline closely, as the replacement tariff architecture that emerges before the July 23 Section 122 expiration will define the durable duty landscape for the remainder of 2026 and beyond.
