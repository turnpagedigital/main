# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 16, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

With four days remaining before U.S. Customs and Border Protection's April 20, 2026 deployment of Phase 1 of its Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries ("CAPE") functionality, the operational architecture for IEEPA refund recovery is now substantially fixed. CBP has confirmed an 8:00 a.m. EDT launch within the ACE Secure Data Portal, with importers of record and authorized brokers required to upload a CSV-formatted "CAPE Declaration" through a new CAPE tab in ACE to consolidate IEEPA-duty refunds, inclusive of statutory interest, rather than process them on an entry-by-entry basis. Phase 1 coverage is limited to most unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation — a scope CBP estimates will capture roughly 60% to 63% of entries on which IEEPA duties were paid or deposited — with valid refunds projected to issue via ACH within 60 to 90 days after CAPE Declaration acceptance, absent compliance review. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cbp-confirms-april-20-2026-launch-of-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process/)) ([CBP CSMS #68340863](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f))

The Court of International Trade cleared the procedural runway for the rollout. On April 1, 2026, Senior Judge Richard K. Eaton concluded that the government "continues to make satisfactory progress" and "is on track to meet the April 20, 2026 deadline" for Phase 1 implementation. The refund docket itself underwent a material restructuring the following week: on April 6, Atmus Filtration voluntarily dismissed the anchor case, and on April 7, Judge Eaton lifted the stay in Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States (No. 1:25-cv-00595-RKE) and reissued his refund order directing CBP to liquidate or reliquidate, without regard to IEEPA duties, three categories of entries — unliquidated entries, liquidated entries under active protest, and finally liquidated entries — a structure that, on its face, extends relief to non-litigants as well as named plaintiffs. The government's stipulation not to object to refunds on liquidated entries remains subject to likely appeal, and resolution of that question is the single most consequential unknown for importers whose entries lie outside the Phase 1 80-day window. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cit-endorses-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-ahead-of-cbps-april-20-rollout/)) ([International Trade Today](https://internationaltradetoday.com/news/2026/04/07/lead-ieepa-litigant-files-notice-of-dismissal-eaton-reissues-refund-order-in-new-case-2604070011))

The tariff-replacement landscape hardened in parallel. A three-judge panel of the CIT heard consolidated oral argument on April 10 in the dual Section 122 challenges — one filed by the Liberty Justice Center on behalf of two small importers, the other by a 24-state coalition led by the New York Attorney General — with at least one judge openly skeptical of the government's theory that a trade deficit alone satisfies Section 122's "fundamental international payments problems" predicate. The current 10% ad valorem surcharge expires by its own terms at 12:01 a.m. ET on July 24, 2026, and the administration has signaled a transition plan: on April 2, 2026, the President issued proclamations imposing 100% Section 232 duties on patented pharmaceuticals and active pharmaceutical ingredients (tiered effective dates of 120 and 180 days), and modifying Section 232 steel, aluminum, and copper derivative-product treatment. The USTR's parallel Section 301 investigations into structural excess manufacturing capacity across 16 economies and forced-labor enforcement across 60-plus economies closed their written-comment window yesterday, April 15, with public hearings at the USITC commencing April 28 — a compressed calendar engineered to have Section 301 determinations in hand before the Section 122 sunset. ([Reason / Volokh Conspiracy](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/10/thoughts-on-todays-oral-argument-in-the-section-122-tariff-cases/)) ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_section_301_investigations_ieepa_tariff_refund_developments_legal_challenges_section_0426.html)) ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/why-ustrs-latest-section-301-actions-matter-for-your-supply-chain))

The downstream exposure picture has also sharpened. Plaintiffs' firms have begun filing consumer class actions against importers that passed IEEPA-duty costs through to buyers while simultaneously pursuing refund recovery — a pattern typified by the March 27 putative class action against Lululemon USA Inc. alleging roughly $240 million in pass-through and characterizing the combination of price increases and federal reimbursement as a double recovery. Causes of action span restitution (unjust enrichment, money had and received), breach of contract, and state unfair-and-deceptive-trade-practices statutes; because only the importer of record holds standing to seek a CBP refund, the counterparty asymmetry is structural, not optional. Public-company disclosure risk runs in the same direction: refund-anticipation accruals, tariff-cost-recovery representations, and MD&A descriptions of expected pass-through economics now warrant renewed scrutiny in light of pending 10-Q cycles. ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/ieepa-tariff-refund-uncertainty-after-supreme-court-decision-retailers-face-disclosure-and-litigation-risks))

## Recommended Actions

Companies that paid IEEPA duties should, in the next 96 hours, finalize ACE Portal readiness: verify an active Importer sub-account, confirm ACH bank-account information is populated under the Importer account (refunds issue solely by electronic funds transfer), and designate the internal owner or broker counterpart responsible for CAPE Declaration filings. Importers should compile and reconcile, at the entry-summary level, all IEEPA-subject entries with liquidation status flagged, because Phase 1 eligibility turns on whether an entry is unliquidated or within 80 days of liquidation on the filing date. For entries falling outside that window, companies should consider individual CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i) and, where liquidation is imminent, protest filings under 19 U.S.C. §1514 to preserve statutory rights pending later CAPE phases or appellate clarification of non-litigant reach. Importers that passed tariff costs downstream should coordinate with securities and commercial-litigation counsel before filing CAPE Declarations: accruals, disclosure language, and customer-facing communications should be harmonized, and documentation of pricing rationale should be preserved against the class-action fact patterns now emerging. Finally, supply-chain, procurement, and treasury teams should model Section 301 and Section 232 pass-through scenarios for the post-July 24 environment, using the April 28 USITC hearing calendar as the operative planning anchor.
