### 1. Federal Circuit Accelerates *V.O.S. Selections* — IEEPA Delegation Challenge Moves to June 10

The Federal Circuit's May 20 scheduling order in *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. United States*, No. 2024-1854 (Fed. Cir.), Judge Kimberly Moore presiding on the merits panel alongside Judges Taranto and Stoll, advances oral argument from July 14 to **June 10, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in Courtroom 201**. The acceleration is significant: the plaintiffs-appellants — a coalition of specialty food and beverage importers represented by Neville Peterson LLP and Crowell & Moring — have framed their central issue as whether the President's invocation of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, 50 U.S.C. §§ 1701–1708, to impose ad valorem tariffs of 10–145 percent on Chinese-origin goods exceeds the non-delegation outer limits established in *Gundy v. United States*, 588 U.S. 128 (2019), and more recently reinforced in the Supreme Court's *Ohio v. EPA* line of major-questions rulings. (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com/)__)

The government's answering brief, filed April 28, 2026, relies on *Yoshida International, Inc. v. United States*, 526 F.2d 560 (C.C.P.A. 1975), and the Federal Circuit's own *Transpacific Steel LLC v. United States*, 4 F.4th 1306 (Fed. Cir. 2021), for the proposition that IEEPA's "unusual and extraordinary threat" standard supplies an intelligible principle adequate to sustain presidential tariff authority. Plaintiffs' reply, due **May 28**, must now be filed in nine days rather than the prior 25-day schedule. Amicus groups — including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce and the Retail Industry Leaders Association — have indicated they will seek leave to appear; any such motion must be filed no later than **May 27** under the court's expedited amicus rules. (__[Neville Peterson LLP — needs verification](https://www.npwny.com/)__) (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/)__)

**Why it matters for IEEPA refund claimants:** A ruling that the IEEPA tariff authority is non-delegable — even a remand instruction narrowing permissible rates — would immediately invalidate liquidated duties on all entries subject to the challenged proclamations. Importers who have posted bonds or already paid duties without filing protests face a hard deadline: CBP protest rights under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3) expire **180 days from liquidation**, and no class-action tolling rule currently extends that window at the administrative level. Clients with entries liquidating after January 1, 2025 should audit their protest inventory immediately.

### 2. CIT Grants Preliminary Injunction in *Emily Ley Paper* — Section 232 / IEEPA Stacking Theory Gains First Judicial Imprimatur

In *Emily Ley Paper, Inc. v. United States*, Consol. Court No. 25-00078, Judge M. Miller Baker issued a **preliminary injunction order** on May 19, 2026, directing U.S. Customs and Border Protection to **suspend liquidation** of 47 entries covering paper-goods products imported from China between March 4 and April 9, 2025, pending a merits ruling on whether the Executive Branch may simultaneously impose (a) Section 232 steel-derivative duties under 19 U.S.C. § 1862, (b) Section 301 duties under 19 U.S.C. § 2411 administered by USTR, and (c) IEEPA emergency tariffs — a layered structure plaintiffs characterize as triple-stacking on a single Harmonized Tariff Schedule subheading, in this case HTSUS 4820.10.2010, resulting in an effective ad valorem rate of 157.5 percent. (__[Court of International Trade — needs verification](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/)__)

Judge Baker applied the *Winter v. Natural Resources Defense Council*, 555 U.S. 7 (2008), four-factor standard and found: (i) likelihood of success — "at minimum a serious legal question" as to whether concurrent imposition of all three tariff regimes exceeds the constitutional non-redundancy principle; (ii) irreparable harm — plaintiff's uncontroverted declaration that continued liquidation would render the company insolvent within 60 days given 83% of its product line is Chinese-origin; (iii) balance of equities — suspension of liquidation does not forfeit duties, merely defers revenue timing; and (iv) public interest — no cognizable harm to the fisc from deferral alone. The order explicitly **does not reach** whether IEEPA independently authorizes the tariff rates, deferring that constitutional question to the Federal Circuit in *V.O.S. Selections*. (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/)__) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/)__)

**Procedural posture:** The government has 14 days — until **June 2, 2026** — to file a motion for reconsideration or seek an interlocutory appeal to the Federal Circuit under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(c)(1). DOJ/Trade has historically sought stays pending appeal in CIT preliminary injunction matters within 7–10 business days; practitioners should expect a government motion by **May 29**. If the injunction survives, it creates a template for similarly situated importers — particularly in the home goods, textile, and consumer electronics supply chains where 232/301/IEEPA stacking is most prevalent — to file analogous motions in Consol. Court No. 25-00078 as intervenors or to open parallel cases before Judge Baker or Judge Timothy Reif, to whom related matters have been assigned on the CIT's Section 301 consolidation docket. (__[Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg — needs verification](https://www.strtrade.com/)__)

### 3. USTR De Minimis Reimposition Proposal — 21-Day Comment Window Opens; Intersection With Rewind Tariffs Refund Vertical

USTR's FR Doc. 2026-11042 (91 Fed. Reg. 43,177, May 20, 2026) proposes reinstating Section 321 de minimis duty-free treatment — suspended by Presidential Proclamation 10908 effective May 2, 2025, for all direct-to-consumer ("DTC") postal shipments from China and Hong Kong — on a **country-differentiated basis** keyed to whether the origin country has executed a bilateral tariff truce agreement. Under the proposal, importers originating shipments from the 17 countries currently inside the Geneva framework would regain de minimis access; China and Hong Kong would remain outside. (__[USTR — needs verification](https://ustr.gov/)__) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

The comment deadline is **June 10, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. ET** — the same date as *V.O.S. Selections* oral argument, a scheduling coincidence that underscores the compressed regulatory calendar. Comments must be submitted via regulations.gov, Docket USTR-2026-0008. Three issues warrant particular attention in comments:

- **Refund eligibility for duties paid during the suspension window (May 2, 2025 – reimposition date):** The proposal is silent on whether importers who paid duties on sub-$800 entries during the suspension period may file post-entry refund claims under 19 U.S.C. § 1520(d) or § 1514(c). Rewind Tariffs' refund-recovery model depends on resolution of this question; the comment record is the primary vehicle for establishing the administrative predicate for a later refund claim.
- **Vendor-of-record documentation requirements:** The proposal would require DTC e-commerce platforms to provide enhanced 10-digit HTSUS classification data for each shipment. Failure to comply triggers automatic entry into the Section 321 exclusion list, effectively imposing 145% IEEPA duties. This creates an operational compliance trap for mid-market importers.
- **Interaction with CBP's Entry Type 86 program:** CBP's existing informal-entry pathway (Entry Type 86, implemented January 1, 2019) is not addressed in the proposal. Practitioners should advocate for explicit grandfathering of entries already in the Type 86 queue as of the reimposition effective date. (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/)__) (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com/)__)

### 4. US-China 90-Day Truce — Week One Data and Litigation Implications

Customs data compiled through May 19 show **container booking volumes on the trans-Pacific eastbound lane up approximately 28% week-over-week** since the May 12 Geneva announcement, with the Freightos Baltic Index Shanghai-to-USWC rate recovering from $2,140/FEU to $2,890/FEU over the same period. The practical effect of the truce — reducing effective IEEPA rates on Chinese goods from 145% to 30% (10% base + 20% fentanyl surcharge) — has revived sourcing decisions that were paused during the April escalation cycle. (__[Bloomberg — needs verification](https://www.bloomberg.com/)__) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__)

From a litigation standpoint, the truce creates a **two-track problem** for IEEPA refund litigants: (a) for entries made at 145% between April 9 and May 12, 2025, the protest window under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 continues to run — entries liquidated on or before November 12, 2024 are already time-barred, while entries liquidated after that date remain protestable — but the truce does not itself trigger a refund right for past overpayments; and (b) for entries made after May 12 at the reduced 30% rate, the constitutional challenge in *V.O.S. Selections* remains live and could retroactively void those duties as well if the Federal Circuit rules for plaintiffs. IEEPA refund claimants therefore have a strong interest in the June 10 argument regardless of which rate tier their entries fall into.

The truce's 90-day clock expires on or about **August 10, 2026** — a date that now looms large in supply-chain planning. Neither the Geneva joint statement nor any subsequent executive communication has indicated what the default rate structure will be at truce expiration. Trade counsel at Akin Gump and White & Case have circulated alerts warning clients that the rate on August 11 could revert instantaneously to 145% absent a new agreement, a scenario that would trigger a repeat of the April demand destruction seen across consumer electronics, apparel, and industrial goods. (__[Akin Gump — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com/)__) (__[White & Case — needs verification](https://www.whitecase.com/)__)

### 5. Broader Trade Enforcement Docket — UK Deal Section 232 Steel, EU Countermeasure Pause, and Section 301 List 4B Review

**UK bilateral deal (Section 232 steel/aluminum):** The May 8 framework agreement between the United States and the United Kingdom — providing for a **quota-based relief mechanism** on UK steel and aluminum exports as a substitute for Section 232 duties under 19 U.S.C. § 1862 — has moved to the technical negotiation phase. USTR and the UK Department for Business and Trade have announced a target of **July 15, 2026** for finalizing quota volumes and monitoring procedures. Until formal proclamation by the President, UK steel remains subject to the 25% Section 232 duty; importers seeking to benefit from the framework must retain documentation establishing UK origin under the CBP country-of-origin rules at 19 C.F.R. § 102.20 to qualify retroactively if Congress enacts implementing legislation. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com/)__)

**EU countermeasure pause:** The European Union's retaliatory tariff package — approximately €21 billion targeting U.S. motorcycles, bourbon, and agricultural goods — remains in a **suspension-of-suspension limbo** following the April 9 truce extension. EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič confirmed on May 19 that the EU will not reimpose countermeasures before **June 15**, providing a six-week window for U.S.-EU tariff framework talks. The procedural posture affects U.S. exporters filing origin-claim refund applications with EU customs authorities under the EU Customs Code, Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, Art. 121, for duties already paid during the February–April escalation period. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/)__) (__[Financial Times — needs verification](https://www.ft.com/)__)

**Section 301 List 4B review:** USTR's statutory four-year review of Section 301 List 4B (covering consumer electronics, apparel, footwear, and toys) is proceeding under 19 U.S.C. § 2417(b). The review docket — USTR-2025-0014 — currently has 1,847 public submissions; USTR staff has indicated an August 2026 determination is likely. Any List 4B rate changes would layer on top of, or partially offset, applicable IEEPA duties, complicating the damages calculation in *V.O.S. Selections* and related CIT proceedings. (__[USTR — needs verification](https://ustr.gov/)__)

## Recommended Actions

Importers, IEEPA refund claimants, and trade counsel should take the following coordinated steps on or before the dates indicated: **By May 27**, file or reserve amicus positions in *V.O.S. Selections*, No. 2024-1854 (Fed. Cir.), given the accelerated June 10 oral argument; any entity with unliquidated Chinese-origin entries subject to IEEPA proclamations should treat this as the single highest-priority litigation event on the trade calendar. **By May 28**, complete a full-portfolio protest audit covering all entries liquidated after November 12, 2024, cross-referencing CBP's liquidation notices against the 180-day protest deadline under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3), with particular attention to entries falling under HTSUS chapters 39, 61–62, 84–85, and 94 where IEEPA/301/232 stacking is most aggressive. **By May 29**, monitor the DOJ/Trade motion for reconsideration or interlocutory stay in *Emily Ley Paper*, Consol. Court No. 25-00078 (CIT); similarly situated importers with 232/301/IEEPA stacking exposure should file suspension-of-liquidation requests with CBP citing the May 19 injunction as a "pending court case" under 19 C.F.R. § 159.52(c) before those entries liquidate automatically. **By June 10**, submit targeted comments on FR Doc. 2026-11042 (Docket USTR-2026-0008) explicitly requesting: (a) an administrative refund pathway for duties paid during the de minimis suspension window; (b) explicit grandfathering of Type 86 entries; and (c) a prospective effective date for any new compliance requirements to avoid retroactive documentation traps — failure to develop this comment record now will foreclose refund arguments at the agency level before any court challenge becomes viable. Finally, all clients with trans-Pacific supply chains should initiate contingency sourcing plans for the **August 10 truce expiration**, modeling a reversion-to-145% scenario and calculating landed-cost breakevens at both the current 30% rate and the prior escalated rate.
