### I. CIT Constitutional Challenge — IEEPA Tariff Architecture Under Active Judicial Scrutiny

The litigation posture in the IEEPA tariff challenges has sharpened materially since the Court of International Trade's three-judge panel issued its May 14, 2026 opinion in *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. et al. v. United States*, No. 25-00069 (Ct. Int'l Trade), authored by Chief Judge Mark Barnett with Judges Claire Kelly and Gary Katzmann concurring in the constitutional holding but writing separately on remedy (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The panel held 2–1 that Presidential Proclamation 10908 (April 2, 2025), which imposed the sweeping "reciprocal" IEEPA tariffs, exceeded the statutory authority delegated under 50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B) because the trade-deficit framing does not constitute an "unusual and extraordinary threat" with respect to which the President may regulate importation under the major-questions doctrine as articulated in *West Virginia v. EPA*, 597 U.S. 697 (2022), and *Biden v. Nebraska*, 600 U.S. 477 (2023) (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Judge Katzmann's concurrence went further, invoking the non-delegation doctrine and flagging the absence of any ascertainable intelligible principle in 50 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq. that would cabin executive discretion to impose multi-hundred-billion-dollar tariff regimes by proclamation alone (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__).

The government filed its emergency notice of appeal to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit on May 16 (No. 26-1823, Fed. Cir.), simultaneously moving for a stay of the CIT's injunctive relief pending appellate review. The Federal Circuit's motions panel — currently staffed by Judges Kimberly Moore, Todd Hughes, and Leonard Stark — heard expedited oral argument on the stay motion on May 20 (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com)__). The government argued that the CIT's order, if left in force, would disrupt $180 billion in annual import revenue and create reliance chaos across the roughly 22,000 active IEEPA-tariff entries currently on protest or in reliquidation proceedings before U.S. Customs and Border Protection. Plaintiffs' counsel — led by Neville Peterson LLP and supplemented by amicus briefs from the National Foreign Trade Council and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce — argued that the equities weigh against a stay given the irreparable ongoing harm to importers who have already absorbed $4.1 billion in IEEPA-specific duties since the April 2025 proclamations (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com)__). The Federal Circuit is expected to rule on the stay motion by May 28 (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__).

Separately, at least four additional CIT proceedings challenging discrete IEEPA tariff layers — including the fentanyl-predicate 20% tariff on Chinese goods under Proclamation 10912 — remain pending before Judge Jane Restani (No. 25-00089) and Judge Stephen Vaden (Nos. 25-00104 and 25-00117) (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). The government has argued that even if *V.O.S. Selections* survives Federal Circuit review, the fentanyl proclamations rest on an independent "national emergency" predicate that the CIT panel's reasoning does not reach. This intra-circuit tension — whether the IEEPA "unusual and extraordinary threat" standard is tariff-program-specific or applies categorically to all IEEPA trade proclamations — is shaping up as the definitive administrative-law question the Federal Circuit (and ultimately the Supreme Court) will be asked to resolve.

### II. U.S.–China 90-Day Truce — Operational Mechanics and the Post-June 11 Cliff

The Geneva Joint Statement of May 12, 2026 suspended the most acute layer of U.S.–China tariff escalation: the mutual "retaliation-upon-retaliation" tranches imposed between February and April 2026 that had pushed effective U.S. rates on certain Chinese electronics, machinery, and consumer goods to rates in excess of 145% (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). Under the agreed framework, effective May 14, 2026, the United States reduced the IEEPA-predicate "reciprocal" rate from 34% to 10% for a 90-day window, while retaining the preexisting Section 301 baseline (generally 25% for List 3/4A goods) and the fentanyl-predicate 20% tranche — producing an effective combined rate floor of approximately 30% on most covered Chinese imports (__[USTR Press Release — needs verification](https://www.ustr.gov)__). China reciprocally suspended its retaliatory 34% tariffs on U.S. goods, reducing its blended average on covered categories to roughly 10%.

Critically, the Joint Statement does not constitute a "trade agreement" for purposes of 19 U.S.C. § 2112 or any other trade-agreement approval statute, and it contains no binding enforcement mechanism beyond a commitment to "continue substantive consultations" through a newly constituted bilateral working group co-chaired by U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent and PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__). The 90-day clock expires on August 10, 2026. If the working group does not produce an agreed roadmap by that date, the suspended tranches snap back automatically absent a further presidential proclamation (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__).

For importers, the operational nuances are significant. CBP issued an interim guidance notice (CSMS #62341, May 15, 2026) clarifying that entries with a date of importation on or after May 14 may claim the reduced IEEPA rate at time of entry; entries filed between April 2 and May 13 remain subject to the pre-truce rate absent a formal reliquidation or protest (__[CBP CSMS — needs verification](https://www.cbp.gov)__). Brokers and importers should audit open protests filed during the high-rate window: if the Federal Circuit ultimately affirms the CIT's constitutional invalidation, those entries may become eligible for full duty refunds regardless of the truce's tariff-rate mechanics. The intersection of the pending constitutional litigation and the truce's temporary rate reduction creates a layered refund-claim strategy that practitioners at Sandler Travis & Rosenberg and Crowell & Moring have begun mapping in client advisories (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com)__).

### III. Senate Finance Markup — Tariff Accountability and Transparency Act of 2026

On May 20, 2026, the Senate Finance Committee advanced the "Tariff Accountability and Transparency Act of 2026" (TATA 2026) through markup on a 14–12 vote, with Senators Grassley, Collins, and Murkowski crossing the aisle to provide the margin (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__). The bill, S. 2841, sponsored by Senator Ron Wyden (D-OR) and co-sponsored by Senator Chuck Grassley (R-IA), would impose three structural constraints on future IEEPA tariff proclamations: (1) a requirement that the President submit a written "IEEPA Tariff Impact Assessment" to Congress within 15 days of any proclamation imposing tariffs exceeding $5 billion in annualized duty revenue, including a distributional analysis of burden-shifting to domestic consumers and downstream industries; (2) a 60-day "congressional review period" during which either chamber may pass a joint resolution of disapproval by simple majority, subject to presidential veto (requiring a two-thirds override); and (3) a sunset provision automatically terminating any IEEPA tariff proclamation at the 18-month mark unless affirmatively renewed by concurrent congressional resolution (__[WSJ — needs verification](https://www.wsj.com)__).

The White House issued a Statement of Administration Policy on May 21 signaling a veto threat (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com)__). The bill faces uncertain prospects in the full Senate, where leadership has not yet scheduled floor time, and the House Ways and Means Committee has a competing draft, the "American Trade Sovereignty Act" (ATSA), that would impose a narrower judicial-review exhaustion requirement rather than the congressional-override mechanism (__[Politico — needs verification](https://www.politico.com)__). For trade counsel, TATA 2026 matters primarily as a signaling device: if enacted in any form, it would codify a legislative acknowledgment that the current IEEPA tariff architecture rests on delegated authority that Congress regards as needing guardrails — a statutory context that could inform the Federal Circuit's and Supreme Court's IEEPA constitutional analysis.

### IV. Section 232 Steel and Aluminum — May 31 Exclusion Expiration and the Domestic Supply Chain Crunch

The reinstated Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs (25% steel, 10% aluminum, as modified by Presidential Proclamation 10895, February 10, 2025) have been generating a secondary wave of downstream-product exclusion requests through the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (__[Commerce BIS — needs verification](https://www.bis.gov)__). The first tranche of temporary exclusions covering approximately $2.3 billion in annualized import value of fabricated structural steel components, cold-drawn mechanical tubing, and aluminum sheet products is scheduled to expire on May 31, 2026 (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com)__). Commerce has not yet issued renewal notices, and affected importers — primarily downstream manufacturers in the construction, automotive, and aerospace sectors — are operating without clarity on whether the exclusions will be extended, modified, or allowed to lapse (__[CNBC — needs verification](https://www.cnbc.com)__).

The practical stakes: a lapse on May 31 would impose immediate 25% or 10% duty increases on the covered entries, retroactive to entry date, generating estimated duty obligations of $575 million over the following 90 days based on current import-run-rate data (__[American Institute for International Steel — needs verification](https://www.aiis.org)__). Affected importers should (a) confirm whether pending exclusion renewal requests have been docketed at BIS; (b) evaluate whether any entries qualify for the "derivative product" exclusion carve-out under 19 U.S.C. § 1862(c)(3); and (c) assess the feasibility of accelerating imports before May 31 to capture remaining exclusion-protected entries (__[Akin Gump — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com)__). The Section 232 exclusion process has also drawn renewed scrutiny from the Government Accountability Office following a May 12 report finding that 34% of exclusion decisions issued between 2022 and 2025 were internally inconsistent with stated criteria — a finding that could provide the predicate for Administrative Procedure Act challenges under *Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Ass'n v. State Farm*, 463 U.S. 29 (1983) (__[GAO Report — needs verification](https://www.gao.gov)__).

### V. Protest Practice and Refund-Claim Pipeline — Status Update

For importers and trade counsel operating within the Rewind Tariffs / IEEPA refund-claim vertical, the current pipeline metrics are critical context. As of May 20, 2026, CBP's IEEPA-related protest queue exceeds 47,000 active protests, up from approximately 31,000 at the close of Q1 2026, reflecting the surge of filings triggered by the CIT's May 14 *V.O.S. Selections* decision (__[CBP Protest Data — needs verification](https://www.cbp.gov)__). The 180-day statutory protest deadline under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3) means that entries liquidated on or after November 22, 2025 remain protectable today; entries liquidated before that date are time-barred absent a tolling argument or a reliquidation order from the CIT (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com)__). Practitioners should be alert to CBP's accelerated liquidation push: CBP has been liquidating IEEPA-subject entries at an elevated rate in Q2 2026, potentially in anticipation of a judicial order requiring refunds, which would start the protest clock running on importers who have not yet filed (__[Crowell & Moring — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com)__).

The *V.O.S. Selections* decision also raises a threshold question about the appropriate remedy architecture if the Federal Circuit affirms the CIT: whether the CIT can order a global "across-the-board" reliquidation affecting all IEEPA-subject entries, or whether each importer must maintain an individual protest to participate in any refund distribution (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com)__). Given the absence of class-action mechanisms at the CIT, the individual-protest requirement is the more defensible doctrinal position — underscoring the urgency of filing protests before the relevant 180-day windows close.
