# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 15, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The IEEPA tariff refund process entered its critical operational phase this week as CBP confirmed the April 20, 2026 launch of Phase 1 of the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) system, now accessible within ACE, CBP's automated commercial environment portal. This marks the transition from litigation and procedural planning to active refund processing of the approximately $165 billion in unlawfully collected duties owed to roughly 330,000 importers nationwide following the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 decision striking down IEEPA tariff authority and the Court of International Trade's March 4, 2026 nationwide refund order. Phase 1 is limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation, with more complex scenarios addressed in subsequent phases; CBP has published comprehensive guidance, including a step-by-step reference guide and dedicated webpage, with refunds expected within 60–90 days of CAPE Declaration acceptance. ([Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cbp-confirms-april-20-2026-launch-of-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process/), [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/cbp-issues-guidance-on-ieepa-duty-refunds-via-new-cape-process-what-importers-must-do-before-april-20/))

Simultaneously, the Section 122 tariff replacement authority faces intense judicial scrutiny as a three-judge CIT panel heard oral arguments on April 10, 2026 in two consolidated challenges—one brought by the Liberty Justice Center on behalf of Burlap and Barrel, Inc. (spice importer) and Basic Fun, Inc. (toy company), and another filed by a 24-state coalition led by New York Attorney General Letitia James. The cases rest on constitutional grounds including the nondelegation and major questions doctrines, with the Liberty Justice Center explicitly contending that Section 122 lacks statutory authority for broad tariffs and that the administration cannot demonstrate a "large and serious" balance-of-payments deficit as required by the statute. Notably, during oral arguments, Trump administration counsel Brett Shumate acknowledged inability to articulate the current balance-of-payments deficit or provide an estimate, potentially undermining the statutory prerequisite for Section 122 authority. ([Reason](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/10/thoughts-on-todays-oral-argument-in-the-section-122-tariff-cases/), [Liberty Justice Center](https://libertyjusticecenter.org/mediaalert/media-alert-federal-court-to-hear-oral-arguments-in-liberty-justice-centers-section-122-tariffs-challenge-at-10-a-m-edt/))

Corporate importers and downstream buyers are now managing a dual-track exposure landscape. As reported this week, cash-constrained businesses are securitizing tariff refund claims as collateral for loans, signaling acute working-capital pressure among importers facing uncertain refund timelines and ongoing Section 122 exposure. The CBP data reveals critical adoption gaps: as of late March 2026, only 26,664 of approximately 330,000 eligible importers (8%) had enrolled in the automated refund system, indicating substantial opportunity and compliance risk for importers yet to establish ACE accounts or prepare entry documentation for CAPE filing. Separately, importers receiving refunds face emerging consumer class-action exposure from downstream retailers and end-consumers alleging improper retention of tariff costs; legal counsel at Troutman Pepper and Jones Walker have flagged this as a material contingency requiring contemporaneous documentation of cost allocation and pricing practices. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/12/us-importers-tariff-refund-claims-used-loan-collateral/), [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/))

Congressional activity remains cautious and fragmented. Representatives Steven Horsford (D-NV) and Janelle Bynum (D-OR) introduced the Restoring Economic Lifelines for Independent Enterprises and Family Businesses (RELIEF) Act, which would require CBP to automatically refund IEEPA tariffs with interest, but the measure lacks Republican co-sponsorship and has not advanced to floor consideration. Conversely, twenty-four state attorneys general, led by New York's Letitia James, formally called on Congress to mandate tariff refunds with interest, signaling pressure on legislative leadership to oversee CIT compliance and potentially prevent future abuse of IEEPA or Section 122 authorities. ([Congress.gov / Bynum](https://bynum.house.gov/media/press-releases/bynum-and-horsford-introduce-relief-act-following-supreme-court-tariff-ruling), [NY Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-calls-congress-pass-legislation-requiring-tariff-refunds))

## Recommended Actions

Importers with unliquidated entries or entries within 80 days of liquidation must prioritize CAPE Declaration preparation and ACE enrollment before April 20, 2026; companies should consolidate entry documentation (bills of lading, commercial invoices, PSC calculations, tariff classification records) and designate authorized brokers or compliance officers to manage ACE filings, as the 60–90 day refund processing window will compress significantly if filings are delayed. Firms that have not yet settled claims on tariff cost allocation and downstream pass-through should immediately document pricing decisions, retail markups, and customer communications contemporaneously to defend against emerging consumer class-action exposure; legal counsel at specialized trade firms recommend preserving all cost-benefit analyses and avoiding any admissions of margin expansion attributable to tariff collection. For corporations with exposure to Section 122 tariffs (imposed March 2026 onwards), maintaining dual-track litigation strategy—filing CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i) and pursuing administrative refund routes—ensures eligibility for government stipulations of interest-bearing refunds to "current and future similarly situated plaintiffs," per CIT practice established post-IEEPA ruling. Finally, importers in cash-constrained positions should evaluate tariff refund securitization arrangements carefully, consulting counsel on security interest disclosure obligations and lender claims hierarchy.
