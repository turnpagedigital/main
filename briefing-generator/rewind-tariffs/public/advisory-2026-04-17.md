# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 17, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Three days before CBP's CAPE system goes live, the operational countdown for the largest tariff-refund event in modern U.S. trade history has entered its final phase. U.S. Customs and Border Protection confirmed this week that primary development of all four core components of the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries ("CAPE") Phase 1 is complete, with the system transitioning to intensive performance and scenario-based testing ahead of the April 20, 2026, 8:00 a.m. EDT deployment inside the ACE Secure Data Portal. Phase 1 remains limited to most unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation — a scope CBP estimates will capture approximately 63% of entries on which IEEPA-based duties were paid or deposited — with valid refunds projected to issue via ACH within 60 to 90 days after CAPE Declaration acceptance, absent compliance review. Importers of record and authorized customs brokers will submit claims through a new CAPE tab in ACE using a CSV-formatted CAPE Declaration, with brokers permitted to bundle up to 9,999 entries across multiple IORs on a single filing. ([CBP IEEPA Duty Refunds](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)) ([CBP CSMS #68340863](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f))

The Court of International Trade continues to shape the procedural architecture around the refund. Following the April 6 voluntary dismissal of the original Atmus Filtration anchor case, Judge Richard K. Eaton's reissued refund order in Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States (No. 1:25-cv-00595-RKE) remains the operative directive, requiring CBP to liquidate or reliquidate entries across three categories — unliquidated, actively protested, and finally liquidated — without regard to IEEPA duties. The order's facial extension to non-litigant importers remains the most consequential open question for companies whose entries fall outside the Phase 1 80-day window, and the government's stipulation not to object to refunds on liquidated entries is widely expected to be tested on appeal. Separately, the Penn Wharton Budget Model's April 15 update placed total customs revenue generated by tariff-rate changes between January 2025 and February 2026 at $224.8 billion, with IEEPA-specific collections through December 14, 2025 reaching approximately $133.5 billion — figures that underscore both the magnitude of the refund liability and the fiscal consequences of the Supreme Court's February 20 ruling in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cit-endorses-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-ahead-of-cbps-april-20-rollout/)) ([Penn Wharton Budget Model](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2026-04-15-effective-tariff-rates-and-revenues-updated-april-15-2026/))

The replacement-tariff litigation calendar is accelerating on a parallel track. The CIT's three-judge panel heard consolidated oral argument on April 10 in the dual Section 122 challenges — one brought by the Liberty Justice Center on behalf of two small importers, the other by a 24-state coalition led by the New York Attorney General — with at least two judges expressing skepticism that a trade deficit alone satisfies Section 122's "fundamental international payments problems" predicate, and one judge reportedly characterizing the government's theory as one that "proves too much" by permitting invocation of Section 122 virtually at will. The current 10% ad valorem surcharge expires at 12:01 a.m. ET on July 24, 2026, and the administration's transition architecture is now visible: USTR's twin Section 301 investigations into structural excess manufacturing capacity across 16 economies and forced-labor enforcement failures across 60-plus economies closed their written-comment period on April 15, with public hearings at the USITC commencing April 28 through May 8 — a compressed timeline designed to produce Section 301 determinations before the Section 122 sunset, layered atop the April 2 proclamations imposing 100% Section 232 duties on pharmaceuticals and modified steel, aluminum, and copper derivative-product treatment. ([Reason / Volokh Conspiracy](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/10/thoughts-on-todays-oral-argument-in-the-section-122-tariff-cases/)) ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production)) ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_section_301_investigations_ieepa_tariff_refund_developments_legal_challenges_section_0426.html))

The consumer class-action exposure for importers who passed IEEPA-duty costs downstream continues to broaden, with filings now spanning multiple federal districts. The March 27 putative class action against Lululemon USA Inc. in the Eastern District of Michigan (No. 2:26-cv-11029) — alleging approximately $240 million in pass-through tariff charges alongside simultaneous government refund pursuit — has established the template: causes of action center on unjust enrichment, money had and received, and state unfair-and-deceptive-trade-practices statutes, exploiting the structural asymmetry that only the importer of record holds standing to seek a CBP refund. Additional complaints have been filed in the Northern District of Georgia, the Southern District of Florida, and the Eastern District of New York, and law firms including Arnold & Porter, Troutman Pepper, and Ballard Spahr have published detailed analyses warning that the convergence of CAPE refund filings with pending 10-Q disclosure cycles creates a compounding risk: refund-anticipation accruals, tariff-cost-recovery representations, and MD&A descriptions of expected pass-through economics all represent potential plaintiff discovery targets. ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) ([Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/03/tariff-ied-of-a-refund-lawsuit-takeaways-from-the-early-complaints))

## Recommended Actions

Companies that paid IEEPA duties should treat this weekend as a hard operational deadline: verify that the ACE Portal Importer sub-account is active and that ACH bank-account information is fully populated — refunds issue exclusively by electronic funds transfer, and accounts without banking data on file will not receive disbursements on launch day. Importers should finalize reconciliation of all IEEPA-subject entries at the entry-summary level, flagging liquidation status for each, because Phase 1 eligibility turns on whether an entry is unliquidated or within 80 days of liquidation on the filing date. For entries falling outside that window, companies should preserve rights through protest filings under 19 U.S.C. §1514 where liquidation is imminent, and evaluate individual CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i) pending later CAPE phases or appellate clarification of the Euro-Notions order's non-litigant reach. Importers that passed tariff costs to downstream buyers should coordinate CAPE Declaration filings with securities and commercial-litigation counsel to harmonize refund accruals, disclosure language, and customer-facing communications against the class-action fact patterns now emerging across multiple districts. Finally, supply-chain and procurement teams should begin modeling post-July 24 tariff scenarios incorporating the Section 301 hearing outcomes (April 28–May 8), Section 232 pharmaceutical duty phase-in (120- and 180-day tiers from April 2), and the potential that the CIT invalidates the Section 122 surcharge before its July 24 statutory sunset.
