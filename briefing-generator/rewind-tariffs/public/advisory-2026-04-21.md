# IEEPA TARIFF ADVISORY | April 21, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The CAPE portal's first full day of operations marks a watershed moment in the largest tariff refund effort in U.S. history, as CBP begins accepting Phase 1 declarations for the estimated $166 billion in IEEPA duties collected from more than 330,000 importers across 53 million shipments between April 2025 and February 2026. Phase 1, which CBP estimates will cover approximately 63% of eligible entries, is limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation, with refunds expected within 45 to 90 days following acceptance of a valid CAPE Declaration filed through the Automated Commercial Environment. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-to-file-for-tariff-refund/)) The launch itself was not without friction — importers reported submission errors including duplicate Tax ID rejections and CSV formatting failures — but the institutional achievement of standing up a system of this scale within 60 days of the Supreme Court's February 20 ruling should not be understated. UPS and FedEx have confirmed they are filing CAPE Declarations on behalf of customers, signaling that the logistics sector is moving quickly to process refunds for the millions of individual shipments routed through express consignment operators. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/20/tariff-refunds-begin-on-monday-these-retailers-are-due-big-paydays.html))

Senior Judge Richard Eaton's April 14 order in the new lead case, *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. U.S. Customs and Border Protection*, confirmed that CBP was on track for the April 20 launch and scheduled the next closed conference for April 28, 2026, at which CBP must file its latest status report on CAPE submission volumes, error rates, and processing timelines. ([Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/cit-endorses-phase-1-of-the-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-ahead-of-cbps-april-20-rollout/)) The government has not filed an appeal of the Supreme Court's decision, nor sought an extension of time, nor publicly signaled any intent to challenge the ruling — a silence that market participants increasingly interpret as acceptance of the refund obligation, though the appeal window remains open through early June. CBP's next status report on April 28 will be the first opportunity to assess whether the system is functioning at scale or whether the launch-day glitches signal deeper capacity constraints that could delay the 45-day refund timeline for early filers.

The broader trade policy landscape is shifting rapidly around the IEEPA refund process. The replacement Section 122 tariffs imposed on February 25, 2026 — a 10% global surcharge — face a statutory expiration on July 24, 2026, and there is no presidential authority to extend them unilaterally; congressional extension would require 60 votes in the Senate, a threshold that appears politically unlikely. ([FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/section-122-tariff-expiration-countdown-july-2026)) Two lawsuits challenging the Section 122 tariffs are pending at the CIT — one filed by 24 state attorneys general (Case No. 26-01472) and another by the Liberty Justice Center — both arguing that the statutory conditions for Section 122 have not been met and that the broad scope of carveouts renders the tariffs inconsistent with the statute's requirement of uniform application. ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/alerts/section-122-tariffs-challenged-in-court-of-international-trade/)) Meanwhile, USTR's March 11 initiation of Section 301 investigations into structural excess capacity and forced labor across 16 economies signals the administration's intent to build targeted, country-specific tariff authority with stronger legal footing, with public hearings scheduled for April 28 (forced labor) and May 5 (excess capacity). ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/new_section_301_investigations_ieepa_tariff_refund_developments_legal_challenges_section_0426.html))

Consumer-side class action litigation continues to expand as the refund process accelerates. Plaintiff firms have filed putative class actions against Costco, FedEx, UPS, and EssilorLuxottica, among others, alleging unjust enrichment and consumer fraud on the theory that importers passed IEEPA tariff costs through to consumers via higher retail prices and are now positioned to receive government refunds without returning the surcharges to end purchasers. ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ieepa-tariff-refunds-may-come-with-an-unforeseen-cost-exposure-to-consumer-class-actions/)) Covington & Burling's March analysis warned that these pass-through theories, while novel, could gain traction in jurisdictions with broad consumer protection statutes, and that importers receiving CAPE refunds should begin documenting their pricing decisions and cost absorption methodologies now to prepare for potential discovery. ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)) FedEx has notably committed to returning refunds to the customers who originally paid the duties, a position that may become the de facto industry standard as litigation pressure mounts.

## Recommended Actions

Companies with unliquidated entries or entries within 80 days of liquidation should file Phase 1 CAPE Declarations immediately through the ACE Portal, ensuring that CSV files comply with CBP's formatting specifications and that each declaration is limited to 9,999 entries per submission. Importers who have not yet established ACE accounts — CBP has indicated that fewer than 10% of eligible importers had done so as of early March — should prioritize enrollment through CBP's ACE portal or authorize their customs broker to file on their behalf. Companies anticipating refunds should begin assembling contemporaneous documentation of pricing decisions, cost pass-through calculations, and internal communications regarding tariff surcharges, as this record will be critical to defending against consumer class action claims alleging unjust enrichment under state consumer protection statutes. Importers with entries approaching the 80-day post-liquidation window should calendar these deadlines immediately, as Phase 1 eligibility is time-limited and entries that fall outside the window will need to await subsequent phases or pursue individual CIT actions under 28 U.S.C. §1581(i). All importers should monitor the April 28 CIT status conference for updated guidance on Phase 2 timelines, error resolution procedures, and any adjustments to the 45-day refund processing estimate.
