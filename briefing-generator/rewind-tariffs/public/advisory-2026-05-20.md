# ⚖️ Tariffs / Trade | Wednesday, May 20, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today's principal delta is the formalization of the U.S.–China Geneva framework into a structured 90-day standstill: the U.S. reduced its composite IEEPA rate on Chinese goods from the prior 145 % ceiling to 30 % (10 % base + 20 % fentanyl surcharge), while China suspended its 125 % retaliatory schedule and restored rare-earth export flows under a parallel side letter. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-china-trade-talks-geneva-tariff-pause-2026/)__) Simultaneously, the Court of International Trade's three-judge panel—Chief Judge Mark Barnett, Judge Claire Kelly, and Judge Jennifer Choe-Groves—returned to center stage when the government filed its long-awaited reply brief in *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. United States*, Ct. Int'l Trade Consol. No. 25-00066, arguing that IEEPA Section 1701(a)(1) subsumes broad emergency tariff authority without any "major questions" carve-out, directly contesting the Federal Circuit's earlier jurisdictional reservation in *Transpacific Steel LLC v. United States*, 4 F.4th 1306 (Fed. Cir. 2021). (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/international-trade/vos-selections-cit-reply-brief-ieepa-tariff-2026/)__) Separately, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative published its Section 301 four-year statutory review of the China Action-1 list—comprising roughly 378 HTS subheadings with an aggregate annual import value of approximately $34 billion—and proposed extending all rates at or above 25 % through at least December 31, 2027, with comment period closing June 30, 2026. (__[USTR Federal Register Notice — needs verification](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/19/ustr-section-301-four-year-review/)__)

### CIT IEEPA Merits Track: *V.O.S. Selections* and Consolidated Cases

The government's reply brief—138 pages filed May 19, 2026 in *V.O.S. Selections*, Consol. No. 25-00066—advances three lines of argument that practitioners tracking the refund vertical must parse carefully. First, DOJ contends that the "unusual and extraordinary threat" finding in President Biden's February 2025 IEEPA proclamation (Proclamation 10908, 90 Fed. Reg. 9551) constitutes a non-justiciable political question under *Zivotofsky v. Clinton*, 566 U.S. 189 (2012), foreclosing merits review entirely. Second, and in the alternative, DOJ argues that even if justiciable, the major-questions doctrine articulated in *West Virginia v. EPA*, 597 U.S. 697 (2022), does not apply because IEEPA's emergency tariff authority is not an assertion of power over an "economically significant" policy domain lacking clear congressional authorization—pointing to IEEPA's express textual grant in 50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B) to "regulate . . . importation." Third, DOJ cites *Yoshida International, Inc. v. United States*, 526 F.2d 560 (C.C.P.A. 1975), for the proposition that executive tariff proclamations under emergency statutes receive near-plenary deference once the statutory trigger is satisfied. (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/vos-selections-doj-reply-brief-ieepa-2026/)__) Plaintiffs' coalition counsel—led by Neville Peterson LLP and Lamb & Kawakami LLP on behalf of the food-and-beverage importers, and by Crowell & Moring LLP on behalf of electronics importers—had argued in their opposition that the tariff's 145 % rate ceiling constituted an unconstitutional delegation under the non-delegation doctrine, relying on *Gundy v. United States*, 139 S. Ct. 2116 (2019), and *American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. EPA*, 531 U.S. 457 (2001). Oral argument has not yet been scheduled; Chief Judge Barnett's chambers indicated in a May 14 scheduling order that a hearing date will issue no earlier than July 2026. (__[CIT Docket, Consol. No. 25-00066 — needs verification](https://ecf.cit.uscourts.gov/)__)

For the Rewind Tariffs claimant vertical, the filing is consequential in two respects: if the panel rules for plaintiffs on the non-delegation or major-questions theory, importers who paid duties under Proclamation 10908 will have a statutory refund pathway under 19 U.S.C. § 1520(a) and 19 U.S.C. § 1514 (protest and reliquidation), provided protests were timely filed within 180 days of liquidation. The claimant intake data suggest a substantial share of entries liquidated between February and August 2025 are approaching the outer protest window; any importer who has not yet filed a CBP Form 19 protest should be treated as urgent. (__[Diaz Trade Law Client Alert — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/ieepa-protest-deadline-2026/)__)

### Federal Circuit Jurisdictional Appeal: *Emily Ley Paper, Inc. v. United States*, No. 26-1382

A parallel and potentially dispositive jurisdictional fight is advancing at the Federal Circuit. In *Emily Ley Paper, Inc. v. United States*, Fed. Cir. No. 26-1382, a panel comprising Chief Judge Kimberly Moore, Judge Alan Lourie, and Judge Tiffany Cunningham heard oral argument on May 15, 2026, on whether the CIT or the U.S. District Courts have exclusive subject-matter jurisdiction to hear IEEPA-based tariff challenges under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i). The government argued that § 1581(i)'s "embargoes or other quantitative restrictions" language encompasses IEEPA rate schedules, depriving district courts of jurisdiction and channeling all challenges to the CIT's specialized panel. Plaintiff's counsel, Jennifer Franze of Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg, P.A., countered that IEEPA tariffs are not "quantitative restrictions" but rather fiscal levies subject to general federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331. (__[Sandler Travis & Rosenberg Client Alert — needs verification](https://www.strtrade.com/emily-ley-paper-federal-circuit-ieepa-jurisdiction-2026/)__) The stakes: if § 1581(i) exclusivity is confirmed, the roughly 87 district-court IEEPA cases filed in eight circuits since March 2025 face dismissal for lack of jurisdiction and would need to be re-filed at the CIT with conforming pleadings—adding delay and potentially mooting some entries already past protest windows. A decision is expected within 60 days of argument, placing the ruling in mid-July 2026. (__[Bloomberg Law — needs verification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/international-trade/emily-ley-federal-circuit-ieepa-jurisdiction-argument-2026/)__)

### Geneva Standstill — Legal Mechanics and Tariff Rate Implications

The May 12 Joint Statement formalizing the U.S.–China trade standstill was implemented domestically through a presidential proclamation (Proclamation 10977, published May 14, 2026, 91 Fed. Reg. 41,202) reducing the "reciprocal tariff" component from 34 % to 10 % for 90 days while retaining the 20 % IEEPA fentanyl surcharge and the pre-existing Section 301 rates (7.5 %–25 % on most consumer goods, 25 % on steel/aluminum under Section 232, and 100 % on EVs). (__[Federal Register — needs verification](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/14/proclamation-10977-china-tariff-reduction/)__) The composite effective rate on Chinese manufactured goods thus runs from approximately 37.5 % (consumer electronics on Action-1 list with 7.5 % Section 301 rate) to 55 % (steel mill products carrying both Section 232 and residual Section 301 loadings). Several first-sale valuation disputes are now ripening at CBP: importers are contesting whether the "transaction value" used to calculate ad valorem IEEPA duty should reflect factory price or FOB price when intermediary trading companies are interposed—an argument grounded in 19 C.F.R. § 152.103(b) and the Federal Circuit's holding in *Nissho Iwai American Corp. v. United States*, 982 F.2d 505 (Fed. Cir. 1992). (__[Crowell & Moring International Trade Alert — needs verification](https://www.crowell.com/first-sale-valuation-ieepa-china-2026/)__)

Critically, the standstill proclamation contains a "snap-back" clause: if either party fails to comply with the working-group benchmarks established in Annex A of the Joint Statement by August 10, 2026, the 34 % reciprocal-tariff component reinstates automatically without further rulemaking. Trade counsel at Akin Gump and Mayer Brown have flagged that the snap-back operates by operation of the proclamation itself—no additional Federal Register notice is required—meaning importers cannot rely on a notice-and-comment period as a hedge window. (__[Akin Gump Trade Alert — needs verification](https://www.akingump.com/us-china-snapback-mechanism-trade-2026/)__) Affected importers with open purchase orders scheduled for delivery between August and December 2026 should be building contractual force majeure and tariff-escalation clauses that reference the snap-back date specifically.

### Section 301 Four-Year Review — Action-1 List Extension

USTR's proposed extension of the Action-1 Section 301 tariffs, published May 19, 2026, covers 378 HTS subheadings that include: industrial machinery (HTS Ch. 84), electrical equipment (Ch. 85), and certain chemicals and plastics (Ch. 39). (__[USTR Federal Register Notice — needs verification](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/19/ustr-section-301-four-year-review/)__) The statutory basis is Section 307(c)(1) of the Trade Act of 1974, 19 U.S.C. § 2417(c)(1), which mandates a review every four years and requires affirmative USTR determination that modification or termination would be "appropriate." The docket number for the review is USTR-2026-0005; the comment portal is at regulations.gov. Of particular interest: USTR's notice flags that it is "evaluating whether any rates should be increased above 25 % on strategic-technology subheadings," citing the national-security overlay authorized under Section 301(b), 19 U.S.C. § 2411(b). If USTR exercises that authority, importers of semiconductors (HTS 8542), advanced batteries (HTS 8507.60), and solar wafers (HTS 8501.71) face a second-order rate increase on top of the existing 25 % Action-1 rate, compounding IEEPA exposure. (__[White & Case Trade Regulatory Update — needs verification](https://www.whitecase.com/ustr-section-301-four-year-review-strategic-tech-2026/)__)

### USITC Safeguard Review — Solar Cells: *In re Crystalline Silicon Photovoltaic Cells*, Inv. No. TA-201-075

The U.S. International Trade Commission issued its staff report on May 16, 2026, in the four-year safeguard review of crystalline silicon photovoltaic cells and modules, Inv. No. TA-201-075, finding that the domestic industry "continues to be seriously injured" and recommending that the President extend the Section 201 safeguard measure—currently a 14.75 % tariff-rate quota on cells and a 14.75 % tariff on modules above the 5 GW in-quota threshold—for an additional four years at rates of 12 % declining to 8 % by Year 4. (__[USITC Staff Report — needs verification](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/safeguards/ta-201-075-staff-report-2026/)__) The Commission's Section 201 analysis under 19 U.S.C. § 2253(e)(3) requires the President to act within 60 days of receipt of the report, placing a presidential decision deadline around July 15, 2026. The Association of Southeast Asian Nations solar-panel exporters—who received country-specific exclusions from earlier 201 orders under the *Solar Energy Industries Ass'n v. United States*, CIT No. 23-00066, consent agreement—are filing comments at the USITC arguing that the new rate recommendation should incorporate the exclusion framework rather than requiring a separate presidential proclamation. (__[Reuters — needs verification](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/usitc-solar-safeguard-review-recommendation-2026/)__)

### CAFC Watch: De Minimis IEEPA Elimination — Statutory Challenge

A coalition of e-commerce importers—led by Shein Operations USA, LLC and CPEX Logistics, Inc.—filed a complaint on May 16, 2026, in the CIT, captioned *Shein Operations USA, LLC v. United States*, CIT No. 26-00089 (assigned to Judge Timothy Stanceu), challenging the President's elimination of the de minimis exemption (19 U.S.C. § 1321) for goods of Chinese origin effective May 2, 2025, under Executive Order 14256 (90 Fed. Reg. 14,635). (__[Law360 — needs verification](https://www.law360.com/articles/shein-de-minimis-cit-challenge-2026/)__) Plaintiffs argue that the de minimis threshold is set by statute at $800 and may not be overridden by executive action under IEEPA because § 1321(a)(2)(C) is a mandatory ministerial duty rather than a discretionary tariff imposition—distinguishing the line of cases permitting IEEPA rate modifications. The government's answer is due June 30, 2026. The case intersects with pending CBP rulemaking (CBP-2025-0018, 90 Fed. Reg. 22,800) proposing a tiered de minimis processing fee for non-Chinese low-value shipments, which could undermine the remedy plaintiffs seek if finalized before a CIT ruling. (__[Diaz Trade Law — needs verification](https://www.diaztradelaw.com/de-minimis-shein-cit-2026/)__)

## Recommended Actions

Counsel representing importers in the Rewind Tariffs refund vertical should immediately audit their client portfolios for entries liquidated between February 2, 2025, and August 31, 2025, under Proclamation 10908 to confirm that CBP Form 19 protests have been filed within the mandatory 180-day window under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3), because the CIT's scheduling order in *V.O.S. Selections*, Consol. No. 25-00066, suggests no merits ruling before July 2026—leaving a six-month gap during which unliquidated entries could become final; simultaneously, monitor the Federal Circuit's decision in *Emily Ley Paper, Inc.*, No. 26-1382, expected by mid-July, to assess whether district-court-filed IEEPA challenges must be transferred to the CIT and, if so, prepare conforming CIT complaints in advance; for clients with China-sourced supply chains, build snap-back trigger clauses referencing the August 10, 2026, deadline under Proclamation 10977 into all purchase orders and freight contracts, price the 34 % differential as a contingency in hedging models, and file comments by June 30, 2026, in USTR Docket USTR-2026-0005 opposing any above-25 % escalation on strategic-technology HTS subheadings (8542, 8507.60, 8501.71); and, for solar-industry clients, submit amicus-level comment to the USITC on the TA-201-075 safeguard review before the President's July 15, 2026, decision deadline, arguing that the ASEAN exclusion framework established in *Solar Energy Industries Ass'n v. United States*, CIT No. 23-00066, must be expressly preserved in any extension proclamation.
