## Analysis & Developments

### CAPE Phase 2 Goes Live Today — $28.7B in Reconciliation-Flagged Entries Now Eligible

The single most operationally consequential development today: 
CBP confirmed via CSMS #69035485 on June 23, 2026 that Phase 2 of the IEEPA CAPE refund process deployed in the ACE Portal on June 29, 2026.
 
Phase 2 covers entries flagged for reconciliation (entry types 01, 02, and 06) where a corresponding reconciliation entry (type 09) has not yet been filed, limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation, consistent with Phase 1.
 The dollar exposure is material: 
CBP estimates Phase 2 will cover approximately 2.8 million additional entries with potential refunds of approximately $28.7 billion, bringing combined Phase 1 and Phase 2 coverage to roughly $130 billion of the $166 billion total.


The Phase 1 throughput numbers, disclosed at the June 9, 2026 hearing before CIT Judge Richard Eaton, frame the baseline. 
Phase 1 of CAPE has accepted claims covering approximately $90 billion of the total $166 billion in IEEPA tariffs collected, and approximately $23 billion has been approved and transmitted to the U.S. Department of the Treasury for refund.
 The processing velocity is notable: 
in all of the last fiscal year, CBP processed only 338,000 entries with tariff refunds of any type; in just the first six weeks of CAPE Phase 1, CBP processed IEEPA refunds on nearly 8.5 million entries.
 
Importers and authorized brokers should anticipate that valid IEEPA refunds are generally issued within 60–90 days following acceptance of a CAPE Declaration, unless a compliance concern requires further CBP review.


One procedural wrinkle introduced with today's deployment: 
effective June 29, 2026, CAPE will accept entries flagged for reconciliation for which the reconciliation entry (type 09) has not been filed, limited to unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation.
 
Entries flagged for reconciliation with the reconciliation entry already on file are not included in the June 29, 2026 CAPE deployment.
 Those filers will wait for a future phase. Importers whose entries carry reconciliation flags and whose liquidation deadlines are pressing should act with urgency: 
if the reconciliation filing deadline is close to expiring — e.g., less than 30 days — the trade will need to prioritize the filing of the reconciliation.

### Phase 3 and the Finally-Liquidated Entry Problem — The Critical Bifurcation

The refund landscape is bifurcated in a way that is now hardening into a permanent disadvantage for non-litigants. 
Phase 3, targeted for late July 2026, will cover finally-liquidated entries — those liquidated more than 80 days before the refund period. Critically, the government has stated that Phase 3 refunds will be processed only for importers who have filed lawsuits at the CIT.
 The downstream consequence: 
approximately 4,000 plaintiff importers who have filed refund lawsuits are expected to have all of their IEEPA tariffs refunded through CAPE Phases 1, 2, and 3 combined, regardless of liquidation status. Under the government's current position, importers who have not filed suit will not receive Phase 3 refunds through CAPE.


This is not a theoretical risk. 
CBP has said any importer whose entries have finally liquidated and who has not filed its own claim at the CIT will not be eligible for a refund unless and until there is a court order in favor of such importer — and CBP says this issue is on appeal, so until it is settled, CBP will not expand CAPE in this context.
 The government's appeal referenced here is the Euro-Nations Federal Circuit appeal. 
The Euro-Nations case is on appeal at the Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, Case No. 26-1898, filed June 2, 2026.
 That appeal directly contests the CIT's authority to order refunds of finally-liquidated entries for non-litigants. Its outcome determines whether the non-litigant universe — the overwhelming majority of the roughly 330,000 importers who paid IEEPA duties — gets made whole or is permanently foreclosed. 
Counsel's practical advice: get a CIT complaint filed before the end of January 2027.


On submission quality: 
submission delays continue to occur as importers navigate CAPE filing requirements and validation procedures, and data formatting issues, CSV preparation requirements, and entry-level validation errors continue to create friction.
 
CBP has strongly inferred it is using the CAPE data for enforcement purposes.
 A fast, defective submission is materially worse than a slower, audited one.

### Seattle Consolidation — Amazon IEEPA Consumer Pass-Through Suits Merged

Overnight, the litigation landscape at the retail-consumer layer shifted. 
A federal judge in Seattle consolidated a pair of proposed class actions brought by Amazon customers looking to recover millions of dollars in refunds for the now-invalidated IEEPA tariffs, as the two suits made essentially identical allegations.
 The docket sits in the 
U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington
 — the same venue where Hagens Berman originally filed the Markland/Cartagenova complaint in May 2026.

The consolidated theory: 
Amazon collected hundreds of millions of dollars in unlawful tariff costs from consumers by raising prices on imported goods while IEEPA tariffs were in effect, is now legally entitled to recover those costs in full from the federal government, yet has refused to seek a refund not because it lacks a legal basis to do so, but because it seeks to curry favor with Trump by allowing the federal government to retain the funds.
 The class period covers 
purchases made between February 4, 2025 and February 20, 2026, the day the Supreme Court voted 6-3 to strike down the reciprocal IEEPA tariffs.


This matters to importers and rights-holders on the refund side for a structural reason. 
The tariffs were ruled illegal by the Supreme Court in February, and the Court of International Trade confirmed in early March that the right to reclaim those tariffs rests solely with importers of record.
 Plaintiffs in the Seattle consumer suits are therefore not pursuing a CBP refund directly — they are pursuing Amazon as a constructive trustee over CBP refunds Amazon would receive as importer of record. The legal theory (Washington Consumer Protection Act + unjust enrichment) is novel and untested. A preliminary injunction requiring Amazon to file CAPE claims would create a precedent that reaches every large retailer-importer that has not yet filed. 
Nike, Shein, Temu, Costco, and Lululemon are all embroiled in similar class-action litigation.
 The CBP pipeline figure underscores the stakes: 
CBP has said that up to $166 billion in tariffs collected under the IEEPA statute could be reimbursed, depending on the total refund requests.

### Section 122 — 24 Days to Sunset; Federal Circuit Stay Holds


The Section 122 tariffs took effect February 24, 2026 and are scheduled to remain in place for a 150-day period, expiring July 24, 2026.
 
The permanent injunction issued against collection of the Section 122 tariffs extends only to three importer plaintiffs found to have standing: the State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Inc., and Basic Fun, Inc. All other importers remain subject to the Section 122 duties unless they bring their own action, the administration withdraws the tariffs, the tariffs expire, or a court orders broader relief.


The appellate posture has not changed materially since our last update. 
The government appealed on May 8, 2026 — the day after the CIT's opinion — and on May 12, 2026, the Federal Circuit granted an immediate administrative stay such that the CIT's permanent injunction is temporarily stayed while the appellate court considers a more prolonged stay pending appeal.
 
The stay allows the government to continue collecting the 10% Section 122 surcharge from the named plaintiffs who had previously won relief, and effectively pauses that relief for all importers throughout the duration of the appeals process.


The 2-1 CIT majority (Chief Judge Mark Barnett and Judge Claire Kelly) held that the administration failed the statutory predicate: 
the administration failed to demonstrate the existence of a balance-of-payments deficit as understood in 1974 when Section 122 was enacted, instead improperly relying on the existence of persistent trade deficits or current-account deficits.
 The dissent (Judge Stanceu) contested the majority's reading of legislative history. That split makes the Federal Circuit briefing consequential — the government's substantive argument that modern economic measures satisfy the 1974 statutory standard has at least one judicial supporter.

Watch for the administration's broader play: 
the administration reportedly intends for its wide-ranging Section 301 investigations to conclude this summer before the expiration of the Section 122 tariffs
 — effectively engineered to have replacement tariff authority ready on July 25. Importers who have paid Section 122 duties should immediately assess liquidation status and protest deadlines; 
even if the CIT's decision is upheld on appeal, further litigation could be required for additional importers to obtain tariff refunds, and importers must weigh the potential for an injunction against the possibility that follow-on litigation will be stayed pending the Federal Circuit appeal.

### USTR Opens Section 301 Investigation Against Germany — Pharmaceutical Pricing

This development sits squarely in the replacement-tariff pipeline. 
On June 18, 2026, the U.S. Trade Representative initiated a Section 301 investigation with respect to Germany's persistent underpayment for innovative pharmaceutical products.
 The docket numbers are 
USTR-2026-0463 and USTR-2026-0464.
 The investigation was self-initiated — no petitioner — and is filed under 
Section 302(b)(1)(A) of the Trade Act, which authorizes the U.S. Trade Representative to initiate an investigation to determine whether an act, policy, or practice of a foreign country is actionable as unreasonable or discriminatory conduct burdening U.S. commerce.


The factual predicate USTR placed on the record: 
U.S. consumers pay approximately 3.9 times as much as the prices consumers in Germany pay for brand-name drugs.
 Two specific German policies are in the crosshairs: 
confidentiality-conditioned discounts requiring a mandatory 9% price reduction, and a 2026 draft law proposing variable rebates on patented medicines that industry estimates could reach 20% by 2030.
 More precisely on the legislative trajectory: 
the legislation would introduce a 3.5% additional mandatory rebate for patented medicines during the first half of 2027, after which the rebate would become variable based on actual drug spending compared to target health-insurance outlays, with one estimate projecting this dynamic rebate could reach 20% by 2030.


The strategic architecture matters: 
together, Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on imported pharmaceuticals and the new Section 301 investigation form a coordinated two-statute strategy to pressure manufacturers to onshore production and foreign governments to raise reimbursement prices.
 Available remedies if USTR makes an affirmative finding: 
tariffs, import restrictions, retaliation on German goods, denial of trade benefits, or a negotiated resolution — and USTR has already requested consultations with Germany under Section 303(a) of the Trade Act.


Process calendar: 
the docket for submission of written comments opened June 25, 2026; written comments and requests to appear at the hearing are due August 10, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. EDT; the Section 301 Committee will convene a public hearing on September 22, 2026.
 The UK precedent is the administration's stated template: 
on April 2, 2026, the United States and the United Kingdom announced an arrangement on pharmaceutical pricing that will help drive investment and innovation in both countries.
 Germany is being told explicitly to follow that model. For pharmaceutical importers, supply-chain counsel, and GCs with German-origin drug procurement exposure, the August 10 comment deadline is the first hard action date.

### Atmus Filtration / V.O.S. Selections — Residual Appellate Posture

The government's appeal from Judge Eaton's CIT reliquidation orders remains the structural backstop for the entire CAPE program's legal validity. The operative CIT cases are *Atmus Filtration, Inc. v. United States*, CIT No. 26-1259, and *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. United States*, CIT No. 25-00066. 
Following the Supreme Court's *Learning Resources* decision, the CIT ordered the federal government to refund all IEEPA tariff payments to all importers of record, then partially stayed compliance with those orders while CBP developed CAPE and provided the court with weekly status updates.
 
Judge Eaton denied CBP's motion to limit reliquidation to litigants, and the government filed an appeal on June 2, 2026
 — that is Euro-Nations, Fed. Cir. No. 26-1898. The government's theory on appeal: 
unless an importer had obtained its own CIT judgment, the agency lacked the ability to issue IEEPA duty refunds for finally-liquidated entries, on the grounds that "once an entry is finally liquidated, CBP has no authority to reliquidate or refund money without a court order."

## Recommended Actions

Importers with entries flagged for reconciliation should file CAPE Phase 2 declarations today through ACE — the 80-day liquidation window governs eligibility and entries approaching that boundary will become ineligible without further notice; confirm with your customs broker that all Phase 1 declarations passed validation at the entry level (roughly 30% of Phase 1 submissions failed initial validation and required correction per CBP's May 26 data) and that ACH account information is registered with CBP via Form 4811 to avoid transmission holds at Treasury. Any importer whose IEEPA entries have finally liquidated and who has not yet filed at CIT should assess the Phase 3 exposure immediately — the government's current position is that non-litigants are permanently foreclosed from finally-liquidated refunds absent a court order, and the Euro-Nations Federal Circuit appeal (No. 26-1898) will likely take months to resolve; a protective complaint at CIT filed under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i) before the end of January 2027 is the minimum preserve-the-rights step. On Section 122: with the statutory sunset on July 24, 2026 now 24 days out, the window to file CIT protective actions and preserve refund rights on Section 122 duties is closing — file protests under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 now and document entry-level Section 122 duty payments for future refund claims if the Federal Circuit ultimately upholds the CIT's invalidation ruling. Pharmaceutical importers and U.S. pharma companies with German market exposure should calendar August 10, 2026 as the USTR-2026-0463/0464 comment deadline and engage trade counsel to assess whether to submit testimony or written comments on the German AMNOG pricing procedures — the evidentiary record built in this investigation will directly drive USTR's determination and any subsequent Section 301 tariff action against German-origin pharmaceuticals.
