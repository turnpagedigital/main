# ⚖️ Tariffs / Trade | Wednesday, July 22, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's Deltas:** Three things happen at once this morning. First, 
at 12:01 a.m. ET on July 22, a 25% ad valorem tariff took effect on many goods originating in Brazil
 under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 — the administration's most significant new tariff action since the IEEPA regime fell in February. Second, the 150-day Section 122 clock is now 48 hours from expiration, with no legislative extension pending and no presidential authority to toll it. Third, Senior Judge Richard K. Eaton issued an order dated July 17 — made available yesterday — directing CBP to reliquidate finally liquidated IEEPA entries for all plaintiffs who filed individual CIT suits, establishing the procedural gateway to CAPE Phase 3 while simultaneously naming a new lead case. Each of these developments reshapes refund math and exposure calculations for any GC or CFO tracking this space.

---

### I. Section 301 Brazil Tariff — In Effect as of This Morning


On July 15, 2026, USTR, at the specific direction of the president, published a Federal Register notice of action imposing an additional duty on goods originating from Brazil under Sections 301(b) and 304(a) of the Trade Act of 1974.
 The investigation, 
which concluded a yearlong inquiry into six categories of Brazilian conduct
, targeted digital trade and electronic payment services, preferential tariffs, anti-corruption enforcement, IP protection, ethanol market access, and illegal deforestation. 
USTR received and reviewed over 360 written comments, and on July 6 and 7 held a public hearing at which 77 witnesses testified.



The additional duty applies to substantially all imports of Brazil-origin goods, subject only to enumerated exemptions. Every importer of Brazilian-origin goods must determine, product by product, whether its imports fall within an exemption or bear the additional duty.
 
Exemptions carve out specified raw materials, civil aircraft and parts, pharmaceutical-application articles, goods already subject to Section 232 duties, humanitarian donations, informational materials, and accompanied personal baggage. Named carve-outs also include beef, orange juice, and energy products.
 
A narrow in-transit grace period runs through July 29, 2026. No additional product-specific exclusion process has been announced, and no phase-in, sunset, or de minimis threshold applies.


The stacking exposure is the immediate planning problem. 
The 25% Section 301 tariff is in addition to the existing 10% Section 122 surcharge on Brazil, which is itself set to expire July 24, 2026.
 
That means importers sourcing from Brazil now have three moving parts to track simultaneously — the 25% Section 301 duty, the expiring 122 surcharge, and USTR's separate 46-country forced-labor Section 301 deadline.
 The Section 301 Brazil duty does not expire; unlike the Section 122 surcharge, it carries no statutory sunset, and the administration has announced no plans to negotiate its removal.

### II. Section 122 — 48 Hours to Expiration, Refund Path Uncertain


The 10% global import surcharge imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132) is scheduled to expire by operation of law at 12:01 a.m. EDT on July 24, 2026 — 150 days after it took effect on February 24. Section 122 caps a balance-of-payments surcharge at 150 days unless Congress affirmatively extends it, and no extension legislation is pending. Absent congressional action, the surcharge terminates on its own terms.


That statutory expiration is legally distinct from the pending appellate challenge. On May 7, 2026, 
a divided three-judge panel at the CIT ruled in *Oregon v. United States* and *Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States* that the administration exceeded its authority under Section 122, finding that the statute requires the president to demonstrate a specific balance-of-payments deficit as defined in formal economic accounting terms — not the broader merchandise trade deficits the administration cited.
 The CIT issued Slip Op. 26-47 in Court Nos. 26-01472 and 26-01606, 
but declined to enter a universal injunction, limiting relief to the importer plaintiffs: the State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Inc., and Basic Fun, Inc.



The government appealed on May 8. On May 12, the Federal Circuit issued an administrative stay, suspending the CIT's injunction while the appellate court considered a more durable stay pending appeal.
 
The CIT separately denied the government's own motion for a stay pending appeal on May 20, finding the government had failed to demonstrate irreparable harm or likelihood of success on the merits. The Federal Circuit's administrative stay nonetheless remained in place.
 
On June 11, 2026, the Federal Circuit granted the government's motion to stay the CIT's injunction in full, allowing the government to continue collecting the 10% surcharge from all plaintiffs, including the State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, and Basic Fun.


The practical effect of Thursday's expiration: 
Capital Economics estimates the average effective tariff rate would fall to roughly 7.2 percent — a swing of nearly six percentage points overnight.
 The Federal Circuit appeal does not become moot on July 24; the question of whether unlawfully collected Section 122 duties must be refunded will survive expiration and will likely be governed by the IEEPA refund precedent from *Euro-Notions* and *V.O.S. Selections* (19 U.S.C. § 1514 protests; § 1581(i) CIT jurisdiction). Importers who have not yet filed § 1514 protests on Section 122 payments should act immediately — the 180-day protest window is the outer boundary, and entries liquidated in January 2026 are approaching it.

### III. CAPE Phase 3 — July 17 CIT Order Unlocks the Gate; Lead Case Switches Again

The most consequential development in the IEEPA refund stream this week came July 17, when Judge Eaton issued an order in *Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States*, 
directing CBP to "reliquidate, without regard to IEEPA duties, any and all of Plaintiffs' entries that have been liquidated for more than 80 days and on which Plaintiffs made estimated deposits pursuant to IEEPA."
 The order followed 
the CIT's July 15 order — the clearest indication to date of next steps for finally liquidated entries — establishing that the court will issue case-specific reliquidation orders across approximately 3,700 individual IEEPA cases.


The mechanics require a preliminary step: 
to successfully submit a CAPE declaration for finally liquidated entries, plaintiffs must first submit to CBP their importer of record identification number(s); CBP will then issue instructions for making the CAPE declaration submission.
 
CBP is next required to file a report on the progress of Phase 3 on August 4, 2026, with a closed settlement conference scheduled for August 5.


Lead-case status shifted again. 
The CIT announced that the plaintiff in Euro-Notions intends to voluntarily dismiss its case and that the new lead case will be *Freestyle World Inc. v. United States*.
 
The new lead case is *Freestyle World, Inc. v. United States of America*, Court No. 1:26-cv-01088.
 The Euro-Notions case (No. 25-00595) had itself only become the lead vehicle in April after Atmus Filtration voluntarily dismissed (Case No. 26-01259) on April 6. Three lead cases in four months is not a sign of a settled pipeline.

The critical caveat: 
the federal government continues to contest the legal basis for refunds on finally liquidated entries, and it remains unclear whether refunds will actually flow once the technical capability is in place.
 
CAPE Phase 3 is available only to importers who have filed at the CIT
 — CBP's position, sustained on appeal, is that a universal refund order covering non-litigants exceeds the CIT's authority. 
Phase 3 covers roughly $11.4 billion in IEEPA duties sitting on finally liquidated entries, and the government has appealed the CIT's universal-refund orders to the Federal Circuit, maintaining that CBP lacks authority to reopen finally liquidated entries unless the importer filed an individual lawsuit.


The aggregate scale gives context to the urgency. 
According to declarations filed with the CIT, around 330,000 importers paid or deposited an estimated $166 billion in IEEPA duties spread across more than 53 million entries.
 
So far, CBP has processed more than 16 million entries under Phase 1. CBP has accepted more than $89 billion in potential and certified IEEPA refunds for processing, of which roughly $22 billion has moved to the U.S. Treasury queue.
 The $11.4 billion in Phase 3 exposure is the contested slice, and the Federal Circuit appeal — *Euro-Notions*, No. 26-1898, filed June 2 — is the governing instrument.

### IV. IEEPA Appellate Posture — The Road That Got Us Here

For context carried forward: 
on February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court issued a landmark decision in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* (No. 24-1287) and *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc.* (No. 25-250), holding 6-3 that IEEPA does not grant the president authority to impose tariffs.
 
The Federal Circuit dissolved the stay on its mandate and ordered it to "issue forthwith" in *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. Trump*, Nos. 2025-1812, -1813 (Fed. Cir. Mar. 2, 2026) (en banc per curiam).
 The CIT refund machinery — including the March 27 and April 7 orders from Judge Eaton and CBP's April 20 CAPE Phase 1 launch — flows directly from that mandate.

The government's replacement strategy (Section 122 → Section 301) has now completed its first rotation. Section 122 expires Thursday. 
The administration has an array of other tariff authorities on which it can rely; in particular, it recently initiated wide-ranging Section 301 investigations, which authorize tariffs on countries engaged in unjustifiable, unreasonable, or discriminatory trade practices.
 Brazil is the first final Section 301 action to take effect; additional country-specific actions are expected. The White House has publicly telegraphed further trade actions "soon," consistent with the pattern of using Section 301 as the post-IEEPA statutory vehicle.

### V. Refund Math and Protest Deadline Calculus

For importers and recovery counsel, the layered question is now: which entries are in which bucket, and what action is required before the window closes?


Effective July 7, 2026, warehouse entries are no longer accepted on CAPE Declarations; warehouse withdrawals continue to be accepted.
 That exclusion narrows the Phase 1/2 universe slightly. On the protest mechanics, 
the 180-day protest deadline under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 runs from liquidation. A timely protest preserves a claim regardless of how the Federal Circuit appeal resolves. Entries that liquidated in the first quarter of 2026 have protest windows closing this summer — this is the single most time-sensitive move on the board.



In March 2026, following the Supreme Court's ruling, the CIT issued an order directing nationwide refunds of unlawful IEEPA tariffs, including through liquidation of unliquidated entries and reliquidation of certain liquidated entries — even for importers that were not parties to the litigation.
 The government's Federal Circuit appeal contests precisely that breadth. If the Federal Circuit sides with DOJ, non-litigant importers without timely protests will likely have no recovery path on finally liquidated entries. The CIT's July 17 order functions as a partial answer: it pays the 3,700 litigants; it does not settle the question for the remaining universe.

## Recommended Actions

Importers and recovery counsel should move on three fronts simultaneously: (1) **Brazil Section 301** — audit all Brazil-origin supply chains for HTS-level applicability before the July 29 in-transit grace period closes; confirm whether any goods qualify for the Section 232 carve-out or the enumerated commodity exclusions (beef, orange juice, energy, aircraft parts, pharmaceuticals), and begin origin-documentation audits for any goods where mixed-origin claims are plausible; (2) **Section 122 protests** — pull liquidation dates for any entries subject to the 10% Section 122 surcharge; any entry liquidated on or before approximately January 24, 2026 is inside or approaching the 180-day § 1514 window and requires an immediate protest decision, because the July 24 expiration does not extinguish refund rights but does not create them either — only a timely protest or CIT filing does; and (3) **CAPE Phase 3 / IEEPA finally liquidated entries** — if your finally liquidated IEEPA exposure exceeds approximately $500,000, file a protective CIT action now; the government's position is explicit that Phase 3 pays only plaintiffs, the Federal Circuit appeal remains live in *Euro-Notions* No. 26-1898, and the August 4 CBP progress report to Judge Eaton in *Freestyle World* (No. 1:26-cv-01088) will be the next dispositive filing; importers who have not filed should treat this week as a hard deadline for strategy confirmation.
