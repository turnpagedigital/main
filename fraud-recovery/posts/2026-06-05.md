## Theme: The bank-liability track — where the real money in a Ponzi recovery often is

### LinkedIn

When a $328 million Ponzi collapses, victims instinctively look to the receiver to claw money back from the people who got out early. That's one recovery vector. It is usually not the largest one.

The larger one is the banks.

On May 6, a class action was filed against the banks and payment processors that moved money through the Goliath Ventures scheme. The theory is the one that made the Madoff and Stanford recoveries work: financial institutions sitting on transaction-pattern data had the means to see red-flag signals consistent with a Ponzi, and didn't act. Irving Picard recovered billions for Madoff victims largely from feeder funds and banks — not from clawing back individual net winners.

Why the institution track matters for an ordinary victim: it is litigation-finance-subsidized, it reaches defendants with the balance sheets to actually pay, and it can recover value the receiver's clawback program never touches. The trade-off is time. Institution-facing outcomes in Goliath are unlikely before 2027.

But the discovery-preservation clock is already running. The complaint, by being filed, attaches preservation obligations to the implicated institutions — and it creates the same obligation for you. If you were a Goliath investor, confirm your class-member registration and preserve every bank record, account statement, and communication with the firm. The bank-liability case is built on exactly that documentary record.

Two recovery tracks, different timelines, different payors. Sophisticated victims work both.
