# Ponzi / Fraud Recovery — Posts for Friday, May 29, 2026

## LinkedIn

If you made money in a Ponzi scheme, the receiver can still come for it. That surprises people every time.

On Tuesday, the receiver in the Goliath Ventures matter — a $328 million alleged crypto fraud in receivership since March in Broward County Circuit Court — files a status update. For investors who got out with more than they put in, that filing is worth reading closely. It is the clearest signal yet of when the clawback phase begins.

Here's the doctrine that catches "net winners" off guard. Under fraudulent-transfer law, the money paid to early investors in a Ponzi scheme came from later investors' principal — not from real returns, because there were none. A receiver can therefore claw back the profit a net winner received, and in many cases the principal too, to redistribute it across all victims pro rata. The investor did nothing wrong, took the returns in good faith, and can still owe them back.

The standard timeline: clawback motions tend to begin 12 to 18 months after a receiver is appointed. Goliath is roughly three months in. That is exactly the window in which to act — not when the demand letter arrives.

What protects a net winner is contemporaneous documentation: bank records showing what you put in, what you took out, and when. The good-faith and net-equity defenses turn on those records. Reconstructing them after a demand lands is far harder and far less persuasive.

If you have exposure to Goliath — or to Paramount Management or First Liberty, both in the SEC's FY2025 enforcement results — pull your records this week. The quiet before the clawback wave is the time to prepare.
