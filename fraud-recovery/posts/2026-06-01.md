## LinkedIn

Tomorrow, the receiver in the Goliath Ventures matter files a status update — and if you took money out of that $328 million scheme, it's the document you should be reading first.

Goliath, a Miami crypto investment firm, has been under a Broward County receivership since March 3 on allegations of a $328 million crypto Ponzi. The June 2 status update will set the baseline for two things that matter to every investor: the recovery percentage the estate is tracking, and the timeline on which clawback motions against net-winner investors will begin.

Here's the part most people miss. If you withdrew more than you put in, the receiver can claw back the difference — and, in many cases, more — under fraudulent-conveyance law. The Ponzi-scheme presumption means the receiver often doesn't have to prove you knew anything was wrong. "Even the winners lose" is not a slogan; it's the doctrine.

Clawback waves typically start 12–18 months after appointment, which puts Goliath's in the first half of 2027. That sounds far away. It isn't — because your defense quality depends almost entirely on records you should be assembling now, while they're still accessible: your investment basis, your proof of loss, your contemporaneous account statements.

If you were a Goliath investor, read tomorrow's filing, then start your documentation file. The work is cheap today and expensive in 2027.
