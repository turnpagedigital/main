## Recent Doctrine

**The Ponzi-scheme presumption.** Establishing that an enterprise operated as a Ponzi is enough to satisfy "actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud" under § 548(a)(1)(A) and state UVTA analogues; the trustee/receiver does not need to prove debtor intent transfer-by-transfer. ([St. John's Bankruptcy Library](https://scholarship.law.stjohns.edu/bankruptcy_research_library/384/); [Lexology – Ponzi Presumption in Avoidance Actions](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1855d3d7-afc5-45e3-9528-133ec7c69231)) Stetson Business Law Review treatment ("Clawing Back Gains From Ponzi Schemes") catalogs the presumption and good-faith defense interplay ([Stetson – Zimmerman](https://www.stetson.edu/law/business-law-review/media/zimmerman.pdf)).

**Madoff trustee precedent (2d Cir.).** *In re Bernard L. Madoff Inv. Sec. LLC*, 12 F.4th 171 (2d Cir. 2021), revived hundreds of millions in trustee clawback actions, and the court has separately backed extraterritorial reach for foreign feeder-fund clawbacks. The trustee can still rely on the Ponzi presumption for § 548(a)(1)(A) actual-fraud claims even where § 546(e) safe harbor blocks constructive-fraud theories. ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2021/11/case-update-second-circuit-breathes-new-life-into-madoff-trustees-efforts-to-recover-ponzi-scheme-payments); [Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2019/03/lw-second-circuit-backs-foreign-clawback-claims-in-madoff-bankruptcy-action); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/new-us-second-circuit-court-of-appeals-ruling-in-madoff-liquidation-extends-the-long-arm-of-fraudulent-transfer-law); [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/bankruptcy-code-%C2%A7-546(e)-shields-madoff-ponzi-scheme-payments)) As of Feb 27, 2026, the **17th interim SIPA distribution** to Madoff customers exceeded $253M, bringing aggregate customer payout to ~$15.38B. ([SIPC](https://www.sipc.org/cases-and-claims/how-a-liquidation-works))

**"Even the winners lose."** Norton Rose Fulbright's canonical framing — net winners must disgorge fictitious profits to fund pro rata distribution to net losers — rooted in *Boale, Wood & Co. v. Whitmore* (BCSC) and Alberta's *Titan Investments*. Useful client-facing label that translates well to U.S. UVTA/§ 548 mechanics. ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/a2e6cab3/even-the-winners-lose-in-a-ponzi-scheme-payments-made-to-investors-in-a-ponzi-scheme-clawed-back-as-fraudulent-conveyances))

**Good-faith defense / § 548(c) — narrowed.** Once the trustee establishes the Ponzi presumption, burden shifts to the transferee to prove receipt in good faith *and* for reasonably equivalent value. The Second Circuit and SDNY have rejected a "futility exception," holding that a recipient on inquiry notice of insolvency or fraud must investigate even if investigation would not have changed anything — failure to investigate forfeits the § 548(c) defense. ([ABI – Futility Exception](https://www.abi.org/feed-item/fraudulent-transfer%E2%80%99s-good-faith-defense-a-futility-exception-to-investigation); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/ruling-explains-and-extends-application-of-good-faith-defense-to-fraudulent-transfer-actions-in-sipa-context))

**Distribution doctrine.** Harvard Law Review's treatment of restitution-and-equity in multi-victim frauds favors pro rata "rising tide" or "net investment" methods — the framework most receivers and SIPA trustees actually use. ([Harvard L. Rev. v.133](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-133/the-future-of-restitution-and-equity-in-the-distribution-of-funds-recovered-from-ponzi-schemes-and-other-multi-victim-frauds/))

## Cross-Border Friction

**28 U.S.C. § 1782** remains the U.S.-side workhorse for foreign-tribunal discovery in fraud cases. 2025 developments: 2d Cir. holds a foreign forum-selection clause can weigh against § 1782 relief where it suggests evasion of foreign proof-gathering rules; 9th Cir. confirms documents obtained for one foreign proceeding may be reused in related/subsequent foreign actions. ([Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/perspectives/2025/9/recent%20developments%20as%20to%20discovery%20applications%20under%2028%20us%20c%20section%201782); [Freshfields](https://blog.freshfields.us/post/102l6p5/u-s-discovery-for-foreign-disputes-whats-new-in-section-1782-litigation); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/section-1782-explained-guidance-on-powerful-tool-international-disputes/); [Miller & Chevalier](https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/us-discovery-use-foreign-proceedings-navigating-cross-border-discovery-under-28-usc-ss))

**Caribbean / shell-jurisdiction patterns.** BakerHostetler reports tracing $1B+ through cross-jurisdictional trust/company structures spanning Bermuda, BVI, Cayman, France, Liberia, Monaco, Switzerland, UK, US. ([BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/services/global-capabilities/global-fraud-and-international-asset-tracing-and-recovery/); [Lewis Baach](https://www.lbkmlaw.com/practices-International-Asset-Tracing-Investigation.html); [CDR – Fraud, Asset Tracing 2026 USA](https://www.cdr-news.com/cdr-essential-intelligence/fraud-asset-tracing-recovery/usa/)) BVI and Cayman remain the dominant offshore venues for recognition/enforcement and Norwich Pharmacal-style disclosure orders that pair with § 1782.

**Insurance recovery for victim entities (D&O / E&O / fidelity).** Three lanes: (1) D&O for entity and individual claims arising from third-party investor suits; (2) E&O/professional liability for advisor/feeder firms; (3) fidelity bonds for direct employee-dishonesty losses. ## Forfeiture vs. Receivership

In a typical collapse you get **three parallel tracks**: DOJ criminal forfeiture (21 U.S.C. § 853 / 18 U.S.C. § 981–982), SEC/CFTC civil receivership, and bankruptcy. MVRA forces prosecutors to use "best efforts" to compensate victims, but DOJ's regulatory mechanics often shortchange crypto victims because forfeited crypto is liquidated at U.S.M.S. auction values rather than tracked to victim wallets. ([Wilmer Hale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/documents/2013-10-01-the-interplay-between-forfeiture-and-restitution-in-complex-multivictim-white-collar-cases.pdf); [Bradley](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2024/12/crypto-fraud-victims-often-shortchanged-due-to-doj-regulation); [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/sbf-prosecution-raises-novel-issues-for-asset-forfeiture-and-victim-restitution))
