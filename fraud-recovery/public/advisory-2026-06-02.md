# 🕵️ Ponzi / Fraud Recovery | Tuesday, June 2, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today, June 2, is the calendared date for the *Goliath Ventures* receiver status update in the Broward County (Florida) Circuit Court — the filing that will set the asset-inventory and clawback-pipeline baseline on the $328 million alleged crypto-investment Ponzi. As of the morning's open, the status filing has not yet appeared on the public docket; it is expected to post intraday and should be obtained and reviewed the moment it does (__[Justice.gov — Goliath Ventures](https://www.justice.gov/usao-mdfl/goliath_ventures)__) (__[Ponzi Perspectives](https://www.ponziblog.com/2026/04/q1-2026-roundup/)__). The parallel Goliath civil class action, filed May 6, 2026 against third parties for allegedly assisting in the fraud — presenting claims against banks and payment processors under theories of aiding and abetting and negligent enablement — has deepened the recovery pipeline beyond the criminal forfeiture track and the receivership. The Okin Adams Bartlett Curry analysis of the litigation wave confirms that the bank-liability theory is the structural innovation of the Goliath recovery cycle, building on the established Madoff and Stanford precedents for financial-institution liability where red-flag signals were allegedly available to the institutions before the scheme collapsed (__[Okin Adams](https://www.okinadams.com/blog/goliath-ventures-collapse/)__). Alongside the Goliath pivot, the residual SEC enforcement cycle continues: *SEC v. Paramount Management Group, LLC* and *SEC v. First Liberty Building & Loan, LLC* — among the headline Ponzi cases in the agency's FY2025 enforcement disclosure — progress on their respective tracks, and the CFTC spring pipeline is expected to produce additional filings before the end of Q2. The Madoff residual disgorgement deadline in S.D.N.Y. on August 25 and the Stanford receiver's final distribution in N.D. Tex. in late July remain the next hard pivots.

The *Goliath Ventures* case continues to anchor this tab's analytical core. CEO Christopher Alexander Delgado was arrested on February 24, 2026, on wire fraud and money laundering charges, with prosecutors alleging that of the more than $300 million collected from investors, only approximately $1.5 million was ever deposited into a legitimate liquidity pool — with the remainder recycled to pay earlier investors and fund Delgado's personal expenditures (__[IRS Criminal Investigation](https://www.irs.gov/compliance/criminal-investigation/goliath-ventures-ceo-arrested-for-wire-fraud-and-money-laundering)__). The court-appointed receiver, Michael Budwick, was appointed March 3, 2026 and has been conducting asset inventory, document-preservation, and fund-tracing activities since appointment. Today's receiver status update is the 60-day reporting milestone under the standard receiver appointment order, and should detail: (a) the current value and location of recovered assets; (b) the status of cryptocurrency tracing — particularly USDT/USDC flow analysis through exchanges — given the crypto-denominated structure of the alleged scheme; (c) the receiver's preliminary assessment of the net-winner population and the timeline for commencing clawback litigation; and (d) any preliminary cross-border asset-tracing results. Investors who received returns above their net contribution from Goliath should anticipate a clawback demand 12–18 months from the March receivership appointment — placing the initial wave in the first half of 2027 — and should document investment basis and proof of loss now while contemporaneous records are accessible (__[Sonn Law Group](https://sonnlaw.com/goliath-ventures-investigation-lawsuit/)__).

## Third-Party Bank Liability Track — May 6 Class Action Filing

The May 6, 2026 class action complaint against banks and payment processors in the Goliath matter adds a parallel recovery vector that is analytically distinct from the receiver-led clawback and the criminal forfeiture. The bank-liability theory — that financial institutions with access to transaction-pattern data facilitated the scheme by ignoring red-flag signals — follows the Madoff and Stanford recovery playbooks in which the Irving Picard and Ralph Janvey receiver/trustee programs extracted significant recoveries from feeder funds and banking institutions. The Okin Adams Bartlett Curry blog notes that this litigation wave is expressly modeled on the *BLMIS* (Madoff) template: plaintiff counsel is pursuing transaction data from financial institutions for the discovery record before commencing individual aiding-and-abetting claims (__[Okin Adams](https://www.okinadams.com/blog/goliath-ventures-collapse/)__). If the Goliath bank-liability track follows the Madoff pattern, institution-facing settlements or litigation outcomes will not begin to emerge until 2027 or later, but discovery-preservation obligations for the financial institutions implicated in the complaint have already attached. Investors and victims should join the class action if they have not already done so, as the class-action recovery vehicle provides a litigation-finance-subsidized path to institution-facing recovery that the receiver's clawback track may not fully cover.

## SEC FY2025 Enforcement — Paramount Management, First Liberty, Q2 Pipeline

The SEC's FY2025 enforcement disclosure confirmed *Paramount Management Group, LLC* — an alleged $400 million Ponzi that defrauded approximately 2,700 investors — and *First Liberty Building & Loan, LLC* — an alleged $140 million Ponzi affecting approximately 300 investors — as the headline Ponzi enforcement cases of the fiscal year (__[SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-34)__). Both cases continue on their respective docket tracks. The Ponzi Perspectives Q1 2026 roundup confirmed that court-appointed receivers played a prominent role through the first quarter, filing clawback actions against "net winner" investors — the pattern that defines the Goliath, Paramount, and First Liberty recovery cycles as they mature. The SEC's spring enforcement pipeline is expected to accelerate through June–August 2026, with a Q2–Q3 new-complaint cluster consistent with the agency's pre-fiscal-year-end enforcement velocity. Practitioners should monitor the SEC enforcement actions database and CFTC enforcement releases daily for new asset-freeze orders and receiver appointments; the FY2026 pipeline is building.

## Madoff Residual Disgorgement and Stanford Final Distribution

The Madoff trustee *Irving Picard v. various financial institutions* residual disgorgement docket — with the August 25, 2026 tail-claim filing deadline in S.D.N.Y. the next hard pivot — continues through settlement-enforcement motions and advisor-disgorgement disputes. Cumulative Madoff customer recovery now exceeds $14 billion; the residual estate-asset pool is primarily composed of outstanding advisor-disgorgement and institution-level clawback proceedings. The Stanford receiver (*SEC v. R. Allen Stanford*) final distribution remains calendared for late July in N.D. Tex., representing the capstone of a recovery program that has returned over $2 billion to Stanford victims. Recovery practitioners should calendar the August 25 Madoff filing deadline and the late-July Stanford distribution as the summer's hard pivots on this tab.

Funders active in this space are also tracking the Paramount and First Liberty pipelines as they mature toward clawback-motion phase. The June 2 status update is expected to confirm whether the receiver has completed the forensic accounting that precedes clawback demand letters, which would accelerate the timeline from the standard 12–18 months (__[Wikipedia — Goliath Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_Ventures)__).

## Recommended Actions

Goliath Ventures investors and creditors should obtain today's receiver status update the moment it posts to the Broward County Circuit Court docket, review the recovery-percentage baseline and clawback-population assessment, and begin documenting investment basis, proof of loss, and wire-transfer records now in anticipation of a 2027 clawback demand. Investors who received returns above their net contribution (net winners) should retain counsel immediately to document the net-winner defense, including proof that distributions received were a return of invested principal rather than a profit — the standard Ponzi-clawback burden falls on the transferee to demonstrate good faith and value-given in a Ponzi-presumption jurisdiction. Victims and investors in the Goliath class action (May 6 filing) should confirm class-member registration and preserve all bank records, account statements, and communications with Goliath representatives for the discovery record supporting the bank-liability claims. Investors in *Paramount Management Group* and *First Liberty Building & Loan* should monitor the SEC enforcement docket for receiver appointment orders; once receivers are in place, proof-of-claim cycles typically open within 60–90 days. Madoff and Stanford residual claimants should track the August 25 S.D.N.Y. disgorgement deadline and late-July Stanford distribution, and review advisor contracts for indemnity language surviving the clawback agreement. Recovery counsel and litigation funders assessing the Goliath pipeline should model the 2027 clawback wave against the 60-day receiver report and begin term-sheet preparation now for high-net-winner targets within the receiver's anticipated clawback cohort.
