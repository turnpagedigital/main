Crypto Insolvency

Daily Advisory · Sunday, May 17, 2026
Briefing — Lead

The BlockFills Chapter 11 (Bankr. D. Del.) remains the central matter in the crypto-insolvency docket as of May 17, with the core legal question — customer-property characterization where the debtor allegedly commingled approximately $77 million in customer assets with operating funds — moving toward a contested first-day-style mini-trial over the next six weeks. The Dominion Capital pre-petition TRO freezing 70.6 BTC ($4.8M at the freeze date) continues in modified form post-petition under the standard 11 U.S.C. § 362 stay framework. No new major crypto Chapter 11 filings or Chapter 15 recognition orders surfaced over the May 15–17 window; the structural watchlist on stablecoin issuer distress and Hong Kong / Cayman parallel proceedings remains quiet but live.

I. BlockFills (Bankr. D. Del.) — Customer-Property Characterization Battle

BlockFills filed under Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026 in the District of Delaware following a February liquidity crisis and the freezing of customer withdrawals. The petition reported $50–100 million in assets and $100–500 million in liabilities. The animating dispute — and the question that will define the entire case under § 541, § 363, and the developing customer-property doctrine — is whether customer crypto held on the BlockFills platform constitutes property of the estate or property held in segregation for customers under a constructive-trust or true-deposit theory.

The Dominion Capital pre-petition litigation alleged that BlockFills commingled customer assets with operating funds on a single balance sheet, generating an approximately $77 million balance-sheet shortfall by year-end 2025. Judge issued a TRO freezing 70.6 BTC (then ~$4.8M) and barring offshore transfers. Post-petition, that injunction remains in modified force under the § 362 stay; the customer-property characterization is now teed up for the bankruptcy court as a contested matter likely to produce the first published opinion on commingled-crypto property questions since the Celsius and Voyager precedents.

The plan-confirmation pathway BlockFills has signaled — customer-led reorganization — will succeed or fail on the customer-property holding. If customer crypto is property of the estate, then a typical pro-rata distribution under § 1129(b) becomes feasible; if customer crypto is held in trust, the recovery posture inverts and the case becomes one of liquidation through § 363 sale plus distribution-against-trust-fund. The Bondoro case summary and the Disruption Banking analysis converge on the view that the court is likely to rely on the books-and-records evidence (single ledger, commingled trading) to find against segregation, but the customer-side argument continues to surface the deposit-account-control-agreement analogy.

II. Legacy Plan-Effectuation — Celsius, Genesis, FTX Distribution Mechanics

The three legacy major crypto chapter 11s — Celsius, Genesis, FTX — continue to run their distribution machinery. No major court orders over the May 15–17 window, but practitioners should note that the in-kind versus fiat distribution mechanics established by the FTX administrator (notably the dollar-denominated petition-date claim valuation combined with in-kind delivery for customers electing crypto distribution) is now serving as the working template for any subsequent confirmation order in the crypto-debtor space, including BlockFills. The Steptoe precedent on petition-date claim valuation continues to be cited as the controlling authority.

III. Stablecoin Issuer Watchlist

The stablecoin issuer distress watchlist remains active but quiet through May 17. No new depeg events, no new redemption-suspension filings, and no Chapter 11 indications from any of the major issuers. The structural risk — that a depeg event in a major stablecoin would generate redemption pressure faster than the reserves architecture could clear, triggering an emergency filing — remains the dominant tail risk for crypto-creditor practice in 2026.

IV. Hong Kong / Cayman / BVI Parallel Proceedings

The cross-border posture remains structurally unchanged. The Hong Kong provisional-liquidator framework and the Cayman court's increasing willingness to recognize U.S. Chapter 15 proceedings under the UNCITRAL Model Law have together stabilized the cross-border architecture for crypto cases. No new Chapter 15 petitions in the U.S. bankruptcy courts over May 15–17.

V. DeFi-as-Debtor Watchlist

The DeFi-protocol-as-debtor question — whether a smart-contract-resident protocol or a DAO can be a debtor under § 109 — remains experimental. No new test cases over the May 15–17 window. The Tornado Cash precedent on smart-contract authorship liability continues to anchor the discussion but does not directly resolve the bankruptcy question.

Recommended Actions

Restructuring counsel and creditor committees with BlockFills exposure should: (i) finalize their § 541 customer-property arguments well before the contested-matter hearing; (ii) prepare books-and-records discovery requests targeting the alleged commingling; (iii) evaluate the constructive-trust theory against the FTX/Celsius precedents. Distressed-debt buyers should: (iv) calibrate BlockFills claim-purchase pricing to the customer-property characterization range. Counsel monitoring stablecoin issuer distress should: (v) maintain the redemption-architecture stress models against potential depeg scenarios. Crypto-debtor plan-strategy counsel should: (vi) adapt FTX in-kind distribution mechanics for plan templating in any next major filing.
