## FTX Recovery Trust — Preferred Equity Distribution Funds Today

The *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* Recovery Trust set today, May 29, 2026, as the funding date for its first direct distribution to Preferred Equity Holders, following the April 30, 2026 record date for eligibility. This is the first direct payout to this class under the Preferred Shareholder Agreement (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/14/ftx-estate-sets-next-creditor-payout-date-as-genesis-digital-assets-fights-usd1-billion-clawback-suit)__). The Recovery Trust's most recent prior tranche — approximately $2.2 billion released on March 31, 2026 to creditors via BitGo, Kraken, and Payoneer — brought cumulative FTX creditor disbursements to approximately $10 billion since petition date; court projections continue to model total recovery rates of 50–70 percent for priority customer claims, subject to final asset realizations and administrative costs (__[Bitget Academy](https://www.bitget.com/academy/ftx-bankruptcy-payou)__). The pending $1 billion clawback litigation against Genesis Digital Assets remains unresolved, with Genesis pressing to dismiss the suit on grounds that the alleged transfers were arm's-length and lawful; the Recovery Trust continues to model the residual clawback recovery pool against that unresolved dispute. Preferred Equity Holders eligible for today's distribution should confirm wallet-onboarding completion and current tax-residency forms with the Recovery Trust administrator before the close of the funding cycle.

## BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group) — § 341 Meeting Completed, Segregation Accounting Now Live

*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* and three affiliated entities, filed March 15, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, continues to anchor the customer-property litigation track. The company reported $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities at the petition date, with the filing triggered by the February 2026 crypto market crash, compounding counterparty losses, a failed mining venture, and the Dominion Capital lawsuit alleging misappropriation of customer assets (__[Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-16/crypto-trading-firm-blockfills-files-for-chapter-11-in-delaware)__). The court's temporary restraining order freezing 70.6 BTC alleged to belong to Dominion Capital and blocking the movement of assets abroad remains in force. The court ordered BlockFills to account for and segregate customer funds, and the § 341 first-creditor meeting held Tuesday, May 27 in Wilmington opened that ledger-reconciliation record to creditor examination — the exercise that will set the boundary between customer property (priority recovery) and unsecured creditor claims (subordinated recovery) for the affected customer base (__[PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/crypto-firm-blockfills-files-for-bankruptcy-after-suspending-transactions/)__). The BlockFills docket is now the second-most-watched customer-property segregation test of 2026 after FTX progeny, and its outcome will feed how district courts treat commingled-asset claims in subsequent crypto-firm filings.

## Bitcoin Depot — First Major U.S. Crypto-ATM Chapter 11 of 2026

The *Bitcoin Depot* filing represents the first major U.S. crypto-ATM Chapter 11 of 2026 and reflects a structural shift in the crypto-accessibility market. Bitcoin Depot, Inc. (Nasdaq: BTM), the largest bitcoin-ATM operator in North America by kiosk count, filed its voluntary petition on May 18 in the Northern District of Georgia citing operational unsustainability driven by state regulatory crackdowns (Massachusetts and Iowa AG enforcement actions alleging facilitation of fraud schemes), the macro decline in BTC-kiosk utility as adoption shifted to mobile and web wallets, and financial deterioration evidenced by a 49 percent year-over-year revenue decline in Q1 2026 and a swing from $12.2 million in operating profit to a $9.5 million operating loss. The petition triggered immediate shutdown of all 9,276 kiosks across the United States, Canada, and Australia, stranding customer BTC transactions in progress. Crypto-ATM fraud losses reached $389 million in 2025, a 58 percent year-over-year increase, and that data point is the through-line in the Massachusetts and Iowa AG complaints now stayed under 11 U.S.C. § 362 (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/18/bitcoin-depot-once-north-america-s-largest-bitcoin-atm-operator-files-for-bankruptcy)__). At eleven days past first-day relief, the customer-property characterization of in-progress kiosk transactions remains the live question; holders should be filing proofs of claim now rather than waiting on the schedules.

## Celsius, Voyager, Genesis Capital, FTX International — Running Docket Threads

The broader cross-firm docket continues to advance on parallel tracks. *Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) remains in ongoing plan-implementation; the residual disclosure-statement work is expected to produce an updated creditor-distribution model in Q2–Q3 2026. *Voyager Digital* wind-down concluded in 2024, but residual customer-claim litigation continues. *Genesis Global Capital, LLC* customer-property segregation litigation in the Southern District of New York continues to test the boundary between segregated customer accounts and estate property, with creditor briefing converging on the *Celsius* precedent stack; claims against parent Digital Currency Group remain preserved for the post-wind-down entity to pursue for the benefit of creditors. The Genesis LOC creditor vote on the $3.2 billion DCG claims plan is calendared for June 18, now twenty days out. *FTX International* (Chapter 15 ancillary recognition proceeding) continues UK liquidator coordination, with cross-border recognition and creditor-voting timelines on track for Q3 disbursement.

## Recommended Actions

FTX Preferred Equity Holders eligible for today's May 29 distribution should confirm wallet-onboarding via BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer is complete and that tax-residency forms are current with the Recovery Trust administrator before the funding cycle closes. Holders of unresolved BlockFills positions should file proof of claim in the D. Del. proceeding as customer-property claims, obtain and review the § 341 examination record from the May 27 meeting for the debtor's segregation-accounting representations, and monitor the Dominion Capital adversary proceeding for guidance on the segregation-versus-commingling boundary. Holders of BTC kiosk transactions in progress at Bitcoin Depot's May 18 petition date should immediately file proof of claim in the N.D. Ga. proceeding as customer-property claims, with characterization aimed at top-priority recovery under the *Celsius* / *Genesis* segregation framework rather than as unsecured creditor claims. Genesis LOC creditors should finalize their voting posture on the $3.2 billion DCG claims plan ahead of the June 18 vote, weighting the preserved DCG litigation value against the certainty of the plan distribution. Crypto-custody and ATM operators in the surviving competitive set (Bitstop, Coin Cloud, RockItCoin) should expect consolidation pressure and elevated regulatory-tail risk pricing as state AG enforcement converges around the Massachusetts / Iowa template. Genesis Digital Assets creditors and trade counterparties should monitor the FTX clawback dismissal motion's adjudication, as a denial would materially expand the recovery-pool model in adjacent crypto-mining bankruptcies.
