# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Wednesday, May 20, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Three intertwined tracks define the crypto-insolvency docket on May 20: the *Bitcoin Depot, Inc.* Chapter 11 petition filed May 18, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Georgia continues to ripple through the bitcoin-ATM operator landscape, with all 9,276 kiosks dark and customer-property questions sharpening (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/18/bitcoin-depot-once-north-america-s-largest-bitcoin-atm-operator-files-for-bankruptcy)__) (__[Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/bitcoin-depot-goes-bankrupt-signaling-demise-of-crypto-atms)__); the *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* (BlockFills) Chapter 11 docket in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware continues to move on customer-property segregation issues, with the court-ordered freeze of 70.6 BTC alleged to belong to Dominion Capital still in force and the customer-asset misappropriation claims awaiting adjudication (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/05/u-s-judge-freezes-blockfills-assets-in-dispute-over-70-bitcoin-with-creditor-dominion-capital)__); and the *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* Recovery Trust has set May 29, 2026 as the funding date for its first direct distribution to Preferred Equity Holders, following the April 30 record-date cutoff (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/14/ftx-estate-sets-next-creditor-payout-date-as-genesis-digital-assets-fights-usd1-billion-clawback-suit)__). Customer-property doctrine — segregation versus estate property — continues to be the unifying analytical frame across all three tracks, with each filing testing variations of the *Celsius Network*, *Voyager Digital*, and *FTX International* precedent stack.

The *Bitcoin Depot* filing represents the first major U.S. crypto-ATM Chapter 11 of 2026 and reflects a structural shift in the crypto-accessibility market. Bitcoin Depot, Inc. (Nasdaq: BTM), the largest bitcoin-ATM operator in North America by kiosk count, filed its voluntary petition on May 18 in the Northern District of Georgia citing operational unsustainability driven by state regulatory crackdowns (Massachusetts and Iowa AG enforcement actions alleging facilitation of fraud schemes), the macro decline in BTC-kiosk utility as institutional and retail adoption shifted to mobile and web wallets, and financial deterioration evidenced by a 49 percent year-over-year revenue decline in Q1 2026 and a swing from $12.2 million in operating profit to a $9.5 million operating loss. The Chapter 11 petition triggered immediate shutdown of all 9,276 kiosks across the United States, Canada, and Australia, stranding customer BTC transactions in progress. Crypto-ATM fraud losses reached $389 million in 2025, a 58 percent year-over-year increase, and that data point is the through-line in the Massachusetts and Iowa AG complaints that have been stayed under 11 U.S.C. § 362's automatic stay (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/18/bitcoin-depot-once-north-america-s-largest-bitcoin-atm-operator-files-for-bankruptcy)__).

## BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group) — Customer-Property Segregation in D. Del.

*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* and three affiliated entities, filed March 15, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, continues to anchor the customer-property litigation track in the crypto-insolvency docket. The company reported $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities at the petition date, with the filing triggered by the February 2026 crypto market crash, compounding counterparty losses, a failed mining venture, and the Dominion Capital lawsuit alleging misappropriation of customer assets (__[Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-16/crypto-trading-firm-blockfills-files-for-chapter-11-in-delaware)__). The court's temporary restraining order freezing 70.6 BTC alleged to belong to Dominion Capital and blocking the movement of assets abroad remains in force. The court ordered BlockFills to account for and segregate customer funds, and that ledger-reconciliation exercise will set the boundary between customer property (priority recovery) and unsecured creditor claims (subordinated recovery) for the affected customer base (__[PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/crypto-firm-blockfills-files-for-bankruptcy-after-suspending-transactions/)__). The BlockFills docket is now the second-most-watched customer-property segregation test of 2026 after FTX progeny, and the outcome will feed into how district courts treat commingled-asset claims in subsequent crypto-firm filings.

## FTX Recovery Trust — Preferred Equity Distribution May 29

The *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* Recovery Trust has set May 29, 2026 as the funding date for its first direct distribution to Preferred Equity Holders, following the April 30, 2026 record date for eligibility. This marks the first direct payout to this class under the Preferred Shareholder Agreement (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/14/ftx-estate-sets-next-creditor-payout-date-as-genesis-digital-assets-fights-usd1-billion-clawback-suit)__). The Recovery Trust's most recent prior tranche — approximately $2.2 billion released on March 31, 2026 to creditors via BitGo, Kraken, and Payoneer — brings cumulative FTX creditor disbursements to approximately $10 billion since petition date. The pending $1 billion clawback litigation against Genesis Digital Assets remains unresolved, with Genesis pushing to dismiss the suit on grounds that the alleged transfers were arm's-length and lawful. The Recovery Trust continues to model the residual clawback recovery pool against the unresolved Genesis dispute.

## Celsius, Voyager, Genesis Capital, FTX International — Running Docket Threads

The broader cross-firm docket continues to advance on parallel tracks. *Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) remains in ongoing plan-implementation; the residual disclosure-statement work is expected to produce an updated creditor-distribution model in Q2–Q3 2026. *Voyager Digital* wind-down concluded in 2024, but residual customer-claim litigation continues. *Genesis Global Capital, LLC* customer-property segregation litigation in the Southern District of New York continues to test the boundary between segregated customer accounts and estate property, with creditor briefing converging on the *Celsius* precedent stack. *FTX International* (Chapter 15 ancillary recognition proceeding) continues UK liquidator coordination, with cross-border recognition and creditor voting timelines on track for Q3 disbursement.

## Recommended Actions

Holders of BTC kiosk transactions in progress at Bitcoin Depot's May 18 petition date should immediately file proof of claim with the BDPT bankruptcy court (N.D. Ga.) as customer-property claims, with characterization aimed at top-priority recovery under the *Celsius* / *Genesis* segregation framework rather than as unsecured creditor claims. Holders of unresolved BlockFills positions should similarly file proof of claim in the D. Del. proceeding as customer-property claims and monitor the Dominion Capital adversary proceeding for guidance on the segregation versus commingling boundary. FTX Preferred Equity Holders eligible for the May 29 distribution should verify wallet-onboarding via BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer ahead of the funding date and confirm tax-residency forms are current with the Recovery Trust administrator. Crypto-custody and ATM operators in the surviving competitive set (Bitstop, Coin Cloud, RockItCoin) should expect consolidation pressure and elevated regulatory-tail risk pricing as state AG enforcement converges around the *Massachusetts* / *Iowa* template. Genesis Digital Assets creditors and trade counterparties should monitor the FTX clawback dismissal motion's adjudication, as a denial would expand the recovery pool model materially in adjacent crypto-mining bankruptcies.
