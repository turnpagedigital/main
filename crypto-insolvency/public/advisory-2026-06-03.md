## BlockFills § 363 Sale — Keyrock at $3.25M, June 16 Hearing

*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* (BlockFills) filed Chapter 11 in the Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities. Keyrock SA — a Brussels-headquartered digital asset market maker and investment firm — was declared the successful bidder for substantially all of BlockFills' operating assets on May 26, 2026, with a purchase price of $3.25 million, subject to § 363 sale approval at the June 16 hearing. The sale encompasses BlockFills' institutional client network (hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies), its proprietary trading technology, and its intellectual property, with Keyrock assuming "certain liabilities" as specified in the sale motion (__[CoinDesk — Keyrock](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/01/crypto-investment-firm-keyrock-is-acquiring-bankrupt-lender-blockfills)__). The critical structural distinction for customer-property recovery is that a § 363 sale transfers designated operating assets but does not extinguish trust-property or customer-property priority claims against the estate's residual funds. Customers asserting that their deposited assets were segregated and traceable — based on the § 341 examination testimony of May 27, 2026 in Wilmington — retain their adversary-proceeding rights against the estate's remaining cash and crypto positions regardless of Keyrock's acquisition. The Dominion Capital adversary proceeding, which triggered the court's TRO over 70.6 BTC on grounds of alleged misappropriation and commingling, is the active boundary-setting proceeding; its outcome will determine whether the segregated-asset characterization extends beyond the Dominion Capital sub-pool to the broader customer deposit base. Customers who have not yet filed proofs of claim should do so immediately, characterizing their claims as customer-property priority claims rather than general unsecured creditor claims — the distinction will govern priority treatment in the post-sale residual estate distribution (__[CoinDesk — BlockFills TRO](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/05/u-s-judge-freezes-blockfills-assets-in-dispute-over-70-bitcoin-with-creditor-dominion-capital)__).

## Genesis LOC Creditor Vote — $3.2B DCG Claims Plan, June 18

The *Genesis LOC* creditor vote on the $3.2 billion Digital Currency Group claims plan is fifteen days out. The *Wind-Down Oversight Committee* (WDOC) continues its oversight role over the plan's implementation mechanics; the Kroll restructuring administration portal for *Genesis Global Capital, LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) remains the operative submission point for creditor elections. The core modeling question for creditors finalizing their voting posture is the recovery delta between the plan's fixed distribution and the probability-weighted expected value of the preserved DCG litigation — a multi-year tail claim with litigation-cost and enforceability drag. *Genesis* completed its debt restructuring and began plan distributions following the May 21, 2024 confirmed plan, and the current vote relates to the LOC-specific creditor class under the amended plan mechanics (__[Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)__). *Genesis* creditors should model the Chapter 7 liquidation analysis — what they would receive if the plan fails and the estate is liquidated — as the comparison benchmark, weighting the DCG litigation at expected-value (rather than face-value), net of multi-year litigation costs. The June 18 vote outcome will also set a precedent for parent-claim preservation in subsequent affiliated-debtor crypto cases; the *Genesis* doctrinal record on affiliated-entity claim treatment is the current leading authority in the Southern District of New York.

## Bitcoin Depot — N.D. Ga. Chapter 11, Day 16; Customer-Property Characterization Pending

*Bitcoin Depot, Inc.* (Bankr. N.D. Ga., Case filed May 18, 2026), the Nasdaq-listed (BTM) BTC-ATM operator and former largest North American kiosk network, is sixteen days past first-day relief with all 9,276 kiosks offline. The company reported a 49 percent year-over-year Q1 2026 revenue decline, a $3.7 million wallet breach, a $9.5 million quarterly operating loss, and was subject to regulatory actions by Massachusetts and Iowa attorneys general — all stayed under 11 U.S.C. § 362 at petition. The $389 million in 2025 industry-wide crypto-ATM fraud losses (a 58 percent year-over-year increase) documented in the state AG complaints forms the through-line that the Massachusetts and Iowa regulators will press in the bankruptcy proceeding as the basis for asserting that customer-deposit obligations in the kiosk payment pipeline are constructive-trust obligations, not merely general unsecured debt (__[Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/bitcoin-depot-goes-bankrupt-signaling-demise-of-crypto-atms)__) (__[Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bitcoin-depot-bankruptcy-chapter-11-atm-wind-down/820755/)__). The customer-property characterization question — whether in-progress BTC transactions in the kiosk payment pipeline at the petition date constitute segregated trust property or estate property — is the live contested issue, distinct from the centralized-exchange context in which the *Celsius* and *Genesis* customer-property doctrine was originally developed. The retail-kiosk payment pipeline involves a distinct technology layer (ATM processing network, crypto-gateway intermediaries, kiosk operators as agent/custodian) that the *Celsius*-to-*Genesis* doctrinal framework was not designed to address. First-day relief orders typically cover operational necessities; the § 341 meeting of creditors has not yet been scheduled. Customers who held in-progress kiosk transactions at the May 18 petition date should file proofs of claim in the N.D. Ga. proceeding immediately, characterizing their claims as customer-property priority claims, and engage restructuring counsel to track the § 341 examination for segregation-accounting admissions.
