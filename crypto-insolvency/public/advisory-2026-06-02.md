# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Tuesday, June 2, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

One significant delta arrived on the wire yesterday: Keyrock, a Brussels-headquartered digital asset market maker and investment firm, announced it is acquiring substantially all of the assets of *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* (BlockFills) out of bankruptcy for $3.25 million, subject to court approval in the Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, with the § 363 sale approval hearing calendared for June 16, 2026 — fourteen days out (__[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/01/crypto-investment-firm-keyrock-is-acquiring-bankrupt-lender-blockfills)__). Keyrock's bid encompassed substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, the firm's institutional client network (hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies), BlockFills' proprietary trading technology, and its intellectual property — a comprehensive § 363 sweep that, if approved, will bind the estate to a distribution model in which the $3.25 million purchase price flows to the creditor waterfall rather than direct customer-property recovery. The critical customer-property question — whether commingled assets segregated pursuant to the court's TRO and the § 341 examination record constitute trust property recoverable at priority ahead of unsecured creditor claims — is not resolved by the § 363 sale; it survives as a residual adversary proceeding for the estates of customers who assert that their specific assets were segregated and traceable. The *Dominion Capital* adversary proceeding, which triggered the court's original TRO freezing 70.6 BTC, remains pending and will determine the segregation-versus-estate-property boundary for the affected customer sub-pool (__[CoinDesk — BlockFills TRO](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/05/u-s-judge-freezes-blockfills-assets-in-dispute-over-70-bitcoin-with-creditor-dominion-capital)__). Alongside the BlockFills § 363 development, the *Genesis LOC* creditor vote on the $3.2 billion DCG claims plan is sixteen days out (June 18), *Bitcoin Depot*'s Chapter 11 in the Northern District of Georgia continues at fifteen days post first-day relief, and the *FTX* preferred-equity distribution remains the current settled baseline with the $1 billion Genesis Digital Assets clawback still unresolved.

## BlockFills § 363 Sale — Keyrock at $3.25M, Hearing June 16

The Keyrock acquisition represents the operative § 363 sale outcome for *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.* (BlockFills), the crypto trading and lending firm that filed Chapter 11 in the Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on March 15, 2026, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities. Keyrock's $3.25 million credit bid (structure to be confirmed in the sale-motion materials) encompasses BlockFills' institutional market-making operations, client network, and technology stack. The hearing to approve the sale is scheduled for June 16, 2026 — the same date as the tentative *Genesis LOC* vote certification deadline, making the week of June 15 the operational peak for this tab. For customers asserting customer-property claims against the BlockFills estate — the Dominion Capital 70.6 BTC class and the broader commingled-asset pool identified in the § 341 testimony — the § 363 sale does not extinguish those claims. The sale transfers operating assets; trust-property and segregation claims survive as adversary-proceeding rights against the estate's residual funds. Customers should file proofs of claim immediately, characterize their claims as customer-property priority claims rather than general unsecured creditor claims, and obtain the § 341 examination transcript from the May 27 Wilmington meeting for the debtor's segregation-accounting admissions — those admissions are the evidentiary anchor for segregation-versus-estate classification. The June 16 sale hearing will also address Keyrock's assumption of "certain liabilities," which may or may not include customer-deposit obligations; the sale-motion materials filed with the court will be the operative document for that determination (__[CoinDesk — Keyrock](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/01/crypto-investment-firm-keyrock-is-acquiring-bankrupt-lender-blockfills)__).

## Genesis LOC Creditor Vote — $3.2B DCG Claims Plan, June 18

The Genesis LOC creditor vote on the $3.2 billion Digital Currency Group claims plan is now sixteen days out, and creditor election strategy is fully locked in its final phase. The operative modeling question is the recovery delta between the plan's fixed distribution and the probability-weighted value of the preserved Digital Currency Group litigation — multi-year tail, litigation cost, and the enforceability of DCG's obligations under the amended plan. *Genesis Global Capital, LLC*'s customer-property segregation litigation in the Southern District of New York has produced the doctrinal record — developed against the *Celsius* precedent stack — on which the creditor committee's recovery analysis rests. Creditors finalizing their voting posture should model the liquidation-analysis comparison (what they would receive in a Chapter 7) against the plan-distribution certainty, weighting the preserved DCG litigation at its expected-value rather than its face-value. The June 18 vote outcome will also establish how district courts treat parent-claim preservation in subsequent affiliated-debtor crypto cases — the Genesis precedent is the current leading authority on this point (__[Cleary Gottlieb](https://content.clearygottlieb.com/corporate/global-restructuring-insights/novel-issues-in-the-crypto-bankruptcy-cluster/index.html)__).

## Bitcoin Depot — N.D. Ga. Chapter 11, Day 15; Customer-Property Characterization Unresolved

*Bitcoin Depot, Inc.* (Bankr. N.D. Ga., petition filed May 18, 2026), the Nasdaq-listed (BTM) BTC-ATM operator and former largest North American kiosk network, is now fifteen days past first-day relief with all 9,276 kiosks offline. First-day relief orders typically cover operational necessities; the substantive customer-property characterization of in-progress BTC transactions — customer funds in the kiosk payment pipeline at petition date — remains the live question. Bitcoin Depot's 49 percent year-over-year Q1 2026 revenue decline, a $3.7 million wallet breach, a $9.5 million quarterly operating loss, and regulatory actions by Massachusetts and Iowa attorneys general (stayed under 11 U.S.C. § 362 at petition) frame the debtor's financial deterioration (__[Bloomberg Law — Bitcoin Depot](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/bitcoin-depot-goes-bankrupt-signaling-demise-of-crypto-atms)__) (__[Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bitcoin-depot-bankruptcy-chapter-11-atm-wind-down/820755/)__). The $389 million in 2025 industry-wide crypto-ATM fraud losses — a 58 percent year-over-year increase — documented in the Massachusetts and Iowa AG complaints is the through-line that will animate the state regulatory argument that customer funds in the kiosk pipeline are trust property. Holders of in-progress kiosk BTC transactions should immediately file proofs of claim in the N.D. Ga. proceeding as customer-property priority claims, not as general unsecured creditor claims, and engage counsel to oppose any estate-property characterization using the *Celsius* and *Genesis* segregation framework as precedent.

## FTX Recovery Trust — May 29 Preferred Equity Distribution Funded; $1B Genesis Clawback Pending

The *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* Recovery Trust's first direct distribution to Preferred Equity Holders — funded May 29, 2026 following the April 30 record date — is now a settled baseline. Cumulative FTX creditor disbursements stand at approximately $10 billion since petition date, with court-projected total recovery rates of 50–70 percent for priority customer claims subject to final asset realizations. The pending $1 billion clawback litigation against Genesis Digital Assets remains unresolved, with Genesis continuing to press for dismissal on the grounds that the transfers were arm's-length and lawful. The FTX clawback dismissal outcome is closely watched in adjacent crypto-mining bankruptcies: a denial would materially expand the recovery-pool model for similar affiliated-entity transfer patterns. Preferred Equity Holders who were eligible for the May 29 distribution but have not confirmed receipt should immediately verify wallet-onboarding completion — BitGo, Kraken, and Payoneer are the three distribution channels — and confirm current tax-residency forms with the Recovery Trust administrator (__[CoinDesk — FTX](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/14/ftx-estate-sets-next-creditor-payout-date-as-genesis-digital-assets-fights-usd1-billion-clawback-suit)__).

Genesis LOC creditors should finalize their June 18 voting posture this week by modeling the plan-distribution recovery against the expected-value of the preserved DCG litigation, accounting for multi-year timeline and litigation cost. Bitcoin Depot in-progress-kiosk-transaction holders should immediately file customer-property proofs of claim in the N.D. Ga. proceeding and engage counsel to monitor first-day-relief compliance and any forthcoming § 341 examination scheduling. FTX Preferred Equity Holders who did not confirm receipt of the May 29 distribution should verify wallet-onboarding via BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer today. Crypto-custody and ATM operators in the surviving competitive set should expect elevated regulatory-tail-risk pricing as state AG enforcement converges around the Massachusetts and Iowa template; legal counsel should be preparing preemptive consumer-fraud compliance audit frameworks. Claims traders evaluating the BlockFills creditor pool should treat the $3.25 million Keyrock acquisition as the floor on § 363 sale value and model customer-property-claim recovery separately from general-unsecured-claim recovery against the residual estate.
