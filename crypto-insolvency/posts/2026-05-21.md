**Headline theme:** Bitcoin Depot Chapter 11 day-four — customer-property characterization is the question of the docket

## LinkedIn Post

Four days into Bitcoin Depot, Inc.'s Chapter 11 in the Northern District of Georgia, the question that matters for everyone with a BTC kiosk transaction in flight is unchanged: are those in-progress transactions customer property under the Celsius / Genesis segregation framework, or general unsecured claims?

The petition went in May 18. All 9,276 kiosks across the U.S., Canada, and Australia are dark. The Massachusetts and Iowa AG enforcement actions alleging facilitation of fraud schemes are stayed under § 362.

The underlying numbers from the first-day filings:

— Q1 2026 revenue: down 49% year-over-year
— Operating result: swing from $12.2M profit to $9.5M loss
— Crypto-ATM fraud losses across the sector: $389M in 2025, up 58% YoY

For customers with funds on the kiosk side of a transaction at the petition date, the path is the same as every customer-property crypto bankruptcy since Celsius: file proof of claim characterized as customer property (priority recovery), not as an unsecured claim (subordinated). The Celsius / Voyager / Genesis Global Capital precedent stack favors segregation where the customer's funds were earmarked for an identified transaction and had not yet been commingled into the operator's operating account.

The parallel that matters for the rest of the operator landscape — Bitstop, Coin Cloud, RockItCoin — is the state-AG enforcement pattern. The Massachusetts and Iowa AG playbook is now a template. Operators in the surviving competitive set should be pricing in elevated regulatory-tail risk on top of the macro decline in BTC-kiosk utility.

Bitcoin Depot is the first major U.S. crypto-ATM Chapter 11 of 2026. It won't be the last.
