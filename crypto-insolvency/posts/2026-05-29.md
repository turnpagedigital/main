# Crypto Insolvency — Posts for Friday, May 29, 2026

## LinkedIn

Today the FTX Recovery Trust funds something it has never funded before: a direct distribution to Preferred Equity Holders.

Every prior wave from the FTX estate flowed to customers and creditors. Today's payout — keyed to the April 30 record date — is the first to reach the preferred-equity class under the Preferred Shareholder Agreement. With it, cumulative disbursements from the estate move past roughly $10 billion since the petition date, and court projections still model 50–70% total recovery for priority customer claims.

That recovery rate is the headline most people miss about FTX. When the exchange collapsed, the working assumption was near-total loss. What actually happened is one of the most successful large-estate recoveries in modern bankruptcy — driven by asset appreciation, disciplined monetization, and aggressive clawback litigation. The $1 billion clawback suit against Genesis Digital Assets is still live, and a denial of Genesis's dismissal motion would expand the residual recovery pool further.

The lesson for anyone holding claims in the current crypto-insolvency cluster — BlockFills, Bitcoin Depot, the Genesis LOC track — is the same one FTX keeps teaching: the characterization fight is the whole game. Customer property recovers ahead of unsecured claims. File early, file as customer property, and preserve every priority and clawback theory the record will support.

Recovery is slow. But in this cluster, it is real.
