# Crypto Insolvency — Posts for Monday, June 1, 2026

## LinkedIn

On June 18, Genesis creditors face a choice that captures the central tension of every crypto bankruptcy: certainty now, or a bigger number later.

The Genesis LOC creditor vote on the $3.2 billion Digital Currency Group claims plan is seventeen days out. Vote yes, and the plan distribution is fixed and near. Hold out for the preserved litigation against parent DCG, and you're betting on a probability-weighted recovery with a multi-year tail and real litigation cost — pursued by the post-wind-down entity for creditor benefit.

There's no universally right answer. There's only your discount rate. A creditor who needs liquidity prices the certain distribution highly. A creditor who can wait, and who believes the DCG claims are strong, may rationally weight the litigation upside.

What the vote should NOT be is a default. Model the delta: the fixed plan recovery versus the expected value of the DCG litigation, net of time and cost. Then vote the number, not the narrative.

The same customer-property and parent-claim questions are running in parallel across the cluster — FTX funded its first Preferred Equity tranche May 29; BlockFills opened its segregation ledger at its May 27 §341 meeting; Bitcoin Depot sits fourteen days past first-day relief with 9,276 kiosks dark. The Celsius and Genesis holdings are setting the baseline the newer filings will be measured against.
