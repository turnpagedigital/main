# Crypto Insolvency — Posts | Wednesday, May 20, 2026

**Single theme:** Customer-property doctrine — segregation versus estate property — and the open question of in-flight kiosk transactions in *In re Bitcoin Depot, Inc.*

## LinkedIn

When Bitcoin Depot, Inc. (Nasdaq: BTM) filed its voluntary Chapter 11 petition Monday in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Georgia, 9,276 kiosks went dark with customer BTC transactions in mid-flight. The narrow legal question facing the court — and every customer who deposited cash but had not yet received bitcoin at the petition moment — is the one crypto-insolvency dockets keep returning to: customer property, or estate property.

The doctrinal frame is settled. In *In re Celsius Network LLC*, Judge Martin Glenn (S.D.N.Y.) held that customer assets in commingled, non-segregated platform accounts become property of the estate under Section 541, leaving customers as unsecured creditors competing pari passu with trade vendors and noteholders. *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* applied the same framework. *In re Genesis Global Capital, LLC* continues to test the boundary between segregated customer accounts and pooled estate assets.

What is open in Bitcoin Depot is the in-flight transaction. A customer who fed cash into a kiosk before the petition but had not yet received the BTC wallet credit holds a claim that is neither pure customer property (no traceable digital asset yet existed in a segregated account) nor a clean unsecured claim (the consideration moved one direction only). The first-day pleadings and the Section 341 creditors' meeting will produce the trustee's initial position on how to characterize this population, and the resulting order is likely to set the playbook for any future crypto-ATM Chapter 11.

The practical posture for affected customers is the same posture that should be standard in any commingled-platform filing: file the proof of claim as a customer-property claim, not as an unsecured creditor claim. The characterization decision is made on the proof of claim itself. A claim filed as unsecured cannot later be elevated to customer-property status without amendment and adversary briefing; a claim filed as customer property forces the trustee to take a position and creates the record for objection if the trustee re-characterizes it downward.
