## Analysis & Developments

### TODAY'S LEDE — Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399): Sale Hearing Opens This Morning

The most time-sensitive development on the watch list is happening right now. 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, with the cases jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
On July 2, the Court entered an order approving the bidding procedures; the auction was scheduled for July 13 at 10:00 a.m. ET, with a post-auction objection deadline of July 14 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and the sale hearing set for **today, July 15, at 10:00 a.m. ET**.


The economics matter. 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors filed for Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026 reporting $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities as of April 30, 2026.
 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale under § 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc. ("Sleep Country"), at $415 million in cash plus assumption of assumed liabilities, pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026.
 To displace Sleep Country, 
a competing bidder must clear an effective floor of $441.45 million — covering the $415 million base price, $16.45 million in bid protections payable in cash, and a $10 million minimum overbid — and must post a $41.5 million good-faith deposit; in the event of a buyer breach, that deposit is the estate's sole remedy absent fraud or willful breach.


The DIP structure is the creditor-rights pressure point. 
To fund the cases, the debtors secured a DIP facility from their existing lender group comprising up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million in a roll-up of prepetition obligations converted on a 3:1 basis.
 
The DIP facility imposes binding case milestones that constitute events of default if missed: the Interim Order was required within three days of the Petition Date; the Final Order within 30 days; qualified bids by July 8; an auction by July 13; and a sale order entered by July 15, with closing by July 31.
 The 3:1 roll-up — $195 million of prepetition exposure converted to superpriority postpetition position — is aggressive and meaningfully subordinates any unsecured committee's ability to challenge the lender group's liens, particularly given the Challenge Deadline ran to the earlier of 60 days after committee formation or the commencement of the sale hearing. 
The initial filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors,
 but whether that holds after the lender roll-up, bid protections, and administrative costs clear will depend entirely on whether the winning bid clears the stalking horse price by a material margin.

The lease portfolio is the other open variable. 
The Company operates 572 leased retail stores across all 50 states — it owns no real property — carrying approximately $348 million in present value of operating lease liabilities as of April 4, 2026.
 
The debtors filed a rejection schedule covering 44 locations — all under the Select Comfort Retail Corporation entity — with rejection effective as of the Petition Date of June 12, 2026, representing approximately 8% of the total store portfolio; landlords of those locations are left with capped rejection-damages claims under § 502(b)(6).
 
The remaining roughly 528 locations are preserved through closing under tight APA restrictions — the debtors cannot enter into, modify, or terminate any lease with annual rent exceeding $50,000 without the buyer's prior written consent.
 For distressed investors tracking landlord rejection claims or § 365 assumption/rejection dynamics, the APA's consent mechanics mean that a buyer has effective veto power over any lease-level restructuring between signing and closing — an unusual degree of buyer control in a live § 363 process.

Debt load and attribution deserve attention. 
The company attributes its distress to a secular consumer shift toward e-commerce that eroded in-store foot traffic, elevated inflation and interest rates, and disrupted supply chains, compounded by pandemic-era overexpansion of store footprint, manufacturing capabilities, and debt levels, and by the April 2025 wave of U.S. tariffs.
 
Debtor counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.
 
The sale would need to close by July 31.
 Today's hearing is the critical gate.

### BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)) — Post-Sale / Plan Phase; Bar Date on Deck

The BlockFills matter has moved well past the sale-hearing stage established in prior advisories. To recap the structural arc: 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), reporting approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing a framework centered on creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


On the sale track, 
as set out in the official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
Per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The sale hearing before Judge Thomas M. Horan was scheduled for June 16, 2026. The tracked-case docket shows three new entries filed July 13 (Docket Entries 525 and 527), with the most recent filing as of July 13, 2026 — suggesting activity in the case continues well past the June sale hearing, consistent with the post-sale plan and liquidating trust mechanics contemplated in the Ad Hoc Group term sheet.

The critical outstanding matter for creditors is the bar date. 
The Governmental Bar Date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 The general bar date order has been entered, and creditors who have not yet filed proofs of claim should treat the September 11 governmental deadline as an outer bound while confirming the general bar date separately via Veritaglobal at `veritaglobal.net/blockfills`.

The valuation gap remains stark and is the central creditor-rights issue in this case. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. reports $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 Against $145 million in general unsecured obligations, the $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price for the operating assets signals that recovery through the § 363 sale alone will be negligible for unsecured creditors. Recovery will depend on: (i) the liquidating trust's ability to monetize residual estate assets and pursue any avoidance actions; (ii) the resolution of the pre-petition customer-asset ownership dispute — 
the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested;
 and (iii) the outcome of the pre-petition litigation in the Southern District of New York. 
That S.D.N.Y. action (Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026) alleged ongoing misappropriation and fraudulent concealment of customer assets.


The capital structure context: 
the capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The Celsius secured claim sits ahead of the entire unsecured pool, which makes the liquidating trust's avoidance-action portfolio the only plausible path to a recovery above nominal for general creditors.

The customer-property question — whether digital assets held by BlockFills for its institutional clients are property of the estate under § 541 or beneficially owned by customers and thus excluded — is structurally identical to the issue litigated in *In re Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) and *In re FTX Trading Ltd.* (Bankr. D. Del.). The BlockFills case has the additional wrinkle of an Cayman Islands affiliate (Reliz CI Ltd.) whose asset-segregation obligations under Cayman law may be relevant to whether U.S. estate property claims can extend to assets nominally held offshore. Judge Horan has not, as reflected in public record, issued a dispositive ruling on that question as of the July 13 docket date.

### Contextual Note: Retail Bankruptcy Macro and the § 363 Sale Calendar

Sleep Number is not an isolated data point. The current macro environment — tariff-induced supply chain cost pressure, post-pandemic over-leveraging, declining discretionary spending, and rising administrative costs in long-duration cases — continues to push retail debtors into compressed § 363 processes rather than plan confirmation. The Sleep Number structure (14-week prepetition marketing, 26-day postpetition diligence window, $415 million stalking horse, July 31 outside closing date) is now a template for lender-controlled retail sales: the DIP facility's 3:1 roll-up and tight milestones effectively preclude a creditors' committee from developing a competing plan. 
The proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, a July 15, 2026 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.


For distressed investors, the consequential residual question in any lender-driven § 363 sale is what "funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors" actually means once the waterfall resolves. The $1.3 billion in Sleep Number liabilities against the $415 million stalking horse bid — even with the administrative cost load — leaves a substantial gap. Whether the DIP roll-up and the bid protections ($16.45 million payable to Sleep Country if the stalking horse is displaced) survive a potential § 1129 challenge in a subsequent plan is moot if the sale closes by July 31 and the estate transitions to a liquidating trust with a fraction of the enterprise value. Creditor claimants tracking Sleep Number should closely monitor today's sale hearing transcript and the post-closing distribution schedule once the plan supplement is filed.
