On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín signed the final approval order in Bartz v. Anthropic — closing out the largest copyright class action settlement in history and clearing the last legal hurdle before authors and publishers see their money. If you filed a claim, this is the moment to understand what you're getting, when it arrives, and what you gave up.

The number that matters: about $3,000 per book.

The Settlement Fund is $1.5 billion, non-reversionary — meaning Anthropic cannot claw back a dollar of it. After attorneys' fees, expenses, and administrative costs come out, the Net Settlement Fund gets divided by the number of Claimed Works. That produces a per-work award of approximately $3,000. For context: that's four times the statutory minimum for ordinary copyright infringement ($750) and fifteen times the statutory minimum for innocent infringement ($200). The court itself called it 'an order of magnitude more than the maximum proposed for books in the Google Books settlement' that was rejected back in 2011.

How the pie gets split for each book.

For each work on the Works List, the per-work share is divided among the rightsholders — typically the author and the publisher. The default rules: for non-education titles, author and publisher split 50/50; either side can override that default during the claims process by submitting the actual publishing contract. Co-authors split their share equally. Education works (textbooks and similar) have no default — claimants make a good-faith split based on the contract, and disagreements go to a court-appointed Special Master, retired arbitrator Theodore K. Cheng, whose decisions are final and non-appealable. Sole owners — self-published authors, authors whose rights reverted, work-for-hire publishers — get 100% of the per-work allocation.

If you filed. If you didn't. If someone else did.

The claims deadline was March 23, 2026, and 91.3% of the 482,460 works on the Works List were claimed by mid-April — an exceptional response rate for a class action. If you filed and no one else did on the same work, you're in line for the full per-work allocation. If a publisher filed first on a book you wrote (or vice versa), the Settlement Administrator, JND Legal Administration, contacted you as a 'Nonfiling Claimant' and gave you 70 days from that notice to file your own claim. Miss the deadline and you still get a check calculated at the default split, mailed to your last known address. Don't cash it within 18 months of final approval and the money is redistributed to the party who did file.

What you gave up — and what you kept.

The release is narrower than it looks. Class members released only claims about past AI inputs — the piracy and copying of the books through the LibGen and PiLiMi shadow libraries — up to the point of any AI output. Class members did not release claims about past AI outputs (what Claude generated using the works), claims about future conduct on or after August 25, 2025, or claims tied to works not on the Works List. That last point matters. If Anthropic scraped a book that isn't on the Works List — whether because it lacked a timely copyright registration or simply wasn't in the LibGen or PiLiMi corpora — those claims survive and can be brought separately. Anthropic also has to destroy the LibGen and PiLiMi files it downloaded, subject to the usual litigation-preservation carve-outs.

What happens next, and when.

JND now calculates per-work and per-claimant distribution amounts — the original schedule contemplated 80 days from the claims deadline, so first payments should follow this fall barring further delay. Payments go out by the method each claimant selected on the claim form: check, Zelle, or U.S. mail if no preference was specified. Disputes go to Special Master Cheng, who is instructed to move quickly. Any residual funds trigger a second distribution to claimants unless it's uneconomic to do so, with a final balance going to a court-approved cy pres recipient.
