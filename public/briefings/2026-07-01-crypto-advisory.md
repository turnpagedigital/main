# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Wednesday, July 01, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Plan Confirmation Imminent; Adversary Proceeding Pending Against Artha

The dominant near-term event on this docket is today's trajectory toward the July 13, 2026 confirmation hearing. 
The Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware has the confirmation hearing calendared for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET
 — twelve days out. 
On May 28, 2026, Judge Thomas M. Horan entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, and establishing deadlines and procedures for claim objections, temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes, and the solicitation package (Docket No. 388).
 That order also set the record date and voting deadline, so balloting should now be substantially closed.

The sale component of the dual-track resolved when Keyrock SA emerged as the winning bidder. 
According to bankruptcy filings, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property, with Keyrock confirmed as Successful Bidder in the court filing dated May 26, 2026.
 The June 16 sale hearing went forward; 
the June 12 agenda reflected that the sale of substantially all assets was proceeding at the June 16 hearing, with objections outstanding from the Chicago Blackhawks Hockey Team and Oracle (Dkt. 424).
 No post-hearing order has been confirmed in this run's search results, but the Verita calendar shows no subsequent sale-related postponement and the September 11, 2026 claim objection deadline is consistent with the sale having cleared. Creditors should treat the sale as approved absent a contrary court filing — but confirm the entry of the sale order directly on the docket.

The asset-coverage gap is severe. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that stack, 
Keyrock's $3.25 million purchase price acquires "substantially all" assets.
 
Reliz reported $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities, with the filing indicating funds would be available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — but at a $3.25 million sale price, distribution recoveries will be measured in cents, not dollars, unless the plan incorporates additional causes of action proceeds or the Celsius lien is subordinated or satisfied at discount.

On the adversary front, 
an adversary proceeding was filed in April 2026 by the Reliz debtors against Artha Investment Partners LLC, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH), in the same court.
 
The summons issued a return deadline of June 22, 2026.
 The nature of the debtors' claims against Artha has not been publicly described in detail in available filings, but the timing — filed after the sale bid was locked — suggests a fraudulent-transfer or preference recovery intended to augment the distributable estate. Creditors should monitor for an amended complaint or answer filed after the June 22 response deadline.

The Unsecured Creditors' Committee is active. 
The U.S. Trustee for Region 3 appointed the Committee on March 27, 2026 (Docket No. 104), with members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
The Committee retained FTI Consulting as financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.
 The presence of Dominion Capital on the UCC is noteworthy: Dominion was the plaintiff in the pre-petition S.D.N.Y. TRO proceeding before Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil, 
alleging that BlockFills misappropriated and unlawfully retained millions of dollars' worth of customer crypto assets, commingled client assets, and concealed heavy losses.
 Dominion's insider position on the Committee gives it discovery leverage in any customer-property dispute — the § 541 "property of the estate" question that remains structurally unresolved in this case.

That § 541 question is the one the docket has not yet publicly answered. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested, with four debtor entities pursuing a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process.
 The plan documents (Dkt. 388 package) will govern how the debtors characterize customer deposits — as general unsecured claims or as held in trust — and whether any customer-property tracing argument survives confirmation. Creditors who held crypto on the BlockFills platform should review the Disclosure Statement before the July 13 hearing to assess which class they have been placed in and whether any § 1126 vote was properly solicited.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Commences July 31, 2026; Disputed Claims Reserve Cut by $600 Million

The FTX docket is now approaching distribution completion for the primary customer classes. 
The FTX Recovery Trust announced that the record date for the fifth distribution is June 16, 2026, for holders of allowed claims, with the distribution expected to commence July 31, 2026.
 
Preferred equity holders will also receive their next payment on July 31, 2026, on the same June 16 record date.
 This fifth distribution follows four prior rounds totaling roughly $10 billion. 
The fourth distribution of approximately $2.2 billion, paid March 31, 2026, brought many customer claim classes — including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B — to 100% cumulative recovery, while Class 7 reached a cumulative 120%.


Critically for any creditor or claim-transferee tracking their position as of today: 
FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $600 million, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims in the next distribution.
 That motion is presumably still pending or recently resolved — it is the most consequential near-term docket event for recovery economics.


For transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims maintained by the Notice and Claims Agent as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 This is a hard mechanic: any claim transfer filed on or after May 26, 2026 that has not cleared the 21-day window by June 16 will result in the distribution going to the transferor. Buyers in the FTX claim-trading aftermarket who acquired positions in late May should verify their transfers are on record and unchallenged before the July 31 pay date.


Additionally, holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims, as defined in FTX's confirmed Chapter 11 Plan, became eligible to begin the NFT Distribution process on June 30, 2026.
 This is a new and distinct process from the cash/crypto claim distributions and requires separate onboarding.

The ongoing clawback litigation remains a secondary recovery vector. 
Genesis Digital Assets, a Bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion, and is pushing to dismiss the suit — part of the broader strategy of challenging transfers made before the exchange's collapse in November 2022.
 Whether estate counsel at Sullivan & Cromwell prevails on fraudulent-transfer grounds will affect the pool available for later distributions.

### Genesis/DCG Wind-Down — Litigation Adversary Portfolio Expands; Four Distributions Completed

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063) entered its wind-down phase with Plan Administrator Mark Renzi overseeing distributions under a plan confirmed May 21, 2024. 
From the Effective Date through the present, the Wind-Down Debtors have made four distributions to creditors pursuant to the Plan.
 What has expanded materially is the adversary docket. The Kroll case page now shows: 
Genesis Global Holdco et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230; Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc., Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312; and DCG International Investments Ltd., Adv. Pro. No. 23-01169; as well as Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Vincent Falco et al., Adv. Pro. No. 25-01111.


The DCG adversary at No. 26-03230 is the most recently opened and is presumably the primary vehicle for the Litigation Oversight Committee (LOC), advised by Selendy Gay PLLC, to pursue estate claims against the parent. The Genesis plan's in-kind distribution framework — the key legal innovation that distinguished this case from FTX — continues to stand. 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value; instead, it sought to provide restitution to creditors through in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 
The court's decision allows creditors to receive in-kind distributions based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, and Genesis is authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 The question for remaining creditors is how much of the distributable estate has been consumed by professional fees and litigation costs across the now-seven adversary proceedings, and what increment of in-kind value each successive distribution delivers relative to the remaining coin shortfall.

### Cross-Docket Observation: Claim-Valuation Methodology Remains the Fulcrum

Across BlockFills, FTX, and Genesis, the single most consequential structural question is whether customer crypto deposits are valued at petition date in fiat (FTX's confirmed approach under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)) or at current coin value (Genesis's in-kind innovation). BlockFills' plan documents have not publicly resolved this for customer accounts. The outcome at BlockFills' July 13 confirmation hearing — particularly how the plan classifies and values customer crypto claims — will determine whether this case follows the FTX dollarization model or attempts a hybrid. Given the $3.25 million sale price against ~$145 million in customer/trade obligations, the methodology question is largely academic for recovery quantum here, but it matters for the customer-property argument: if customer deposits are characterized as held in constructive trust under state law rather than general unsecured claims under 11 U.S.C. § 541, a subset of claimants may be entitled to priority treatment ahead of trade creditors. Dominion Capital's presence on both the pre-petition TRO docket and the UCC suggests this argument will be pressed.

Creditors holding allowed FTX claims who transferred their positions on or after May 26, 2026 must confirm immediately — through the Kroll claims register at cases.ra.kroll.com/FTX — that the transfer has been processed and the 21-day notice period cleared before the June 16 record date; failure to confirm risks the July 31 distribution going to the transferor rather than the transferee. BlockFills claimants should obtain and review the full Disclosure Statement solicitation package (Dkt. 388 and related solicitation materials available at veritaglobal.net/blockfills) before the July 13, 2026 confirmation hearing at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Horan; in particular, examine the plan's classification and treatment of customer crypto deposit claims, whether any § 541 property-of-the-estate carve-out is proposed, and how the Artha adversary (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230) proceeds are to be distributed. Any institutional creditor with a claim exceeding the Celsius lien's $4.8 million priority position should consider filing a § 1128(b) objection to confirmation if the plan's classification scheme improperly conflates customer property claims with general trade claims. Genesis creditors awaiting distributions should monitor the LOC's adversary activity — particularly the newly filed Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230 against DCG — as any recovery there flows through the same distribution framework; consult Selendy Gay PLLC or Cleary Gottlieb if you hold an unliquidated in-kind entitlement and have not yet completed KYC verification with Kroll.
