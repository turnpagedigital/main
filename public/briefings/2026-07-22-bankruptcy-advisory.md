### Sleep Number — Sale Order Entered; Clock to July 31 Close Ticking

The dispositive event on this beat since yesterday: Judge Kyu Y. (Mike) Paek of the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York entered the sale approval order in *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399, at the July 20 hearing. 
Sleep Number announced it had received court approval for the sale of substantially all of the company's assets and ongoing business operations to SNBR Inc., a wholly owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc., after making significant improvements to its prior proposal.
 
The sale remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31.


The structural story here is worth holding. 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, with the case structured as an expedited sale of substantially all assets under § 363 rather than a reorganization, built around a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 That stalking horse figure maps against a balance sheet that filed showing 
$642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 The DIP financing underpinning operations through close is a $260 million facility — 
up to $260 million from existing lenders, structured as up to $65 million of new-money superpriority term loans and up to $195 million of roll-up loans, with interest at SOFR plus 8% or base rate plus 7%.


The timeline compressed sharply before this week's hearing. 
The sale-approval hearing, originally set for July 15, was adjourned to July 20, 2026, with the close still aimed at July 31.
 
No competing bid was publicly reported, so Sleep Country Canada's affiliate remained the lead buyer at approximately $415 million.
 For unsecured creditor holders, the relevant forward-looking event is now July 24: 
the Meeting of Creditors (the § 341 meeting) has been continued and will be held by Zoom conference on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET.
 Additionally, 
a hearing on a motion for relief from the automatic stay is calendared for August 20, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The structural overhang for residual creditors — those holding unsecured paper with no direct claim on sale proceeds — is the arithmetic: a $415 million headline purchase price drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt leaves an acknowledged $257.5 million gap before the waterfall reaches unsecured claims. 
As Stretto's analysis framed it, this is a case of a "self-priming lender DIP, drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt," with a $257.5 million shortfall setting the terms.
 The DIP priming structure is unambiguous: 
DIP lenders receive superpriority administrative expense claims and liens, including first-priority on unencumbered DIP collateral, priming senior security interests on prepetition collateral, and junior liens where prior senior liens exist — positioning DIP lenders ahead of existing unsecured creditors and shareholders.
 
Sleep Number explicitly warned that the Chapter 11 process may be highly adverse for existing shareholders, specifically highlighting the likely cancellation of its common shares.


Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick) represents the Debtors. Kroll Restructuring Administration is the claims and noticing agent at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber.

### BlockFills — Sale Closed, Plan Confirmation the Only Remaining Question

The BlockFills § 363 track is effectively resolved. 
Keyrock completed its acquisition of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business on July 16, 2026, following BlockFills' Chapter 11 bankruptcy case.
 The bankruptcy court had approved the sale on June 16: 
a Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepared for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.


The cross-border texture of the close is notable. 
The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) oversees one entity Keyrock has already taken on — that piece is done. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) still has to sign off on the other entity before it transfers. The split structure lets Keyrock start serving BlockFills' Cayman-registered clients immediately while the slower UK license process runs on its own track.
 
The aggregate price of approximately $3.25 million was paid in two tranches subject to regulatory contingencies.


What this means for the ~$145 million unsecured estate is the critical unresolved question. 
The Debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The plan confirmation posture as of today: 
the Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The last docket entry in the tracked ground truth is dated July 17, 2026, which is after the July 13 confirmation hearing date — confirmation status is not yet verified by a primary source in this run. Practitioners should check CourtListener docket 72494188 or Veritaglobal.net/BlockFills for the confirmation order or any adjournment. The governmental bar date remains September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET per the claims administrator's calendar.

A legally significant structural issue pervades the creditor recovery calculus here. 
The BlockFills Client Agreement contains three separate provisions explicitly disclaiming any fiduciary relationship, and the agreement authorizes BlockFills to transfer margin to exchanges and clearing houses at its sole discretion, with customers ranking "only as a general creditor" upon third-party default.
 That language was tested at the first day hearing: 
debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate — a position the court noted it could not contradict on the first-day record, but preserved for further hearing.
 Whether commingled crypto assets are estate property or customer property under § 541(b) remains an open doctrinal question in this case; it has direct bearing on pro-rata distribution percentages for the 4 debtors: Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (6265), Reliz Technologies LLC (1968), Reliz LTD (N/A), and Reliz CI LTD (N/A), all administered jointly at Bankr. D. Del. Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) before Judge Horan, Courtroom No. 5, 824 Market Street, 5th Floor, Wilmington.

### Broader Landscape: § 363 Sales, DIP Mechanics, and the Valuation-Gap Problem

The Sleep Number confirmation of the sale order this week offers a clean recent data point on what a compressed, stalking-horse-anchored § 363 process looks like in SDNY in 2026: 
the DIP financing, the operational motives, and the compressed deadline calendar all existed to move the sale to closing before liquidity ran out.
 The six-week petition-to-sale-approval window (June 12 petition to July 20 sale order) is aggressive even by recent § 363 standards and reflects a pre-packaged marketing process — 
the CFO's first-day declaration described a "robust" 14-week prepetition marketing process as the foundation for the expedited sale timeline.


For distressed investors watching this space, the Sleep Number mechanics illustrate a structural risk in self-priming DIP facilities: when the DIP lender and the prepetition secured creditor are the same constituency, the roll-up component ($195 million here) converts prepetition debt to superpriority administrative status without any new-money quid pro quo for unsecured classes. The Bankruptcy Code's § 364(d) permits priming liens with adequate protection, but "adequate protection" is frequently judge-dependent and contested at the interim/final hearing sequence. Here, the interim DIP order entered June 15 and the final DIP hearing ran July 9 — 
the Bankruptcy Court entered the interim DIP order on June 15, 2026, with a final hearing to consider full approval scheduled for July 9, 2026.


The Celsius clawback litigation, a downstream consequence of the same 2022–2023 crypto crash that generated exposures for BlockFills, surfaced in a July 13 Law360 headline: 
a judge set a damages framework in Celsius Chapter 11 clawbacks on July 13, 2026.
 The Celsius-BlockFills nexus — BlockFills owed Celsius approximately $4.8 million under secured promissory notes per the first-day record, and faced an adverse Celsius arbitration award contributing to the liquidity collapse — means that any further development in Celsius clawback precedent can have downstream implications for what BlockFills creditors ultimately receive after plan distributions.
