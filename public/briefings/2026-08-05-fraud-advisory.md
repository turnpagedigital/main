One substantive development worth noting for claims-positioning purposes: 
Gibson Dunn published its *Securities Enforcement 2026 Mid-Year Update* approximately 17 hours ago, documenting three defining developments in the first half of 2026 at the SEC's Division of Enforcement and reinforcing a "quality over quantity," back-to-basics enforcement program focused on fraud and manipulation.
 For recovery-desk purposes, the forward signal in that report is specific: 
despite lower headline numbers, Division leadership has signaled a coming uptick in core fraud enforcement, with accounting and disclosure fraud, private funds, cross-border misconduct, and retail-facing fraud at the top of the agenda.
 That posture is consistent with Director Woodcock's stated priorities: 
in his first public remarks at the MFA Legal & Compliance 2026 Conference, Woodcock outlined a traditional enforcement agenda with specific focus on offering fraud, accounting and disclosure fraud, market manipulation, insider trading, private funds, and cross-border fraud.
 A more active cross-border fraud pipeline increases the probability of new receiver appointments — and new claim pools — in Q4 2026 and into 2027.

On the OneCoin / Ignatova remission track: the DOJ victim petition deadline of June 30, 2026 has now closed. 
The remission process was managed through `onecoinremission.com` under U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton's office, with the Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section administering the process.
 Post-deadline, the next milestone is the MLNAR Section's review and proposed allocation — no timeline has been publicly stated. Ignatova herself remains a fugitive; 
the U.S. offers a $5 million reward for information leading to her arrest and conviction, and she has been on the FBI's most wanted list since 2022.


On BLMIS/Madoff: 
the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced February 27, 2026, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totaled nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.
 
As of July 24, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 No eighteenth distribution motion has been announced; the next motion, when filed, will follow the established pattern of allocating newly recovered funds to the Customer Fund before the Bankruptcy Court for the S.D.N.Y. under Case No. 08-01789 (J. Bernstein). That is the next scheduled milestone to watch.
