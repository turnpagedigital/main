### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Bankr. D. Del.) — Confirmation Hearing July 13


On March 15, 2026, all four debtor entities filed voluntary petitions under Chapter 11, with the cases jointly administered before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the District of Delaware.
 The filing posture has shifted materially since then.


On May 28, 2026, Judge Horan entered the disclosure statement approval order — Docket No. 388 — scheduling the confirmation hearing and establishing deadlines for objections, claim allowance for voting, and solicitation procedures.
 
The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the confirmation objection deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the confirmation hearing is set for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 Both deadlines have now closed without any publicly filed extension, putting the case squarely on the July 13 track.

The sale leg of the dual-track process closed ahead of plan confirmation. 
Brussels-based Keyrock agreed to acquire BlockFills' assets for $3.25 million — a purchase price that measures against the debtor's reported $100 million to $500 million in liabilities and only $50 million to $100 million in assets.
 
The acquisition gives Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, spanning hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies.
 The sale hearing was set for June 16. The Veritaglobal key-dates page reflects a "Notice of Cancellation of Auction" and a "Notice of Rescheduled Auction" — indicating the bidding process saw at least one procedural disruption before the sale proceeded. 
A June 12 hearing agenda confirmed the sale of substantially all assets was going forward at the June 16 hearing, while a protective-order matter was adjourned and sale objections from Chicago Blackhawks Hockey Team, Inc. and Oracle remained pending at that stage — Docket No. 424.


The central legal question hanging over confirmation is whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in trust or bailment for customer account. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
The commingling is documented: on January 16, 2026, creditor 1548199 Alberta Ltd. transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; those assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes, with the wallet emptied within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."



Notably, the debtors have also raised a procedural defense: objecting creditors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for a determination of property of the estate and have not attempted asset-level tracing.
 If that argument carries at confirmation, customer claims fold into the general unsecured pool and recover pro rata under the plan. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The adversary proceeding against Artha Investment Partners LLC — Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH) — is an active fraudulent-transfer vehicle in the case. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. as plaintiffs filed that adversary in the same court (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)) against Artha Investment Partners LLC.
 Given that five new docket entries were logged July 6 (entries 492–493 per the tracked ground truth), activity this week almost certainly reflects pre-confirmation administrative filings — fee applications, plan supplement filings, or potential stipulations resolving sale-related objections — though descriptions for those entries were not available at time of publication.

**What to watch July 13:** Whether Judge Horan confirms the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan over any remaining objections and whether any creditor objection on the § 541 property-of-estate issue survives to a contested evidentiary hearing. Any adjournment of the confirmation hearing would extend the case timeline past the current September 11, 2026 claims bar date.

### BlockFills — Background and Liability Genesis


BlockFills attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty exposure — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, with compounding balance sheet pressure from stranded capital in a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.



In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026) and
 
1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al. (N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:26-cv-02451, filed March 5, 2026), with similar claims against multiple debtor entities and named individuals including Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace — and a TRO granted in that case as well.



On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil (S.D.N.Y.) issued an ex parte temporary restraining order freezing 70.56 BTC (approximately $4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 The automatic stay mooted both TROs upon filing. 
In August 2025, the company retained BRG to assist with restructuring and financial reporting, with Mark Renzi, a Managing Director at BRG, appointed Chief Restructuring Officer.
 
McDermott Will & Schulte LLP serves as lead bankruptcy counsel, with Katten Muchin Rosenman LLP as co-counsel.

### Power Block Coin, LLC (Case No. 24-bk-23041 — Bankr. D. Utah) — Committee Liquidation Track

Power Block Coin (dba SmartFi) has been in Chapter 11 in the District of Utah since June 20, 2024, before Judge Joel T. Marker (now Judge Cathleen D. Parker per the tracked ground truth — the case appears to have undergone a judicial reassignment). 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041-JTM), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing the plan on January 30, 2025 to wind down the company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.



The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 
The committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions — including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, whose unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property drew committee scrutiny.


Three new docket entries were filed July 6 in this case (entries 529 and 531 per ground truth), suggesting active administration — likely fee applications, trustee reporting, or asset-disposition filings at this stage of the case. The $192 million claim pool and the Celsius exposure (itself a claimant distributing under its own confirmed plan) create cross-estate complexity: recoveries flowing out of Power Block Coin to Celsius would effectively be redistributed to Celsius's own creditors under Celsius's confirmed plan, a second-order recovery dynamic worth monitoring for timing coordination.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Scheduled July 31

The FTX estate's fifth distribution round is now pending. 
The FTX Recovery Trust issued an operational update setting June 16, 2026 as the record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims and interests, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
Payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders are also scheduled for July 31, 2026.



As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the trust filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from approximately $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 If approved, that reduction unlocks additional funds for distribution.


For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 This is a hard cutoff: any claim transfer that had not cleared the register by June 16 misses the July 31 payment cycle and rolls to the next round.

For context on the prior distribution track: 
distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion in September 2025, and a fourth round launched on March 31, 2026.
 
Cumulative distributions since early 2025 stand at roughly $10 billion.


The trust's clawback litigation against Genesis Digital Assets — FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets Limited et al. (see Kroll docket, Adv. Pro.) — remains active. 
The FTX Trust filed a $1.15 billion lawsuit against Genesis Digital Assets (a Bitcoin mining firm) alleging fraudulent transfers of funds from the bankruptcy estate.
 
The trust asserts claims under both federal bankruptcy law and Delaware's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act, alleging actual and constructive fraud.
 Resolution of that adversary — along with the remaining disputed claims reserve — will set the ceiling for any sixth distribution.

### Genesis/DCG — Distributions Complete; Litigation Fund Active

For the carry-forward record: 
Genesis Global Capital, along with Genesis Global Holdco and Genesis Asia Pacific, completed its restructuring and began distributing approximately $4 billion to creditors.
 
Per the initial distribution, Bitcoin creditors received 51.28% recoveries, Ether creditors 65.87%, and Solana creditors 29.58%.
 
As part of the bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action against third parties including DCG — comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.
 That fund is separately managed and the DCG litigation posture is the remaining open variable in Genesis recovery economics. No new DCG appellate filings were identified in this run.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and claim-recovery counsel should focus on two immediate calendared events. First, the BlockFills confirmation hearing on July 13 (Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan) is the decisive moment for institutional customers holding claims in the roughly $145 million general unsecured pool: any party that filed a timely objection by the July 1 deadline must appear or risk waiver, and any party that failed to file an adversary proceeding on the § 541 property-of-estate question faces the debtors' procedural defense that Bankruptcy Rule 7001(b) forecloses that determination outside an adversary. Claim traders who acquired BlockFills positions should confirm their transfers were logged on the Veritaglobal register before any voting record date; the claims administrator is KCC dba Verita Global at veritaglobal.net/blockfills. Second, FTX claim holders who completed KYC and onboarding with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer and whose claim transfers cleared the June 16 record date should expect the July 31 distribution — but should verify claim-register status now given the 21-day objection window on transfers. For Power Block Coin creditors, the committee's Greenberg Traurig-led liquidation plan with the $17.4 million Solara Communities loan as a contested asset warrants monitoring for any sale motion or litigation filing targeting that collateral, particularly as the estate's three July 6 docket entries suggest active administration.
