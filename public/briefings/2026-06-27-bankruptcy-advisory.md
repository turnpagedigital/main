### Lede: BlockFills Confirmation Hearing Sixteen Days Out — Customer Property Question Still Unresolved

The most consequential near-term event on this beat is the July 13, 2026 confirmation hearing in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.* (dba BlockFills), Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371, before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan. 
On May 28, 2026, Judge Horan entered the Disclosure Statement Order at Docket No. 388, which approved the Disclosure Statement, set the voting deadline, and scheduled the confirmation hearing.
 
The voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline both run to July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The stakes at confirmation are not trivial. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That concession is the case's defining creditor-rights question: if the court confirms the Debtors' characterization, unsecured creditors recover against a pool that includes customer deposits; if a challenger prevails, customer assets may be carved out and recovered in priority. No court ruling on this specific question has been issued as of this writing — it remains the central open issue heading into confirmation.

The plan's economic framework pairs a Section 363 asset sale with a liquidating trust for residual claims. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock confirmed on May 26, 2026 that it had been declared the Successful Bidder.
 
A hearing to consider approval of the sale was originally scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 The docket reflects 11 new filings in the 72 hours ending June 26 (Docket Entry 467, filed June 26, 2026, through at least Entry 455), consistent with the pre-confirmation surge typical of this stage. The content of those filings — including any confirmation objections — is not retrievable from public sources at press time; treat the June 26 activity as a live signal that the record is still being built and that objections may be pending.

The gap between Keyrock's $3.25 million purchase price and the Debtors' petition-date liabilities of 
approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations
 is the clearest indicator of recovery exposure. Against 
$50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in total liabilities,
 the Keyrock consideration is unlikely to satisfy more than a fraction of unsecured claims unless the liquidating trust generates material recoveries from avoidance actions or litigation against former officers and directors. The Special Committee formed prepetition — 
constituted March 12, 2026 with authority to investigate, release, or settle potential claims against insiders
 — is therefore a material recovery variable that creditors should be tracking. Whether the plan releases those claims in exchange for insider support of confirmation, or reserves them for trust prosecution, will drive long-tail recovery math.

One additional complication: 
a creditor motion filed May 5, 2026 as Docket No. 282 asks the court to appoint an independent examiner and Chapter 11 trustee, and also seeks confirmation that the automatic stay does not apply — or relief from it — so that creditor Artha may pursue arbitration against non-debtor officers, directors, and affiliated parties.
 
The motion alleges the Debtors solicited 3,500 ETH without posting required collateral and commingled customer digital assets while insolvent.
 Whether that motion has been resolved, or remains sub judice heading into the July 13 hearing, is unconfirmed from available public sources; creditors' counsel should verify the docket status directly.

### Sleep Number: Compressed § 363 Sale Timeline; Bid Deadline in Eleven Days

*In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 26-11399, is running on one of the tightest sale timelines in recent SDNY retail practice. 
Sleep Number and its subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on June 12, 2026 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 
Judge Kyu Young Paek is overseeing the case.


The case is structured as a pure § 363 asset sale. 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale of substantially all assets under Section 363, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., who committed — subject to Court approval — to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus assumption
 of certain liabilities. 
The bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, a July 15, 2026 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.
 That is a 49-day cradle-to-close schedule from petition to anticipated consummation — fast even by SDNY standards.

The DIP structure is significant for creditor-priority analysis. 
To fund the cases and the sale process, the Debtors secured a DIP facility from their existing lender group comprising up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million in a roll-up of prepetition obligations.
 The roll-up — converting prepetition revolver exposure into super-priority DIP debt — compresses the estate's free cash position and reduces the distributable value available to general unsecured creditors downstream. 
The DIP facility imposes binding case milestones that constitute events of default if missed; the Interim Order must be entered within three days of the Petition Date and the Final Order within 30 days.
 
These milestones run concurrently with the Challenge Deadline, compressing the effective window for any committee investigation.


Petition-date financials frame the creditor recovery picture starkly. 
Sleep Number reported $319 million in net sales and a net loss of $50 million for the first quarter of 2026.
 
The company entered bankruptcy with $672 million in debt.
 
The bankruptcy petition showed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion with liabilities of more than $1 billion.
 Against a $415 million stalking horse, the math for junior constituencies is adverse absent competitive auction tension. 
The prepetition marketing process solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, generating 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals,
 which suggests Sleep Country's $415 million was the best offer available after a real process — a fact that will inform any committee objection to bid procedures or the stalking horse protections.


Court filings cite President Trump's "unpredictable" tariff policy as a material factor undermining the company's global supply chain for smart beds and mattresses, increasing operating costs and materially impacting its bottom line.
 That framing has strategic relevance: if a buyer files a MAC claim post-auction based on tariff-driven cost escalation, the case could reopen. Creditors should monitor whether the stalking horse APA contains tariff-specific MAC carveouts.

The § 341 creditors' meeting is scheduled for 
July 8, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET.
 The bidding procedures hearing is also 
scheduled for July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 That is this coming Tuesday — the first formal opportunity for any creditor, landlord, or party-in-interest to object to the sale timeline and stalking horse protections before the record closes.

### BlockFills Background: Commingling, Customer Property, and the Insolvency-Timing Dispute

A key background issue that will shape plan confirmation is the unresolved question of when BlockFills became insolvent — and whether the CRO's admitted failure to make that determination impairs the estate's capacity to pursue avoidance actions. 
At an April 16, 2026 hearing, BRG's Chief Restructuring Officer — retained in late August 2025 — testified that when asked directly whether BlockFills was insolvent as of August 2025, he stated the company "had a liquidity issue" and that a determination of insolvency would require "a more extensive analysis," declining to opine on whether liabilities exceeded assets, citing the absence of audited financial statements.
 
The CRO also testified that he had "not done an analysis to conclude on the timing" of insolvency.


That gap matters for preference and fraudulent transfer exposure. Under 11 U.S.C. § 547(b), the preference lookback runs 90 days for non-insiders and one year for insiders from the petition date. If insolvency predates August 2025 — as the Dominion Capital complaint suggests — the estate may have a broader avoidance action universe than the CRO's analysis captures. 
Dominion further alleges that BlockFills' board discovered the commingling misconduct no later than August 2025 but concealed it, continued to solicit customer activity, and ultimately suspended withdrawals.


The structural complexity of the estate compounds the challenge. 
The four Debtor entities are Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Delaware), Reliz Technologies LLC (Illinois), Reliz LTD (Cayman Islands), and Reliz CI LTD (Cayman Islands); the broader corporate structure includes numerous non-debtor subsidiaries spanning Brazil, Ireland, the United Kingdom, the UAE, Lithuania, Wyoming, and the Cayman Islands.
 Cross-border asset flows between debtor and non-debtor entities — particularly the Cayman Islands subsidiaries — represent a material recovery risk for unsecured creditors that the plan's liquidating trust will need to address explicitly.

### Broader Context: The Retail Tariff-Distress Wave and § 363 Speed Risk

Sleep Number is not an isolated data point. The company's court filings explicitly attribute distress to tariff-driven cost escalation, which is the same macro narrative running through a cohort of consumer goods and retail debtors filing in 2026. The pattern raises a structural creditor-rights concern: compressed § 363 timelines — driven by DIP milestone covenants controlled by secured lenders — systematically limit the time available for unsecured creditor committees to investigate prepetition transfers, challenge DIP roll-up terms, or mount meaningful objections to stalking horse protections. The Sleep Number bidding procedures, with a bid deadline 26 days post-petition and a sale hearing 33 days post-petition, are representative of this trend. Committees formed in the second week of a case face a near-impossible investigation window when milestone defaults are triggered within 28 days.

The Second Circuit's May 27, 2026 decision in *Ryniker v. Sumec Textile Co.*, No. 24-2090, 
concerning the Chapter 11 bankruptcy of Décor Holdings, Inc. and affiliates, sellers of decorative fabric, with Sumec Textile Company Limited listed as a major unsecured creditor,
 may have bearing on the rights of foreign trade creditors in cross-border retail insolvencies — but the full opinion is not yet available through public sources at press time; practitioners should pull the slip opinion directly before relying on it.
