## Analysis & Developments

**Today's two headline events**: Sleep Number Corporation's freefall Chapter 11 filing in the Southern District of New York — the largest retail bankruptcy of 2026 so far — enters its first critical week with a compressed 363 sale timeline that will determine whether unsecured creditors see any recovery beyond landlord cure amounts. Simultaneously, the BlockFills sale-approval hearing before Judge Horan in Delaware was on the docket yesterday (June 16), with Keyrock SA's $3.25 million stalking horse bid for substantially all assets awaiting judicial sign-off — a price point that implies severe impairment for the $145 million unsecured creditor pool. On the broader landscape, the WSJ reported this week that Georgia-Pacific is exploring a second Chapter 11 to resolve asbestos claims, a potential strategic pivot from the now-decade-old Bestwall Texas Two-Step that has paid zero dollars to approximately 56,000 current claimants. And the Law360 headline "Prince Global Tests Ch. 15 Limits" signals continuing scrutiny of the cross-border recognition proceeding in SDNY — most recently punctuated by Chief Judge Martin Glenn's indication that he would grant recognition of the BVI proceeding, notwithstanding the Sullivan & Cromwell AI-hallucination episode from April.

### Sleep Number Corporation — *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) | Judge Kyu Young Paek


Sleep Number filed for bankruptcy on Friday, June 12, 2026 in the Southern District of New York, docket No. 26-11399.
 The judge assignment confirmed this week: 
the First Day Motions hearing was held June 12, 2026 at 3:00 p.m. ET before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 The case is a prepackaged-adjacent 363 sale, not a reorganization — the company entered the courthouse with an executed stalking horse agreement.


Sleep Number reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 
The company enters bankruptcy with $672 million in debt, blaming tariffs and inflation for the filing.
 
Sleep Number cited "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."


The capital structure and DIP terms are material for creditor-recovery analysis. 
The company intends to fund its case with DIP financing from its existing lenders, including up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via a roll-up of prepetition debt.
 The roll-up mechanism — converting $195 million of prepetition debt to postpetition priority status — is the immediate pressure point for unsecured creditors: it structurally subordinates their claims below a larger senior tranche if the sale process falters.

The sale timeline is deliberately compressed. 
Pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the Stalking Horse Bidder — Sleep Country Canada — committed to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The proposed bidding procedures contemplate a 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, and a July 13, 2026 auction.
 
Because Sleep Number tried to sell itself in the months leading up to the filing, it is seeking a 26-day sale process, with competing bids due July 8 and a sale close targeted for July 31.


Pre-petition marketing context matters for assessing whether the stalking horse price is full value or a motivated seller's floor. 
The company conducted a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, generating 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals.
 
Beginning in March, Sleep Number solicited recapitalization and bridge financing proposals through Guggenheim, which contacted 33 parties — 26 of whom executed NDAs. Only one party submitted a written recapitalization proposal, which the company determined was not actionable. While Sleep Number received several proposals for senior bridge financing, the existing lender group would not consent to being primed, and no actionable proposals for unsecured or junior financing were received.


The practical implication: an exhaustive prepetition process with no actionable alternative to a Section 363 sale. For unsecured creditors, the critical question is whether the $415 million gross purchase price — after satisfying the $260 million DIP (new money plus roll-up), cure costs across 
572 U.S. retail stores
, and senior secured claims — leaves any waterfall residue. The bidding procedures hearing is scheduled for 
July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 Creditors intending to object to bid procedures or the DIP terms must monitor that docket. 
The Section 341 meeting of creditors is set for July 8, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET by Zoom.
 
A final hearing on First Day Motions is scheduled for July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The $415 million stalking horse offer from Sleep Country Canada creates an interesting dynamic given the cross-border combination rationale; Canadian regulatory approval could independently slow the close timeline even if Judge Paek approves the sale on schedule.

One structural oddity worth tracking: 
Sleep Number recently entered into a sponsorship agreement with NFL star Travis Kelce, making him a top shareholder with a 5% stake; it was not clear how the sponsorship agreement will be treated in the bankruptcy.
 Equity-to-sponsorship arrangements of this type — where a celebrity endorser holds an equity interest — raise assumption/rejection questions under Section 365 and potential preference exposure depending on when the equity stake was issued.

### BlockFills — *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) (Bankr. D. Del.) | Judge Thomas M. Horan

The live data shows 8 new docket entries in the last 72 hours, with entries 431 and 433 filed June 16 — the date the sale-approval hearing was calendared. 
The sale approval hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 As of this writing, the outcome of that hearing has not been confirmed in publicly available sources; this advisory will update once the order posts to the Veritaglobal claims administrator page or the CourtListener docket (ID 72494188).

The economic context for that hearing: 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 Against a creditor pool where 
unsecured creditors have $145 million at stake
 and 
the company reported assets of $50 million to $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million
, a $3.25 million gross sale price implies recovery in the low single digits for unsecured creditors — if any — after DIP payoff and administrative expenses.

The case's central unresolved issue — whether customer digital assets constitute estate property — is now converging with the sale approval in a way that matters for recovery allocation. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 Keyrock's bid assumes "substantially all" assets, but if a court later determines that segregated customer funds are not estate property, the purchase price denominator shrinks and any trust-like obligation to return those assets to customers sits outside the distribution waterfall entirely.

Background on the liquidity cascade that drove the filing: 
the company attributes its crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, and stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 Compounding those structural impairments, 
Dominion Capital sued Reliz alleging misappropriation of funds and concealing losses, charging that BlockFills "admitted to having commingled customer assets and having a balance sheet shortfall" in a February 7, 2026 call.
 
The courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.


The confirmation timeline — which runs in parallel with the sale track — shows a plan confirmation hearing set for 
September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 This bifurcated structure (363 sale to Keyrock + a residual plan for remaining estate administration and distributions) means creditors holding Rule 3001 claim transfer notices should monitor whether the plan provides any meaningful recovery mechanism beyond the sale proceeds — and at this price, the math is difficult.

### Georgia-Pacific / Bestwall LLC — Texas Two-Step, Second Bankruptcy Reported

The WSJ reported this week that Georgia-Pacific is exploring a second Chapter 11 filing to resolve its asbestos claims — a significant potential shift from the Bestwall Texas Two-Step strategy in place since 2017. The underlying Bestwall case (*In re Bestwall LLC*, No. 17-31795, Bankr. W.D.N.C., Judge Laura Turner Beyer) has been running for nearly nine years. 
As recently as February 19, 2026, the Bestwall docket reflected the 19th continued hearing on the disclosure statement for the amended plan, with objections from the Official Committee of Asbestos Claimants and the Future Claimants' Representative.



Georgia-Pacific is reportedly preparing a new bankruptcy filing to resolve tens of thousands of pending asbestos and mesothelioma claims.
 
According to court filings and the Wall Street Journal's reporting, Georgia-Pacific plans to use an existing affiliate or a newly created entity to file a fresh Chapter 11 case, seeking enough claimant support to fast-track a resolution.
 
Law firms representing claimants have characterized this as the company having given up on the Bestwall approach after years of procedural battles and failed plan negotiations.


The financial stakes in any new filing are substantial. 
Koch Industries received $2.5 billion in dividends in 2022 from Georgia-Pacific and has received $5 billion in dividends since asbestos liabilities were moved to Bestwall.
 
Since its bankruptcy filing, Bestwall has paid zero dollars to its approximately 56,000 current asbestos claimants, a number that rises with every passing day.
 The Senators' amicus brief to SCOTUS frames the core legal issue: 
the Fourth Circuit approved the stay of asbestos litigation against Georgia-Pacific, with the court's dissenters arguing the bankruptcy system was not designed to provide solvent non-debtors with the option to decline liability for alleged wrongdoing.



The financial terms of any bankruptcy trust — payout schedules, disease category values, documentation requirements, and claimants' rights to challenge the plan — are what determine whether victims receive meaningful compensation, and none of those terms are yet public.
 For distressed investors, a second filing presents an information asymmetry play: claim values in any new trust depend entirely on the trust distribution procedures negotiated between debtors' counsel and the FCR/ACC, and that negotiation has not started. Trading in Bestwall claims is constrained by the current stay; a new filing would reset the trading market.

### Prince Global Holdings Ltd. — *In re Prince Global Holdings Ltd., et al.*, No. 26-10769 (MG) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) | Chief Judge Martin Glenn


On April 8, 2026, the Prince Group filed for Chapter 15 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, seeking recognition of insolvency proceedings in the British Virgin Islands.
 
Sullivan & Cromwell represents the Joint Provisional Liquidators — Paul Pretlove, David Standish, and James Drury — of 30 BVI-incorporated Prince Group entities. The liquidators were appointed by the Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (BVI) upon an application by the Attorney General of the BVI in what is understood to be the first public interest provisional liquidation in BVI history.



The filing follows the indictment of Chen Zhi, the controlling owner of the Prince Group, by the U.S. Department of Justice for crimes related to the Prince Group's operation across Cambodia that stole billions of dollars from victims in the United States and around the world.
 The underlying fraud was described by U.S. and U.K. authorities as a Cambodia-based "pig butchering" network. 
The liquidators are court-appointed representatives of companies linked by U.S. and U.K. authorities to a massive Cambodia-based network that used human-trafficked captives to sell scam crypto.


The case has been generating attention for two distinct reasons. First, recognition: 
a New York bankruptcy judge said he would grant recognition of the British Virgin Islands insolvency proceeding of Prince Global Holdings.
 The Law360 headline from May 22 — "Prince Global Director Asks Court To Deny Ch. 15 Recognition" — suggests at least one insider is contesting the recognition order, which would be the mechanism for unlocking U.S. asset freezes and coordinating cross-border recovery for fraud victims.

Second, the AI sanctions episode: 
Sullivan & Cromwell issued a rare public mea culpa after discovering that an emergency motion it filed contained fabricated and misquoted legal authorities generated by AI. In an April 18 letter to Chief Judge Martin Glenn, partner Andrew Dietderich acknowledged the firm "deeply" regrets that its April 9 emergency motion for provisional relief contained "inaccurate citations and other errors," some of which were AI "hallucinations."
 
Sullivan & Cromwell self-reported to Chief Judge Glenn that the filing contained fabricated citations, identifying inaccurate references to *In re Three Arrows Capital, Ltd.* (struck entirely from paragraphs 27, 34, and 55), *In re Soundview Elite Ltd.*, *In re BYJU's Alpha*, and *In re Team Systems International*, among others.
 This episode is now a reference point in the ongoing judicial discussion about AI-assisted brief drafting — and it surfaces in a high-profile Chapter 15 fraud-recovery matter where the creditors are primarily individual fraud victims, not institutional holders. The reputational and sanctions exposure for counsel is disproportionately high in cases where victims are watching.
