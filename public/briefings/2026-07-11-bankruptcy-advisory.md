# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Saturday, July 11, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The weekend opens at a pivot point for two key-focus cases.** Sleep Number Corporation heads into Monday's auction with its July 31 close target intact but unsecured creditors facing near-certain zero recovery. Separately, in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.* (BlockFills), the confirmation hearing scheduled for July 13 — the same morning as the Sleep Number auction — will test whether Judge Horan approves the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan that channels Keyrock SA's $3.25 million asset-sale proceeds into a liquidating trust structure for approximately $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations. The gap between those two numbers concentrates the recovery question for every creditor in that case.

### Sleep Number — *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, No. 26-11399 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) (Judge Kyu Young Paek, Courtroom 610)

The auction is Monday, July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, Courtroom 610 at 450 Lexington Avenue. 
The sale objection deadline passed July 10 at 4:00 p.m. ET; a post-auction objection deadline runs July 14 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the sale hearing is set for July 15 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
The entire construct was built around a 26-day postpetition diligence period, with a closing by July 31, 2026.


The structural terms are now locked. 
SNBR Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc., committed to purchase substantially all assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities under a Stalking Horse APA signed June 12, 2026.
 
To displace the stalking horse, a competing bidder must clear an effective floor of $441.45 million — the $415 million base price plus $16.45 million in bid protections payable in cash plus a $10 million minimum overbid — with the stalking horse receiving a credit equal to the full bid protections amount in each auction round.


The capital structure sets the ceiling on any unsecured recovery. 
The first-day declaration identifies approximately $672.5 million of funded debt, comprising roughly $475 million outstanding under a revolver, $177.5 million under a 2021 term loan, and an additional $20 million term loan entered into in 2026.
 
The bid that emerged covers roughly 62 cents on the dollar of the funded debt, well short of the full payment the milestones contemplated.
 
Sleep Number reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities, and the filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — but that statement requires context: the waterfall to secured lenders first absorbs the entire $415 million floor, leaving nothing for general unsecured claims absent a materially higher competing bid.


The DIP facility comprises up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million in a roll-up of prepetition obligations converted on a 3:1 basis.
 That roll-up is the structural element creditors' counsel will scrutinize at the July 15 sale hearing: prepetition lenders converted their exposure into DIP priority, further subordinating any residual unsecured claim. 
Davis Polk & Wardwell is debtors' legal advisor, Guggenheim Securities its investment banker, AP Services (an affiliate of AlixPartners) its interim management.
 CRO Kent Percy of AP Services was appointed June 12. Debtors' counsel Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk has been lead signatory on all filed pleadings.

The lease portfolio adds a variable the sale price alone does not resolve. 
Sleep Number operates 572 leased retail stores across all 50 states — it owns no real property — carrying approximately $348 million in present value of operating lease liabilities as of April 4, 2026.
 
Sleep Number filed a motion with the bankruptcy court to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations, with A&G Real Estate Partners assisting with that review.
 The Stalking Horse APA explicitly excludes any executory leases or contracts the purchaser chooses to reject, meaning landlords on the rejected list face administrative-period rent exposure and rejection damages capped at the greater of one year of rent or 15% of remaining lease value under 11 U.S.C. § 502(b)(6). The PCI limited objection filed at Dkt. 242 on July 10 — among the last-day sale objection filings — reflects the contested assumption-and-assignment track that will run in parallel with the sale hearing.

One pre-petition liquidity move is worth tracking for claim-recovery purposes. 
On May 13, 2026, pursuant to a participation agreement under the Thirteenth Amendment, SNBC sold to a third-party buyer all of its rights in claims for tariff refunds previously paid to U.S. Customs and Border Protection under IEEPA, along with related rights (the "IEEPA Tariff Refund Sale").
 That monetization reduced the estate's contingent asset base; distressed buyers who purchased those IEEPA refund claims now hold rights outside the bankruptcy estate.

### BlockFills — *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-10371 (TMH) (Bankr. D. Del.) (Judge Thomas M. Horan)


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11. The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.


The plan confirmation hearing is Monday, July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET before Judge Horan. 
The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the Confirmation Hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The plan's economic core is a severe haircut. 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 Set against that figure, 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The arithmetic is stark: $3.25 million against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a sub-3-cent recovery before administrative costs and plan expenses.

The sale to Keyrock cleared the court. 
As set out in an official Bankruptcy Court document filed May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
A bankruptcy court hearing to approve the sale was scheduled for June 16, with completion of the transaction also dependent on obtaining the necessary regulatory approvals.
 The Veritaglobal case calendar confirms the sale hearing ran June 16, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET, and the case continued post-sale on a plan confirmation track.


On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order Approving Disclosure Statement; Scheduling Hearing on Confirmation of Plan; Establishing Deadlines and Procedures for Filing Objections to Confirmation of Plan; Claim Objections; and Temporary Allowance of Claims for Voting Purposes, among other relief (Dkt. 388).
 That order operationalized the solicitation process and set the July 13 hearing date now imminent.

The fraud backstory remains a live creditor-recovery issue. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two customer lawsuits: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026). Both courts granted TROs that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 Any causes of action against former directors and officers that survive plan confirmation represent the only meaningful upside recovery vector for general unsecured creditors given the Keyrock sale price. Whether those claims were preserved in the liquidating trust and how they were valued in the plan will be central to any objection at Monday's confirmation hearing.

The claims bar deadline remains open: 
the general claims bar date runs through September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Any creditor who has not filed a proof of claim remains at risk of disallowance. Claims are processed through Veritaglobal (formerly KCC), BlockFills Claims Processing Center, 222 N. Pacific Coast Highway, Suite 300, El Segundo, CA 90245.

### Broader Landscape: Confluence of § 363 and Plan Confirmation Risk

The Sleep Number and BlockFills cases, arriving at their respective decision points simultaneously on Monday, July 13, illustrate a recurring structural tension in rapid-timeline § 363 sales. In Sleep Number, the compressed 33-day petition-to-sale-hearing schedule — justified by the debtors as necessary to prevent administrative-cost bleed and preserve workforce and supplier confidence — also compresses the window for any Unsecured Creditors' Committee to mount a meaningful challenge to the DIP roll-up or the bid protections structure. 
The tax-attribute motion preserves roughly $75.9 million in federal and state net operating losses and interest carryforwards; in a § 363 asset sale, those attributes typically stay with the selling entity rather than transfer to the buyer, so their near-term utility is limited.
 They represent the only residual asset in the reorganized shell, and their ultimate realization depends on whether the debtors have any post-sale taxable income against which to apply them.

In BlockFills, the pre-negotiated Ad Hoc Group structure — customers who were simultaneously the largest creditors and the constituency most motivated to support a new operating entity — created a confirmation dynamic that may not have been fully adversarial. Smaller creditors who did not participate in the Ad Hoc Group's negotiations face the same plan treatment with no equivalent leverage. The committee counsel (Morris James / Bingham Greenebaum Doll) retained at Dkt. 262 is the institutional check on that dynamic, and its pre-hearing filing posture will signal whether a contested confirmation is in prospect for Monday.
