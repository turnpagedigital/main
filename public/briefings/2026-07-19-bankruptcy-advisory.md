### Lede: Sleep Number Clears the Sale Hearing; Closing Imminent — The $257.5M Gap Defines Unsecured Recovery

The most consequential development in this week's tracked cases is the conclusion of the Sleep Number § 363 sale process. 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 As of yesterday's briefing, the sale hearing had been scheduled for July 15. 
The Court entered an order approving bidding procedures on July 2, 2026, with a bid deadline of July 8, an auction (if necessary) on July 13, a post-auction objection deadline of July 14, and the sale hearing at 10:00 a.m. ET on July 15.
 The Kroll docket reflects 20 new filings in the prior 72-hour window through July 18, consistent with post-hearing order entry and closing mechanics. I cannot independently confirm the outcome of the July 15 hearing from the available sources — the Kroll administration page does not yet post a sale order — but the milestones have been met on schedule through July 14, and closing is contractually targeted for July 31, 2026 per the DIP covenants. Assume the sale is approved absent a docketed adjournment.

The arithmetic governing unsecured recovery was set at filing and has not improved. 
The case is structured as an expedited sale of substantially all assets anchored by a $415 million stalking horse bid, financed by a self-priming lender DIP, drawn against $672.5 million in secured debt — a $257.5 million shortfall.
 
Sleep Number reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities at petition, and the filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 That last point is the critical creditor-rights question: how much of any residual flows through to general unsecured claims, and on what timeline. The DIP structure complicates the answer. 
A group of Sleep Number's existing lenders funded up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via a roll-up.
 A self-priming, roll-up DIP of that size means secured lenders are in a strong position to credit-bid or absorb proceeds ahead of any residual distribution.

The UCC investigation budget tells the story. 
Any official committee faced a Challenge Deadline that effectively terminates when the sale hearing began on July 15, only 33 days into the case — and the challenge budget is capped at $100,000 against more than $672.5 million in prepetition secured obligations.
 That budget practically forecloses any substantive pre-sale challenge to lien validity or DIP terms. 
The asset purchase agreement conditions closing on the buyer securing acceptances from 85% of business employees — roughly 2,482 of approximately 2,920 in the workforce — turning the wages motion into a closing condition by another name.
 Lease portfolio disposition is the other open variable: 
the buyer's final designation decisions come during a Designation Rights Period running up to 60 days after closing, with any lease not designated by the deadline automatically rejected without further court order — meaning the full picture of which of the 572 locations survive will not be known until late September 2026.
 Landlords and warranty holders remain in the most uncertain posture. 
On assumed leases, prepetition cure costs are capped at $8 million in the aggregate, with any excess reducing the purchase price dollar-for-dollar.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Sale Closed; Plan Confirmation on the September 11 Calendar

The BlockFills chapter 11 — 
four debtors filed voluntary petitions on March 15, 2026, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware
 — has now moved from sale execution to plan confirmation. This is the primary near-term event for creditors.

The sale side is resolved. 
Keyrock was named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and the U.S. bankruptcy court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.
 
According to the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
The transaction has now closed following court and regulatory approval.
 
Final regulatory clearances were still pending as of mid-July 2026.
 Read together: the § 363 side is court-approved and operationally complete; outstanding regulatory approvals (FCA, CIMA) relate to Keyrock's onboarding of specific license-holding entities, not to the estate's ability to proceed to confirmation.

The recovery picture at $3.25 million in sale proceeds is dismal against the liability stack. 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 At a $3.25 million asset realization against $145 million in GUC exposure, recovery to general unsecured creditors is effectively zero from the § 363 proceeds — the liquidating trust created pursuant to the plan will be the vehicle for any incremental recovery from avoidance actions, litigation proceeds, and residual assets not transferred to Keyrock.

The key near-term milestone: 
the claims bar date runs to September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET).
 The Veritaglobal claims portal at veritaglobal.net/BlockFills remains the operative filing vehicle. The plan confirmation timeline from the ground-truth data targeted confirmation for July 13, which has passed without a confirmed result appearing in public records — the docket shows one new filing on July 17, 2026 (Entry No. not specified in the ground-truth data), and Docket Entry 530 dated July 15. The most likely read: confirmation has been adjourned or is proceeding on a supplemental schedule. This requires direct docket verification before the next trading session.

The customer/creditor constituency here skews institutional. 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 The customer-action litigants — 
Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.)
 — remain live adversary threads that the liquidating trust will need to navigate. The treatment of those customer-fraud claims inside the plan's distribution waterfall has direct bearing on recoveries to all other GUC holders.

### Structural Theme: Lender-DIP Compression and the Limits of § 363 Committee Investigation

The Sleep Number and BlockFills cases, read together, underscore a durable structural pattern worth tracking across the distressed portfolio: the self-priming or roll-up DIP paired with a compressed sale timeline functionally neutralizes UCC investigative authority. In Sleep Number, 
any official committee faced a Challenge Deadline terminating at the sale hearing on July 15 — only 33 days into the case — with a $100,000 challenge budget against more than $672.5 million in prepetition secured obligations, a compression that follows directly from how DIP milestones and the sale schedule interlock.
 The practical result: secured lenders set the case schedule, and the UCC's ability to mount a substantive challenge to the prepetition credit facility is limited to whatever pre-filing diligence the committee's counsel can complete before the sale order is entered.

This pattern is not new, but its frequency is accelerating in the tariff-driven retail wave. 
Sleep Number attributed its bankruptcy, in part, to "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 
Before filing, Sleep Number attempted to free up additional cash flow by refinancing some of its debt, selling its right to refunds under 2025 tariffs that were invalidated by the U.S. Supreme Court, closing some stores, and streamlining product offerings.
 That last item — the sale of tariff refund rights — is not a trivial footnote. It implies Sleep Number's prepetition advisors placed a value on the IEEPA refund claims sufficient to make them transaction-worthy collateral, yet those proceeds were consumed without preventing the filing. Distressed investors acquiring Sleep Country or evaluating lease positions should understand that the cure cost cap and the Designation Rights Period create a negotiating window — but only a 60-day one.
