## Analysis & Developments

**Today's signal event is a $20 million SEC whistleblower award — the program's largest payout of 2026 to date — tied to a securities manipulation scheme that generated more than $75 million in sanctions.** The award, formalized in an SEC order dated June 25, confirms that the current Commission continues to grant high-quality original-information awards even as it tightens eligibility standards agency-wide. ### 1. SEC Whistleblower Award — June 25, 2026 Order: $20M, $75M+ Underlying Sanctions


Silver Law Group and the Law Firm of David R. Chase announced Thursday that their client received an SEC whistleblower award of approximately $20 million, per an SEC order filed June 25.
 
The claimant provided information connected to an alleged securities manipulation scheme.
 
The SEC's enforcement action in the underlying matter exceeded $75 million in sanctions.
 
The order does not publicly identify the whistleblower or the company; it notes that the claimant provided significant information about misconduct that would have been difficult to detect without the tip.


The award is material for claims-side practitioners for two reasons. First, it confirms that the manipulation-scheme track — distinct from the Ponzi-scheme and accounting-fraud tracks that dominate whistleblower lore — remains an active enforcement target under the current Republican-majority Commission. 
If successful, whistleblowers under the program may receive between 10% and 30% of sanctions collected on a judgment of at least $1 million.
 Backing into the math: a $20 million award on a $75+ million sanctions base implies an award percentage near the 26–27% range — above the program midpoint, suggesting the claimant received credit for cooperation quality and/or for detecting conduct the SEC could not have uncovered on its own.

Second, the award has to be read against the program's broader 2025–2026 trajectory. 
The SEC has been denying a record percentage of whistleblower claims, with the commission — now with a Republican majority — issuing 31 consecutive denials between April 21 and July 15, 2025, covering at least 55 different tipsters.
 
Final orders from that period show a clear shift away from the prior commission's practice of waiving program rules for tipsters who published online or shared information with media before coming to the SEC — a practice the prior commission was more likely to accommodate.
 
Notably, the SEC's own list of its largest awards includes a $50 million award dated April 7, 2026
, confirming that large awards are still being granted this year alongside the tighter screening posture. For potential whistleblowers and their counsel: the program remains structurally viable, but procedural compliance — particularly the requirement to file with the SEC before self-publishing or going to media — is now enforced strictly.

### 2. Madoff / Picard: Seventeenth Distribution Underway, Total Recovery Approaches $15.411B


On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion — which includes approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances — with the seventeenth distribution representing 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.



Combined with the prior sixteen distributions, approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.
 
As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion in total.
 The case — *Securities Investor Protection Corp. v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, No. 08-01789 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — remains pending before Judge Lori G. Belair (having migrated from the late Judge Bernstein). 
No recovered funds are used to pay costs associated with the recovery; all trustee, legal, and accounting fees and administrative expenses are paid by SIPC.


For claims traders: at 72.848% cumulative recovery against admitted claims of approximately $20.3 billion, the residual distribution potential depends entirely on additional litigation proceeds. 
As of April 24, 2026, SIPC had committed approximately $850.9 million in advances to the liquidation; SIPC is reimbursed once each respective customer claim is fully satisfied, with $272.4 million already reimbursed and $578.5 million in SIPC advances still outstanding.
 The Fiftieth Fee Application — noted on the trustee's site as filed February 13, 2026 — is the next signal to watch for fee-creep implications on residual distributions.

### 3. Stanford Financial Receivership: Final Distribution Plan Executing; Escalona Challenge Filed

The Stanford Financial receivership (U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division; Receiver Ralph Janvey, represented by Baker Botts) has entered terminal distribution mode. 
In preparation for commencing the Final Distribution, during the first two months of 2026 the receivership issued payments to eligible claimants totaling more than $100 million pursuant to Distribution Plans 1–11.
 
Payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026, per the Receiver's update posted April 6, 2026.



As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimants
 under the court-approved Final Distribution Plan (as amended). The cross-border dimension that has historically complicated this receivership — the Sociéte Générale settlement and the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal — remains unresolved on at least one piece. 
Distribution of funds from the $157 million Sociéte Générale settlement must await the final resolution of the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal.
 That is a material holdback for claimants who have been tracking the receivership's UK/Antigua coordination track.


A new challenge surfaced: in May 2026, Jaime Escalona submitted materials to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division regarding the Receiver's distribution process and included requests for relief from the court; on June 1, 2026, the Receiver responded, including an update on the status of ongoing distributions.
 This is unverified as to merit — the Receiver's June 1 filing is the operative document to review — but any court-directed modification to the Final Distribution Plan at this stage would affect timing for claimants waiting on later tranches. Claims investors holding Stanford CD claims should verify their claimant IDs are current in the Gilardi system before any stale-check cutoffs are applied.

### 4. Power Block Coin (d/b/a SmartFi) — *In re Power Block Coin, LLC*, No. 24-23041 (Bankr. D. Utah): Liquidating Trust Plan, $192M+ Claims Stack, Insider-Transaction Disputes

The case filed June 20, 2024 continues under the jurisdiction of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah. Per the tracked-case ground truth, the most recent docket entry confirmed is No. 519 (filed June 9, 2026), with the case assigned to Judge Cathleen D. Parker. (Note: earlier published materials referenced Judge Joel T. Marker; the ground truth block governs — Judge Parker is the current presiding judge as of the data snapshot.)


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors filed a comprehensive liquidating plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041), filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, proposing to wind down the company through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
Power Block Coin, which operated under the name SmartFi, filed for bankruptcy on June 20, 2024; the plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.



The committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions — including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, which the committee flagged for its unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 The Solara loan is the highest-risk insider-transaction item on the docket: if it is recharacterized as equity or avoided as a fraudulent transfer under 11 U.S.C. § 548 or applicable Utah UVTA provisions, recovery to the unsecured estate improves materially.


The Celsius-related objecting creditors have asserted over $133 million in preference claims against Power Block Coin in connection with the pending Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy (Case No. 22-10964, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.).
 That inter-estate coordination — Celsius as creditor here, debtor elsewhere — creates a structurally unusual dynamic in which any Celsius distribution to SmartFi creditors is itself subject to Celsius's own plan waterfall. Claims buyers entering here need to understand that the effective recovery is two-estate dependent.

The June 9, 2026 docket entry (No. 519) carries no description in the CourtListener feed. Given the May 2026 noticing activity visible in Doc 502 (filed May 7, 2026), which included service of plan-related documents on Celsius, Eckert Seamans parties, and Jones Waldo, the June filing likely relates to plan confirmation process milestones. Unverified as to specific content; review the CourtListener docket directly at https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68873409/power-block-coin-llc/ before trading.
