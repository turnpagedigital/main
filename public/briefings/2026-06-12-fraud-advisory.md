### I. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: June 16 Record Date, July 31 Payment, and the $600 Million Reserve Release

The FTX reorganization — the largest crypto bankruptcy in history, pending before the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware — is now in its fifth and most consequential distribution cycle. FTX Trading Ltd. (d/b/a FTX.com) and the FTX Recovery Trust announced that the record date for the next distribution will be **June 16, 2026** for holders of allowed FTX claims and interests, with the distribution itself expected to commence on **July 31, 2026**.

The financial stakes are substantial. The fifth creditor distribution has a June 16, 2026 record date and payment commencing July 31, 2026; the disputed claims reserve is simultaneously being reduced from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion — a $600 million cut, subject to court approval — freeing additional liquidity, with cumulative distributions now exceeding $10 billion.

Separately, holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims, as defined in FTX's confirmed Chapter 11 Plan of Restructuring, will be eligible to begin the NFT Distribution process on **June 30, 2026**; and FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which — subject to court approval — would release cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims.

Operationally, the compliance requirements are hard gates, not soft recommendations. Customers and creditors must complete KYC verification, submit required tax forms, and onboard with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer to receive a distribution; for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.

Recovery counsel should flag a structural grievance that remains live for many creditors: the "120% recovery" headline is calculated against the USD value of assets on November 11, 2022 — when FTX filed for bankruptcy, with Bitcoin trading at approximately $16,871 — meaning a creditor who held 1 BTC receives approximately $20,245 (120% of petition-date value), while the same Bitcoin is now worth multiples of that amount. This valuation methodology has been the subject of sustained creditor dissatisfaction but is locked into the confirmed plan. Phishing emails impersonating the FTX Recovery Trust are already circulating — a material fraud-on-fraud vector that claim-holders and their counsel must actively monitor.

The June 9, 2026 Entry No. 519 filing almost certainly reflects continued adversary-proceeding activity or plan-confirmation preparation.

### III. *SEC v. Heller et al.* — E.D. Pa. — $770 Million ATM Ponzi, $400 Million in Investor Losses, Parallel Criminal Prosecution

The SEC's civil complaint against Daryl F. Heller — filed September 3, 2025 in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania — remains the most significant active Ponzi recovery matter in the eastern federal corridor. The SEC announced charges against Heller and his businesses, Prestige Investment Group, LLC and Paramount Management Group, LLC, for a multi-year Ponzi scheme that cost investors approximately $400 million; the alleged fraud took place from 2017 through 2024 and involved over $770 million across roughly 2,700 investors who were told they were investing in an ATM network.

The scheme's mechanics were elaborately deceptive. The complaint alleges that over seven and a half years, Heller and Prestige raised more than $770 million from approximately 2,700 investors — many of them retail investors and members of the Amish and Mennonite communities in the Lancaster, Pennsylvania area — promising an approximately 25% return from fixed monthly distributions from income earned from pooled ATM transaction fees. Internal Paramount records contradicted inflated figures provided to investors, showing discrepancies of over 10,500 ATMs in service and over $380 million in ATM fee revenue compared to actual performance in a 21-month period; ATM portfolios sold to investors were made up of used and often damaged or inoperable ATMs that Paramount bought for as little as $527 each, with Heller marking up ATM values by sometimes over 42-fold.

The complaint further alleges that Heller misappropriated over $185 million in investor funds, which he used to acquire a beach house and redirect funds into his other businesses. Distributions stopped in April 2024, followed by false assurances of a full capital return.

The civil complaint — which seeks permanent injunctions, disgorgement with prejudgment interest, civil penalties, and an officer-and-director bar under Sections 17(a) of the Securities Act of 1933 and 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5 — runs parallel to criminal proceedings. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania announced criminal charges against Heller in a parallel action. Recovery counsel representing the approximately 2,700 victims should note that financial institutions that made loans to Heller collateralized by the ATMs are pursuing their debt in bankruptcy proceedings — a secured-creditor priority fight that could materially compress the unsecured investor recovery pool. Third-party promoter liability is also in scope: investors in Prestige Funds and WF Velocity Funds may have options to seek compensation from third-party entities that played a role in promoting, enabling, and/or assisting the alleged fraud, per law firms actively investigating broker-dealer and referral-network liability.

### IV. First Liberty Building & Loan — $140 Million Georgia Ponzi — State and Federal Enforcement Stack

The First Liberty Building & Loan collapse — a $140 million Ponzi scheme operating from at least 2014 through June 2025 — continues to generate layered civil and criminal exposure across state and federal tracks. The SEC filed a formal complaint against First Liberty and Frost IV, labeling the operation a $140 million Ponzi scheme, on July 10, 2025. Investigators have recovered only $3.5 million of the $140 million defrauded from approximately 300 investors since 2014 — a 2.5% recovery rate that signals the estate's assets have been substantially dissipated or remain untraced.

At the state level, enforcement has escalated sharply in 2026. Georgia Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger issued an emergency order against Timothy Nathaniel Darnell of Powder Springs, barring him from securities activity in Georgia, imposing a $500,000 civil penalty, and referring him for potential criminal prosecution in connection with the collapse of First Liberty Building and Loan, which has a consent judgment identifying it as a Ponzi scheme. The enforcement action came one week after the Secretary of State's office issued a similar $500,000 fine and criminal referral against Edwin Brant Frost V, a principal figure in the case; the February 24, 2026 Emergency Order to Cease and Desist alleges Darnell violated multiple provisions of the Georgia Uniform Securities Act while recommending First Liberty investments to clients.

The recovery math for the roughly 300 investors remains deeply unfavorable. With $3.5 million traced against $140 million in losses, any meaningful recovery will depend on (a) successful disgorgement in the SEC's federal civil action, (b) criminal restitution orders in parallel proceedings, and (c) third-party claims against financial institutions, referral agents, and promoters who facilitated investor onboarding.

### V. SEC FY2025 Enforcement Backdrop — Institutional Context for Ponzi Recovery Posture

The SEC's April 7, 2026 announcement of FY2025 enforcement results (covering October 2024 through September 2025) provides the institutional framework within which all current Ponzi recovery matters are developing. In FY2025, the SEC filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions — a decrease of 22% and 30%, respectively, from FY2024. The current SEC administration under Chairman Paul Atkins is prioritizing cases involving "fraud, market manipulation, and abuses of trust" — with a stated emphasis on "holding individual wrongdoers accountable."

Critically, the FY2025 headline dollar figures require disaggregation. The Commission obtained orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion, of which $10.8 billion was disgorgement of ill-gotten gains and prejudgment interest and $7.2 billion in civil penalties. However, after excluding "deemed satisfied" amounts and judgments against Robert Allen Stanford and other defendants in the Commission's long-running litigation concerning their $8 billion Ponzi scheme, the monetary relief obtained in FY2025 totaled $1.4 billion in disgorgement and prejudgment interest and $1.3 billion in civil penalties. The Stanford carve-out matters operationally: it signals that courts and regulators continue to treat legacy mega-Ponzi estates as a distinct remediation track, and counsel should not assume that aggregate SEC statistics map directly to available recovery pools in active matters.

The Division highlighted enforcement actions including: Paramount Management Group, LLC, Prestige Investment Group, LLC, and their founder Daryl F. Heller, in connection with a Ponzi scheme that allegedly defrauded approximately 2,700 investors and resulted in $400 million in investor losses; and First Liberty Building & Loan, LLC and its owner Edwin Brant Frost IV, in connection with an alleged Ponzi scheme that defrauded approximately 300 investors of more than $140 million.

Ponzi litigation continues to proliferate, with numerous complaints filed across dozens of federal district and state courts in the first three months of 2026 alone, driven both by smaller schemes and larger schemes spawning a constellation of related lawsuits. The McGuireWoods Ponzi Perspectives team notes that government enforcement actions continue to play a significant role in Ponzi litigation, often serving as the catalyst for subsequent civil litigation — a sequencing dynamic that recovery counsel should exploit by monitoring SEC and DOJ press releases as early-warning indicators for civil filing windows.
