### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399 (KYP) — Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — New Filing, Fast-Track § 363


Sleep Number Corporation filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on June 12, 2026, assigned case number 26-11399.
 
The petition reports assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion and liabilities exceeding $1 billion.
 
As of the petition date, substantially all of the company's debt — approximately $672.5 million in aggregate principal — is in default and accelerated, subject to the automatic stay under the Bankruptcy Code.


The judge designation is confirmed as KYP on the docket. 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale of substantially all assets under § 363, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc.
 
Pursuant to a Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the stalking horse bidder has committed — subject to court approval — to acquire substantially all of the company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.


The sale timeline is compressed. 
Building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties (19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals), the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, and a July 13, 2026 auction.
 Debtors' counsel is 
Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.



The company's distress was compounded by pandemic-era overexpansion of its store footprint, manufacturing capabilities, and debt levels, as well as the April 2025 wave of U.S. tariffs — including IEEPA-based levies — followed by a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs, leaving the company navigating continuing regulatory uncertainty.
 
Cost-cutting under CEO Linda Findley, appointed April 2025, reduced operating costs by $136 million versus 2024, and the company monetized liquidity through April 2026 prepetition term loans and an IEEPA tariff-refund claim sale, but a parallel recapitalization outreach yielded only a single non-actionable proposal.



The case is jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), and § 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code authorizes the U.S. Trustee to appoint an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors.
 
The organizational meeting for unsecured creditors' committee formation is set for June 22, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. ET.
 
The petition reports 50-99 creditors
 — a notably small number for a retailer of this scale, suggesting the capital structure is tightly held and the UCC formation process may move quickly. The key creditor-rights question here is whether a UCC with meaningful leverage can extract any value improvement over the stalking horse floor, or whether the speed of the sale process forecloses it. The debt/asset gap — $672.5 million in liabilities against $642.3 million in assets — implies that even at the $415 million sale price, unsecured recoveries will depend entirely on what liabilities are assumed by the buyer and what administrative claims consume before distribution.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Bankr. D. Del.) — Sale Approval, Plan Confirmation in View

The sale-approval hearing in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), was held June 16 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in Courtroom No. 5, U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, 824 Market Street, Wilmington. 
Keyrock SA has been declared the successful bidder for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors, having agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.



Reliz is operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with the sale hearing set for June 16, 2026, and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 
The June 12 agenda confirmed the sale of substantially all assets was going forward at the June 16 hearing, while a protective-order matter was adjourned and sale objections remained from Chicago Blackhawk Hockey Team and Oracle, per the hearing agenda at Docket No. 424.



On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the disclosure statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing voting deadlines and procedures, and granting related relief [Docket No. 388].
 The claims bar date for general creditors is 
September 11, 2026, at 4:00 p.m. ET
, per the Veritaglobal case page.

The economics remain stark. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt: the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against a $3.25 million sale price, recoveries for general unsecured creditors under the plan — even after factoring in residual cash, avoidance actions, and potential litigation proceeds — face severe compression. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate
 — a position with profound implications for the roughly 2,000 institutional clients who may have expected their assets to be segregated.


Two customer lawsuits followed the withdrawal suspension: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026. On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (approximately $4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 Those adversary dynamics continue to run alongside the plan process. Distressed investors watching the 3001 transfer register should note: with confirmation targeted July 13 and a compressed timeline, any claim trading window will be narrow.

### Georgia-Pacific / Bestwall — Texas Two-Step Fails; Second Bankruptcy in View

The WSJ reported this week that Georgia-Pacific, the Koch Industries subsidiary, is now actively exploring a second Chapter 11 filing to resolve its asbestos liability — a direct concession that the Bestwall two-step strategy, launched in 2017, has reached an impasse after nearly a decade of procedural attrition. 
According to court filings and the Wall Street Journal's reporting, Georgia-Pacific plans to use an existing affiliate or a newly created entity to file a fresh Chapter 11 case, seeking enough claimant support to fast-track a resolution.
 
Law firms representing claimants have characterized this as the company having given up on the Bestwall approach after years of procedural battles and failed plan negotiations.


The stakes are substantial. 
In 2017, Georgia-Pacific created Bestwall LLC specifically to hold all its asbestos liabilities, gave it $175 million, and then immediately had Bestwall file for Chapter 11 in North Carolina — a maneuver that involved temporarily moving headquarters to Texas for a single day and is being challenged as a solvent parent company's bad-faith filing, attempting to avoid responsibility for more than 62,000 pending mesothelioma lawsuits.
 
During normal operations, Koch Industries received $2.5 billion in dividends in 2022 from Georgia-Pacific and has received $5 billion in dividends since asbestos liabilities were moved to Bestwall.



None of the financial terms of any new bankruptcy trust are public yet.
 That is the critical creditor-rights variable. 
The financial terms of any bankruptcy trust — payout schedules, disease category values, documentation requirements, and claimants' rights to challenge the plan — are what determine whether victims receive meaningful compensation or a fraction of what a jury might have awarded.
 Any new filing will immediately trigger a race between plaintiffs' firms seeking to lock in favorable trust distribution procedures (TDPs) and Georgia-Pacific seeking the broadest possible channeling injunction under § 524(g). The SCOTUS *Bestwall* cert petition — supported by amicus from Senators Durbin, Whitehouse, and Hawley — remains an overhang: 
Senators Whitehouse and Hawley submitted an amicus brief to the Supreme Court in *Official Committee of Asbestos Claimants of Bestwall LLC v. Bestwall LLC*, supporting the hundreds of thousands of victims of exposure to Georgia-Pacific's asbestos-linked products.
 If SCOTUS takes the case, the good-faith/distress threshold for mass-tort two-step filings becomes federal law — and any new Georgia-Pacific structure filed in that environment faces heightened dismissal risk.

### Prince Global / Chapter 15 — Recognition Signaled; AI Hallucination Residue; Cross-Border Limits Tested

Law360 reported June 15 that a New York bankruptcy judge signaled he would grant recognition of the British Virgin Islands insolvency proceeding of Prince Global Holdings. 
A New York bankruptcy judge said he would grant recognition of the British Virgin Islands insolvency proceeding of Prince Global Holdings, which is part of a Cambodian conglomerate accused of running
 what court documents describe as a large-scale criminal organization. 
On October 14, 2025, Mr. Chen, the alleged chairman and key figure of the Prince Group, was charged by the DOJ with wire fraud conspiracy and money laundering conspiracy. The scams involved cryptocurrency investment fraud and other schemes that leveraged the Prince Group's network of businesses to launder the illegal proceeds of criminal activity.


The case is *In re Prince Global Holdings Ltd.*, No. 26-10769 (MG) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), before Chief Bankruptcy Judge Martin Glenn. Two structural issues carry real precedential weight. First, the § 1519 extraterritoriality problem: 
the proposed order included language staying "all rights and remedies of any person or entity," and the objecting debtors argued this relief was not confined to the United States and was impermissible to the extent extraterritorial — asserting it would appear to prevent parties from participating in the April 16-17 BVI hearing, which does not concern the court when sitting in a Chapter 15 capacity.
 Second — and separately significant — 
Sullivan & Cromwell LLP, representing the Joint Provisional Liquidators, self-reported to Chief Bankruptcy Judge Glenn that its April 9, 2026 Emergency Motion for Provisional Relief contained AI-generated "hallucinations," including fabricated case citations and misquoted authorities. Partner Andrew G. Dietderich signed the disclosure letter, identifying inaccurate citations to *In re Three Arrows Capital, Ltd.* (struck entirely from paragraphs 27, 34, and 55), *In re Soundview Elite Ltd.*, *In re BYJU's Alpha*, *In re Team Systems International*, and other authorities.
 
The firm acknowledged its written AI policies, which require independent verification of all AI output, were not followed.


For practitioners in distressed cross-border situations, Prince Global now stands for two propositions simultaneously: Chapter 15 recognition is available even in proceedings involving alleged criminal enterprises (comity prevails absent a clear public-policy bar), and AI-assisted brief drafting in emergency motions before experienced senior judges carries real sanctions risk when verification protocols are skipped. The broader Chapter 15 circuit split on § 109(a)'s property-in-the-U.S. threshold — 
courts are split over whether § 109(a) is a precondition to recognition under Chapter 15, with the Second Circuit holding it is (*In re Barnet*, 737 F.3d 238 (2d Cir. 2013)) and the Eleventh Circuit holding it is not (*In re Al Zawawi*, 97 F.4th 1244 (11th Cir. 2024))
 — remains unresolved and directly relevant to any foreign-enterprise creditor attempting to use the Chapter 15 mechanism against BVI or Cayman holding structures.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors, claim-holders, and distressed investors should move on three fronts simultaneously. In *Sleep Number* (26-11399 (KYP)), the UCC formation meeting is June 22 — any unsecured creditor with a material claim should contact Davis Polk (Brian Resnick, (212) 510-0500) or the U.S. Trustee Region 2 now to seek Committee appointment; the 26-day diligence window closes July 8, leaving essentially no time to evaluate the stalking horse bid, challenge bidding procedures, or negotiate sale-order carve-outs from the sidelines. In *BlockFills* (26-10371 (TMH)), the commingling admission from first-day proceedings means the customer-property vs. estate-property fight is the controlling recovery variable — creditors holding customer-account claims should retain independent counsel before the confirmation vote closes, scrutinize plan distribution mechanics for treatment of the Celsius secured claim relative to general unsecured class, and watch the 3001 transfer register for any claims trading at distressed levels ahead of the July 13 confirmation target; the September 11 bar date remains the backstop for creditors who have not yet filed. On Georgia-Pacific, the strategic moment is now: any second filing will come with a pre-packaged or pre-arranged structure designed to minimize claimant leverage, and plaintiffs' counsel must act during the negotiation phase — before a new petition is filed — to shape TDP values, disease-tier categories, and the scope of channeling injunction under § 524(g). Track the *Bestwall* SCOTUS docket closely; cert granted would change the risk calculus for any new filing structure materially.
