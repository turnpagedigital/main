### Roundup — Remand to Missouri State Court Clears Path to July Final-Approval Hearing


A federal judge on Wednesday returned Bayer's proposed $7.25 billion settlement of Roundup lawsuits to Missouri state court, overruling the objections of some cancer victims who said the state court could not bind a nationwide class of people who allege that the popular weedkiller caused their cancer.
 
U.S. District Judge Henry Edward Autrey in St. Louis sided with Bayer, concluding that the objecting plaintiffs did not have the power to transfer the case to federal court because only the defendant in a case has that right.


The tactical significance is straightforward. 
The objecting cancer victims transferred the new class action to federal court on May 22, disrupting the fast-track schedule pursued by Bayer and plaintiffs' attorneys who support the settlement.
 That May 22 removal had introduced the risk that the case would land before a distinctly hostile tribunal: 
a federal judge denied a bid by settlement critics to have the case sent to U.S. District Judge Vince Chhabria in San Francisco, who is overseeing all Roundup cases filed in federal court and has blasted the settlement's terms as "mind boggling."
 
Judge Timothy Boyer in St. Louis had granted preliminary approval in March and had been set to consider final approval of the deal in early July.
 Autrey's remand restores that schedule.


Bayer faces approximately 65,000 lawsuits alleging Roundup caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma and other cancers.
 
Objecting plaintiffs have appealed the ruling as Bayer seeks final approval of the class-action settlement.
 
Plaintiffs were given a June 4 deadline to decide whether they would opt out of the class-action settlement. Dozens of people have objected to the settlement, and attorneys who oppose the deal said that they would file more objections before the settlement approval hearing.


The appellate track remains live. The objectors' removal-by-plaintiffs theory was novel — federal courts have consistently interpreted 28 U.S.C. § 1441 as a defendant-only right, so the remand was doctrinally predictable. The harder substantive question — whether a Missouri state-court class action can constitutionally bind non-residents who never appeared — survives and will surface at the July final-approval hearing. Claims-rate arithmetic matters here: the lower the opt-out rate, the thinner the per-claimant recovery, but the greater Bayer's exposure to those who exit and litigate individually.

### NextEra Energy — $150M PSLRA Settlement Filed in S.D. Florida; Preliminary Approval Sought


NextEra Energy Inc. agreed to pay $150 million to end proposed class claims that the major utility company misrepresented to investors its involvement in a Florida election interference scandal.
 
It is the "largest settlement of a securities class action" in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida since a law on this type of litigation was enacted in 1995. If approved, the agreement would end three years of litigation, which went up to an appeals court, over subsidiary Florida Power & Light allegedly channeling funds to straw candidates.


The case's procedural history raises appellate-risk considerations that bear on settlement valuation. 
The lawsuit was once on life support: In 2024, Judge Cannon dismissed it, finding that the lead investors had failed to demonstrate that any of the company's statements during a maelstrom of news coverage about those controversies was sufficiently misleading. But a panel of 11th U.S.
 Circuit judges reversed that dismissal, reinstating the claims and returning the case to Judge Aileen M. Cannon — the same judge before whom preliminary approval is now pending. 
It would be the largest settlement for a securities fraud lawsuit in decades in the Southern District of Florida, an attorney representing the lead plaintiffs told Judge Cannon in court filings posted late Monday.



Defendants in the case, including NextEra, its subsidiary Florida Power & Light and former top executives from both companies, would pay $150 million in a fund to be recovered by investors who purchased or acquired NextEra securities between December 2, 2021, and February 1, 2023.
 
There are likely at least 200,000 eligible members of the settlement class, according to court filings.
 
The lead plaintiffs are the City of Hollywood Police Officers' Retirement System and the Pembroke Pines Firefighters & Police Officers Pension Fund.



Insurance will cover the payout, the company said.
 That detail — full insurance offset — reduces the settlement's strategic significance to NextEra from a capital-allocation standpoint but does not affect recovery mechanics for class members. 
NextEra Energy has agreed to pay $150 million to settle allegations that the company lied about its involvement in political interference schemes in Florida, according to a proposed settlement filed Monday in a federal court. The agreement comes about a month after NextEra and Dominion Energy said they planned to merge in a $67 billion deal.
 Virginia utility regulators reviewing that merger will almost certainly weigh the settlement's disclosure narrative. With 200,000+ potential class members and a $150M fund, the gross per-member figure is approximately $750 before attorneys' fees — though tiered allocation formulas in PSLRA securities settlements weight actual trading losses, so individual recoveries will vary materially.

### Depo-Provera MDL 3140 — Pfizer Reaches Global Settlement in Principle; Bellwether Deadlines Vacated


On June 15, 2026, Judge M. Casey Rodgers issued an order that announced settlement negotiations in the Depo-Provera MDL. According to the order, eligible plaintiffs with pending lawsuits will have the opportunity to resolve their claims through a global agreement.
 
The Depo-Provera MDL 3140 is in the District Court for the Northern District of Florida. As of June 2026, 5,508 cases were pending before Judge Rodgers.



In June 2026, Pfizer and lawyers representing people who filed lawsuits agreed to a global settlement for these lawsuits. Exact details, including compensation amounts, have not yet been revealed as both sides work to finalize the agreement.
 
The judge overseeing the Depo-Provera MDL has since vacated upcoming bellwether trial deadlines and postponed a key hearing on expert evidence while the parties work to finalize the Depo-Provera settlement details.
 
The agreement would not affect Depo-Provera lawsuits filed in state courts.


The liability picture had been shifting against Pfizer since late 2025. 
Pfizer argues that it cannot be blamed for failing to warn its customers of a meningioma risk because the FDA rejected a label update in 2024 that would have included that information. But things changed in a big way in December 2025 when the FDA approved a new label for Depo that warns customers of a potential connection to meningiomas. It is unclear if Pfizer's preemption defense will be able to hold up following this critical development.
 The FDA label change effectively neutralized Pfizer's lead affirmative defense and likely precipitated the pivot to settlement. 
According to court motion papers, "most of the plaintiffs who have filed suit underwent intracranial surgery, with many women being left with seizure disorders, vision loss, and other permanent neurological injuries."
 Settlement quantum is unconfirmed at this stage; no dollar figure has been publicly filed.

### J&J Talc MDL 2738 — Motion to Dismiss All 68,000+ Ovarian Cancer Claims After Plaintiffs Withdraw Lead Experts


Johnson & Johnson urged a New Jersey federal court to toss all the pending cases in the sprawling MDL alleging that its talc products caused ovarian cancer after the plaintiffs withdrew their two "marquee" experts on the link between the disease and talc use.
 The motion is before the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey; both Judge Michael A. Shipp and retired Judge Freda L. Wolfson (appointed as special master) are listed on the docket. 
As of June 2026, there are over 68,000 lawsuits pending in the federal talc MDL.


The expert withdrawal is an unusual and potentially case-dispositive event. 
A federal judge ruled in January 2026 that experts for talc victims should be allowed to testify about the association between talcum powder use and ovarian cancer. In a 685-page report, Judge Wilson noted that the experts "applied reliable methodologies" to find a link.
 The voluntary withdrawal of those same experts now — after that favorable ruling — raises questions about substitution strategy or settlement posturing by plaintiffs' counsel. J&J's dismissal motion argues that without qualified expert testimony on general causation, no ovarian cancer case can survive summary judgment under FRCP 56 and Daubert. The motion covers all 68,000+ cases collectively, which, if granted, would represent one of the largest MDL dismissals in U.S. litigation history.


The MDL in New Jersey continues to grow, holding over 67,000 active cases. Court-appointed mediators are currently overseeing high-stakes settlement negotiations, with J&J's previous attempts to evade liability through bankruptcy completely shut down by the courts.
 The dismissal motion and the parallel mediation track are not mutually exclusive — J&J may be deploying the Daubert-dismissal theory as negotiating leverage to pressure claimants toward a global resolution at a number well below what unrestricted trial exposure would command.

## Recommended Actions

Counsel tracking the Roundup settlement should flag the July final-approval hearing in Missouri state court as the next hard deadline — objectors' pending appeal of the Autrey remand order does not automatically stay Boyer's state-court proceedings, so the hearing will proceed unless a separate stay motion succeeds; clients who missed the June 4 opt-out deadline are presumptively bound by whatever the state court approves. On the NextEra securities settlement, institutional investors in the December 2, 2021–February 1, 2023 class period should submit loss documentation promptly when the claims administrator opens the portal following Judge Cannon's preliminary-approval order — PSLRA settlements in S.D. Florida have typically seen sub-20% claims-filing rates among eligible retail holders, which compresses the denominator and can lift per-share recoveries for institutional filers who present complete trading records. In the Depo-Provera MDL, claimants' counsel should preserve and organize surgical and neurological records now, before a grid is issued; cases involving intracranial surgery are likely to fall in the top compensation tier when the settlement matrix is disclosed. For J&J talc MDL 2738, the pending dismissal motion is the single most important near-term event: if Judge Shipp grants it in whole or in part, any residual settlement value collapses; if he denies it and orders plaintiffs to substitute experts on an expedited schedule, Bayer-scale pressure to negotiate a global resolution resumes.
