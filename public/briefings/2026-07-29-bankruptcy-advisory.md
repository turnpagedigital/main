### Sleep Number — § 363 Sale Approved; Closing Imminent; Unsecured Recovery Still Uncertain


On June 12, 2026, Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under chapter 11 of title 11 of the United States Code in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 That assignment — Judge Paek, not the placeholder the tracked-case ground truth showed at filing — is now confirmed on the Kroll case page.


Pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the stalking horse bidder committed to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.
 
The transaction was undertaken pursuant to § 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, with Sleep Country serving as the stalking horse bidder, and on July 2, 2026 the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the bidding procedures in connection with the sale of all, substantially all, or any portion of any of the Debtors' assets.


Following a competitive auction, the sale price moved materially upward. 
A New York bankruptcy judge on Monday approved the nearly $702 million sale of mattress company Sleep Number to a Canadian bedding retailer, following an agreement to postpone potential future severance payments until the court determines if they are warranted.
 
The purchase was made through SNBR Inc., a wholly owned Sleep Country subsidiary, which had served as the stalking horse bidder.
 
Sleep Number received court approval for the sale of substantially all of its assets and ongoing business operations to SNBR Inc. after making significant improvements to its prior proposal, with the sale — subject to customary closing conditions — expected to close by July 31.


The capital structure context matters for recovery analysis. 
Sleep Number reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities at filing, with the filing indicating that there would be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 
The debtor indicated the number of creditors in the range of 50–99.
 The sale headline of $702 million against $1.3 billion in liabilities means that, absent significant assumed liabilities or administrative and secured claims that absorb the surplus, general unsecured creditors should see some recovery — but the margin between what the secured stack extracts and what flows to the estate's unsecured waterfall is not yet public in final form.

The UCC is already organized and mobilized. 
The unsecured creditors' committee for Sleep Number tapped a team of lawyers from Pachulski Stang Ziehl & Jones LLP as the debtor works its way through chapter 11.
 
Debtor's restructuring counsel of record is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.
 The U.S. Trustee's challenge to the debtor's staff retention plan — flagged in the Law360 docket sidebar as of July 15 — remains a live issue; the outcome will affect administrative expenses and, derivatively, the net pool available for distribution. 
Following the acquisition, Sleep Country says it will have more than 800 store locations and will become the second-largest sleep retailer in the world.
 That strategic rationale suggests Sleep Country bid aggressively to close the gap from $415 million to $702 million — a ~69% increase over the stalking-horse floor — which is a favorable data point for the estate.

For distressed investors: if the July 31 closing occurs as scheduled, the plan and wind-down trust mechanics become the operative focus. Watch for post-closing filings establishing the distribution waterfall, claims reconciliation procedures, and any professional-fee carve-outs that could compress unsecured recoveries. ### BlockFills — Post-Sale, Pre-Confirmation Posture; September 11 Bar Date Governs the Next Phase


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under chapter 11 of the Bankruptcy Code; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.


The case background: 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026 (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)); the Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations; the filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.



The case began after a liquidity crisis tied to counterparty exposures, the Babel Finance bankruptcy, a failed equipment-loan settlement with Nexo, an unpaid Celsius arbitration award, customer litigation and TROs, and a February 2026 crypto-market decline that forced the debtors to suspend deposits and withdrawals.


The § 363 sale component resolved in mid-June. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 That sale hearing occurred June 16, 2026, per the Verita case page. The plan process was put in motion earlier: 
a Delaware bankruptcy judge granted conditional approval to the Chapter 11 plan disclosure statement of cryptocurrency trading platform BlockFills, noting that issues over the opt-out release mechanism in the proposal could be argued at a confirmation hearing.



The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested,
 and it is the most consequential unresolved creditor-rights issue in the case. The answer determines whether customers whose assets were held on the platform have priority claims akin to § 541 exclusions or whether those assets fall into the general unsecured pool — a question that has fractured creditor-recovery outcomes in analogous crypto cases (Celsius, FTX, Voyager).

The UCC structure is confirmed. 
The Committee of Unsecured Creditors was appointed March 27, 2026 [Docket No. 104], with members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 FTI Consulting serves as UCC financial advisor, engaged as of April 1, 2026. 
The retention of FTI was sought by the Committee for the purposes set forth in its application.

Descriptions are not available in the data feed, but the timing — post-sale approval, pre-bar date — is consistent with plan solicitation mechanics, professional fee filings, or claim-objection procedures. 
The governmental bar date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Non-governmental creditors should confirm their bar date against the May 28 scheduling order, as the governmental deadline is typically later than the general claims bar date.

For distressed investors: the opt-out release dispute flagged at disclosure statement approval is the principal plan-level risk. If creditors holding disputed claims opted out of third-party releases and the court narrows the release scope at confirmation, professional fee recovery and plan economics could shift materially. ### Alkegen — Prepackaged Texas Filing; $3.3B Debt Stack, $315M DIP, 60-Day Target

The week's most consequential new filing landed Sunday. 
On July 26, 2026, Alkegen, a leading specialty materials manufacturer, voluntarily commenced prepackaged chapter 11 cases in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Texas to implement the Restructuring Support Agreement announced on July 20, 2026; under the RSA, Alkegen will eliminate approximately $3.1 billion of total debt.
 
Alkegen and dozens of affiliates filed the prepackaged case, where the specialty materials maker intends to cut the bulk of its $3.3 billion of funded debt obligations.


The RSA mechanics are unusually clean. 
Alkegen entered chapter 11 with overwhelming support from key financial partners, including 99% of first lien and 80% of second lien holders, and secured $315 million in new money debtor-in-possession financing to support ordinary course operations.
 
Upon emergence, Alkegen expects approximately $200 million in available liquidity.
 
The arrangement opens the way for the Clearlake Capital-backed company to file the prepackaged bankruptcy in Texas in which its lenders would take control of the firm.



Alkegen's plan leaves trade creditors unimpaired and paid in the ordinary course.
 
Having secured the support of the overwhelming majority of its financial partners, Alkegen is well-positioned to progress through a quick and efficient process of approximately 60 days.
 That 60-day target, if met, would bring emergence to approximately late September 2026. The case is styled *Lydall, Inc.* in the Northern District of Texas per the Law360 docket sidebar — which signals that Alkegen's U.S. operating entities may include the former Lydall business, acquired by Clearlake. A judge assignment has not appeared in publicly available sources as of this writing.

For distressed investors: trade and vendor claims are unimpaired, so the recovery opportunity here is in the loan-to-own dynamic at the senior lien levels, not in claim trading. The 99% first-lien adherence rate means the lender group is effectively acting in concert; there is no material inter-creditor dispute visible at this stage. The case is worth monitoring for any second-lien holdout litigation and for the treatment of any asbestos or mass-tort legacy liabilities that may attach to the Lydall manufacturing lineage — a category that has derailed faster prepackaged timelines in comparable industrial restructurings.

### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Sale Closed or Closing Imminently; Residual Estate Dynamics Now the Operative Question


Sleep Number filed for bankruptcy in the Southern District of New York on June 12, 2026, in a case docketed at No. 26-11399.
 
The cases are jointly administered before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 The judge assignment resolves the "NOT ASSIGNED" designation carried in prior advisories — Judge Paek has been presiding throughout.


Sleep Number reported $642.3 million in assets against $1.3 billion in liabilities at filing.
 
The case was structured from the outset as an expedited §363 sale of substantially all assets, built around a stalking-horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The Chapter 11 process was funded by a debtor-in-possession financing package of up to $260 million from the company's current lenders, including $65 million in new funds.


The auction proved genuinely competitive. 
On July 2, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the bidding procedures.
 
Following the court-supervised auction process, on July 18, 2026, the company entered into an Amended and Restated Asset Purchase Agreement with the purchaser, which increased the base purchase price payable in cash from $415,000,000 to $529,500,000, subject to certain purchase price adjustments.
 
Court documents place Sleep Country's winning proposal at approximately $701 million in total value, including $529.5 million in cash and the assumption of selected obligations.
 
Reuters reported that Sleep Country agreed to increase its offer by approximately $286.8 million through the auction process. Brooklyn Bedding was selected as the backup bidder.



The U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York approved the sale on Monday, July 21, 2026, through the §363 process.
 
The company set a July 31 target to close the transaction.
 That deadline is today. Parties should confirm via the Kroll claims administration portal whether closing has in fact occurred; a slip beyond July 31 would extend the DIP financing period and begin to erode the reserve available for unsecured distributions.

What the $286-million auction uplift means for creditors: the gap between the $415 million stalking-horse floor and the $701 million total consideration represents real value that flows to the estate before unsecured creditors are addressed. 
The filing indicated that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 
Before filing, Sleep Number tried to free up additional cash flow by, among other things, selling its right to refunds under 2025 tariffs that were invalidated by the U.S. Supreme Court.
 The tariff-refund monetization is a data point worth tracking: the extent to which prepetition tariff-refund rights were transferred to a third party — and at what discount — affects the pool of assets remaining in the estate. 
Sleep Number's court filings did not say how much it paid in 2025 tariffs or how much money it gained by selling its refund rights.
 That gap in disclosure warrants a targeted review of the asset purchase agreement schedules and any retained-claims carve-outs.

Counsel of record: 
Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP serves as lead debtor counsel.
 Claims administration is through Kroll at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber. 
The petition reports the number of creditors in the range of 50–99
 — a narrow class relative to the debt stack, which should concentrate the negotiation dynamics among institutional holders and facilitate an orderly unsecured distribution process once the sale closes and administrative claims are resolved.

The residual-estate phase — plan of liquidation, bar date enforcement, DIP repayment, and distribution waterfall — is the operative focus from today forward. Investors holding unsecured claims should be tracking: (i) whether closing occurs today; (ii) the quantum of assumed vs. excluded liabilities under the APA; and (iii) the timeline for a plan of liquidation or trust mechanism to be filed with Judge Paek.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — §363 Sale Closed; Plan Confirmation and Regulatory Clearance Remain Open


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on March 15, 2026, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH).
 The case sits before Judge Thomas M. Horan in Courtroom No. 5, 824 Market Street, 5th Floor, Wilmington, Delaware 19801. 
The Debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


The case ran a dual-track from the outset. 
The Plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a §363 competitive sale process.
 The 363 track resolved first. 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock was named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and the U.S. bankruptcy court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.


The $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate on the order of 2 cents on the dollar from the sale proceeds alone — a figure that should be the baseline assumption for any holder underwriting unsecured exposure at this point. The plan track, however, carries additional recovery variables. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement and scheduling the confirmation hearing, with a Voting Deadline of July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET and a Confirmation Objection Deadline of the same date [Docket No. 388].
 
The Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The docket ground truth shows three new filings in the 72 hours through July 27 (docket entries 539 and 541), but their descriptions are not populated in the live data. The confirmation hearing set for July 13 likely proceeded — consistent with the July 10 filing at Docket No. 523 (a service list filing from Verita) — but a confirmed order has not been independently verified in this run. The Veritaglobal key dates page lists a September 11, 2026 date that may represent a plan effective date or administrative claims bar date. That forward date suggests the case has not yet fully closed.

The regulatory dimension is the most exposed variable. 
The transaction folds in a CIMA-registered Cayman entity acquired outright and seeks to transfer a proposed FCA-authorised U.K. entity to Keyrock. The U.K. transfer still requires FCA approval, which would expand Keyrock's ability to face certain institutional clients in that jurisdiction.
 
Final regulatory clearances for the transaction were still pending as of mid-July 2026.
 A failure to obtain FCA approval could impair asset value transferred under the APA, potentially triggering a purchase-price adjustment or clawback, with downstream consequences for the liquidating trust's distributable assets.

The Committee of Unsecured Creditors — comprising SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc. — is advised by FTI Consulting (retained April 1, 2026) and Morris James LLP as Delaware co-counsel (per Docket No. 262). 
The Debtors are represented by McDermott Will & Emery LLP (Darren Azman, Gregg Steinman, Joseph Evans) and Katten Muchin Rosenman LLP (Peter Siddiqui) as legal counsel, with BRG (Mark Renzi) as financial advisor and CRO.
 
The Ad Hoc Group is represented by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP (Sean O'Neal, Jane VanLare) and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor, LLP (Sean Greecher).


The unresolved customer-property question remains the case's sharpest legal issue for distressed investors. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 The onboarding agreement language — which specifically stated that customer deposits would be "completely segregated from the general balance sheet" — was cited at the first-day hearing by at least one creditor as a basis for a constructive trust or segregation argument. Resolution of that issue will govern how the liquidating trust distributes residual value beyond the $3.25 million sale proceeds: if customer assets are estate property, the pool is shared ratably among all unsecured creditors; if they are not, affected customers may recover ahead of the class.
