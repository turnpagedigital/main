# 📜 Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | Saturday, July 04, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

### Lede: BlockFills Hits the Final Straight — Voting Closed Yesterday, Confirmation Nine Days Out


The Confirmation Hearing in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.* (BlockFills), Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., is set for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan. The voting deadline and the confirmation objection deadline both expired yesterday — July 1, 2026, at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Today, the critical question is whether any objections landed before that bar, and what the vote tabulation looks like when the parties appear before Judge Horan in Courtroom No. 5 at 824 Market Street, Wilmington, in nine days' time.

The case has moved fast and through several structural iterations since petition day. 
On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities — collectively operating as "BlockFills" — filed Chapter 11 petitions before Judge Horan, following years of cascading financial losses, two prepetition customer lawsuits with temporary restraining orders, and the collapse of multiple recapitalization attempts.
 
The debtors reported approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


The plan architecture is a dual-track toggle. 
The Debtors filed the Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket 128), the Disclosure Statement (Docket 129), and the Solicitation Procedures Motion (Docket 130), establishing the complete framework for plan solicitation and confirmation.
 That plan was subsequently amended — 
the operative document is the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and Its Debtor Affiliates, Docket No. 391.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the order approving the disclosure statement and scheduling the confirmation hearing (Docket No. 388).


The §363 sale component of that toggle has already been resolved. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors, as set out in the official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
The deal provides that Keyrock will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The sale approval hearing occurred on June 16, 2026. 
The acquisition is subject to court approval and the regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.


What makes the July 13 confirmation hearing the decisive event — not the sale approval — is the asset bifurcation. The Keyrock deal addresses the operating-business assets (technology, IP, client book). What remains for the plan to resolve is the distribution question: how the $145 million in unsecured claims get treated through the liquidating trust, and whether the customer-property characterization — litigated since day one — survives into the confirmed plan. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission remains the single most consequential legal position in the case. If confirmed without a successful objection, it locks in the estate-property framing and defeats any customer-priority argument under a trust or bailment theory, placing institutional counterparties — who had assets on the platform — at pari passu with trade creditors in the general unsecured pool.

The creditor constituency is notable. 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, formed effective March 30, 2026, retained Morris James LLP as counsel, and the creditor matrix includes Celsius Network Ltd., Basis Group Holdings, BlockFills Digital Markets Ltd. (Cayman), CME Group Inc., Copper Technologies (UK) Ltd., Fireblocks Inc., and Tradestation Group Inc., among others.
 The presence of Celsius as a creditor here — itself a debtor in a now substantially concluded SDNY case — creates a layered recovery dynamic worth watching.

For distressed-claims buyers: 
the debtors report $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The Keyrock purchase price is $3.25 million.
 The residual estate, net of Keyrock consideration and administrative claims, is the fund available for distribution. The gap between the $3.25 million sale price and $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate in the low single digits on face value at best — absent material litigation recoveries from the liquidating trust's pursuit of insider claims and preference actions. Claim transfers (Rule 3001(e) notices) are the signal to watch heading into and out of the July 13 hearing; any spike in transfer filings would confirm that sophisticated buyers are moving at current implied recoveries.

### Sleep Number: Auction Week Is Here — Final Day Motions July 9, Auction July 13


To facilitate a combination with Sleep Country Canada, Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
The petition showed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion, with liabilities in excess of $1 billion.
 
Substantially all of the company's debt — approximately $672.5 million in aggregate principal — is in default and accelerated as of the June 12, 2026 petition date, triggered by the commencement of the Chapter 11 cases under the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement dated February 18, 2018, as last amended by the Forbearance Agreement and Thirteenth Amendment dated April 27, 2026.


Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (lead: Brian M. Resnick), and the case is before Judge Paek — the "KYP" designation confirmed by PACER. 
A final hearing on certain of the Debtors' First Day Motions is set for July 9, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET
 — five days from today. 
The auction is scheduled for July 13, 2026, and the company is open to higher and better offers.


The stalking horse bid anchors the floor. 
Pursuant to the Stalking Horse Asset Purchase Agreement dated June 12, 2026, SNBR Inc. — an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc. — committed, subject to court approval, to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties (19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals).
 Bid deadline was yesterday, July 3 — meaning the court and parties will now know by auction day whether any qualified competing bids materialized.


Sleep Number reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities. The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 That representation deserves scrutiny: the $415 million stalking horse against $672.5 million in accelerated senior secured debt leaves a meaningful coverage gap before unsecureds see anything. Whether DIP financing, break-up fee carve-outs, or asset-specific realizations bridge that gap will be the central confirmation-phase valuation fight — assuming the auction produces a going-concern buyer rather than a liquidation cascade across 572 stores.


The company's distress was compounded by pandemic-era overexpansion of its store footprint, manufacturing capabilities, and debt levels, and by the April 2025 wave of IEEPA-based tariffs on certain direct-import products — followed by regulatory uncertainty after subsequent court rulings on IEEPA tariff authority.
 The company also monetized an IEEPA tariff-refund claim through a prepetition sale — an unusual asset class that will surface in plan distributions if proceeds were not already swept to secured lenders. 
The firm had reported that operating costs fell by $136 million in 2025, but its net loss still widened as net sales dropped 16%.


For landlords and lease counterparties: 
Sleep Number operates 572 stores and will continue operations while seeking a quicker-than-usual court-supervised sale process.
 Lease assumption/rejection elections are the immediate exposure point for shopping center owners; the July 13 auction and any subsequent sale order will trigger the 60-day assumption/rejection clock under 11 U.S.C. § 365(d)(4). Creditors holding unexpired leases should monitor Docket No. 26-11399 on PACER closely through auction week.

### Power Block Coin: Late-Stage Wind-Down, Fee Applications Signal Approaching Close


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, LLC filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah (Case No. 24-bk-23041), with Greenberg Traurig, LLP filing the plan on January 30, 2025, outlining a strategy to maximize value through a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The Honorable Cathleen D. Parker is now the presiding judge (the case having been reassigned from Judge Joel T. Marker), and a June 9, 2026 hearing was set on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for allowance of compensation.


The Amplēo final fee application is the tell: final professional fee applications appear when a case is approaching plan effectiveness or administrative closure. 
At the hearing, the court considered the Amplēo Fee Application, requesting final compensation for services rendered in the amount of $10,436.50 for the period April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026, plus previously allowed interim fees.



The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 
The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC — an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah — whose unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property drew committee scrutiny.


The Celsius claim overhang is the structural complexity here. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against Power Block Coin in connection with the Celsius Network LLC et al. bankruptcy (Case No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The interplay between two bankruptcy estates — one a liquidating trust in Utah, one a substantially concluded SDNY case — means recoveries on the Power Block Coin side depend in part on how Celsius's own preference claims are ultimately quantified and pursued. Claims buyers evaluating Power Block Coin exposure need to model both sides of that ledger.

### Broader Landscape: Compressed-Timeline §363 Sales and the Customer-Property Problem

Two of the three key focus cases this week sit at the same structural fault line: whether customer assets held on a platform constitute property of the estate, or whether customers have a superior property interest that survives the estate's insolvency. In BlockFills, the debtor has staked out an explicit pro-estate position (commingling = estate property). In Sleep Number, the question doesn't arise — the assets are conventional retail inventory and real estate. But the structural pattern of freefall-into-compressed-363-sale is common to both: both cases ran a 14-to-26-week prepetition marketing process, filed with a stalking horse in hand, and targeted sub-30-day postpetition sale timelines.

The precedential risk for distressed investors is the growing number of cases where the compressed timeline limits the Official Committee's ability to investigate estate claims before the §363 sale order is entered. In BlockFills, the Committee (formed March 30) had roughly 47 days before the Disclosure Statement hearing (May 12) to investigate commingling, insider transfers, and the $17.4 million-analogue transactions flagged in the case. That is a structurally short window. Sleep Number's Committee — if one was formed following the June 22 creditor questionnaire deadline — faces an even tighter window given the July 13 auction. This is the pattern that the Third Circuit's *Fisker* line and the Second Circuit's *Chrysler* precedent enable, and that reform-minded creditors' bars have flagged as systematically disadvantaging unsecured constituencies.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed-claims buyers in the BlockFills estate should confirm by early next week whether any objections to plan confirmation were filed before the July 1 deadline — the docket (CourtListener ID 72494188, Bankr. D. Del. 26-10371 (TMH)) will reflect any opposition papers; the presence of committee or customer-group objections targeting the estate-property/commingling issue would signal a contested July 13 confirmation hearing and potentially depress trading prices on general unsecured claims, creating a secondary-market entry point for buyers who can hold through a contested confirmation. In Sleep Number (SDNY 26-11399 (KYP), Davis Polk/Resnick for debtor), bid deadline passed yesterday — the auction on July 13 will reveal whether the $415 million stalking horse holds or faces competition; landlords with leases at any of the 572 Sleep Number stores should appear or file reservation-of-rights papers before the sale order is entered, and should calendar the July 9 final second-day hearing before Judge Paek as the last pre-auction opportunity to raise procedural objections to bidding procedures. In Power Block Coin (Bankr. D. Utah 24-23041, Judge Parker), the filing of Amplēo's final fee application signals the case is moving toward administrative close; unsecured creditors with claims in the Celsius preference overhang should coordinate with Celsius recovery counsel to ensure their interests are accounted for in any interpleader or joint-distribution mechanics across the two estates before a final decree is entered.
