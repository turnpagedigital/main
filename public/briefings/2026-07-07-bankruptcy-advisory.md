**NFE Global Holdings / New Fortress Energy — English Court Sanction Issued; Chapter 15 Recognition Granted June 26; Implementation Pending Q3 2026.** The cross-border restructuring of NFE Global Holdings Limited and NFE Brazil Newco Limited — the largest restructuring under Part 26A of the UK Companies Act 2006 to date — has cleared its two primary judicial hurdles and is now in implementation. This is the most significant cross-border restructuring development of the summer and a landmark for English plan jurisprudence. 
In *Re NFE Global Holdings Limited* [2026] EWHC 1620 (Ch) (Cawson J, sanction judgment) and [2026] EWHC 1223 (Ch) (Hildyard J, convening judgment), the English court sanctioned two restructuring plans under Part 26A of the Companies Act 2006 that extinguished approximately **US$9.6 billion of debt** across the New Fortress Energy group.
 
All seven creditor classes voted in favor — six unanimously and one by **99.84%** — making cross-class cram-down unnecessary.
 
Total plan creditor consideration comprised new equity and new takeback debt instruments totalling **US$971 million**.
 
Under the restructuring, creditors will exchange more than $9 billion of debt for a combination of equity and new debt, with the group separated into a private "BrazilCo" and a public "New NFE."
 
Chapter 15 recognition was subsequently granted in the United States on **June 26, 2026**, though Judge Glenn's detailed reasoning is awaited.
 
The transactions are expected to be fully implemented by Q3 2026, subject to customary conditions and regulatory approvals.


The doctrinal contribution is substantial. 
The court confirmed that "good forum shopping" is a legitimate basis for using an English restructuring plan where it produces a superior outcome for creditors, and offered notable guidance on class constitution, third-party releases, conditions precedent, and international effectiveness.
 Cawson J's approach to the governing-law complexity is worth examining. 
Cawson J drew comfort from the fact that restructuring finance documentation was negotiated and drafted by "numerous law firms of the highest calibre" with substantial New York presence — including creditor-side firms — reducing the need for comprehensive expert evidence on every governing law.
 
Restructuring benefits were allocated in two stages: first between five collateral pools by reference to the increase in value each pool derived versus the relevant alternative; then pari passu within each pool.
 This waterfall methodology — and the court's treatment of collateral-pool hierarchy — is directly instructive for any cross-border restructuring in which New York-law credit agreements are being compromised through a non-U.S. insolvency proceeding.
