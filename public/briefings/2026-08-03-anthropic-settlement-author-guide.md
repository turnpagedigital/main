# The Anthropic Settlement: A Plain-English Guide for Authors

*Updated August 3, 2026. This guide is maintained as the settlement moves through distribution — dates below reflect court filings and administrator materials as of the update date.*

If you wrote a book that Anthropic downloaded from a pirate library, a court has now approved a $1.5 billion settlement that should pay you at least a few thousand dollars per book. The claims deadline has passed, the money is being assembled, and the remaining questions are mostly about timing, amounts, and what happens next. This guide answers those questions in plain language.

## What happened, briefly

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, downloaded millions of pirated books from the "shadow libraries" LibGen and PiLiMi and used them in building its systems. Three authors sued in 2024 on behalf of everyone whose books were taken. Rather than take the case to trial, Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion plus interest — the largest copyright settlement on record. On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín granted final approval, overruled all 53 objections, and entered judgment. The settlement is now in its distribution phase.

## Are you in the class?

You are a class member if you hold copyright interests in a book on the settlement's Works List — 482,460 titles that met all of these conditions:

- The book was downloaded by Anthropic from LibGen or PiLiMi
- It has an ISBN or ASIN
- It was registered with the U.S. Copyright Office within three months of publication, or within five years of publication and before Anthropic's download

The official Works List is searchable at the administrator's site, anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com. If your book isn't on the list, you were never part of this settlement — which also means you gave up nothing, and any claims you might have remain yours.

## How much will you receive per book?

The working estimate is **at least $3,000 per title**, and the court noted this is roughly four times the minimum statutory damages for copyright infringement. The final number depends on a few things:

- **Deductions.** Attorneys' fees (the court awarded about $101.5 million — well under the 25% or more that is typical in class actions) and administration costs come out of the fund before distribution. Interest earned by the fund is added to it.
- **Who shares the check.** For most traditionally published books, the default is a **50/50 split between author and publisher**, per the Plan of Allocation. Self-published authors who own all rights receive the full amount. Textbook and educational works, where contracts vary widely, can be adjudicated individually — with a special master resolving disputes.
- **How many works you have.** Payment is per work. Ten eligible books means roughly ten shares.

The Authors Guild's guide to the settlement covers the split mechanics in more detail; it is the best author-side resource on claim logistics and is linked at the bottom of this page.

## When will the money arrive?

The honest answer: in stages, starting soon, finishing in 2027 — and no first-check date has been formally announced yet. What the schedule actually says:

Because the fund itself fills in installments, claimant payments are also expected in waves rather than a single check. If you filed a claim, watch for administrator mail and keep your contact details current with them.

## Could an appeal delay everything?

It could, but so far none has been filed, and the window closes on or about August 19, 2026. Only the 53 overruled objectors would realistically have standing to appeal; the 350 authors who opted out left the settlement entirely and are pursuing their own cases. If an appeal is filed, distribution typically pauses while the Ninth Circuit reviews — historically a 12–18 month process that ends with the settlement intact far more often than not. If the window closes quietly, the schedule above runs clean.

## What if you didn't file a claim in time?

The deadline was March 30, 2026, and it hasn't been extended. Three things are still true:

1. **You may still receive something.** The Plan of Allocation directs the administrator to send checks to non-filing class members on August 21, 2026. Make sure the administrator can find you.
2. **You are still bound by the settlement** unless you opted out — the release applies to the whole class.
3. **The release is narrower than most people think** — see the next section.

## What rights did authors keep?

The settlement releases claims about Anthropic's *past acquisition and copying* of the pirated books, through August 25, 2025, and nothing else. It does not release:

- Claims about **AI outputs** — if a model reproduces your work, that theory remains open
- Claims about **anything Anthropic did after August 25, 2025**
- Claims against **any other company** — the OpenAI consolidated litigation, the Meta cases, and the music-industry suits against Anthropic itself all continue

## If you'd rather not wait for the schedule

Some claimants — estates settling accounts, authors with many titles, anyone with near-term needs — prefer a fixed amount now over installments running into 2027. Two mechanisms exist: selling the claim outright, or taking a non-recourse advance against it.
