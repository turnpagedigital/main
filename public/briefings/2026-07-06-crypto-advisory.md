## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. — Confirmation Hearing Seven Days Out; Sale Approved; Customer-Property Question Still Controls Recovery Math**

The operative development heading into this week is the confirmation hearing scheduled for **July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET** before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the Bankr. D. Del. 
(Case No. 26-10371).
 The voting and confirmation-objection deadlines expired July 1 — one working day before today. 
The Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 That means the objection record is now closed and the debtors will present the plan to Judge Horan in seven days. Any last-minute objector who missed the July 1 deadline has largely forfeited its right to contest confirmation on the merits.

The sale component of the dual-track structure has already closed at the court level. 
On June 16, 2026, a Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 That buyer is Keyrock SA. 
According to the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property — and as of May 26, 2026, Keyrock had been declared the Successful Bidder in the court-filed document.
 The sale order cleared the June 16 sale hearing; what remains is confirmation of the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Dkt. 391), which will determine how the net proceeds and any remaining estate assets are distributed to the roughly $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations identified at filing. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


**The Customer-Property Question: §541 and the Commingling Record**

The central legal issue in this case — whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate or are held in trust or bailment outside §541 — has never been formally resolved by adversary proceeding. That is a structural creditor-rights problem, not a technical one. 
On February 11, 2026, the debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet." The debtors have also noted a procedural deficiency: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 This matters enormously for recovery. 
If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, to be distributed pro rata under the Plan, and to serve as the basis for the cash collateral arrangement.
 The debtors have explicitly relied on that characterization to support cash collateral usage throughout the case. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.


Customer creditors who have not yet challenged estate-property treatment through an adversary proceeding have, on the debtors' argument, effectively ceded that fight. With the voting deadline passed and objection record closed, the window is narrow. If the plan confirms on July 13, the commingling admission becomes the operative factual foundation for the distribution waterfall under 11 U.S.C. § 1129 — and unsecured customer claimants will recover pro rata against a liquidating estate, not on a segregated-asset basis.

The prepetition litigation context reinforces the stakes. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two customer lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., filed February 27, 2026).
 
On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte temporary restraining order freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 A second action followed: 
1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al. (N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:26-cv-02451, filed March 5, 2026), which asserted similar claims against multiple debtor entities and named individuals, including Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace.
 Both TROs were subsumed into the Chapter 11 automatic stay upon filing.

The adversary proceeding record adds another layer. 
In Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH), the debtors are plaintiff against Artha Investment Partners LLC as defendant, with the case docketed under the jointly-administered Case No. 26-10371 (TMH).
 The debtors have deployed their adversary-proceeding capacity offensively — pursuing asset-recovery claims against counterparties while simultaneously arguing that inbound customer claims require adversary-proceeding procedural formality that objectors have not followed.

**Liquidity Origins and the Cascade That Forced the Filing**


The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 
On February 19, 2026, CoinDesk reported that BlockFills had incurred approximately $75 million in losses and was seeking a buyer. On February 25, CoinDesk confirmed the former CEO's departure, reporting that Hammer had allegedly urged some clients to withdraw assets before the platform froze deposits and withdrawals.
 That allegation, if developed in litigation, is the paradigmatic fraudulent-transfer and breach-of-fiduciary-duty claim that drives Chapter 11 trustee motions and adversary proceedings post-confirmation.


The petition schedules report $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities, with the filing indicating that funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 At a $3.25 million gross sale price against a $145 million unsecured pool, the arithmetic is stark: recoveries depend almost entirely on what, if anything, the plan's litigation trust or equivalent mechanism can monetize through the adversary-proceeding estate.

**FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Imminent; Disputed-Claims Reserve Cut by $600 Million**

The FTX matter (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068, Judge John T. Dorsey) advances toward its fifth creditor distribution with both a near-term payment event and a meaningful reserve reduction on the record. 
The anticipated record date for the next distribution was set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence July 31, 2026. Payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders are also scheduled for July 31, 2026.
 
As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the FTX Recovery Trust filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reserve reduction directly expands the pool available for distribution to allowed claims.


The fourth distribution was on March 31, 2026, bringing cumulative distributions to more than $10 billion.
 
The main classes — 5B and 6A/6B — are at 100%, and Class 5A (the Dotcom group) has been paid approximately 96% of principal so far, with a projected total of around 118–120% once the same 9% annual interest is applied.
 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 That procedural gate has closed. Transferees of FTX claims who did not complete docket registration by June 16 are not eligible for the July 31 distribution.

The claim-valuation-date tension — petition-date dollar value vs. in-kind recovery — remains the defining structural legacy of the wave. FTX distributes at November 2022 fiat values. 
Every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ recovery is worth less in today's terms.
 The contrast with Genesis is instructive: 
the Genesis plan went effective in August 2024, when BTC creditors received 51% of their in-kind, coin-for-coin value (166% of their petition-date dollar value), ETH creditors received 66% of their in-kind value (153% of petition-date dollar value), and U.S. dollar creditors received 100% of their in-kind and petition-date value.


The next omnibus hearing in the FTX docket is scheduled for **July 23, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. ET**. 
The Kroll case site confirms the July 23, 2026 omnibus hearing.

**Cross-Case Pattern: The §541 Property-of-Estate / Commingling Divide**

Three data points now sharpen this beat's core analytical question. At BlockFills, the debtors admittedly operated on commingled customer balances and affirmatively assert estate-property status, while disqualifying customer objectors who failed to pursue Rule 7001(b) adversary proceedings. At Genesis, the court approved an unprecedented in-kind distribution framework that resisted petition-date dollarization — a creditor-favorable outcome that turned on the specific structure of lending agreements, not on segregation. At FTX, petition-date dollarization controls. The divergence is not random: it maps to whether customer deposits were documented as loans (Genesis — recoverable with upside), title-transfer arrangements (FTX — dollarized and capped), or simply commingled without formal documentation of the operative legal relationship (BlockFills — estate property by default). Creditors in emerging crypto insolvencies who cannot produce contemporaneous documentation establishing a bailment, trust, or loan structure will inherit BlockFills' outcome, not Genesis's.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and counsel monitoring the BlockFills / Reliz confirmation on July 13 should confirm by today whether any timely plan objection was filed before the July 1 deadline; if none was filed on behalf of a given claimant, the confirmation will likely be uncontested on that claimant's account and the focus shifts entirely to post-confirmation claim-allowance and plan-distribution mechanics under Dkt. 391. FTX claimants who transferred their claims prior to June 16, 2026 should verify with their distribution provider (BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer) that the transfer is fully reflected on the claims register, as claims not appearing on the register as of the record date are ineligible for the July 31 fifth distribution; the disputed-claims reserve reduction to $1.8 billion signals continued trust-level confidence in the reconciliation process but does not automatically expand any individual claimant's allowed amount. Genesis creditors should monitor the July 7 LOC Town Hall and the July 15 omnibus for developments in Adv. Pro. Nos. 26-01053 and 26-03230 against DCG, as those proceedings represent the primary remaining distribution lever for the $70 million creditor litigation fund. Across all matters, practitioners advising clients on newly failing crypto platforms should document the legal character of customer deposits at the time of contracting — the BlockFills commingling admission and the resulting §541 default illustrate that silence on segregation translates to estate property as a matter of course.
