# Poolin Briefing — August 7, 2026

Today's objection deadline has now closed — or closes by end of business — and the docket has delivered its most significant procedural development since the support cluster filed August 6: Dkt. 98, filed this morning, is logged as an Objection, the first contested filing against the bidding procedures and sale motion in *In re Poolin Technology PTE*, No. 26-18325 (Bankr. D.N.J., Judge O'Hagan). The identity of the objecting party and the precise grounds are not confirmed from docket text available to this briefing, but the filing's timing — the deadline itself — is consistent with a creditor, committee, or regulatory body preserving its objection rights for the August 14 hearing. Whether this is a targeted objection to a specific bid term, a broader challenge to the § 363 framework, or a cure-related filing cannot be stated with certainty at this stage; that content governs how the August 14 hearing is shaped and whether Judge O'Hagan proceeds, adjourns, or takes the matter on a contested basis.

Also filed this morning is Dkt. 97, logged as a Meeting of Creditors Chapter 11. 
Objections to the bidding procedures and sale motion were due August 7, and the court has scheduled a hearing for August 14 at 11 a.m. ET.
 The § 341 meeting entry is procedurally routine but confirms the case is tracking standard Chapter 11 administration in parallel with the sale process — it does not bear directly on recovery posture unless an examiner or U.S. Trustee observation surfaces anything adverse at that session. The undocketed Notice of Docketing Error, also filed August 7, has no confirmed substantive effect; such notices typically correct a filing deficiency rather than alter the underlying record. Against this backdrop, the six support filings from August 6 (Dkt. 89–94) now sit alongside a live objection: the procedural picture heading into August 14 is contested, not clean.

The objection's existence sharpens the stakes of the two concurrent pressures already in play. 
Thor may terminate through August 9 if, in its sole and absolute discretion, it finds diligence unsatisfactory, for any reason or no reason, leaving a no-cause exit three days before the requested bid-procedures hearing.
 A stalking-horse termination arriving simultaneously with a contested objection hearing would present Judge O'Hagan with a materially weakened sale framework: no floor bid, no approved procedures, and a contested § 363 record. 
Neither purchase agreement fixes how consideration divides between the two Lonestar estates — each leaves allocation to the buyer to be determined within 180 days after closing — and Poolin Technology is not a seller under either agreement.
 For the 11,700 wallet holders carrying $163,723,500 in unsecured IOU claims, that structural gap remains the central unresolved variable; 
the amount available to wallet creditors depends on estate-specific claims, any valid liens against net proceeds, transfer taxes, professional fees, other administrative costs, and the treatment of Poolin's intercompany claim.
 The next two milestones arrive in sequence: Thor's no-cause diligence exit right expires August 9, and the bid-procedures and sale hearing before Judge O'Hagan follows on August 14 at 11 a.m. ET — now against a docket that carries at least one live objection.
