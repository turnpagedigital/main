# Anthropic Settlement Advance: How to Get Cash Before the Checks Arrive

**The short answer:** if you have a filed Bartz v. Anthropic claim, you don't have to wait for the fund's installment schedule to run its course into 2027. There are two ways to convert the claim into money now — **sell it outright**, or take an **advance against it** — and the differences between them matter more than most funding companies will tell you. ## Why "waiting it out" has a real cost

Final approval landed July 20, 2026, but the fund fills in stages: Anthropic's first $450 million installment is due September 25, 2026, and the final $450 million not until **September 25, 2027**. Checks go out in waves ([the full schedule](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-payment-timeline)), and an [appeal filed before ~August 19](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-appeal-risk) could stall everything for a year. If you have works pending, your claim is an asset with a fuzzy date on it. An advance or sale puts a firm date on it: this week.

## Option 1 — Sell the claim (assignment)

You transfer the payment right for a fixed cash price and you're done — no repayment, ever, because there's nothing to repay. The buyer collects from the fund whenever and whatever it pays. This is the cleanest structure for estates, multi-work authors, and anyone who wants the claim fully off their books. [Full walkthrough of how a sale works →](/briefings/2026-08-03-sell-your-anthropic-settlement-claim)

## Option 2 — Advance against the claim

You get cash now and remain the claimant; when the settlement pays you, the funder's share comes out of that payment. The critical term to demand: **non-recourse**. A true non-recourse advance means if the settlement pays less than expected — or somehow nothing — you owe nothing beyond what the claim itself produces. If an agreement lets the funder come after your other assets when the fund disappoints, that's a loan wearing a costume, and it deserves loan-level scrutiny (and interest-rate math).

## Questions to ask anyone offering you money for your claim

1. **Is this recourse or non-recourse?** (Non-recourse or walk away.)
2. **What's the all-in cost?** A flat discount is easy to compare; "monthly fees" that compound during an appeal delay are not.
3. **Who bears delay risk?** If checks slip to 2027, does your cost grow?
4. **Do they understand *this* settlement?** Generic lawsuit-funding shops price Bartz like a car-accident case. The Works List, the allocation splits, the installment dates — [the actual math](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-how-much-per-book) — should be reflected in your quote.
