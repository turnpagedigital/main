# Anthropic Settlement Advances: What They Are and How They Work

An advance converts part of a pending settlement payment into cash now. For Bartz v. Anthropic claimants — whose fund pays out in waves into 2027 — advances and outright sales are the two mechanisms for not waiting. Turnpage Digital Markets offers both structures, worth knowing as you weigh what follows.

## The two structures, side by side

**Selling the claim** transfers it entirely. You receive a fixed price, the buyer collects whatever the fund eventually pays, and nothing about the settlement concerns you again. [How sales work, in detail →](/briefings/2026-08-03-sell-your-anthropic-settlement-claim)

**An advance** leaves the claim in your name. You receive cash now; when the administrator pays your claim, the funder's share comes out of that payment, and the remainder — if the agreement leaves one — is yours.

Neither is better in the abstract. A sale is cleaner and final; an advance can preserve some upside. Which one makes sense — and whether either does — depends on your amounts, your timeline, and the terms offered.

## The term that matters most: non-recourse

A legitimate settlement advance is **non-recourse**: repayment comes only from the settlement payment itself. If the fund pays less than expected, or an [appeal delays distribution](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-appeal-risk), or your claim resolves smaller than projected, a non-recourse funder absorbs that — they cannot pursue your other assets.

If an agreement gives the funder recourse to you personally, it is functionally a loan, and it should be compared against loan alternatives — including much cheaper ones like a bank line — rather than against other advances.

## How costs are structured — and where they hide

- **A flat discount** ("we advance $X against an expected $Y") is the easiest structure to evaluate: the cost is visible at signing and does not change.
- **Periodic fees** ("X% per month until payment") shift timing risk onto you: if checks slip from fall 2026 into 2027, the cost compounds during exactly the delay you were trying to escape. Given that this settlement's own schedule runs into September 2027, this structure deserves particular caution here.
- **Stacked charges** — application, processing, documentation fees — can meaningfully change the effective cost of a small advance. Ask for the all-in number.

## Pricing should reflect this settlement, not a generic one

The Bartz fund has published mechanics: a Works List, a default 50/50 author–publisher split for traditionally published titles (self-published authors keep the full amount), scheduled installments, and [a court-estimated payout of at least $3,000 per work](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-how-much-per-book). A funder who has done the work prices against those specifics. A quote that ignores them — treating a 12-work self-published claim the same as a single co-authored title — is guessing, and the guess is rarely in your favor.
