### I. Sleep Number (No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Bidding Procedures Hearing Tomorrow; Auction Clock Running


Sleep Number Corporation filed a voluntary Chapter 11 in the Southern District of New York on June 12, 2026, reporting assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion against liabilities exceeding $1 billion.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
The petition schedules $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 
Debtors' counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.


The case is structured as a pure Section 363 sale. 
Sleep Country Canada Inc. affiliate SNBR Inc. is serving as the stalking horse bidder, with the transaction to be conducted pursuant to Section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code.
 
The stalking horse bid is $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, subject to purchase price adjustments.
 
The company entered bankruptcy with $672 million in debt, attributing the filing in part to tariffs and inflation.
 Specifically, 
Sleep Number attributed the filing in part to "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."


The sale timeline is DIP-milestone-governed and non-negotiable in practice. 
The debtors are seeking a July 2 hearing on bidding procedures, a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, and a July 15 sale approval hearing — with the sale required to close by July 31.
 
To displace the stalking horse, a competing bidder must clear an effective floor of $441.45 million — comprising the $415 million base price, $16.45 million in bid protections payable in cash, and a $10 million minimum overbid.
 
A good faith deposit of $41.5 million is required; in the event of a buyer breach, forfeiture of that deposit is the estate's sole remedy absent fraud or willful breach.



The debtors justify the expedited timeline by reference to a prepetition "robust, 14-week marketing process" during which 53 potential strategic and financial purchasers were contacted, 19 executed NDAs, and five submitted preliminary proposals.
 
The court entered an interim DIP order approving the financing through a final hearing currently scheduled for July 9, 2026.
 
DIP obligations are guaranteed by each subsidiary of the company and are entitled to superpriority administrative expense claim status, secured by a perfected first priority lien on all DIP Collateral and a perfected priming senior security interest in prepetition collateral.


The DIP milestones create a tight sequencing risk for unsecured creditors and landlords. 
Per the DIP terms, if qualified bids are received, an auction must be conducted by July 13, 2026; a sale order entered by July 15; and the sale closed by July 31.
 
The Interim Order must be entered within three days of the Petition Date; the Final Order within 30 days; and bidding procedures approved within 28 days.
 Any DIP milestone breach is an event of default — a structural feature that effectively eliminates the estate's flexibility to negotiate extensions or entertain late-stage bidders.

What remains open and material for unsecured creditors: the treatment of Sleep Number's 572-store retail lease portfolio. 
The lease portfolio represents one of the more consequential open variables in this sale.
 Lease assumption or rejection decisions under 11 U.S.C. § 365 will govern landlord recoveries; absent a competing bid that captures those stores at premium values, the Section 363 timeline effectively forecloses meaningful landlord negotiation leverage. The July 2 bidding procedures hearing is the last procedural moment at which objectors can shape the auction mechanics before the bid deadline runs on July 8.

### II. BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group (No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Post-Sale Activity; Plan Track Uncertain


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 in the District of Delaware on March 15, 2026 (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), reporting approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 
The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing a framework for a reorganization plan centered on a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.


The case pursued a dual-track process. 
The debtors simultaneously pursued a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale, with the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remaining contested throughout.
 That property-classification question — analogous to the issue litigated extensively in the Celsius Network and Voyager Digital bankruptcies — has direct bearing on whether customers' deposited crypto is available for distribution under a plan or held in trust outside the estate.


On May 26, 2026, an official Bankruptcy Court document confirmed that Keyrock SA had been declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 A sale approval hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026 before Judge Horan. The tracked-case ground truth reflects six new docket entries between June 28 and June 30 (Dkt. Nos. 474–479), with Dkt. Nos. 478 and 479 filed on June 30. The substance of those filings is not yet available in the public scan, but the volume of terminal activity — concentrated in the two weeks following the June 16 hearing — is consistent with post-sale order implementation, plan supplement filings, or lingering objections to the Keyrock transaction.

The asset-to-liability gap is severe. 
BlockFills reported between $100 million and $500 million in liabilities against only $50 million to $100 million in assets at petition.
 A $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate on unsecured claims that is, without plan contributions from the liquidating trust, negligible. 
The liquidity crisis was driven by cascading counterparty defaults — including an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment.



BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase year-over-year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 That institutional base — hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies — is now effectively transferring to Keyrock. The practical recovery question for general unsecured creditors is whether the liquidating trust, funded from the $3.25 million sale proceeds and any remaining unencumbered estate assets, will generate meaningful distributions — or whether the priority waterfall is consumed by administrative and priority claims before unsecured creditors see a dollar.

### IV. Macro: Section 363 Velocity and the DIP-Milestone Trap

The Sleep Number and BlockFills cases together illustrate a structural dynamic worth flagging at the portfolio level: DIP lenders are increasingly embedding hard, non-waivable sale milestones that convert what is nominally a debtor-controlled Section 363 process into a lender-controlled liquidation timeline. In Sleep Number, the July 31 hard close is an event of default trigger — not a target. In BlockFills, the Keyrock bid was confirmed on May 26 with a June 16 hearing, approximately 73 days after the petition date. Both timelines compress the period during which unsecured creditors, landlords, and trade creditors can organize, retain counsel, form official committees, and mount substantive objections.

The broader appellate landscape on DIP milestones has not shifted materially since the Fifth Circuit's analysis in Ultra Petroleum, and the Delaware and Southern District of New York courts — the two dominant large-case jurisdictions — have consistently approved expedited sale timelines where pre-petition marketing processes are credibly documented. Sleep Number's 14-week, 53-party prepetition process (__[Pitchbook/PitchBook, June 2026](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/sleep-number-files-chapter-11-has-stalking-horse-bidder-seeks-260m-dip)__) is precisely the kind of evidentiary record that insulates bidding procedures from objection. Parties hoping to challenge the timeline at tomorrow's hearing face a high burden. The more productive intervention point is the July 8 bid deadline — i.e., arriving as a qualified competing bidder rather than as an objector.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and distressed investors tracking this advisory should move on two parallel tracks immediately. On Sleep Number (No. 26-11399): today is effectively the last pre-hearing window to review the proposed bidding procedures and assess whether to organize as a competing bidder or to file a targeted objection on the July 2 hearing — the $441.45 million effective floor, the $41.5 million good faith deposit requirement, and the July 8 bid deadline are all live parameters. Landlords with lease exposure across Sleep Number's 572-store portfolio should engage restructuring counsel now, before the assumption/rejection schedule crystallizes; waiting until the sale order is entered on July 15 will be too late to negotiate meaningful cure protections. On BlockFills (No. 26-10371): institutional creditors with unsecured claims should obtain the June 30 docket entries (Nos. 478–479) from CourtListener or PACER to determine whether a plan supplement or sale order has been filed, and should calculate their position in the priority waterfall against the $3.25 million gross sale proceeds — at that price point, unsecured recovery depends almost entirely on the liquidating trust structure and any preference or avoidance recoveries available to the estate. Docket Nos. 478–479 should clarify the current plan timeline and whether the confirmation bar date of September 11, 2026 (per Veritaglobal key dates) remains operative.
