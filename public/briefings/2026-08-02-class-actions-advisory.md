### *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Post-Final-Approval Distribution Watch

**N.D. Cal. No. 3:24-cv-05417 · Docket ID 69058235 · Judge Martínez-Olguín**


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment in the case.
 
The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," found that it "was negotiated at arm's length," and overruled all 53 objections filed with the court on various grounds.


**Claims rate and opt-out tally.** 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works — approximately 0.03% of the total number of works in the settlement fund.
 That figure is the headline denominator fact: at a 91.3% claims rate, the per-work recovery is determined almost entirely by the claims-made allocation formula, not by attrition. The opt-out count is low enough to treat as functionally immaterial to fund distribution math.

**Fee award and expense reimbursement.** 
Plaintiffs' counsel urged the court to grant an attorney fee request that had been revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million.
 
The final approval order authorized reimbursement of expenses totaling $2,635,197.46, a cost reserve of $18,220,000 in anticipated expenses, and service awards of $15,000 for each of the three Settlement Class Representatives, totaling $45,000.
 The fee award quantum is confirmed in the order; the precise approved fee figure beyond the expense line items is not separately broken out in publicly available order excerpts retrieved this run and should be verified against the full order at the Ars Technica PDF when the full text is accessible.

**Late opt-out rulings.** 
The court disallowed opt-outs submitted after the February 9, 2026 deadline, with the exception of two untimely requests — Esquivel's and Castells's — which were allowed.
 The ClaimsHero-affiliated opt-out campaign, which Judge William Alsup (the prior presiding judge) had called a "blatant attempt" to "trick people into opting out," 
produced a secondary skirmish at the district court level, with the court warning ClaimsHero against what one order characterized as a legal strategy it labeled "extortion."
 The final approval order appears to have resolved these collateral disputes; no new ClaimsHero-related filings appear in the last 24 hours.

**Distribution timeline.** 
August 2026 is the earliest anticipated date on which payments to class members may be sent, assuming no objections to the settlement delay the process.
 
The plan of allocation had designated summer 2026 as the earliest payment window if objectors were present but no appeals were filed.
 The open question — which the desk has not yet been able to confirm as of this morning — is whether any of the 53 overruled objectors have filed or indicated intent to file a notice of appeal to the Ninth Circuit within the standard 30-day FRAP 4(a) window running from the July 20 final judgment. 
The settlement's own notice documents flagged that payments may be slower than predicted if the settlement is appealed.
 That appellate exposure is the single most material variable for distribution timing right now.

**Settlement administrator.** JND Legal Administration is administering the fund. 
The deadline to submit a claim was March 30, 2026, and the deadline to exclude (opt out) or object passed on February 9, 2026.
 The administrator's next disclosed action is initiating payment processing; no formal announcement has been published as of this morning.

**What to watch this week.** The 30-day notice-of-appeal window from the July 20 final judgment closes on or about August 19, 2026 (FRAP 4(a)(1)(A)). Any objector who intends to pursue Ninth Circuit review must file by that date. If the window closes without a notice of appeal, the settlement becomes non-appealable and JND can move to first-distribution immediately. That is the next hard milestone.
