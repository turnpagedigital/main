### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del.) — Confirmation Window


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The debtors — Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (EIN last four: 6265), Reliz Technologies LLC (1968), Reliz LTD (N/A), and Reliz CI LTD (N/A) — 
report $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.


The capital structure is lopsided. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The Promissory Notes include a $12.6 million note and a $3.6 million note, purportedly secured on a first-priority basis by substantially all of the Debtors' assets, bearing interest at 12% per annum.
 The liquidity crisis had compounding causes: 
the company attributes its crisis to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.


The case dynamics were shaped early by two prepetition customer lawsuits. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026),
 and 
1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026), resulted in temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 
The automatic stay imposed by the Chapter 11 filing on March 15, 2026 halted these actions.


The case pursued a dual-track structure from the outset. 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursued a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 
Reliz operated under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing this dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with a sale hearing set for June 16, 2026 and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 The sale process did not produce a competing buyer of substance; 
the June 12 agenda indicated the sale of substantially all assets was going forward at the June 16 hearing, while sale objections remained from Chicago Blackhawks Hockey Team and Oracle, per the hearing agenda at Docket No. 424.


On the plan side, the disclosure statement was approved and solicitation ordered on May 28. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement; scheduling the Confirmation Hearing; establishing deadlines and procedures for filing objections to confirmation, claim objections, and temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes; setting the record date; and approving solicitation packages and voting procedures at Docket No. 388.
 
The operative document is the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and Its Debtor Affiliates at Docket No. 391.
 
The Voting Deadline and the Confirmation Objection Deadline both ran on July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors was formed March 30, 2026. 
Committee members are: (1) SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; (2) Dominion Capital LLC; (3) Karol Przybytkowski; (4) Jeffrey Brandt; (5) Tod Skarecky; (6) Energy Conversion Group; and (7) Fuel Labs Inc.

The operative posture as of today — voting closed, objection deadline closed, confirmation nine days out — is unambiguous: Judge Horan will hear whatever plan objections were timely filed on July 13 and rule, presumably within days thereafter, on whether the Amended Plan satisfies 11 U.S.C. § 1129. 
The filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Countdown (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068)

The FTX Recovery Trust's fifth distribution commences July 31, 2026 — 27 days from today. 
The Next Distribution is expected to commence on July 31, 2026, with the next payments to eligible holders of allowed preferred equity interests also made on July 31, 2026, with a June 16, 2026 record date.


The most consequential procedural development preceding this distribution was the reserve reduction. 
FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release cash to be distributed to holders of allowed claims in the next Distribution.
 The $600 million reserve release — if approved — meaningfully increases the pool available to Class 5B and 6A/6B claimants on July 31.

Distribution mechanics are strict. 
Once a claim is Allowed, the holder must submit a completed valid tax form (step 7 on the FTX claims portal), select and successfully onboard with a distribution service provider (step 8), and pass sanctions screening, in each case, by the June 16, 2026 record date; failure to complete these steps results in the holder not receiving their distribution on July 31, 2026.
 
For transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims maintained by the Notice and Claims Agent as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 That last point is critical for the claims-trading aftermarket: any transfer executed in the last three weeks of May or first two weeks of June that did not clear the 21-day notice window will not pay on July 31.

Distribution context: 
distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion last September, and a fourth round launched on March 31.
 
In total, roughly $10 billion has already gone back to creditors.
 The July 31 round is therefore incremental — but the $600 million reserve unlock is a material new development. The next omnibus hearing is scheduled for 
July 23, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. ET at US Bankruptcy Court, 824 Market Street, 6th Floor.
 That hearing may address final approval of the reserve reduction.

The petition-date valuation methodology remains the case's defining creditor grievance. 
Every percentage recovery is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ in nominal dollars is worth less in today's terms.

### Genesis/DCG — Wind-Down Posture and Adversary Proceedings (Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.)

Genesis completed its effective date distribution in August 2024, following the May 2024 confirmation victory. 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the chapter 11 plan confirmed by the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on May 21, 2024.
 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value; instead, Genesis sought to provide restitution to creditors through the chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the New York Attorney General, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.


The Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) is now managing post-effective-date cleanup, and multiple adversary proceedings remain live. 
Active adversary proceedings include: Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053; Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., 26-03230;
 
Digital Currency Grp. v. Genesis Global Cap., Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129;
 and 
Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097.
 A WDOC Town Hall is scheduled for 
July 7, 2026 at 12:00 p.m. ET.
 
A motion to dismiss was heard on March 25, 2026, and a pretrial conference was held June 9, 2026
 in connection with the DCG-related adversary proceeding. The July 15, 2026 omnibus hearing will be the next substantive milestone. 
The Wind-Down estate previously commenced a $33 million adversary proceeding against DCG seeking repayment of amounts paid to Three Arrows Capital.


The Genesis confirmation record established a durable precedent: 
the decision allows creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to the value as of the petition date; as a result, Genesis is authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors, even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 That in-kind methodology is now the benchmark against which every other crypto insolvency's petition-date valuation approach is measured — and it is the framework that BlockFills creditors should be tracking most carefully in connection with the July 13 confirmation hearing.

### Cross-Docket Pattern Recognition: The § 541 Customer-Property Question

Three of the tracked cases — BlockFills, the completed Celsius proceeding (now in distribution), and Genesis — converged on the same threshold question: whether customer-deposited crypto is property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or is held in trust for customers. In Celsius, the amended Terms of Service language (transferring title to the debtor on deposit) drove the court's finding that custodial assets were estate property. Genesis resolved the question differently — through an in-kind return structure — without requiring a § 541 adjudication. BlockFills's dual-track structure (NewCo path plus § 363 sale) also defers that question to the plan, where the treatment of "customer digital assets" in the Amended Plan at Docket No. 391 will be the operative text. Practitioners watching July 13 should read the plan's definition of "Customer Claims" and trace how those claims are classified relative to general unsecured claims — the classification controls § 1129(b) cramdown risk.
