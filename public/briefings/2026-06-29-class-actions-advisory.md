# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Monday, June 29, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The headline delta since Friday: the Social Media MDL's first federal school-district bellwether — Breathitt County Board of Education v. Meta, Snap, TikTok, and YouTube — quietly died before opening statements, with all four defendants settling on the eve of trial.** That pre-verdict settlement now stands as the strongest settlement signal yet across a docket carrying roughly 2,664 federal MDL cases, nearly 800 school-district claims, and parallel state AG actions from 42 jurisdictions. Simultaneously, Bartz v. Anthropic (3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) is awaiting Judge Martínez-Olguín's final approval order after a May 14 fairness hearing that the settlement largely sailed through — the last substantive judicial output before a $1.5 billion fund enters distribution. On the mass-arbitration side, Keller Postman's Google advertiser campaign, announced May 11, remains the most structurally significant pending demand portfolio on the docket: established liability, mandatory arbitration clauses, and fee-pressure economics that precede any merits hearing.

### I. Social Media MDL 3047 — Breathitt County Bellwether Settles; State AG Trial Clock Now Governs


The first federal bellwether trial in *In re Social Media Adolescent Addiction/Personal Injury Products Liability Litigation*, MDL 3047 (Case No. 4:22-md-03047-YGR), N.D. Cal., before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, was set for June 15, 2026 — Breathitt County School District (Kentucky) v. Meta, Snap, TikTok, and YouTube. It never reached a jury: Snap, TikTok, and YouTube settled in mid-May 2026, and Meta settled days later, on the eve of trial.
 
Breathitt was set to be the first school-district bellwether and had sought over $60 million in damages. Meta was the final defendant to settle, after Snap, TikTok, and YouTube settled earlier in the month. More than 1,300 other school-district claims remain pending. The settlement amount was not disclosed.


The significance of the Breathitt settlement cannot be overstated for valuation purposes. 
Local reporting puts the combined value at roughly $27 million for a single rural Kentucky district — a settlement that followed a plaintiff verdict the defendants could not ignore: on March 25, 2026, a Los Angeles jury in *K.G.M. v. Meta & YouTube* awarded $6 million ($3M compensatory split 70% Meta / 30% Google, plus $3M punitive) — the first social-media product-liability verdict of its kind, after Mark Zuckerberg's first-ever jury testimony on February 18, 2026.


Structurally, 
as of June 2026, MDL 3047 holds roughly 2,664 pending cases spanning individual personal-injury claims, claims by nearly 800 school districts, and actions by state attorneys general. Counting state-court filings, more than 1,300 school districts nationwide have sued.



Judge Gonzalez Rogers selected six school districts — from Maryland, Georgia, Kentucky, New Jersey, South Carolina, and Arizona — as the first bellwether wave.
 With Breathitt County resolved, the remaining five bellwether school-district cases are awaiting their own trial settings. The next inflection point is the state AG track: 
the state AG bellwether trial is scheduled to begin August 6, 2026, with a second state AG pretrial conference set for June 26 and a final pretrial conference July 17.


The pre-verdict settlement dynamic mirrors opioid and tobacco precedent. 
The two March 2026 jury verdicts of $375 million (with $3.7 billion requested in abatement) and $6 million represent only the beginning of a litigation cycle that mirrors, structurally, the trajectory of tobacco and opioid litigation. As the federal MDL trial proceeds in Oakland in summer 2026 and as 42 state AG cases advance, settlement pressure across the full docket will increase. Each MDL trial verdict that goes against Meta reduces its negotiating position in the remaining pool of cases and increases its financial burden.


On the New Mexico AG front, 
trial in the first state AG case began February 2, 2026 in the First Judicial District Court of New Mexico; on March 24, 2026, a jury returned a verdict ordering Meta to pay a civil penalty of $375 million. The New Mexico AG has indicated intent to seek approximately $3.7 billion in abatement costs as well as injunctive relief.
 
As of June 2026, no final ruling on remedies has been issued
 — that bench trial on abatement remains the largest single pending remedies proceeding in the litigation.

Insurance coverage remains a separate exposure vector. 
Meta's insurers are refusing to defend child safety claims, arguing the damages arise from intentional design choices.
 That coverage dispute shifts the entire settlement calculus: every dollar Meta pays comes from its own balance sheet.

**Section 230 trajectory.** 
In April 2026, the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court issued a unanimous decision in *Commonwealth v. Meta Platforms Inc.*, holding that Section 230 does not shield Meta from claims about its own design choices and deceptive statements about Instagram's safety. Justice Dalila Argaez Wendlandt wrote that the immunity is "much narrower" than Meta argued, applying only when litigation specifically targets "publishing activities."
 That ruling is persuasive authority in other jurisdictions.

For claims recovery practitioners, the operative question is per-claim value in a global resolution. 
Multiple social media companies settled the school-district bellwether trial in May for approximately $27 million. The next trial is scheduled for July 2026.
 The $27 million figure for a district that sought $60 million — a rough 45 cents-on-the-dollar outcome for a single bellwether — sets an early floor for the school-district track but is unlikely to represent the eventual MDL-wide settlement rate once more verdicts accumulate.

### II. Bartz v. Anthropic, 3:24-cv-05417 — Final Approval Order Pending; Distribution Clock Running


Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín held the final approval fairness hearing for *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* (3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) on May 14, 2026. Following the hearing, the court issued an order directing Anthropic to file by May 21, 2026 a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored.
 
Class Counsel Justin Nelson stated the payout per work will be approximately $3,100. The class had a 92.77% participation rate for works on the works list.


The final approval order has not yet been entered as of the CourtListener last-update timestamp. 
The docket was last updated June 25, 2026.
 No final approval order appears in the record — meaning the settlement remains in a post-hearing, pre-order posture. That gap is consequential for the distribution timeline.


Authors who alleged Anthropic pirated their work urged the court to grant final approval to the $1.5 billion settlement, along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 
Separately, Anthropic argued that the $75 million in fees requested by nonclass counsel is far too high, with "scant justification" for the request.
 Fee disputes of this kind — class counsel at $187.5 million, nonclass counsel seeking an additional $75 million — are the last structural obstacle before the order issues.


Attorneys told the court that the settlement claims rate had grown to nearly 93%, while the opt-out and objection rates were "minuscule" — a strong sign that the deal meets the standard of being "fair, reasonable, and adequate."


The settlement's installment structure is material for claimants tracking distribution timing. 
Anthropic will fund the $1.5 billion settlement fund in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 With the first two installments presumably funded, the September 2026 tranche — the largest interval distribution — is the next cash-flow event for the administrator. 
After the fairness hearing, the settlement administrator was expected to calculate distributions by June 11, 2026
 — meaning distribution calculations should already be complete, assuming the order follows promptly.

### III. BCBS Antitrust Settlement, MDL 2406 — Tier-1 Wave Ongoing, Tier-2 Notifications Active

The $2.67 billion *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation* settlement (MDL 2406, N.D. Ala., Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP) entered active distribution in May 2026 and continues through June. This is a stale-but-critical carry-forward for claims-rate monitoring.


On April 23, 2026, subscriber plaintiffs filed a motion in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Alabama asking the court to approve a distribution plan for nearly 7.8 million claimants.
 
The official settlement website stated that initial distribution would begin in May 2026, and a later court order directed the Settlement Administrator to begin issuing payments during the week of May 11, 2026.



Claimants with a calculated distribution of $200 or more will receive 90% in the initial round, with 10% held in reserve for a second distribution. Claimants receive funds by check, PayPal, Venmo, or electronic debit card. JND began sending payments on or about May 11, 2026.



Tier-2 (employee) notification emails started June 1 for the first batch — roughly 15% of validated Tier-2 claims — with payments expected 4–8 weeks after notification. Tier-3 (employer) payments have not yet started.
 
The Eleventh Circuit previously denied the final pending appeal on Tier-2 subclass methodology, clearing the path for full Tier-2 distribution to begin in Q2/Q3 2026.


Recovery practitioners: the claims window is permanently closed as of November 5, 2021. 
The parallel provider settlement fund (bcbsprovidersettlement.com) began its first wave of physician and clinic payouts from a separate $2.8 billion fund, with small practices reporting payouts in the $5K–$25K range.

### IV. Keller Postman / Google Mass Arbitration — The Largest Pending Advertiser Campaign


On May 11, 2026, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic and anticompetitive conduct, Keller Postman announced it is representing U.S. businesses in a coordinated mass arbitration seeking recovery for alleged advertising overcharges.
 The campaign was formally initiated at Chicago-based Keller Postman, 150 N. Riverside Plaza, Suite 4100, with Ashley Keller and Warren Postman as lead partners.

The liability predicate is established by two separate courts. 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) have ruled in favor of the U.S. Department of Justice, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in its advertising business.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs, creating the potential for substantial recovery under federal antitrust law.



The $218 billion figure attributed to the campaign is an estimate, not a filed demand amount. In mass arbitration, the leverage comes from the volume of individual filings and the per-claim fee obligations they trigger — not from any single damages number.
 
Potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law (Clayton Act § 4).



These claims are being pursued in individual arbitration because Google's terms of service require advertisers to resolve disputes through binding arbitration.
 The FAA mechanics, *Concepcion* notwithstanding, operate in claimants' favor at this scale: 
for each individual claim, Google is typically required to pay the arbitration forum's per-case initiation fees, which at AAA's current rates run into the thousands of dollars per case. At the scale Keller Postman is assembling — potentially thousands or tens of thousands of advertiser claimants — the fee exposure alone creates enormous settlement pressure before a single arbitration hearing occurs.



This is the same fee-leverage model that drove Uber to settle 60,000 arbitration demands for between $146 million and $170 million, that forced DoorDash to pay $9.5 million just to initiate proceedings for 5,000 workers, and that caused Amazon to abandon its mandatory arbitration clause entirely rather than face the economics of 75,000 individual arbitrations. The Google campaign has one structural advantage those earlier campaigns lacked: established liability.



According to Keller Postman, any company that purchased ads on Google on or after August 2016 may qualify for the arbitration process.
 
In addition to representing private claimants in arbitration, Keller Postman represents seven state AGs in a parallel case.
 The dual-track structure — private arbitration plus state AG suits — is a pressure multiplier. No global settlement figure has been announced; the campaign is in the filing-and-intake phase.

### V. Visa / Mastercard Interchange — Equitable Relief Track Active

The *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation* (E.D.N.Y.) equitable relief settlement has cleared preliminary approval. 
The proposed settlement would trim credit interchange fee rates by 10 basis points applied to varying rates for five years and impose a 1.25% rate for standard consumer cards for eight years. It would also give merchants the right to decline some higher-cost Visa and Mastercard premium and commercial credit cards — a departure from the networks' "honor all cards" rule.
 
Unlike the separate $5 billion monetary settlement, this equitable relief settlement requires no action or claim filing — relief will be automatic once the settlement is approved.
 The monetary track's claims filing deadline of February 4, 2025 has passed; no new monetary claims are being accepted.
