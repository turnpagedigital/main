### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Final Approval Entered; Distribution Clock Running


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the landmark $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* (No. 3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) and entered final judgment in the case.
 The 23-page order — the last substantive docket event logged in the CourtListener feed (Entry 681, Jul. 21, 2026) — resolved all outstanding administrative questions and disposed of the fee dispute.


Judge Martínez-Olguín called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," overruling all 53 objections filed with the court opposing the settlement on various grounds.
 
The court declined to entertain objections from non-class members, reminding those individuals that, precisely because the settlement does not cover them, they remain free to bring their own claims.


**Fee award.** 
Class counsel initially sought $300 million, then later trimmed the request to $187.5 million; the court approved approximately $101.5 million in attorney fees.
 
By rough math, that works out to approximately $2,953 per hour across the 34,381.6 hours of attorney time the court credited.
 The lodestar cross-check discipline here is notable: the court's willingness to cut a fee request by roughly two-thirds — from $300M to $101.5M — will reverberate in fee applications across comparable AI-copyright settlements, where plaintiffs' counsel in parallel actions against OpenAI and Meta are watching this benchmark closely.

**Claims posture and recovery math.** 
In aggregate, this decision marks the largest class action copyright settlement in history, with a reported claims rate of 92.77 percent.
 
The settlement pays approximately $3,100 per author of more than 480,000 works, with claims being filed for 440,490 works downloaded from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror by Anthropic.
 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate, with 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works, or 0.03% of the total number of works.
 The near-total claims rate is structurally significant: with almost no unclaimed residual in the fund, the settlement administrator has minimal cy pres exposure, and distribution arithmetic is straightforward.

**Distribution timing.** 
The Authors Guild looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process; while a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not been announced, Authors Guild hopes and expects this will happen quickly.
 Rights-holders who filed valid claims should monitor the Anthropic Copyright Settlement Administrator's portal (AnthropicCopyrightSettlement.com) for payment disbursement notices. Given the 30-day window for Rule 60(b) motions post-final judgment, the earliest realistic first distribution is mid-to-late August 2026, absent objector appeals.

**Appellate exposure.** The court overruled all 53 objectors. The record does not reflect any notice of appeal filed as of the last CourtListener sync (Jul. 21, 2026). However, objectors have 30 days from entry of final judgment to notice an appeal to the Ninth Circuit. Monitor the docket through August 20, 2026 for any Rule 23(e)(5) or FRAP 3/4 notices.

**Downstream AI-copyright posture.** 
The court's order may prompt other claimants to follow suits such as the Chicken Soup for the Soul and Carreyrou actions, which remain separate individual actions.
 
Prior to the class certification, Judge William Alsup held that AI companies can use legally purchased books for training — but not illegal ones,
 a ruling that frames the liability theory in parallel proceedings against OpenAI, Meta, and others. The Bartz settlement amount and fee award are now the benchmark against which those cases will be valued.

### II. BCBS Subscriber Settlement — *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation* — Mid-Distribution


The case is *In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Alabama, Southern Division.
 
U.S. District Judge Anna M. Manasco signed the distribution order on April 24, 2026, granting Class Counsel's motion (filed April 23, 2026) for distribution of the Net Settlement Fund to approved Damages Class Members.



The initial distribution of payments to Damages Class Members with valid claims began starting May 11, 2026.
 
The $2.67 billion gross settlement (approximately $1.9 billion Net Settlement Fund after attorneys' fees and administrative costs) is one of the largest antitrust settlements in U.S. healthcare history.
 
After court-approved attorney fees of 25% (approximately $667.5 million) and administration costs (roughly $100 million), approximately $1.9 billion remains for distribution to roughly 6 million claimants.



Combined with a separate $2.8 billion provider settlement that received final approval on August 19, 2025, the total BCBS antitrust resolution exceeds $5.47 billion.


The distribution is rolling. 
May 11, 2026 was the start of the BCBS payment rollout, not the single day every claimant receives money.
 Claimants can check status at BCBSsettlement.com using their Claim Number or Unique ID. No second distribution order has been filed as of this writing; the claims rate and residual pool will drive the timing of any supplemental distribution. The case is closed for new claims — the deadline was November 5, 2021.

### III. Visa/Mastercard Interchange — *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee*, MDL 1720 (E.D.N.Y.) — Second Distribution Approved for September 2026


*In re Payment Card Interchange Fee & Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, No. 1:05-md-01720-MKB-JO (E.D.N.Y., Dec. 13, 2019) produced a cash settlement of approximately $5.54 billion.



On May 26, 2026, Class Counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds; on June 15, 2026, the court approved the motion.
 
The second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026.


On the injunctive-relief track, a separate rule-change settlement has its own appellate trajectory. 
Keefe, Bruyette & Woods predicts a final approval and decision on that track late in 2026 or early 2027, with a formal objection period and follow-up hearing still to come.
 
KBW also predicts a "lengthy" appeal process will follow, driven by large merchants, which could push final implementation to 2029, assuming those terms survive appeals.


On the individual opt-out track in the Southern District of New York: 
U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein formally closed the opt-out merchant litigation after Amtrak, Crate & Barrel, Dick's Sporting Goods, Nike and other merchants settled their claims over the past six weeks.
 
A second group of merchants, led by GrubHub Holdings, remains in settlement discussions.


The UK dimension adds cross-border complexity: 
Visa and Mastercard are challenging a judgment that found their default multilateral interchange fees charged to retailers infringe competition law; London's Court of Appeal ruled in March 2026 that the networks may appeal the Competition Appeal Tribunal's ruling, which found their fees breached European competition law.


**Recovery lens for merchants:** The September 2026 second distribution is the actionable event. Merchants who accepted Visa/Mastercard between January 1, 2004 and January 25, 2019 and filed claims by the February 4, 2025 deadline should expect payment notices from the Class Administrator in August–September 2026. No further claim submissions are accepted.

### IV. Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Keller Postman — Estimated $218 Billion


Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings that found the company engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.



Ashley Keller estimates potential claims for online search and display ads could reach $218 billion or more, based on calculations from an economist his firm has hired.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade.
 
Potential damages range from 15% to 30% of total spend; the recovery period spans from 2016 onward.



The firm represents approximately 10,000 clients with total spend exceeding $20 billion since 2016, and has commenced arbitration proceedings for the majority of these claimants.
 
Due to contractual arbitration provisions implemented by Google, these claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation.
 
The firm operates on a contingency fee basis of 40% with no upfront costs to clients.



Similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between the filing of claims and resolution.
 On the remedies front, 
Judge Brinkema's ad tech remedies ruling is expected in 2026; Keller Postman is actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines.


The structural mechanics here are critical. 
Arbitration clauses in Google's advertiser contracts effectively preclude damages via class action litigation; instead, advertisers join together to make claims as part of a group arbitration, handled by a single firm, which provides more leverage in settlement negotiations.
 The AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules govern filing fees and batch scheduling. Google's filing-fee exposure across 10,000+ claims is material — it is the same economic pressure that prompted Amazon to drop mandatory arbitration after 75,000 demands in a prior Keller Postman campaign.
