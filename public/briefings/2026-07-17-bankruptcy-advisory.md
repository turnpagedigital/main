### LEDE — BlockFills: Confirmation Hearing Passed, Claims Bar Date Looms; Keyrock Sale Absorbed into Plan Track

The two most consequential events on this beat as of this morning are closely related: the BlockFills confirmation hearing on July 13 and the Sleep Number § 363 sale hearing on July 15, both of which were either completed or adjourned within the last 48 hours, and the landmark July 14 Chief Judge Glenn opinion in the New Fortress Energy chapter 15 proceeding that will reshape how practitioners think about cross-border restructuring plans and COMI analysis for years.

**BlockFills — *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. (Judge Thomas M. Horan)**


The BlockFills confirmation hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, with a voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline of July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 As of the tracked docket (three new entries filed between July 13–15, Dkt. Nos. 528–530), the case has moved through the plan solicitation phase. 
On May 28, 2026, Judge Horan entered the order approving the disclosure statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, and establishing solicitation procedures — including deadlines for claim objections and temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes — at Docket No. 388.


The plan structure to understand here is not straightforward. 
The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers, providing a framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 The § 363 track ran in parallel and produced a buyer: 
Keyrock SA was declared the successful bidder at a purchase price of $3.25 million and agreed to assume substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, and its customer lists and proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
The June 16, 2026 sale hearing was the procedural checkpoint for the Keyrock transaction, with confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 The docket as of July 15 shows three new filings in the 72-hour window, but the specific content of those entries was not available through the docket description field — what is verifiable is that the case has not been dismissed and activity is ongoing.


The next major docket milestone visible on the Veritaglobal case page is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 — which corresponds to the claims bar date or a related post-confirmation deadline. Creditors holding disputed, unliquidated, or contingent claims who have not yet filed proofs of claim should treat September 11 as a hard deadline and confirm directly with Veritaglobal at veritaglobal.net/BlockFills.

The recovery math remains challenging. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against that, Keyrock's $3.25 million purchase price covers a going-concern technology and client-list sale — it does not represent aggregate recovery for unsecured creditors. The liquidating trust is the vehicle through which general unsecured creditors, including former customers, will receive distributions. The central open legal question — whether customer digital assets constitute estate property or were held in trust and thus not available to general unsecured creditors — was never definitively resolved pre-plan. 
The BlockFills Client Agreement authorized the firm to transfer margin to exchanges and clearing houses at its sole discretion, stated that customers "rank only as a general creditor" upon third-party default, explicitly excluded margin from treatment as "Client Money," and on February 11, 2026, Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 That commingling disclosure largely resolved the property question in the estate's favor under the *Celsius* / *Voyager* line of cases, and the plan proceeds on that basis.

### Sleep Number — § 363 Sale Hearing Adjourned to July 20; Adjourned DIP Final Hearing Follows

**Sleep Number Corporation — *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. (Judge Kyu Young Paek)**

The most important procedural development in this case since Wednesday is the adjournment. 
The sale hearing, originally set for July 15, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, has been adjourned to July 20, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, along with the final hearing on the DIP motion and hearing on the disclosure statement, per the notice of adjournment filed on the docket.
 This is a five-day slip and likely reflects either a negotiation over the form of sale order or a need to resolve outstanding objections from landlords, the creditors' committee, or other parties in interest. The Kroll case page as of July 15 recorded 20 new docket entries in the preceding 72 hours — a very high volume that is consistent with a sale hearing and contested DIP final order proceeding simultaneously.

The capital structure context: 
Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated Debtors filed for Chapter 11 protection on June 12, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, reporting $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities as of April 30, 2026.
 
According to the first-day declaration, the company has approximately $672.5 million of funded debt, comprising about $475 million outstanding under a revolver, $177.5 million outstanding under a term loan entered into in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered into in 2026.
 Davis Polk & Wardwell represents the Debtors; 
Guggenheim Securities is the investment banker, AP Services (an affiliate of AlixPartners) is the interim manager, and Joele Frank, Wilkinson Brimmer Katcher is the strategic communications advisor.


The DIP facility is the creditors' committee's primary battleground. 
Sleep Number expects up to $260 million of DIP financing from its existing lenders, including up to $65 million of new-money superpriority term loans and up to $195 million of roll-up loans, with interest at SOFR plus 8% or base rate plus 7%.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans mature on September 16, 2026 and are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral, subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 The $195 million roll-up is the structural aggressor here: it elevates prepetition revolving lenders to superpriority administrative expense status on a dollar-for-dollar basis against new money, effectively subordinating all other unsecured claims and constraining the investigation rights of any committee. 
Parties in interest may investigate — but not prosecute — the prepetition liens and obligations within a $100,000 budget, and the challenge deadline runs to the earlier of 60 days after committee formation (or 75 days if no committee) and the commencement of the Sale Hearing.


The stalking horse: 
Sleep Country Canada will serve as the stalking horse bidder under the § 363 process.
 
Sleep Country Canada's offer is approximately $415 million.
 
Sleep Country is the largest mattress retailer in Canada, with around 307 stores, and was taken private in October 2024 by Fairfax Financial in a deal worth approximately $1.7 billion.
 If no auction occurred — the Kroll page confirms July 13 as the scheduled auction date, and no competing bid announcement has surfaced publicly — Sleep Country remains the sole buyer, and the July 20 sale hearing is effectively an uncontested approval proceeding. Distressed investors should note: 
the buyer's real decisions on lease portfolio composition come during a post-closing Designation Rights Period running up to 60 days after closing, with any lease not designated automatically rejected without further court order, meaning the full picture of which locations survive under Sleep Country's ownership will not be known until potentially late September 2026.
 Rejected landlords face the § 502(b)(6) cap — limited to the greater of one year's rent or 15% of the remaining term not to exceed three years — and face little realistic recovery in the unsecured pool at current capital structure metrics.


Before filing, Sleep Number tried to free up cash by refinancing debt, selling its right to refunds under 2025 tariffs that were invalidated by the U.S. Supreme Court, closing stores, and streamlining product offerings, per court documents.
 The tariff-refund monetization is worth flagging: it means the IEEPA/Section 301 refund litigation created enough expected value that Sleep Number securitized it pre-petition — one of the first documented instances of a retailer treating tariff-refund claims as a distinct balance sheet asset.

### New Fortress Energy — Chief Judge Glenn's July 14 Opinion Redraws Chapter 15 / COMI Analysis

**In re NFE Global Holdings Limited, et al., Case No. 26-11268 (MG), Bankr. S.D.N.Y.**

This is the most consequential jurisprudential development of the week. 
On July 14, 2026, Chief Judge Martin Glenn of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York issued a memorandum opinion in *In re NFE Global Holdings Limited, et al.*, Case No. 26-11268 (MG), recognizing two English Part 26A restructuring plan proceedings as foreign main proceedings under Chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code and giving full force and effect in the United States to the English court's sanction order and restructuring plans, including the plan releases.


Background on what this restructuring actually did: 
the restructuring plans were designed to reduce the New Fortress group's funded debt owed to external creditors from approximately $5.7 billion to less than $1 billion, exchange existing debt for new debt and equity, and separate the group into two independent companies — BrazilCo and CoreCo — with existing shareholders retaining 35% of CoreCo common stock before dilution from the management incentive plan and convertible preferred equity.



New Fortress Energy Inc. is a Delaware corporation based in New York. The Chapter 15 debtors, NFE Global Holdings Limited and NFE Brazil Newco Limited, are UK-incorporated affiliates. NFE Global was incorporated in the UK in 2021, and NFE Brazil was incorporated in April 2026, shortly before the English proceedings, specifically to promote the restructuring of the group's Brazil-related business.
 That timeline for NFE Brazil — incorporated approximately two months before the restructuring commenced — is precisely the "COMI tourism" fact pattern that drew scrutiny.

Chief Judge Glenn's analysis is the headline. 
The decision cautions that "COMI tourism" is not a per se bar to recognition, but abusive use of UK plan companies may justify denial of recognition or enforcement in future cases.
 
Where a Part 26 scheme or Part 26A plan is used to restructure the debt of a non-UK company, the Court stated that a Chapter 15 court should scrutinize COMI closely to ensure that the foreign proceeding has not been used unfairly to favor one creditor group over another or to achieve a result that could not be achieved in Chapter 11, with insider exploitation, untoward manipulation, or overt thwarting of creditor expectations potentially supporting denial of recognition or enforcement.
 
The Court found no such abuse in New Fortress: there were no objections, creditors overwhelmingly supported the restructuring, affected creditors had notice and hearing rights in the English proceedings, and the A&M analysis showed better projected recoveries under the restructuring plans than under the relevant alternative — and in those circumstances, the Court concluded that the plans were not the product of exploitation or improper manipulation.


The practical import for the restructuring bar: the opinion provides a defensible roadmap for U.S.-based corporate groups that wish to use English Part 26A plans — but it also telegraphs the specific facts that will defeat recognition in a contested proceeding: late-stage UK incorporation, disparate treatment between creditor classes, and recovery outcomes inferior to what Chapter 11 would produce. Any GP contemplating a "London scheme" for a U.S.-domiciled enterprise should expect Chapter 15 recognition to be contested on those grounds and plan accordingly. 
NFE's creditors voted 99% in favor of the UK restructuring plan at meetings convened on June 15, with unanimous consent obtained in nearly all classes.
