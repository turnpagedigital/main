### BlockFills — Confirmation Posture as of July 3


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary petitions under Chapter 11; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The estate reported $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities at the petition date.
 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The plan architecture is a dual-track toggle. 
Less than four weeks after the petition date, the Debtors filed a Joint Chapter 11 Plan, Disclosure Statement, and Solicitation Procedures Motion on April 7, 2026; the plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing election between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a § 363 competitive sale process.
 The § 363 track resolved before solicitation closed. 
According to bankruptcy filings, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million for substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" in a court document filed May 26, 2026.
 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge on June 16, 2026 agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale to the Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepares for its confirmation hearing.


The current confirmation calendar is locked. 
The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the confirmation-objection deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the confirmation hearing is set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
On May 28, 2026, the Court entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing claim-objection and temporary-allowance procedures, determining treatment of unliquidated and contingent claims for voting purposes, setting the record date, and approving solicitation packages, ballots, and voting-tabulation procedures at Docket No. 388.
 The Amended Joint Plan and Amended Disclosure Statement were filed at Docket Nos. 391 and 392 respectively, with a confirmation-hearing notice at Docket No. 393.

**The unresolved § 541 commingling question.** The most consequential open issue for general unsecured creditors has not changed since the first day hearing. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission — which mirrors the Celsius first-day posture in *In re Celsius Network LLC*, Case No. 22-10964 (MG) (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — eliminates any trust or bailment theory for customer-deposit holders absent a successful adversary proceeding challenging the estate's § 541(a)(1) characterization. 
The company's liquidity crisis traced back to cascading counterparty exposures: an $8.5 million impairment from the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy, alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 The UCC — composed of SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs Inc. — 
selected FTI Consulting, Inc. as financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.


**Adversary proceedings.** 
An adversary proceeding, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH), was opened against Artha Investment Partners LLC, with the Debtors as plaintiffs.
 The prepetition customer litigations — 
*Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, S.D.N.Y. No. 1:26-cv-01672 (filed February 27, 2026), in which Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring foreign asset transfers, and ordering accounting and segregation of customer funds
 — and 
*1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al.*, N.D. Ill. No. 1:26-cv-02451 (filed March 5, 2026), which also produced a TRO and named Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace
 — remain stayed by the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 but will re-activate against non-debtor defendants absent a plan-injunction provision.

The September 11, 2026 bar date visible on the Veritaglobal case page represents the claims filing deadline for the reorganized entity; creditors who have not yet filed proofs of claim against the BlockFills estate should treat the ten days between today and the July 13 confirmation hearing as an operational deadline to assess plan treatment and vote.

### Bitcoin Depot — Five-Buyer Auction Closes; Sale Hearing Set July 8


On May 17, 2026, Bitcoin Depot Inc. and sixteen affiliated entities, including BCD Merger Sub LLC, BitAccess Inc., Bitcoin Depot Operating LLC, BT HoldCo LLC, and Lux Vending Kiosk LLC, each filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas.
 
The petition listed $11.3 million in assets and $26.9 million in liabilities as of March 31, 2026.
 
A first day hearing was held May 19, 2026 before Judge Christopher M. Lopez.


The asset-sale process moved to conclusion this week. 
On June 10, 2026, the Court entered an order approving bidding procedures for the sale of all or substantially all assets; on June 29, 2026, the Debtors conducted the auction via video and telephone conference and selected the winning bidders; the sale hearing is adjourned to July 8, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. CT.
 
Bitcoin Depot told the Texas bankruptcy judge that five buyers had prevailed in the auction for the company's kiosks, business lines, and other assets.
 The claims-administration deadline calendar is significant for unsecured creditors: 
the general bar date is July 21, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. CT; the governmental bar date is November 23, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. CT.
 
The Court will hold a combined hearing on the Disclosure Statement and confirmation of the Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation on August 7, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. CT.


The prepetition balance sheet context matters for recovery expectations. 
The Chapter 11 filing triggered an event of default under the company's term loan credit agreement, though creditor enforcement is stayed by the Bankruptcy Code.
 
The primary vehicle for any committee challenge is to Silverview's $3.1 million exit fee; adequate protection consists of a $17.2 million segregated reserve held at People First Bank, capped replacement liens, superpriority claims, and a $100,000 cap on Silverview's professional fees during the interim period.
 
Bitcoin Depot issued WARN Act notices to all employees, including executives, with executive terminations anticipated effective July 17, 2026.


The macro driver of this liquidation is regulatory compression. 
Bitcoin Depot faced a high-profile lawsuit led by the attorneys general of Massachusetts and Iowa over allegations that it facilitated crypto scams; crypto ATM fraud hit a record $389 million in reported losses last year, a 58% increase from 2024, drawing intensified regulatory and prosecutorial scrutiny.
 
The firm had overhauled its leadership in March 2026, appointing Holmes as CEO after Connecticut suspended its money transmission license; a month later, hackers breached the company's IT systems and stole $3.7 million from its crypto wallets.
 The five-buyer auction outcome — rather than a single strategic acquirer — reflects both the breadth of the asset pool (kiosks across 47 states, the BitAccess software stack, BDCheckout, the Kutt peer-to-peer platform) and the fragmented demand for distressed BTM infrastructure. Kroll is administering claims at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/bitcoindepot.

### Sector Pattern: BTM Regulatory Collapse as Systemic Risk

The Bitcoin Depot liquidation is worth reading as a structural signal, not just an isolated filing. 
Restructuring advisor Roshan Dharia described Bitcoin Depot's bankruptcy as "a preview of what the broader crypto ATM industry will face in the United States over the next several years," explaining that operators' traditional model depended on high transaction spreads and limited regulatory scrutiny to offset compliance, logistics, fraud remediation, and retail revenue-sharing costs — and that states "increasingly impose consumer protection standards that compress fees, expand operator liability for scam-related activity, and raise expectations around transaction monitoring and reimbursement."
 Any creditor or litigation funder assessing exposure in the BTM sub-sector should factor that structural thesis into valuation of remaining operators' claims.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and counsel with open positions across these two cases should move on parallel tracks before July 8 and July 13. In *BlockFills* (Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del.), the voting and objection periods closed July 1; any party that believes the plan's customer-fund treatment — which proceeds from the estate's contested § 541(a)(1) property-of-the-estate characterization — is inadequate should confirm with Delaware local counsel whether a late-filed confirmation objection is available or whether a post-confirmation appeal under § 1144 is the correct vehicle; the July 13 confirmation hearing before Judge Horan is ten days away. Creditors who have not yet filed proofs of claim against the BlockFills estate should treat the September 11 bar date as a hard deadline and should also evaluate whether the Keyrock $3.25 million purchase price generates sufficient distributable value under the plan waterfall given the $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations identified in the first-day record. In *Bitcoin Depot* (Bankr. S.D. Tex., Judge Lopez), the July 8 sale hearing is the next gating event; interested parties should review the Notice of Winning Bidders filed by the Debtors and assess whether any objections to sale approval remain viable; the general bar date of July 21 at 5:00 p.m. CT is non-negotiable for any creditor wishing to participate in the liquidating plan distribution, and the combined confirmation hearing on August 7 provides the outer limit for Silverview-lien challenges before the non-debtor challenge period running to approximately August 2 forecloses those claims.
