### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — KEY FOCUS: Confirmation Track Live


Four Reliz debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on March 15, 2026; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The docket now carries 537 entries as of July 23, with six new filings in the prior 72 hours — activity consistent with a case on final confirmation approach.

The structural tension here is the same one that animated Celsius and Voyager: are customer deposits estate property or do they ride in trust? 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Those Celsius liens — secured by assets that BlockFills itself received as a creditor in the Celsius estate — create a recursive lien priority dispute that remains unresolved and could affect what unsecured customers actually recover.


In the weeks immediately preceding the filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two customer lawsuits asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026), and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.).
 Both actions remain live alongside the bankruptcy; their resolution — whether stayed pending confirmation or settled — will directly shape distributions to the general unsecured class.


The UCC membership includes SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Karol Przybytkowski, Jeffrey Brandt, Tod Skarecky, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
FTI Consulting was retained as financial advisor to the Committee effective April 1, 2026.
 
On April 7, 2026, the debtors filed the Joint Chapter 11 Plan at Docket No. 128, along with the accompanying Disclosure Statement at Docket No. 129.
 
The confirmation hearing is now scheduled for September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 — the key near-term milestone for all creditor-class recovery calculations. The docket's six new July 23 entries (Nos. 533–537) are not yet described in the public feed; counsel tracking this case should pull them immediately from CourtListener docket 72494188 and from the Veritaglobal claims portal to identify any plan amendment, objection, or discovery dispute that could push that date.

### Poolin Technology — New Chapter 11 Filing, D.N.J. (July 22, 2026)

This is the most consequential new crypto-insolvency filing of the week. 
Singapore-based Poolin Technology, along with U.S. subsidiaries Lonestar Dream Inc. and Lonestar Taproot LLC, submitted voluntary bankruptcy petitions on July 22 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey.
 The judge and docket number have not yet been publicly confirmed in the sources reviewed this run; counsel should verify on PACER before citing.

The claims picture is stark. 
A declaration by Chief Restructuring Officer Michael DuFrayne specifies total prepetition obligations of approximately $173.1 million, of which roughly $163.7 million consists of unsecured IOUs provided to Poolin Wallet users following the suspension of withdrawals during the 2022 crypto market decline.
 
Court documents show between 10,001 and 25,000 creditors, with liabilities estimated between $100 million and $500 million, while assets are valued at just $1 million to $10 million.


The 11 U.S.C. § 541 property-of-estate question lurks here just as it did in Celsius and Voyager. Poolin Wallet users were issued IOUs — not held in segregated custody — following the 2022 halt. That structure almost certainly places them as general unsecured creditors rather than trust beneficiaries, which matters because the only material recovery pool is the Texas real estate auction.


A $52 million stalking-horse bid from Thor CALAP LLC sets the auction floor.
 
The bankruptcy aims for a court-supervised sale of assets including two Texas sites, targeting buyers interested in AI and high-performance computing infrastructure.
 
The $52 million in signed offers covers the Texas mining assets, not wallet cash, and equals just 31.8% of the IOUs before costs.
 
With only $1.2 million in remaining cash and no active operations since July 10, 2026, when the company ceased mining and hosting at both Texas sites, the estate has little buffer beyond the auction proceeds.



The debtors say they contacted more than 335 strategic, financial, and hybrid prospects, including crypto miners and AI or high-performance computing data-center operators.
 The AI/HPC pivot thesis for power-rich Texas land is real — that demand pool could push the auction above the stalking-horse floor. But 
the offers cover assets held by Lonestar affiliates rather than cash in Poolin Technology's wallet business
, raising estate-boundary issues: whether proceeds flow through cleanly to satisfy Technology's wallet creditors depends on intercompany claim treatment and substantive consolidation arguments — analysis the CRO's first-day declaration will address but which UCC counsel must scrutinize closely.


In September 2022, the company declared a liquidity crisis and halted withdrawals, replacing customer deposits with IOUs
 — a fact pattern that mirrors Celsius's contested Terms of Service litigation nearly point-for-point. The IOU-for-deposit exchange is the single most important document for recovery counsel; its precise language will determine whether any trust or bailment argument survives a § 541 motion.

### MVMT Labs (Movement Labs) — Subchapter V, D. Del. (Filed July 15)


MVMT Labs, Inc., the Delaware corporation historically associated with the Movement blockchain, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The petition was filed under Subchapter V, a streamlined reorganization process for qualifying small businesses.


The asset/liability spread is remarkable for a once-hyped project. 
A company that was reportedly on the cusp of a $3 billion valuation in early 2025 now lists estimated assets of just $100,001 to $500,000, against liabilities of between $1 million and $10 million.
 
The startup raised a $38 million Series A led by Polychain Capital in April 2024, following a $3.4 million pre-seed round; the company later pursued a roughly $100 million Series B led by CoinFund and backed by Brevan Howard's digital assets arm, at a reported $3 billion valuation.
 That equity has been entirely destroyed.

The triggering event is the MOVE token dump. 
Binance later said the market maker rapidly sold 66 million MOVE tokens, or about 5% of the total supply, contributing to a sharp decline in the token's price.
 
Coinbase also suspended MOVE trading after saying the token no longer met its listing standards.
 
MVMT Labs CEO Rushikesh Manche, who holds a 34.25% equity stake, has the largest unsecured claim at over $1.6 million.
 A co-founder holding the largest creditor claim in a Subchapter V proceeding is an unusual and adversarially significant posture — UCC counsel and any remaining token holders should examine whether Manche's claim is subject to equitable subordination under § 510(c) given his role in the token-distribution arrangement.


The court approved interim requests allowing Movement Labs to maintain its bank accounts and cash management systems and obtain debtor-in-possession financing to fund operations during the bankruptcy process.
 
Creditors have until September 14, 2026 to file claims, and the court expects a restructuring plan by October 13, 2026.
 The entity distinction matters: 
Move Industries, which assumed full control of the development and operational management of the Movement ecosystem in December 2025, continues to operate normally and is unaffected by the filing.
 That separation — and whether the December 2025 assumption of operational control was a fraudulent transfer or value-stripping transaction — is the central question for any creditor challenging the entity line.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Opens July 31


FTX Trading Ltd. and the FTX Recovery Trust announced that, consistent with FTX's Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization, distributions will commence on July 31, 2026 to holders of allowed claims in the Plan's Convenience and Non-Convenience Classes that completed pre-distribution requirements by the record date of June 16, 2026 (the "Fifth Distribution").



FTX will start distributing the fifth payout of roughly $900 million to creditors beginning July 31, 2026; the bankruptcy estate has distributed nearly $10 billion to creditors and claimants since repayments began in 2025.
 
Eligible creditors should expect to receive funds from their selected distribution service provider — either BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer — within one to three business days from July 31, 2026.



The FTX estate has applied for court approval to shrink the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, which would bring the total reserve down from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, releasing funds that can flow to creditors instead of sitting in legal limbo.
 For claim-trading desks, the July 31 payment date is the effective settlement anchor: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims as of future record dates, where the 21-day notice period has lapsed without objection.
 Any claim transfer notice filed after June 16 will miss this distribution and queue for the sixth round.

## Recommended Actions

Counsel and claim-recovery advisors tracking this complex should act on three fronts before close of business today. First, on BlockFills: pull CourtListener docket entries 533–537 from Case No. 26-10371-TMH (Bankr. D. Del.) immediately — six new July 23 filings in a case approaching a September 11, 2026 confirmation hearing almost certainly include a plan amendment, objection deadline notice, or voting-procedures order, each of which resets the creditor action calendar. Second, on Poolin: the IOU-for-deposit documentation is the single most important document for recovery analysis — the operative Poolin Wallet terms of service and the September 2022 IOU exchange mechanics must be reviewed against the § 541 property-of-estate framework before any recovery estimate is credible; the 31.8% gross auction-to-IOU ratio is a ceiling, not a floor, once inter-affiliate transfer claims, CRO fees, and estate costs are deducted. Third, on MVMT Labs: file a proof of claim by September 14, 2026 in the Bankr. D. Del. Subchapter V proceeding and immediately assess whether Rushi Manche's $1.6 million co-founder claim is subject to equitable subordination under 11 U.S.C. § 510(c) — any token holder with a colorable securities-fraud theory should preserve that ground before the October 13 plan deadline.
