# 💰 $1B+ Class Actions & Mass Arb | Friday, June 12, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**Today's Deltas:** Two high-value developments broke within the past 72 hours that demand immediate attention from claimants, recovery counsel, and GCs tracking the mega-litigation landscape. First, U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan in Brooklyn granted preliminary approval Tuesday, June 9, to Visa's and Mastercard's revised $38 billion settlement with merchants who accused the card networks of charging too much to process payments on their credit cards — ruling nearly two years after a different judge rejected a proposed $30 billion settlement as too small. Second, a group of farmers have asked a North Carolina federal judge to preliminarily approve an $85 million settlement with Corteva Inc. to resolve antitrust claims that the company used loyalty rebate programs to artificially extend their patent monopolies over certain pesticides — the motion filed June 11 pursuant to mediation milestones reached in March and May 2026. Both matters are discussed in full below. On the mass-arbitration front, the Keller Postman / Google advertiser campaign — announced May 11, 2026 and targeting up to $218 billion in trebled antitrust damages — continues to accrue claimants and awaits a remedies ruling from Judge Leonie Brinkema in the ad-tech case that will materially shape the damages theory. And in the AI copyright corridor, *Bartz v. Anthropic*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), remains under submission before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín following the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing — making it the single highest-stakes pending approval in this advisory's coverage universe.

### I. Visa / Mastercard Interchange MDL — $38 Billion Preliminary Approval (E.D.N.Y.)

**In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation** — the case that has been running since 2005 — cleared a critical procedural gate this week. Judge Cogan said the settlement covering more than 12 million merchants was "fair, reasonable, and adequate," and that he was likely to eventually grant final approval. The revised deal, announced in November 2025, supplants the prior $30 billion accord that was rejected in June 2024. In rejecting that accord, U.S. District Judge Margo Brodie said fees would have still been above where they were absent any antitrust violations, and merchants would remain stuck with the "Honor All Cards" rule. The revised structure addresses Brodie's core objections. Two experts hired by the plaintiffs, Nobel Prize-winning economist Joseph Stiglitz and University of Washington professor Keith Leffler, said the changes could save merchants $38 billion by 2031 and provide $224 billion of benefits overall, including to consumers.

Preliminary approval is not final approval. The court will now notify class members, solicit objections, and eventually hold a fairness hearing before deciding whether to grant final approval. Several powerful objectors remain in the field and have signaled they will press further challenges: some groups including the National Retail Federation, the world's largest retail trade group, also opposed the new settlement and plan further challenges. The NRF's core position is structural — the NRF and allied groups contend that even $38 billion doesn't go far enough to reform how Visa and Mastercard set fees in the first place, arguing the settlement treats symptoms rather than the underlying disease: a duopoly that controls the vast majority of card-based payment processing in the U.S.

For merchant class members, the clock is now running on notice and objection periods. Recovery counsel should calendar the fairness hearing once the court sets the date, monitor the NRF's anticipated formal objection for arguments that could reduce or restructure the fund, and assess whether institutional retail clients that opted out of prior settlement rounds preserved claims that can now be re-evaluated in light of the $38 billion structure. The Electronic Payments Coalition, which advocates for credit unions and community banks, characterized the deal as a positive outcome: the EPC said the agreement between Visa, Mastercard and merchants guarantees lower processing rates and other concessions that amount to more than $200 billion over eight years.

### II. Crop Pesticide Antitrust MDL — Corteva $85 Million Settlement Motion Filed (M.D.N.C. / No. 22-cv-828)

**FTC et al. v. Syngenta Crop Protection AG et al.**, No. 22-cv-828-TDS-JEP (M.D.N.C.), before Judge Thomas D. Schroeder, is the government-side action. The parallel farmer class action is consolidated in the same district. The settlement resolves antitrust claims that Corteva used loyalty rebate programs to artificially extend their patent monopolies over certain pesticides. Structurally, the loyalty program scheme worked as follows: the loyalty programs include making payments to distributors if the distributors keep their purchases of competing generic pesticides below a certain threshold. The FTC-sponsored enforcement action, joined by the attorneys general in California, Colorado, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Minnesota, Nebraska, Oregon, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin, alleged that the scheme allowed the defendants to inflate prices by approximately 20% over what a competitive market would have produced. (__[Agweek / Reuters, Sept. 29, 2022](https://www.agweek.com/news/policy/u-s-federal-trade-commission-sues-pesticide-makers-corteva-and-syngenta-alleging-price-scheme)__)

The path to this settlement was mediation-driven. Corteva took part in mediation conferences on March 10 and May 14, according to the report of a mediator filed in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of North Carolina on May 29; the mediator told the court a settlement had been reached only with Corteva. The preliminary approval motion for the $85 million class settlement was filed June 11, 2026. (__[Law360, June 11, 2026](https://www.law360.com/classaction/articles/2488510)__) Critically, the deal does not impact the farmers' claims against Syngenta AG over a similar rebate program — meaning Syngenta remains a live defendant heading toward the government's preferred October 2026 trial date. A large group of farmers sued Corteva, along with CHS Inc., Nutrien, BASF, Syngenta and others beginning in 2022, alleging the farmers suffered financial losses from the companies paying distributors to block competitors from selling less-expensive generic crop protection products.

For farmer-plaintiffs and their counsel: the Corteva settlement carve-out does not resolve Syngenta exposure; claimants should monitor the preliminary approval briefing for opt-out deadlines, review whether their crop-protection purchase histories covering the relevant period satisfy the class definition, and assess whether the $85 million fund is proportionate to the estimated overcharge (the FTC pegged annual overcharges at hundreds of millions of dollars annually at ~20% above competitive pricing). The MDL docket is the proper source for class notice scheduling.

### III. Bartz v. Anthropic, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.) — Under Submission After May 14 Fairness Hearing

The $1.5 billion Anthropic copyright settlement — the largest in U.S. copyright history — awaits final approval from Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín. Per the tracked-case ground truth, the docket was last updated June 4, 2026 (Entry 674, an attorney withdrawal order). The fairness hearing in *Bartz v. Anthropic* took place Thursday, May 14, 2026, before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín in the Northern District of California, and addressed approval of the $1.5 billion class action settlement. The matter is now under submission; no ruling has issued as of this writing.

The settlement's financial architecture is structured in four installment tranches: Anthropic will pay $1.5 billion into a settlement fund, distributed in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027. Class counsel's fee request has been revised from the initial $300 million ask: authors urged the California federal judge to grant final approval to Anthropic's $1.5 billion settlement, along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections. Anthropic has separately contested nonclass counsel's $75 million fee request, arguing there is "scant justification" for it. (__[Law360](https://www.law360.com/cases/66c42a501562e5852bfc1794/articles)__)

The claims period has closed. The deadline to exclude yourself passed on February 9, 2026, and the claim filing deadline was March 30, 2026. Around 120,000 authors and rightsholders filed claims in the case — reflecting a 91.3% claims rate among identifiable class members, a figure noted on the eve of the fairness hearing. Payments could potentially begin as early as mid-2026 if the court signs off. The per-work floor under the settlement is $3,000, with an incremental $3,000 per work if affected works exceed 500,000. (__[allaboutlawyer.com, April 2026](https://allaboutlawyer.com/bartz-v-anthropic-1-5-billion-copyright-settlement-authors-claim/)__)

Key outstanding issue: Judge Martínez-Olguín has received multiple objections raising publisher-favoritism, inadequate individual compensation, and transparency concerns regarding the work-identification database. (__[Authors Alliance, April 14, 2026](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-opted-out-and-unsealed-objections/)__) The court's resolution of the competing fee requests and the substantive objections will shape the per-claimant recovery math materially. Rights-holders with filed claims should watch for the final approval order — expected within weeks — as it will trigger the processing of the second installment tranche already funded by Anthropic on April 30, 2026.

### IV. Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — $218 Billion Campaign Active

The largest mass-arbitration campaign ever attempted by dollar value is now in its active filing phase. In April 2026, Keller Postman's Ashley Keller began filing mass arbitration claims against Google/Alphabet on behalf of advertisers, seeking damages tied to two federal court rulings in 2024 that found Google illegally monopolized online search and advertising technology — potential claims are estimated at $218 billion or more.

The legal scaffolding rests on two confirmed judicial findings of liability. Following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic and anticompetitive conduct, Keller Postman is representing U.S. businesses in a coordinated mass arbitration seeking recovery for alleged advertising overcharges. The $218 billion figure is based on calculations from an economist Keller's firm has retained, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly — the two separate rulings that established Google's liability.

The procedural architecture is dictated by Google's contracts: Google's advertiser contracts require mandatory arbitration, blocking court access and funneling claims into exactly the kind of mass arbitration process that has cost companies billions in prior campaigns. Due to contractual arbitration provisions implemented by Google, these claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation; Keller Postman is coordinating thousands of individual claims on behalf of businesses nationwide, allowing each claimant to pursue recovery based on its own advertising spend.

The forward-looking catalyst for this campaign is Judge Brinkema's remedies opinion in the ad-tech case, which remains pending after the November 21, 2025 close of the remedies trial. A 2026 Judge Brinkema remedies ruling is expected, with Keller Postman actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines; both the DOJ and Google filed competing remedies proposals in November 2025, with the government seeking forced divestiture of Google's ad exchange (AdX), while Google proposed behavioral remedies only. The remedies outcome — divestiture vs. behavioral consent decree — will affect the damages calculus since it will establish the scope of the unlawful conduct period and the applicable overcharge metric.

Recovery timeline: similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between the filing of claims and resolution. Eligible businesses may recover up to triple the amount of their actual damages; Keller Postman estimates most qualifying businesses could recover up to 30% of their total Google Ads spend during the relevant period. The eligible period covers Google advertising purchases from August 2016 onward. (__[Keller Postman, May 11, 2026](https://www.kellerpostman.com/keller-postman-represents-thousands-of-u-s-businesses-in-mass-arbitration-seeking-billions-in-google-ad-overcharge-recovery/)__)

Google's public litigation posture is categorical denial. Google spokesperson Christa Muldoon said: "These claims are unfounded. In a highly competitive market, advertisers choose our tools to grow their businesses and reach new customers." The company's SEC filings acknowledge the private claims without quantifying exposure. (__[Bloomberg, April 13, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-13/google-faces-mass-arbitration-by-advertisers-seeking-billions)__)

### V. Ongoing Stale-But-Active Watch: American Express, BCBS, Roundup, Purdue

**Roundup MDL (In re Roundup Products Liability Litigation, No. 3:16-md-02741, N.D. Cal., Hon. Vince Chhabria)** — Bayer continues to manage the long tail of resolved and unresolved Roundup cancer claims. The global resolution program has addressed the bulk of filed claims, but the opt-out and future-claimant trust mechanics continue to generate docket activity. GCs monitoring corporate exposure should track any recurrence of trial-track activations, which Bayer has sought to minimize through the trust structure.

**Purdue Pharma / Sackler Opioid Litigation** — Following the Supreme Court's June 2024 ruling in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma* (No. 23-124) rejecting the nonconsensual third-party Sackler release, the restructuring plan has been remanded and renegotiated. Claimants in state opioid abatement funds and individual creditor trusts should monitor the reconstituted plan's trust-distribution mechanics, as any modifications to the Sackler contribution amounts will flow directly to per-creditor recovery percentages.

**Visa/MA Ongoing Fee Battles** — Even with preliminary approval of the $38 billion class settlement in hand, a class-action suit filed by more than 12 million merchants in 2005 accused Visa and Mastercard of charging excessive processing fees; in 2025, the credit card networks charged merchants almost $119 billion in swipe fees. The NRF's structural objections preview a continued friction track regardless of settlement finalization.
