**The controlling delta this week is the BlockFills / Reliz sale hearing.** The June 16 courtroom proceeding before Judge Horan was the case's most consequential event since the petition date: a confirmed successful bidder, a $3.25 million purchase price against $100–$500 million in stated liabilities, and a hard confirmation target of July 13. ### BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group — §363 Sale Heard June 16; Confirmation Targeted July 13


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Reliz debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The capital structure is acutely distressed. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The path to this filing was a cascade of counterparty failures. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius; stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials compounded those pressures.
 
As of the petition date, BlockFills employed 14 full-time salaried employees and one independent contractor; headcount reflected a reduction in force conducted on March 6, 2026 — nine days before the filing.


The §363 sale process was contested and repeatedly rescheduled. 
The docket reflects a Notice of Cancellation of Auction, a Notice of Further Rescheduled Auction, and a Notice of Rescheduled Auction before the sale hearing was fixed for May 28 and ultimately June 16, 2026.
 
Keyrock SA was declared the successful bidder in a court document filed May 26, 2026; per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.



Reliz is now operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with the sale hearing set for June 16, 2026 and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 The sale hearing was held as scheduled; whether a sale order issued has not been confirmed in available public sources as of this writing — the docket shows eight new entries between June 18 and June 19 (Dkt. Nos. 442–445 and related entries, per the live Veritaglobal feed), but descriptions are not yet reflected in the publicly available record. Creditors should assume the sale is either approved or under judicial advisement, and that the July 13 confirmation proceeding remains on the calendar either for a plan layered on top of the sale or as a standalone path.

**Customer-property exposure.** 
In the weeks immediately preceding the filing, certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill.).
 These pre-petition customer actions closely track the misappropriation theories that drove Celsius's adversary-proceeding litigation. The central question — whether customer crypto deposits held in BlockFills' omnibus accounts were property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or subject to a constructive trust — is not resolved by the §363 sale and will not be resolved at confirmation unless the plan explicitly addresses customer claims and their priority tier.

**Adversary proceeding: Artha Investment Partners.** 
A related adversary proceeding — *Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. v. Artha Investment Partners LLC*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH) — was filed April 9, 2026, and a hearing in that matter is set for June 22, 2026.
 That adversary closes the current week as the next active docket event; the June 22 status conference will indicate whether the matter is proceeding to discovery or toward settlement.

**Recovery arithmetic.** Against $145 million in stated unsecured exposure, a $3.25 million all-asset purchase price implies a recovery of roughly 2.2 cents on the dollar from sale proceeds alone — a structural deficiency that makes plan litigation over claim classification, customer-property treatment, and fraudulent-transfer avoidance actions the operative recovery levers for creditors. The estate's ability to bring § 548 and § 550 avoidance actions against insiders will depend on whether confirmation is achieved before the two-year petition-date anniversary in March 2028.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Wave; Record Date Passed June 16


FTX Trading Ltd. (d/b/a FTX.com) and the FTX Recovery Trust announced that the record date for the next distribution is June 16, 2026, for holders of allowed FTX claims and interests; the next distribution is expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
The next payments to eligible preferred equity holders will also be made on July 31, 2026 on the same June 16 record date; additionally, holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims will be eligible to begin the NFT Distribution process on June 30, 2026.



FTX also filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release cash to be distributed to holders of allowed claims in the next distribution.
 This $600 million reserve reduction is the mechanical lever for the July 31 payment. The fourth distribution, completed March 31, 2026, illustrated what the fifth wave builds on: 
the FTX Recovery Trust distributed approximately $2.2 billion to creditors on March 31, and the latest distribution lifts recovery rates so that many customer claim classes, including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B, reach 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent.



Creditors must complete KYC verification, submit required tax forms, and onboard with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer to receive a distribution; for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 The June 16 record date is now passed. Any transferee who failed to meet that deadline is cut off from the July 31 wave and must wait for the next distribution cycle.

**Genesis Digital Assets clawback.** The FTX estate's approximately $1 billion clawback suit against Genesis Digital Assets (Adv. Pro. No. 24-50222, Bankr. D. Del.) remained active through Q1 2026. 
Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion.

### Genesis Global / DCG — Post-Distribution Litigation Posture

Genesis Global Capital's Chapter 11 plan was confirmed by Judge Sean Lane in the S.D.N.Y. in May 2024, and the effective date distribution of approximately $4 billion has already been completed. The current posture is post-distribution litigation. 
As part of Genesis' bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action against various third parties, including parent company DCG; the fund comprises $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.


The Wind-Down Oversight Committee's docket reflects continued adversary activity: 
a pretrial conference in the pending proceeding was noticed for a hearing on June 9, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 Active adversary proceedings include *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230; *Digital Currency Group v. Genesis Global Capital*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129; *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; and *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028. (__[Kroll Restructuring / Genesis docket page](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__)

The framing that mattered most at the plan confirmation stage — and that carries forward into the litigation fund's work — was the court's rejection of DCG's petition-date valuation argument. 
Breaking new ground, the confirmation decision allows creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to the value as of the petition date; Genesis is authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 
The Bankruptcy Court ruled that DCG lacked standing to object to the plan because it could not recover on account of its equity interests, which were junior to tens of billions of dollars in allowed claims including at least $11 billion in governmental claims; even if the court had limited recoveries to petition-date values, DCG would not have been entitled to anything for its equity.


This in-kind / current-value precedent sits in direct tension with the FTX plan's petition-date cash-valuation methodology and with the treatment BlockFills' general unsecured creditors may receive. It is not binding across jurisdictions, but every institutional creditor in any open crypto-insolvency case should be pressing the in-kind argument as an alternative to petition-date fiat recovery.

### Cross-Docket Pattern: The §541 Customer-Property Divide

The BlockFills customer litigation, the Celsius TOS litigation (now largely resolved through the Fahrenheit plan), and the Genesis/Gemini Earn proceeding all collided on the same statutory question: whether customer-deposited crypto constitutes property of the debtor estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or is held in constructive trust or bailment. The answer turns entirely on terms of service. Celsius's "general terms" language — providing that title to assets transferred to Celsius — placed those assets inside the estate. BlockFills' pre-petition customer actions assert the opposite: that assets were held on behalf of clients and never became debtor property. 
BlockFills has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy to pursue a customer-led reorganization after a liquidity crisis triggered by the February 2026 crypto market crash, compounding counterparty losses, a failed mining venture, and lawsuits alleging misappropriation of customer assets.
 Whether those misappropriation allegations survive an adversary proceeding — or are subsumed into a plan waterfall that treats customers as general unsecured creditors — is the creditor-rights question of the case.
