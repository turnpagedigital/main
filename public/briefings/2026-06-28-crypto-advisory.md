**The Customer-Property Issue: The Central Unresolved Question**

The threshold issue that will govern creditor recovery — and that has direct precedential relevance across the crypto-insolvency cycle — is whether customer digital assets deposited with BlockFills constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in trust or bailment outside the estate. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
The court noted that no evidence before it established the funds as anything other than estate property, but preserved the issue for further hearing.


That concession — commingling plus a debtor position that the assets are estate property — is structurally identical to the posture that produced the Celsius litigation, where Judge Martin Glenn in the Bankr. S.D.N.Y. ultimately concluded that yield-bearing account holders had transferred property of the estate under Celsius's Terms of Service. BlockFills' TOS analysis has not yet been adjudicated. If Judge Horan follows Celsius, customer claims in the general unsecured pool will compete pro rata against trade creditors; if a constructive-trust or bailment theory survives, certain identified customer assets could be distributed outside the § 541 estate. Given the $145 million unsecured pool against a $3.25 million sale price plus whatever residual unencumbered assets exist, the distinction is outcome-determinative for most claimants.

Two prepetition customer lawsuits further color the estate: 
Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd. (Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, S.D.N.Y., filed February 27, 2026) and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. (Case No. 26-cv-02451, N.D. Ill., filed March 5, 2026), with plaintiffs seeking emergency injunctive relief — and courts granting temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business.
 
Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO on March 3, 2026, freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 Both actions are stayed by the Chapter 11 filing; the underlying fraud and misappropriation allegations will inform any adversary proceedings or § 548 fraudulent-transfer claims the Committee pursues post-confirmation.

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors was appointed March 27, 2026 (Docket No. 104). 
Committee members include SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
The Committee is represented by Morris James LLP.

named Artha Investment Partners LLC as defendant

**Genesis Global Holdco — Wind-Down Phase; Four Distributions Complete; DCG Litigation Cluster Active**

Genesis Global Holdco (Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean Lane) reached its plan effective date on August 2, 2024. 
From the effective date through the present, the Wind-Down Debtors have made four distributions to creditors pursuant to the plan.
 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors, in addition to approximately $2.2 billion distributed to Gemini Trust Company LLC earn users between May and June 2024.


The distinguishing structural feature of the Genesis plan — in-kind coin-for-coin distribution rather than petition-date dollar caps — remains directly relevant as interpretive backdrop for cases like BlockFills where the same § 541 and § 1129 valuation disputes will arise. 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value; instead, it sought to provide restitution through in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.


The active litigation layer is now the DCG/Genesis adversary cluster. The Kroll claims-administration site identifies the following open adversary proceedings:

No. 25-01129; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028; and Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc., Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.
 The litigation oversight committee, represented by Selendy Gay PLLC, is actively managing this cluster. The 
$70 million litigation fund established under the plan — comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD — is the war chest funding these actions.


**Cross-Docket Pattern Recognition: The § 541 Customer-Property Question in 2026**

The structural tension running across BlockFills, and carrying forward from Celsius and Genesis, is the same: did the platform's terms of service create a debtor-creditor relationship (estate property under § 541) or a trust/bailment (asset outside the estate)? Celsius resolved this against customers in the Earn product context. Genesis resolved it in favor of in-kind restitution through a negotiated plan rather than judicial determination. BlockFills has now reached the confirmation-hearing phase without judicial resolution of the property question — the Debtors' concession at the first day hearing that assets are commingled and constitute estate property is the current operative posture, but that concession was made by the Debtors, not adjudicated by the court. Any customer who believes their assets were segregated or held in a separate account structure should file a targeted objection before the July 1 deadline, as that argument, if not preserved at confirmation, may be waived.
