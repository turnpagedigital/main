### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — § 363 Sale Cleared, Confirmation Clock Now Running

The most consequential development this cycle: Judge Thomas M. Horan of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware approved the $3.25 million § 363 sale of substantially all BlockFills assets to Brussels-based Keyrock SA at the hearing held yesterday, June 16, 2026. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge on Tuesday agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 The sale clears the single largest near-term execution risk in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (Bankr. D. Del.), and triggers the next phase: plan confirmation, currently targeted for July 13, 2026.

The transaction's scope is broad. 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
As of a court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA had been declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 Regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid remain outstanding and represent the residual closing condition.

The capital structure context matters for understanding what creditors actually face. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 That $145 million general unsecured pool against a $3.25 million sale price signals a deeply distressed recovery for unsecured creditors. The gap between consideration and liability quantum — roughly 44:1 — almost certainly means the plan will be a liquidating structure, not a reorganization. 
Reliz was operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.


The liquidity crisis that drove the filing had several identifiable triggers. 
The case began on March 15, 2026, when the BlockFills parent filed Chapter 11 after a liquidity crisis tied to counterparty exposures, the Babel Finance bankruptcy, a failed equipment-loan settlement with Nexo, an unpaid Celsius arbitration award, customer litigation and TROs, and a February 2026 crypto-market decline that forced the debtors to suspend deposits and withdrawals, as described in the first-day Renzi declaration, Dkt. 3.
 On the fraud-allegation side, pre-petition litigation compounded the structural problems: 
Dominion Capital's February 27, 2026 lawsuit alleged misappropriation of funds and concealing losses, charging that BlockFills "admitted to having commingled customer assets and having a balance sheet shortfall" in a February 7, 2026 call.


Named creditor exposures in the unsecured pool are substantial and concentrated. 
Unsecured creditors have $145 million at stake; those claims include customer liabilities to Coinmint Co-founder Ashton Soniat ($5.65 million), Bitfarms (NASDAQ: BITF) subsidiary Backbone Hosting Solutions ($4.22 million), and Simple Mining ($3.73 million).
 Institutional clientele — 
BlockFills provided institutional clients with liquidity, financing and risk-management services, including crypto lending and borrowing, derivatives trading, and OTC execution; its customer base includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers and mining companies
 — means the creditor group is sophisticated and likely actively trading claims. Counsel and creditors should monitor Dkt. 431 and 433 (both filed June 16, 2026) for sale-order entry and any objection dispositions. Claims administrators are Veritaglobal (KCC dba Verita), 222 N. Pacific Coast Highway, Suite 300, El Segundo, CA 90245; tel. (866) 554-5810.

The customer-property question that pervades this beat is present here but has not yet been litigated publicly. BlockFills operated primarily as an institutional platform — not a retail deposit-taking exchange — so the Celsius-style § 541 property-of-the-estate vs. bailment/trust contest may be less acute. That said, the commingling allegations in the Dominion lawsuit echo the same structural defects that made Celsius's TOS litigation so prolonged. Any claim-holders who tendered crypto assets to BlockFills under a structure that could be characterized as a custodial arrangement should assess that posture before the confirmation hearing, where plan treatment of customer-creditor claims will be fixed.

### FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets — $1.15B Clawback Dropped With Prejudice

A significant development in the FTX litigation estate. 
An FTX trust voluntarily dismissed its lawsuit against Bitcoin mining company Genesis Digital Assets Ltd., ditching its attempt to recover $1.15 billion in funds it said were fraudulently transferred; the trust dropped all claims with prejudice, according to a Monday filing in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The dismissal is dated June 15, 2026. The case caption is *FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets Ltd.*, Bankr. D. Del., No. 1:25-ap-52358.

The theory of the case, now abandoned, was specific. 
The lawsuit alleged Genesis Digital, along with its affiliates and co-founders, received commingled and misappropriated funds from FTX co-founder Sam Bankman-Fried between August 2021 and April 2022.
 More precisely, 
the trust alleged that Bankman-Fried used his personal hedge fund, Alameda Research LLC, to purchase several tranches of shares in the Bitcoin mining company at heavily inflated prices.
 According to earlier filings, 
between 2021 and 2022, Alameda spent over $500 million on 154 preferred shares of GDA and wired another $550.9 million directly to GDA's co-founders, Rashit Makhat and Marco Krohn.
 
Genesis Digital, a Cyprus-based company headquartered in Dubai, had moved to dismiss the lawsuit in January.


A with-prejudice dismissal — as opposed to without prejudice — bars re-filing. The trust's counsel, Sullivan & Cromwell LLP and Landis Rath & Cobb LLP, have offered no public explanation. 
Counsel for the FTX trust didn't immediately respond to a request for comment about why it dropped the suit.
 
Genesis Digital is represented by Latham & Watkins LLP and Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor LLP.


The practical consequence for FTX creditors is a reduction in the potential recovery pool by up to $1.15 billion — though that figure assumed full success on the merits, which GDA's January motion to dismiss had contested vigorously. From a creditor-recovery standpoint, the better data point is the distribution trajectory: 
FTX Recovery Trust has been clawing back investments to refund creditors; creditors received roughly $6 billion between February and May of this year, with another $1.6 billion scheduled for disbursement this month.
 The voluntary dismissal of the Genesis Digital adversary does not disrupt those distribution mechanics. It does signal, however, that the trust is making litigation-by-litigation cost-benefit assessments — possibly reflecting settlement dynamics not publicly disclosed, or a judgment that GDA's motion to dismiss had enough merit to warrant an exit before adjudication.

### Celsius Preference Litigation — 174 Customer Defendants Confined to Bankruptcy Court After SDNY Venue Denial

A ruling from December 2025 surfaced this week in academic commentary and law firm client alerts, and it carries forward-looking procedural weight. 
The Litigation Administrator appointed under Celsius' reorganization plan has pursued preference claims against former Celsius Network customers who withdrew digital assets shortly before Celsius' July 2022 bankruptcy filing; a group of 174 former Celsius customers sought to withdraw the reference of bankruptcy avoidance proceedings from the Bankruptcy Court to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York; on December 9, 2025, District Court Judge John G. Koeltl denied the motion.


Judge Koeltl's reasoning covers both mandatory and permissive withdrawal grounds. 
On mandatory withdrawal, the court found the motion untimely and concluded that it appeared to constitute tactical forum shopping; the District Court also rejected the novel argument that determining whether Celsius' customer rewards program was a security required significant interpretation of non-bankruptcy law.
 
On permissive withdrawal, the District Court emphasized that entitlement to a jury trial does not automatically warrant withdrawal and allowed the Bankruptcy Court to continue managing pre-trial proceedings, with the note that defendants can renew their motion when those claims are ready for trial.


The procedural significance is cross-docket. 
The preference claims at issue involve former Celsius Network customers who withdrew digital assets during the ninety days preceding Celsius' July 2022 bankruptcy filing.
 Judge Koeltl's rejection of the securities-law mandatory-withdrawal argument — specifically, the claim that the rewards program's security status required non-bankruptcy-law interpretation — forecloses the most creative procedural off-ramp available to preference defendants in crypto cases. It also keeps 174 defendants under the Bankruptcy Court's pre-trial management, where discovery pace and settlement pressure tend to favor the estate. Any party holding similar preference exposure in a post-2022 crypto case (BlockFills, Voyager, Genesis) should treat the SDNY ruling as highly persuasive authority against the same mandatory-withdrawal maneuver.

### Background: Cross-Docket Pattern Recognition

The three developments above read as a coherent pattern. At the § 363 end (BlockFills), consideration recovery is so compressed — $3.25M against ~$145M in GUC exposure — that unsecured creditors face sub-5 cent recoveries absent fraudulent-transfer claims that could augment the estate. At the avoidance-action end (Celsius), the estate-side Litigation Administrator now has a clean forum with no viable mandatory-withdrawal escape hatch, increasing leverage over the 174 defendants. And the FTX trust's withdrawal of the GDA suit with prejudice — silent as to rationale — illustrates the trust's willingness to cut losses on litigation that faced procedural headwinds, while the $6B+ distribution already made to creditors is the more material measure of recovery performance.

The central § 541 question — customer crypto deposits as property of the estate vs. trust/bailment — remains structurally unresolved across the post-2022 wave. The SDNY's December 2025 ruling in *Celsius* explicitly declined to elevate the customer-rewards-as-security argument to mandatory withdrawal status, which means that question stays embedded in bankruptcy court proceedings rather than escalating to district court, at least for now. That framing matters for all pending cases where customers argue their deposits were held in trust rather than on the debtor's balance sheet.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and claimants holding BlockFills general unsecured claims (Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan) should treat the § 363 sale approval as a wake-up call: with the sale price locked at $3.25 million against a $145 million GUC pool, recovery at plan confirmation will be driven by whatever fraudulent-transfer or preference claims the estate can assert against insiders, and by the scope of assets excluded from the Keyrock sale — monitor Dkt. 431 and 433 (June 16, 2026) for the sale order's asset-exclusion schedule and any carve-outs from the § 363 transfer, because residual assets not transferred to Keyrock form the creditor pool; probe whether the Dominion Capital commingling allegations will generate adversary proceedings under 11 U.S.C. §§ 548–550 that could supplement estate value before the July 13 confirmation hearing. FTX creditors expecting September 2026 distributions should note the with-prejudice dismissal of *FTX Recovery Trust v. Genesis Digital Assets* (No. 1:25-ap-52358) does not affect the $1.6 billion disbursement currently scheduled this month, but does reduce the outer bound of potential creditor recovery by up to $1.15 billion — if your claim thesis assumed GDA litigation proceeds, revise that model now. Any party in a post-2022 crypto preference proceeding contemplating a motion to withdraw the bankruptcy court reference to district court should read Judge Koeltl's December 9, 2025 ruling in the Celsius preference litigation carefully: the court rejected both mandatory withdrawal (untimeliness; no significant non-bankruptcy-law issue despite the securities characterization argument) and permissive withdrawal (jury trial right alone insufficient), and that analysis will be cited against you in every analogous motion in SDNY and, very likely, in Delaware and the Southern District of Texas.
