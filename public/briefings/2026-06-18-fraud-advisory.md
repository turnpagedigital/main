### OneCoin Remission — June 30 Deadline Is Live; Claims Window Is Imminent

The most time-sensitive matter in the desk's portfolio is the DOJ's remission process for OneCoin victims. 
Between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, co-founders of OneCoin Ltd., orchestrated a large international cryptocurrency investment scheme; OneCoin, based in Sofia, Bulgaria, marketed a fraudulent cryptocurrency through a global MLM network, and victims invested over $4 billion worldwide.
 
More than $40 million in forfeited assets are currently available for victim compensation.
 The recoverable pool is therefore approximately 1% of admitted losses — thin by any measure, but meaningfully priced for claims buyers who acquired positions at steep discounts before the remission window opened.


The deadline for filing a petition is June 30; the Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission compensation process.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator in this matter.
 Petitions are filed at www.onecoinremission.com. 
Through the remission process, victims who purchased the fraudulent OneCoin cryptocurrency between 2014 and 2019 may be eligible for compensation.


The fugitive principal remains at large. 
The FBI maintains its commitment to locating Ruja Ignatova, an FBI Top Ten Fugitive, alongside partners at IRS-CI and the Southern District of New York.
 Cross-border asset recovery has continued in parallel through non-conviction-based forfeiture mechanisms: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order for GBP 8,590,200.92, following international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany; the German proceedings found OneCoin was released with fraudulent intent, and because criminal proceedings against Ignatova could not conclude given her unknown whereabouts, the German authorities initiated independent confiscation proceedings, applying the order to the remaining proceeds of sale of two London flats owned by the associated companies.
 That Guernsey recovery flows through the German non-conviction-based forfeiture track, not through the DOJ's U.S. remission fund — distinct pools, distinct eligibility frameworks.

Claim-pricing note: with $40 million against a $4+ billion loss base, recovery per dollar claimed is structurally capped at roughly a cent before pro-rata allocation among successful petitioners. Secondary market positioning on this remission right is therefore speculative, though acquirers who assembled distressed OneCoin receivables at single-digit-cent prices retain a viable recovery spread. 
Greenwood pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 20 years in prison in September 2023, along with $300 million in restitution.
 That restitution order is a separate track from the remission process; enforcement against Greenwood's assets is ongoing but the restitution pool is not part of the www.onecoinremission.com distribution.

### Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution Rolling Forward; Swiss $8.1M Status Unresolved

The Stanford receivership (SEC v. Stanford Int'l Bank, Ltd., No. 3:09-cv-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Receiver Ralph Janvey) has made material progress under its court-approved Final Distribution Plan. 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver continues to issue additional payments to claimant groups pursuant to the court-approved Final Distribution Plan (as amended), and has issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups totaling more than $112 million.


The plan's total scope is substantially larger. 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule — which total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups — are being issued on a rolling basis.
 
The court approved an amendment to the Final Distribution Plan on November 25, 2025, under which the Receiver will be making payments totaling more than $339 million to over 14,000 claimant groups.
 The $112 million disbursed to date against the $339 million authorized total indicates that roughly 33% of the final distribution has been paid, with the remainder still in process. Rolling distributions cut both ways for secondary claim holders: receipt timing depends on where a specific claimant group sits in the Receiver's issuance sequence, not on overall plan progress.

Two operational matters are live. First, 
on May 12, 2026, the Court granted the Receiver's motion for authorization to dispose of certain Stanford records and equipment.
 Disposal of records is a late-stage receivership signal — it suggests the Receiver is moving toward wind-down rather than continued asset pursuit. Second, the status of $8.1 million in Swiss assets transferred to the Receiver in December 2025 remains publicly ambiguous. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded; the JLs transferred to the Receiver, pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol, $8.1 million from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding, and the JLs further informed the Receiver that no further distributions of funds are expected.
 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver on March 11, 2026 regarding the status of these $8.1 million Swiss funds; to date, the Receiver and his legal team have not responded; the request asked whether and when the Swiss assets would be distributed and whether they would be included in a future distribution separate from the current Final Distribution Plan.
 The 90-day check-cashing requirement is a particular trap for secondary holders who acquired claims from investors in Venezuela or other jurisdictions with irregular mail service. 
If a payee does not cash a check or request reissuance within 90 days from the date of issue, such payee shall not be eligible to receive any other payment under the Final Distribution Plan; all funds represented by void checks not subject to a timely reissuance request shall revert to the Receivership Estate.

### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Estate in Quiet Period

The Madoff estate (SIPC v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC, No. 08-01789-LGB, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) shows no material delta since the seventeenth interim distribution launched February 27, 2026. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced; the Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, aggregate payments to eligible BLMIS customers total nearly $15.38 billion, including approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances; the seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


The cumulative recovery number has climbed further since. 
As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion.
 The incremental $45 million between the February distribution announcement ($15.366 billion) and the May 26 figure suggests ongoing settlement activity that has not yet been publicly detailed as a named recovery. 
As of May 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 SIPC advances remain flat at the $850.9 million level, indicating the short-claim satisfaction pace has not accelerated. The practical consequence for claim investors: the estate is in an inter-distribution quiet period. The next distribution motion will require new settlements or recoveries to fund it; no timeline has been stated publicly. The fiftieth fee application — filed February 13, 2026 per the trustee's website — is a latent overhead marker; BakerHostetler's fees continue to run against SIPC rather than the Customer Fund under the SIPA structure, but the application is worth monitoring as a gauge of ongoing litigation posture.

### Power Block Coin (SmartFi) — Chapter 7 Conversion Trajectory; Judge Parker Presiding

Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) drew ECF No. 519 on June 9, 2026, the most recent filing per tracked-case ground truth, though the entry description is not available from public docket feeds as of this advisory. The case posture, however, is well-established from prior filings. 
Power Block Coin LLC, operating under the SmartFi brand, requested to convert its Chapter 11 reorganization case to a Chapter 7 liquidation, citing administrative insolvency and failed reorganization efforts in the wake of the 2022 cryptocurrency market collapse.
 
The case is being handled by Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah under Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP.


The claim-size dynamics are dominated by a single creditor. 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 
A key component of the creditors' committee's proposed liquidation plan involves appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage assets and pursue potential legal claims; the committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates.
 
A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah; the committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.


The Solara loan is the key recovery variable for non-Celsius general unsecured creditors. If the loan proves unenforceable or the underlying real property is fully encumbered, recoveries outside the Celsius preference track will be sharply compressed. The prior Subchapter V trustee, Joli A. Lofstedt, has been compensated on a final basis per ECF No. 315 (fees allowed at $8,925; expenses at $0) — a clean administrative close on the earlier trustee's compensation, consistent with the case's transition to the Chapter 7/converted posture under Judge Parker.

## Recommended Actions

**OneCoin — deadline in 12 days:** Any party holding OneCoin victim claims or having acquired such positions in the secondary market must file at www.onecoinremission.com by June 30, 2026 or forfeit entitlement to the $40 million DOJ forfeiture pool; the petition is filed directly with Kroll Settlement Administration LLC, administered by DOJ's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section — confirm the purchased claim's original purchase date falls between 2014 and 2019 before filing. **Stanford receivership — check monitoring:** Secondary holders of Stanford CD claims should actively confirm whether their payment has been issued under the rolling Final Distribution Plan; the 90-day check-cashing window runs from issuance date (not receipt date), and any check not cashed or reissued within that window reverts to the receivership estate permanently — there is no second opportunity. Claimants should also formally press the Receiver regarding the $8.1 million Swiss tranche, which was transferred to the estate in December 2025 but has not been publicly allocated. **Madoff — no action required in the near term:** The estate is in a quiet period post-seventeenth distribution; the next distribution depends on new settlements and is not calendared; secondary buyers should price the time-value of the remaining ~27% of allowed claims against a realistic 12-to-24-month wait for the eighteenth distribution motion. **Power Block Coin — Chapter 7 watch:** Monitor ECF No. 519 (June 9, 2026) and subsequent entries for any Chapter 7 trustee appointment, which would trigger a new fiduciary with independent authority to pursue the Solara Communities loan and insider-transaction clawbacks under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and state UVTA; the Celsius preference claim exceeding $133 million structurally subordinates general unsecured recoveries unless the trustee can grow the estate through avoidance actions.
