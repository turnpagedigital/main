### Stanford Financial Receivership — N.D. Tex., Dallas Div. (Janvey) | Final Distribution Execution + Fee Appeal Fully Briefed

The most active near-term distribution story remains the Stanford receivership. 
On July 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey submitted a Status Report to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, concerning payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 That filing is the latest update in a rolling payment process that has been running since early April. 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver had issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups totaling more than $112 million.
 The full final distribution schedule, 
totaling more than $339 million and covering payments to over 14,000 claim groups, is being issued on a rolling basis; the process of issuing those payments was expected to take several weeks from the March 9 filing date.


The parallel fee appeal is the more structurally important proceeding for claims pricing. 
The district court entered an order on October 8, 2025, approving the Receiver's proposed final distribution, and among other matters the court awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million that was sought.
 
The Receiver and certain of his professional firms appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals, filing their brief on appeal on March 23, 2026.
 
FTI Consulting filed a separate brief in support of the appeal; the SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 
The court-appointed Examiner was granted leave to participate as amicus curiae and filed a brief supporting the lower court's order on June 9, 2026.
 
The Receiver and his professionals filed their Reply Brief on June 12, 2026; the appeal is now fully briefed.
 
It is not yet certain whether the Fifth Circuit will schedule oral argument, nor when such argument might be scheduled.


**Claims-pricing read:** The fee gap — $43 million sought, ~$14.9 million awarded — is meaningful. If the Fifth Circuit reinstates the larger figure, available distributable funds from the final plan's approximately $339 million pool compress proportionally. Monitor the scheduling order. There is also a residual tranche: 
on December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, with $8.1 million transferred to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol.
 
As of early April 2026, claimant advocates had sent a formal written request asking whether those Swiss funds would be included in a future distribution separate from the Final Distribution Plan, and the Receiver had not responded to that request.
 The July 9 status report may address that question; the substance of that filing has not been publicly confirmed in this run.

### Madoff / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, Judge Loretta G. Bianco) — Post-Seventeenth Distribution Posture


On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced commencement of the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid when combined with the prior sixteen distributions.


The estate is now in a post-distribution quiet period. 
As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
As of the same date, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation in advances.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The fee side remains a signal event to watch. 
On February 13, 2026, SIPA Trustee Picard and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, covering work from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025.
 
The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 10% public-interest discount continues to apply, per long-standing BLMIS practice. No objection deadline or hearing date for the 50th application has been confirmed in this run.

The remaining recovery upside lies in unresolved clawback litigation and offshore proceedings. Admitted claims stand at approximately $20.3 billion against recoveries of ~$15.456 billion, leaving a coverage gap of roughly $4.85 billion before full satisfaction — though the "75 cents on the dollar" figure cited by Picard's team reflects the net equity basis, not the broader admitted-claim universe. Claim-secondary-market pricing should be sensitive to any signal of an eighteenth distribution motion, which historically has followed 9–14 months after the prior distribution; on that cadence, a motion could emerge as early as Q4 2026.

### OneCoin / DOJ Remission — Petition Window Closed June 30, 2026


The DOJ announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin Ltd. international investment scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood orchestrated a large international cryptocurrency investment scheme defrauding investors from around the globe.
 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency as a result of misrepresentations.
 
The DOJ launched a remission process with over $40 million in forfeited assets available for distribution.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator.
 
The petition deadline was June 30, 2026.


The June 30 deadline has passed. The DOJ has not publicly announced the number of petitions received or the timeline for reviewing claims. The recovery rate is stark — over $40 million against $4 billion in investor losses is less than 1% of total harm. Recovery at this stage depends heavily on continued enforcement against fugitive and offshore assets.

On the asset-forfeiture side: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order for funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 That order followed cooperation between the German Regional Court in Bielefeld and Guernsey authorities, and represents a non-conviction-based forfeiture model that has broader implications for cross-border asset recovery in fugitive cases. 
Ignatova remains an FBI Top Ten Fugitive; law enforcement, including FBI and IRS-CI, continues to seek her location.
 Any future remission rounds will depend on additional forfeitures, making new seizure announcements the primary signal event for this matter.

### SEC Retail Fraud Working Group — July 7, 2026 | New Enforcement Architecture


On July 7, 2026, the SEC announced the creation of the Retail Fraud Working Group to strengthen the Division of Enforcement's efforts to identify and combat fraud targeting everyday investors.
 
The working group will leverage staff and resources across the Commission to identify fraud and other misconduct targeting retail investors, including offering frauds, pump-and-dump schemes, market manipulation, and breaches of duties to customers by investment advisers and broker dealers.
 
The group is led by Deputy Director Kate Zoladz (West) and Assistant Director Kim Frederick (Asset Management Unit).


This matters to the recovery desk on two levels. First, the working group is an institutional accelerator for new receiver appointments — each SEC-driven action against an offering fraud or Ponzi typically spawns an FRCP 66 receivership within weeks of a complaint, and those receiverships are the raw material of claim-acquisition opportunities. Second, it signals that the Atkins-era SEC's stated pivot away from crypto-regulation enforcement toward offering fraud is now operational, not merely rhetorical. 
In April 2026, David Woodcock took the reins as Director of Enforcement; in his first public remarks he outlined a traditional enforcement agenda focused on high-impact cases involving investor harm, market integrity, and core fraud priorities, with specific areas including offering fraud, accounting and disclosure fraud, market manipulation, insider trading, private funds, and cross-border fraud.


The prior fiscal year provides the pipeline context: 
the SEC's FY 2025 enforcement actions included a $400 million Ponzi scheme involving Paramount Management Group / Daryl F. Heller that allegedly defrauded approximately 2,700 investors, and a $140 million Ponzi scheme involving First Liberty Building & Loan / Edwin Brant Frost IV that defrauded approximately 300 investors.
 Each of those matters would now fall within the Retail Fraud Working Group's mandate.

## Recommended Actions

Claims investors and recovery counsel should treat the Stanford final distribution status report filed July 9 as a live document requiring review — the rolling payment process is ongoing, the 90-day check-cashing deadline is running from individual issuance dates, and the $8.1 million Swiss tranche remains in limbo pending a receiver response; confirm whether those funds are captured in the current plan or will constitute a separate distribution before pricing any residual Stanford positions. On the Fifth Circuit fee appeal, the fully briefed posture as of June 12 means a scheduling order for oral argument could arrive any time in Q3 2026 — a ruling restoring the ~$28 million in cut fees would reduce distributable funds in the final plan commensurately, so that risk should be modeled into NAV estimates. For OneCoin, the June 30 petition window has closed; the next actionable date is DOJ/Kroll's claims-review timeline, which has not been published, but advisable to monitor DOJ press releases at justice.gov for any supplemental forfeiture announcements that could fund a second remission round. On Madoff, the 50th BakerHostetler fee application is pending before Judge Bianco (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789); any objection or adverse ruling could signal a shift in fee structure for remaining recovery work and is worth tracking through PACER. The SEC's new Retail Fraud Working Group (July 7) is the most forward-looking signal: watch the Commission's press release feed for fresh receivership-predicate complaints, since those appointments are the earliest entry point for distressed-claim acquisition before secondary market pricing firms up.
