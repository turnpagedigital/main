# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Monday, July 20, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The dominant development this week is the close of the Keyrock/BlockFills sale.** Keyrock SA formally completed its acquisition of BlockFills' institutional trading and brokerage assets on July 16, 2026 — four months and one day after Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. filed chapter 11 in Wilmington. The sale closed at a purchase price that stands in stark relief against the liability mass it leaves behind, and the post-close posture of the estate now turns entirely to plan confirmation and the harder creditor-recovery work: reconciling approximately $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations against a $3.25 million asset sale. Meanwhile, the FTX Recovery Trust announced on July 17 its fifth distribution — $900 million commencing July 31 — pushing cumulative creditor recoveries above $10 billion and lifting most claim classes to or past 100% of allowed claim value. The Genesis/DCG adversary track continues churning, with a pre-trial conference scheduled for tomorrow, July 21.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. (Judge Thomas M. Horan)

The sale closed. 
Keyrock completed its acquisition of BlockFills' institutional digital asset trading and brokerage business on July 16, 2026.
 
Per the bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, assuming "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock had been named the successful bidder on May 26, 2026, and Judge Horan's court formally approved the sale on June 16, 2026.
 The regulatory piece remains technically open: 
the deal expands Keyrock's regulatory footprint into the Cayman Islands and the United Kingdom, with a CIMA-registered entity acquired outright and an FCA-authorised entity still awaiting FCA approval.


Set that $3.25 million sale price against the capital structure. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The gap between enterprise value realized and claims outstanding is not bridgeable by sale proceeds. What is left for unsecured customers flows through whatever the plan's liquidating trust can recover from residual estate assets, insider-claims investigation, and the adversary-proceeding track.

The commingling question — the structural issue that has shadowed this case from day one — remains unresolved at the plan level. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission tracks the § 541 property-of-estate framing that proved so costly for Celsius customers; here, unlike Celsius, the debtors volunteered it at the outset rather than litigating it into appellate posture. The practical effect: no tracing argument, no § 541(d) constructive-trust theory, no bailment defense. Customer claims are unsecured general creditors by the estate's own admission. Any creditor holding a claim who believes their assets were segregated should preserve that record now, before a plan confirmation order enters with broad claims-discharge language.

The commingling narrative was partly predicated on the pre-petition fraud allegations. 
Two customer lawsuits followed the withdrawal suspension, each alleging misappropriation of customer funds, commingling, and fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd. (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026), which alleged ongoing misappropriation and fraudulent concealment of customer assets, asserting that BlockFills had used pooled customer assets for mining operations and equipment purchases.
 
On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 Those pre-petition TROs were automatically stayed upon filing; the underlying factual record feeding them now belongs to the estate's Special Committee and the official Committee of Unsecured Creditors.

The UCC is active and well-resourced. 
The Committee members are SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 
FTI Consulting was retained as financial advisor to the Committee, effective April 1, 2026.
 FTI's presence signals the Committee intends to scrutinize asset values, insider transfers, and plan feasibility with professional rigor — a point that matters for any creditor deciding whether to sell into the claim-trading market at a discount or hold through confirmation.

The tracked docket shows one new filing dated July 17 (entry number not yet populated in the CourtListener sync as of July 19). That filing warrants direct inspection at the Veritaglobal case portal. The prior filing of record was Docket No. 530 (July 15). The case had been tracking toward a confirmation hearing originally targeted for July 13; that date has apparently slipped based on the continued docket activity. No confirmed new confirmation date has been verified as of this run.

The macro read on BlockFills: the business is gone, sold for a fraction of the liability stack. The residual estate is a liquidating trust vehicle pursuing insider liability, fraudulent-transfer claims, and claim reconciliation against approximately $145 million in unsecured obligations. For creditors holding claims, the practical question is whether incremental investigation value justifies the wait, or whether the claim-trading market — if one has developed, which should be monitored via Rule 3001 transfer notices on the docket — offers a better risk-adjusted exit. 
The liquidity crisis originated in cascading counterparty exposures: an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy, alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution, July 31, 2026

The most concrete near-term creditor action: fund receipt. 
FTX Trading Ltd. (d/b/a FTX.com) and the FTX Recovery Trust announced on July 17, 2026 that, consistent with the Plan, FTX will commence distributions on July 31, 2026 to holders of allowed claims in the Convenience and Non-Convenience Classes that completed pre-distribution requirements by the June 16 record date.


The waterfall mechanics of the fifth distribution are specific. 
Allowed Class 5A Dotcom Customer Entitlement Claims will receive an incremental 9% distribution (105% cumulative to date); Allowed Class 5B U.S. Customer Entitlement Claims will receive a 5% distribution (105% cumulative to date); Allowed Classes 6A General Unsecured Claims and 6B Digital Asset Loan Claims will each receive a 3% distribution (103% cumulative to date); and Allowed Class 7 Convenience Claims will reach a cumulative 120% distribution.


That arithmetic requires one qualification creditors must internalize. 
The percentages do not mean creditors are receiving 105% or 120% of the current value of the Bitcoin, Ethereum, or other digital assets they previously held on FTX. FTX's official claims documentation states that customer balances are reflected as of November 11, 2022; their U.S. dollar values were calculated using the Digital Asset Conversion Table approved during the proceedings. A creditor who held an appreciating asset such as Bitcoin is being repaid against its court-recognized claim value rather than receiving the original number of coins or their present market value.
 The petition-date Bitcoin price was approximately $16,000; current market levels are multiples of that figure. The "above 100%" language is a legal artifact of dollarized claim treatment, not a market-value surplus.


The Chapter 11 Plan took effect January 3, 2025. The first distribution arrived in February at roughly $1.2 billion, followed by $5 billion in May 2025, $1.6 billion in September 2025, and $2.2 billion in March 2026.
 The fifth distribution of ~$900 million brings the running total toward $11 billion. 
Separately, FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to approval, would release additional cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims.
 That reserve reduction, if approved, expands the pool available for future rounds.

Third-party liability recovery continues in parallel. 
In May, law firm Fenwick & West agreed to pay $54 million to resolve claims that it helped enable misconduct at FTX before the company filed for bankruptcy.
 For claim-trading market participants: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims maintained by the Notice and Claims Agent as of future record dates, where the 21-day notice period has lapsed without objection.
 Buyers of secondary claims who missed the June 16 record date wait for the next round.

Political context: 
just before the distribution announcement, the U.S. Senate unanimously adopted Senate Resolution 772 on July 16, expressing bipartisan opposition to any potential presidential pardon for convicted FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried. Though non-binding, it underscored lawmakers' view that executive clemency would undermine accountability.

### Genesis Global Holdco, LLC — Case No. 23-10063 (SDNY) / DCG Adversary Proceedings

The Genesis/DCG adversary track moves to a pre-trial conference tomorrow, July 21, at 10:00 a.m. ET. 
The Kroll case calendar confirms: July 21, 2026 — Pre-trial Conference, 10:00 a.m., Wind-Down Oversight Committee of Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al., Case No. 23-10063.
 The specific adversary proceedings active on the docket include: 
Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053; Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230.


The post-confirmation litigation posture has been clear since the August 2024 effective date. 
Post-confirmation litigation between the wind-down debtors and DCG has continued. Genesis Global Capital sued Digital Currency Group seeking to recover billions in assets and preferential transfers, alleging that DCG insiders engaged in fraud and mismanagement while Genesis was insolvent. DCG filed its own adversary complaint, and the wind-down debtors moved to dismiss that action. The claims objection process also remains active, with the court granting the wind-down debtors' second extension of the deadline to object to claims in February 2026.


The structural significance of the Genesis plan for the broader crypto-insolvency wave bears repeating. 
In the wave of "crypto winter" bankruptcy cases, the valuation of crypto creditors' claims became a key issue. Virtually all crypto debtors "dollarized" claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values. Genesis, however, obtained court approval of a Chapter 11 plan that provided "in-kind" recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values. Because some crypto prices (like Bitcoin) nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected recoveries for crypto creditors.
 That model — rejected everywhere else, including FTX — is the analytical anchor for any creditor in a current case arguing against dollarized treatment.

### Cross-Docket Pattern Notes — Customer-Property Theory

The central jurisprudential question running through the wave — whether customer crypto deposits constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in trust/bailment — has, in the current cases, resolved largely against customers through terms-of-service analysis and, in BlockFills' case, first-day admissions. The Celsius TOS litigation established the governing methodology: courts examine whether the operative user agreement conveyed title or established a bailment/custody relationship. Where, as in BlockFills, debtors' own counsel concede commingling and estate ownership at the first-day hearing, no TOS litigation is necessary — the estate's framing is controlling unless a creditor can make an in-kind tracing argument under state law. The Genesis model (in-kind, coin-for-coin) remains the counterexample: confirmation of a plan that respects the economic bargain rather than the dollarized bankruptcy abstraction. That model required a sympathetic creditor coalition, a cooperative debtor, and an equity holder (DCG) with no economic standing to block it. BlockFills' creditors have none of those conditions.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors in the BlockFills case (Bankr. D. Del. 26-10371) should immediately verify their claim is on file with the Veritaglobal claims portal and monitor Docket No. 530 and the unrecorded July 17 filing for any plan modification, confirmation scheduling order, or disclosure statement amendment that could reset objection deadlines; those holding claims who received pre-petition commingling representations from BlockFills should preserve that record — emails, account statements, marketing materials characterizing assets as custodied or segregated — as potential ammunition in UCC-driven adversary proceedings against insiders under § 548 fraudulent-transfer and § 547 preference theories. FTX creditors who completed KYC and onboarding with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer before the June 16 record date will receive funds on or around July 31 — no further action required; those who missed the June 16 date must complete KYC, tax form submission, and provider onboarding at claims.ftx.com before the next record date, and should note that transferred claims require the 21-day transfer-notice period to lapse without objection before they appear on the register. For the Genesis/DCG pre-trial conference on July 21, parties monitoring the adversary proceedings (Adv. Pro. Nos. 26-01053 and 26-03230) should pull the agenda when filed, as any scheduling order or discovery ruling from that conference will govern the timeline for asset-recovery claims against DCG that, if successful, could fund additional distributions to unsecured creditors.
