# The Allocation Phase Has Started: Check Your Split Before the 30-Day Clock Runs Out

On September 4, 2026, the settlement administrator began sending allocation notices to every claimant in *Bartz v. Anthropic*. Each notice carries a unique Claim Number, a PIN, and a link to a secure portal. You have **30 days from the date printed on your notice** to respond.

This is the last stage at which the amount you personally receive can still change. Everything before it — filing, final approval, the works list — is settled. What is being decided now is who gets which slice of the money for each book. And in the first week of notices, authors have been finding publishers claiming a share of works those publishers no longer have any interest in.

## What the notice actually is

It is not a check and it is not a claim confirmation. For each work you claimed, the notice and portal list **everyone else who filed a claim on that same work** and the percentage each of them requested. The administrator has sorted your works into two buckets.

**The No Disagreements page** holds works where the claimed percentages do not conflict. Expand an entry to see the current rightsholder allocations. If you accept them, click "No Changes." If you do not, "Update Allocation" moves that work to the Disagreements page.

**The Disagreements page** holds works with conflicting claims, plus anything you moved there yourself. Expanding an entry shows each claim submitted for the work with the claimant's name.

Read that first bucket carefully. "No disagreement" does not mean "correct" — it means the numbers reconcile without conflicting. A publisher claiming 50% of a book whose rights reverted to you years ago produces no conflict at all if you claimed 50% yourself. It just quietly costs you half.

## The default split, and the two cases where it is wrong

For non-education works, the portal's default is **50% of the payment split equally among all authors and 50% split equally among all publishers**. That tracks the standard trade-contract language on copyright litigation recoveries.

There are two situations where the default should not apply:

- **You are the sole rightsholder.** Self-published, rights reverted, or the contract otherwise terminated. The correct allocation is 100% to you and nothing to the publisher.
- **The work is an education work.** There is no 50/50 default. Contributors set their percentage from their contract, usually the royalty rate.

One more mechanic worth knowing: where the percentages for a work add up to **less than 100%**, the remainder is not redistributed to whoever showed up. It is held and paid out later, once the nonfiling rightsholder is identified.

## What authors are finding in week one

Writer Beware, reporting on September 4, documented publishers claiming 50–100% on books whose rights had reverted, and 100% on in-print titles where the 50/50 default governs. HarperCollins/Harlequin, Kensington and Macmillan were among those named; educational publishers including Wiley and Pearson were reported claiming 75–90%. One author found sixteen reverted titles claimed by a single publisher. Another found eleven. Victoria Strauss, who wrote the piece, was measured about cause — she suspects "poor recordkeeping" rather than bad faith.

The Authors Guild has separately reported that certain publishers told the administrator they mistakenly selected a 100% allocation instead of the default, and that those errors are being corrected.

Either way, the burden of catching it sits with you, inside a 30-day window.

## How to respond

1. **Locate your notice.** You need the Claim Number and PIN. The administrator has contact details for roughly 66% of authors on the works list and 97% of publishers, so a meaningful share of authors will not receive one automatically. If nothing arrived, call **1-877-206-2314** or email **info@AnthropicCopyrightSettlement.com**.
2. **Review every work on both pages** — not just the flagged ones.
3. **Gather documentation before you dispute.** A reversion letter, a termination notice, or the contract clause covering copyright litigation recoveries. A percentage assertion with nothing behind it is weak in front of a neutral.
4. **On the Update Allocation screen**, confirm each listed rightsholder through the dropdown — *yes* if they should be paid, *no* if you believe they are not a legal or beneficial owner, *duplicate* if the same party is listed twice — then either take the default or assign custom percentages totaling 100%, and upload your supporting documents.
5. **Certify.** The summary compares the original allocation to what you are requesting. Read the certification statements, type your name as signature, and submit.

Filing a change generates a fresh dispute notice to the other claimants on that work, so the process can iterate. Start early in your 30 days rather than late.

## If you cannot resolve it

The escalation path runs in three stages. Co-claimants get the 30-day window to work it out between themselves. If that fails, the administrator makes contact and tries to broker a resolution. Anything still open goes to a **court-appointed Special Master**, whose determination is final.

Escalation costs time, and time is the currency in this settlement. A clean, uncontested work moves with the early distribution waves. A work sitting in front of a Special Master waits.
