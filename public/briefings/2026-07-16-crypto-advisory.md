### I. BlockFills — Sale Approved June 16; Confirmation Hearing Held July 13; Near-Term Distributions Unclear


On March 15, 2026, the four Reliz debtor entities filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
Reliz reported $100 million to $500 million in debt against $50 million to $100 million in assets.


The sale track closed first. 
On June 16, 2026, a Delaware bankruptcy judge approved the $3.25 million sale of BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepared for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 That buyer is Keyrock SA. 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
The acquisition remains subject to court approval and regulatory clearances.


The confirmation track ran concurrently. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Disclosure Statement Order at Docket No. 388, approving the solicitation package and establishing the confirmation timeline.
 
The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, as was the confirmation objection deadline.
 
The confirmation hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket through July 14 reflects five new entries — Docket Entries 528 and 529 filed that day — but description fields remain blank in the CourtListener sync fetched July 15. Whether Judge Horan confirmed the plan at the July 13 hearing, adjourned it, or entered a confirmation order post-hearing cannot be verified from currently available data. Creditors and claimants should check the Veritaglobal docket portal directly. 
The next calendared date on the Veritaglobal page is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 — consistent with a post-confirmation effective date or administrative wind-up milestone, though the nature of that hearing is unspecified.

The structural question underlying creditor recovery has never been more concrete: 
the capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt, with the first-day record identifying roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
On January 16, 2026, Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours. On February 11, 2026, Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 
If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property under 11 U.S.C. § 541, they are available to fund the reorganization, to be distributed pro rata under the Plan, and to serve as the basis for the cash collateral arrangement.


The prepetition litigation record reinforces the commingling concern. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026)
 
and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026); plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and both courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.


The unsecured creditors' committee is represented by FTI Consulting as financial advisor. 
The Committee members are SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 McDermott Will & Schulte LLP serves as lead debtor counsel; 
Berkeley Research Group serves as financial advisor, with BRG Managing Director Mark Renzi appointed as Chief Restructuring Officer.


The arithmetic is stark. A $3.25 million sale against $145 million in unsecured claims implies a recovery well below 3 cents on the dollar from the sale proceeds alone, before any litigation recoveries or residual asset distributions. Whether the plan provides for additional distributions — from the customer asset pool, from avoidance actions, or from litigation against former directors and officers — is a function of plan mechanics that cannot be fully assessed until the confirmation order and any filed plan supplement become publicly available.

### II. FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Commences July 31; Disputed Claims Reserve Cut to $1.8B

The FTX saga is entering what may be its penultimate distribution cycle. 
The FTX Recovery Trust announced that the anticipated record date for the next (fifth) distribution is June 16, 2026, for holders of allowed FTX claims and interests, with that distribution expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
Preferred equity holders will also receive payments on July 31, 2026, with the same June 16, 2026 record date.



FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release cash to be distributed to holders of allowed claims in the next distribution.
 That reserve reduction is the mechanical precondition to the July 31 payout — if the court has not yet approved it, claimants should expect a short delay. 
Customers and creditors must complete KYC verification, submit required tax forms, and onboard with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer to receive a distribution.
 
For transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim reflected on the official register as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.


Cumulative recovery context: 
on March 31, 2026, the FTX Recovery Trust distributed another $2.2 billion to FTX customers, on top of $8.1 billion distributed in 2025, yielding total payouts to date of $10.3 billion.
 
The latest distribution lifted recovery rates so that many customer claim classes — including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B — reached 100 percent, while Class 7 is set to receive a cumulative 120 percent.


The structural valuation dispute that defined FTX's plan — petition-date dollar value versus current market value — remains germane to understanding why creditors holding Bitcoin-denominated claims feel undercompensated even at 100% fiat recovery. 
Repayments have been calculated using the U.S. dollar value of crypto assets at the time of the November 2022 bankruptcy filing. Some creditors have objected to this method, arguing that cryptocurrency prices, especially Bitcoin, have risen sharply since then.
 The FTX plan locked in dollarization; the Genesis plan did not. That divergence — now fully realized — is the central policy fault line for the next wave of crypto insolvencies. Practitioners and claimants should read the two plans together.

The clawback front remains active. 
Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion. The case is part of the broader clawback strategy — not just identifying what assets remain, but challenging transfers made before the exchange's collapse. Genesis is pushing to dismiss the suit, Bloomberg Law reported, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.
 
The FTX Recovery Trust is represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP as legal counsel, Alvarez & Marsal North America, LLC as financial advisor, Perella Weinberg Partners LP as investment banker, Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan, LLP as special counsel, and Landis Rath & Cobb LLP as Delaware counsel.

### III. Genesis/DCG — Active Adversary Proceedings; July 21 Pre-Trial Conference

The Genesis wind-down entered its post-effective-date litigation phase in August 2024, and that phase is now bearing down on DCG. The Kroll docket for *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al.*, Case No. 23-10063 (S.D.N.Y.), lists a dense adversary proceeding portfolio:


*Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053, and a companion action under 26-03230.
 
A pre-trial conference is calendared for July 21, 2026 at 10:00 a.m.
 — next week. 
*Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129
 represents DCG's counterstrike. 
The insider defendant action (*Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097) and the redacted-defendant action (No. 25-01028) are also live.
 The litigation fund that creditors established at plan confirmation — 
$70 million comprising $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD — is the war chest for these campaigns.


The in-kind recovery model Genesis used is worth restating as precedent. 
In the wave of "crypto winter" bankruptcy cases, the valuation of crypto creditors' claims became a key issue. Virtually all crypto debtors "dollarized" claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values. Genesis took a different approach, obtaining court approval of a Chapter 11 plan providing in-kind recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values — because some crypto prices like Bitcoin nearly tripled after the petition date, significantly affecting recoveries for crypto creditors.
 The DCG adversary proceedings will test whether residual amounts from that recovery architecture flow to creditors through litigation judgment rather than plan distributions.


Cleary Gottlieb is working with the plan administrator to effectuate approximately $200 million in distributions to altcoin creditors as soon as practicable.
 The timing of those distributions appears contingent on claims reconciliation progress and the outcome of the adversary proceedings against DCG. Creditors who have not completed the distribution onboarding process should not assume prior distributions are sufficient — ongoing recoveries depend on the adversary proceedings docket moving forward.

### IV. The Customer-Property Question — Structural Pattern Across Cases

BlockFills, Celsius, and Genesis each present a variant of the same foundational issue under 11 U.S.C. § 541: whether digital assets deposited by customers constitute property of the estate or are held in trust or bailment. The answer is entirely document-dependent — terms of service, wallet architecture, and commingling conduct determine the outcome, not the label. In BlockFills, the February 11, 2026 disclosure that customer assets sat in a single commingled balance sheet is functionally dispositive under the *Celsius* framework unless creditors can trace specific assets under a constructive trust or resulting trust theory — and the Debtors have already noted the objectors have not filed the requisite adversary proceeding under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001(b). That procedural posture matters. Without an adversary, the trust/bailment argument is not properly before Judge Horan and cannot override the plan's § 1129 confirmation standards.

For creditors in the 2025–26 wave who are watching BlockFills as a template, the key variables are: (1) whether the terms of service explicitly created a custodial or fiduciary relationship; (2) whether assets were physically or cryptographically segregated on-chain; and (3) whether any insider-transfer avoidance claims under 11 U.S.C. § 548 or § 547 can supplement the sale proceeds. None of those three questions has been resolved in the BlockFills docket as of this advisory.
