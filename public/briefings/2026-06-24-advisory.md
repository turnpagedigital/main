### Bartz v. Anthropic — Final Approval Limbo, Distribution Timeline Pressure, and the Fee Award


After a 75-minute hearing on May 14, 2026, Judge Martínez-Olguín declined for now to approve the $1.5 billion settlement, though the tone of the hearing led most observers to expect the court will grant final approval relatively quickly.
 The delay matters structurally: 
after the fairness hearing, the settlement administrator was scheduled to calculate distributions by June 11, 2026, with payment disbursement expected to begin in June 2026 or later, subject to whether appeals are filed.
 That distribution timeline assumes a final approval order; without it, the June 11 calculation benchmark slips, and the entire four-installment payment waterfall — 
funded by Anthropic in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027
 — is effectively paused pending the written order and any Ninth Circuit objector appeal.

The claim economics are now locked. 
Class counsel filed papers reporting that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% rate, vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in the Motion for Final Approval of Settlement filed March 19, 2026, eleven days before the March 30, 2026, claims deadline.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson confirmed at the May 14 hearing that the payout per work will be approximately $3,100.
 
The high claim rate means that earlier forecasts of a larger payout based on the March 19 report of 54% participation will not materialize; the actual payout will be closer to the original $3,000-per-work figure.


The fee question remains the most significant unresolved variable for net claimant recovery. 
Plaintiff lawyers are seeking 25% of the fund — $375 million — with administration by JND Legal Administration likely costing several million dollars, and service awards of $50,000 per named plaintiff (totaling $150,000 for the three class representatives).
 
At the May 14 hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín heard from seven objectors, each allocated two minutes, and questioned class counsel Justin Nelson and Rachel Geman specifically about service awards, cost reserves, attorneys' fees, and the fee multiplier.
 The court's post-hearing order required Anthropic to file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages by May 21, 2026, addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored, and directed plaintiffs to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30. 
The court stated it would not require or consider any further submissions from objectors.


One structural point bears emphasis for claims investors: 
Anthropic denied any wrongdoing in agreeing to the settlement, and the infringement question has not been adjudicated.
 The settlement resolves only the piracy-based claims — the training-data fair-use question was decided in Anthropic's favor at summary judgment by Judge Alsup before the case transferred to Judge Martínez-Olguín. 
In June 2025, Judge William Alsup ruled on summary judgment that using books without permission to train AI was fair use if they were acquired legally, but denied summary judgment related to piracy — finding that piracy was not fair use.
 The $1.5 billion covers the piracy-specific class; it does not establish that AI training on legally acquired material is compensable, a distinction that will define the next wave of litigation.


Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 Any timely objector who files a Ninth Circuit notice of appeal triggers the appellate standstill; 
the plan of allocation contemplates summer 2026 as the earliest payment date if there are objectors but no appeals.

### Concord I (5:24-cv-03811-EKL) — Cross-MSJs Fully Submitted; First Adjudicated Fair-Use Ruling Now Imminent

*Concord Music Group, Inc., et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL, before 
Judge Eumi K. Lee, with discovery matters referred to Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen, last updated in CourtListener on June 22, 2026
, is the more consequential matter for the broader AI-copyright docket. The case originated in October 2023 in the Middle District of Tennessee and was transferred to the Northern District of California. 
Plaintiffs — Concord Music Group, Universal Music Corp., ABKCO Music, Inc., Polygram Publishing, Capitol CMG, and related entities — allege that Anthropic infringes their exclusive rights to musical lyrics by using their works to train Claude and by distributing lyrics in Claude's outputs.


Cross-motions for summary judgment were filed and the briefing schedule closed June 8, 2026. The publishers' March 23, 2026 partial summary judgment motion is the most aggressive framing to date. 
The motion for partial summary judgment was accompanied by a 47-page statement setting out 218 "undisputed facts" supported by deposition testimony, internal Anthropic documents, and Anthropic's own admissions.
 On direct infringement, 
the filing states that Anthropic admits at least one Claude model was trained on a dataset containing lyrics to at least 100 of Publishers' Works, and that Anthropic has never sought nor obtained a license from Publishers to use the Works.
 On fair use, 
the publishers argue Anthropic's use was purely commercial — noting the company is "a for-profit technology company valued at $380 billion or more" with a revenue run rate approaching $14 billion — that it copied lyrics in full, and that its output directly competes with licensed lyrics services such as LyricFind and Musixmatch.


The § 107(4) market-harm factor is where the publishers' brief is most developed. 
On market harm, the filing states that when Claude outputs lyrics in response to user requests, Anthropic provides the same service to its users as Publishers' licensees, without Publishers' authorization; it cites data showing over 60,000 AI-generated tracks were being submitted to Deezer daily by January 2026, accounting for 3% of total streams.
 The output-side infringement record is specific: 
Anthropic's own records show Claude reproduced lyrics in response to a wide range of prompts — translation queries, chord requests, SEO article generation, homework assistance, and requests to write new songs — and in many cases Claude output lyrics even when users did not ask for them or requested "new" content.


Judge Lee's prior rulings on the preliminary injunction motion are directionally relevant. 
The court found in favor of Anthropic in two consecutive decisions: first, denying the preliminary injunction as overbroad and failing to establish irreparable harm, and second, granting Anthropic's motion to dismiss contributory and vicarious infringement claims with leave to amend.
 The motion-to-dismiss rulings are procedurally distinct from the merits record now before the court at summary judgment — the dismissal turned on pleading sufficiency for secondary liability, not on training-data infringement or fair use as applied to admitted conduct. 
The court also found that plaintiffs did not sufficiently allege that Anthropic acted intentionally as to the removal of CMI, given plaintiffs' own acknowledgment that CMI was not removed across the board throughout Claude's training process.
 Those CMI claims under the DMCA remain a contested issue on the cross-MSJs.

Ruling timeline: the briefing closed June 8. Judge Lee has no standing order-mandated decision deadline on dispositive motions. A decision before Labor Day 2026 is plausible but not guaranteed; the case is complex, the record large. When the ruling comes, it will be the first post-*Bartz* adjudication on AI training-data fair use by a court that has already rejected Anthropic's secondary-liability defenses on pleading grounds once before.

### Concord II (5:26-cv-00880-EKL) — Second Music-Publisher Complaint, Anthropic's Stay Motion, and the $3 Billion Satellite Case

The publishers filed a second, materially larger complaint against Anthropic in January 2026, assigned to 
Judge Eumi K. Lee in *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, 5:26-cv-00880 (N.D. Cal.), filed January 28, 2026.
 
This second action covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion in statutory damages.


Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending final resolution of Concord I. 
Anthropic's motion to stay, heard May 20, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. in Courtroom 7, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, San Jose Division, 280 South 1st Street, sought to stay *Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK, pending final resolution of the substantially similar litigation in *Concord Music Group, Inc., et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK, or in the alternative, pending resolution of the parties' motions for summary judgment and any subsequent appeal.
 The outcome of Judge Lee's ruling on the stay motion has not been confirmed in sources available to this desk as of today; the motion was fully briefed by March 31, 2026, heard May 20, and no public order has surfaced. Rights-holders in the Concord II class — covering more than 20,000 works — face a materially longer timeline to any recovery depending on the stay ruling.

Anthropic's strategic rationale for the stay is well-documented: 
Concord I has completed fact and expert discovery and is approaching summary judgment practice that will determine several threshold legal issues governing the claims, including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use, which could have preclusive effect in Concord II; allowing the cases to proceed in parallel would result in duplicative, time-consuming discovery.

### Parallel Docket Watch — In re OpenAI Copyright MDL (N.D. Cal., No. 3143) and the Broader System

The framework established in *Bartz* — piracy-specific class certification, training-data fair use reserved to per-plaintiff adjudication, settlement structured around per-work LibGen and PiLiMi scrape-lists — continues to define how plaintiffs must structure AI copyright claims. 
For many authors implicated in *Bartz*, the litigation around AI training and book piracy is not ending; it is shifting to other cases, including consolidated actions against OpenAI and Microsoft, and active putative class actions against Meta.
 The *In re OpenAI Copyright Litigation* MDL, No. 3143 (N.D. Cal.), the *Authors Guild v. OpenAI*, and *NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft* all turn on the same § 107(2) and § 107(4) factors now being briefed in Concord I. Judge Lee's Concord I ruling on commercial use, market substitution, and the training-data pipeline will be the first detailed fair-use analysis on an AI training case to reach the merits on a fully developed record. It will be cited in every parallel proceeding the moment it issues.

The § 504 statutory damages exposure across the system remains staggering. The Concord II complaint alone seeks over $3 billion, 
asking the court to rule in the publishers' favor on core elements without a full trial — a step granted only when the underlying facts are not meaningfully contested.
 If the publishers prevail on partial summary judgment in Concord I even on direct training-data infringement only, every defendant in the parallel AI-copyright docket faces renewed settlement pressure at multiples of what *Bartz* established as a per-work baseline.

In *Concord I* (5:24-cv-03811-EKL), the cross-MSJ ruling from Judge Lee is the single most important near-term development in the AI-copyright system — counsel advising defendants in *In re OpenAI Copyright MDL* (No. 3143), *Authors Guild v. OpenAI*, and *NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft* should have Concord I's briefing record in hand now so they can model immediate response briefs and settlement authority adjustments the day the ruling drops; the ruling on § 107(4) market harm, in particular, will carry direct weight in the MDL. For Concord II (5:26-cv-00880-EKL), the unconfirmed stay ruling is the gating item — if Judge Lee grants the stay, the $3 billion complaint is parked behind Concord I's appellate cycle, and claimants in that class face a multi-year horizon; if the stay is denied, publishers will immediately commence discovery on 20,000+ works and Anthropic faces parallel litigation resource drag heading into trial on Concord I.
