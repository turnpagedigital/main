### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. · Sale Hearing TODAY

On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities — collectively operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed Chapter 11 petitions in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware before the Honorable Thomas M. Horan. Court documents list BlockFills' assets at between $50 million and $100 million, against liabilities ranging from $100 million to $500 million. The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.

The precipitating facts for creditors remain the most legally consequential dimension of this case. On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet." At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate. The court noted that no evidence before it established the funds as anything other than estate property, but preserved the issue for further hearing. This characterization — estate property vs. customer-owned digital asset — is the central creditor-rights fault line in the case: customers who successfully prove their assets were held in trust and never legally transferred to the Debtor could claim priority outside the general unsecured pool; those who cannot are junior creditors sharing in whatever the estate generates.

The prepetition litigation that forced the bankruptcy also shaped the creditor landscape. Two customer lawsuits followed the withdrawal suspension, each alleging misappropriation of customer funds, commingling, and fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026 — on March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte temporary restraining order freezing 70.56 BTC (approximately $4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds. The filings followed years of cascading financial losses, two prepetition customer lawsuits with temporary restraining orders, and the collapse of multiple recapitalization attempts.

**TODAY's hearing is the pivotal gating event.** According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property. Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors per a court document filed May 26, 2026. A hearing to consider approval of the sale is currently scheduled for June 16, 2026.

The $3.25 million asset sale purchase price is striking against a $145 million unsecured claims pool. Even assuming the transaction closes cleanly at today's hearing, the operative recovery question shifts immediately to the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket No. 391) and the liquidating trust mechanics. The claims administrator, Veritaglobal (KCC dba Verita), is administering solicitation materials for the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket No. 391) and the related Disclosure Statement (Docket No. 388). On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, and establishing deadlines for plan objections, claim objections, and temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes. The Voting Deadline is July 1, 2026.

The Plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a Section 363 competitive sale process. If the Keyrock sale is approved today, the Alternative Transaction track will likely be elected, collapsing the NewCo pathway. Creditors — especially institutional customers whose claims are currently in the non-voting impaired class under the notice from Veritaglobal — should file timely ballot responses and, if eligible, motions for temporary allowance of disputed claims for voting purposes before July 1. Any creditor who asserts their digital assets were not property of the estate must also evaluate filing an adversary proceeding: the Debtors themselves note a procedural deficiency — objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of ownership rights in digital assets.

The financial backdrop involves layered counterparty losses. The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, and stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. · First-Week Dynamics; No Judge Assigned

The mattress retailer's Chapter 11 filing is four days old, and the creditor-rights posture is still in its first-day architecture. On June 12, 2026 (the "Petition Date"), Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 of Title 11 of the United States Code in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. The case carries Case No. 26-11399 (judge not yet assigned per the tracked-case ground truth), and the claims administrator is Kroll, whose portal is live at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber.

The capital structure is deeply inverted. According to the first-day declaration, the company has approximately $672.5 million of funded debt, comprising approximately $475 million outstanding under a revolver, $177.5 million outstanding under a term loan entered into in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered into in 2026. Against this, the DIP facility is structured in two tranches. The DIP facility comprises $65 million in new money term loans and a $195 million roll-up, bearing SOFR plus 8.00% (or base rate plus 7.00%) and maturing in three months, to fund operations through the process.

The roll-up — converting $195 million of prepetition revolver debt into superpriority DIP paper — is the creditor-rights pressure point that unsecured creditors and any committee forming this week will immediately contest. The claims of the DIP lenders will be entitled to superpriority administrative expense claim status and, subject to customary exclusions in the credit documentation, secured by a perfected first-priority lien on all DIP Collateral (to the extent unencumbered), a perfected priming senior security interest in and liens on prepetition collateral, and a perfected junior security interest in and liens on the DIP Collateral to the extent such collateral is subject to other existing liens. The practical effect: prepetition revolving lenders have structurally leapfrogged the entire pre-existing lien stack via roll-up, and any unsecured estate assets available for general creditor distribution will reflect the residual after DIP/roll-up payoff, break-up fee, and administrative claims — before any unsecured dollar recovers.

The §363 sale process dictates the creditor-recovery timeline. The stalking-horse transaction provides for a purchase price of $415 million in cash, subject to certain adjustments and the assumption of certain liabilities. The transaction is being undertaken pursuant to Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, with Sleep Country Canada serving as the "stalking horse" bidder in a court-supervised sale process. The deal carries a 3% break-up fee along with an expense reimbursement cap of $4 million. The court has set an auction for July 13, 2026, with sale approval targeted around July 15 and a close aimed at July 31.

Management explicitly warns that common shares are "significantly out of the money," expects a complete or significant loss for shareholders, and anticipates a Nasdaq delisting. For trade creditors, the first-day motions include a standard critical-vendor/employee-wage package. Sleep Number fully expects to pay suppliers for goods and services provided after the filing date. Whether the §363 purchase price — net of the DIP payoff and administrative costs — generates any meaningful distribution to pre-petition unsecured trade creditors remains the open legal question. At $415 million against $672.5 million in funded debt alone (before any unsecured trade, lease-rejection, or warranty claims), the waterfall leaves no obvious surplus absent a higher competing bid.

Davis Polk & Wardwell is the company's legal advisor, Guggenheim Securities its investment banker, and AP Services its financial advisor. A&G Real Estate Partners is assisting the company with store-footprint review, and the debtor filed a motion with the bankruptcy court to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations. Rejection-damage claims arising from those 44 locations will constitute general unsecured claims subject to the §502(b)(6) rent-cap formula (typically one year's rent plus past-due amounts), further diluting the unsecured pool. The company's 572 stores are reported to remain open, vendors are being paid, and day-to-day operations continue.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and practitioners across all three tracked cases face compressive near-term deadlines: in *BlockFills* (26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan), the July 1, 2026 voting deadline for the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket No. 391) requires creditors to have already received solicitation packages from Veritaglobal and to return ballots on time — impaired creditors in the non-voting class designated "Notice of Non-Voting Status (Impaired)" must immediately evaluate filing adversary proceedings under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001(b) to assert ownership rights in commingled digital assets before the plan's liquidating-trust mechanics extinguish those claims; the Keyrock $3.25 million sale approval hearing today is the last realistic juncture to lodge a sale objection or higher bid before the Alternative Transaction track is locked. In *Sleep Number Corporation* (26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.), trade creditors whose post-petition invoices will be paid should confirm purchase-order dates straddle the June 12, 2026 petition date, and any creditor holding pre-petition exposure should submit proofs of claim promptly after the bar date is set by Kroll (restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber), scrutinize the interim and final DIP orders for adequate-protection provisions protecting any junior secured interests, and press the U.S. Trustee to constitute an official committee of unsecured creditors before the July 13 auction date so the committee has standing to review bidding procedures, objection rights, and any sale-order credit-bid mechanics that could impair recoveries further; committee counsel should seek a carve-out from the DIP roll-up priming lien to fund a meaningful administrative reserve. In *Power Block Coin* (24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah, Judge Parker), no action is required this week, but creditors should monitor the docket for any plan filing or §1112(b) motion given the case's age.
