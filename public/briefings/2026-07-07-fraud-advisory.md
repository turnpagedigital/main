### Power Block Coin, LLC — Three New Filings July 6; Case Posture


The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, LLC — represented by Greenberg Traurig, LLP — filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah proposing a Liquidating Trust structure; the company, which operated under the name SmartFi, filed its Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024.
 The tracked-case ground truth confirms three new docket entries (Entries 529, 531, and one unnumbered entry) filed July 6, 2026, the most recent activity on CourtListener docket ID 68873409. The judge currently assigned is the Honorable Cathleen D. Parker. Note: earlier rulings in the case — including the October 2024 Subchapter V eligibility decision — were issued by Judge Joel T. Marker; 
the May 2026 docket entry (Doc. 502, filed 05/07/26) carried the CDP suffix
, confirming the judge assignment had transitioned to Judge Parker by spring 2026.

The structural stakes are significant. 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.
 
A key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to pursue potential legal claims and oversee distributions; the committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions.
 The most material identified asset at risk: 
a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah — a loan the committee characterized as unsecured and encumbered post-petition.


For claims buyers, the Celsius-nexus is the analytical pivot. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the debtor in connection with the pending bankruptcy case of Celsius Network LLC et al. (Case No. 22-10964) in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The intersection of a $192 million claim pool, a disputed $17.4 million real-property loan, and the Celsius preference litigation makes recovery trajectory highly path-dependent. Clawback exposure under 11 U.S.C. § 548 and applicable UVTA provisions for any net-winner counterparty to SmartFi's pre-petition crypto transfers remains a live concern; the committee's allegations of "unexplained losses" and insider loans are the predicate for § 548 constructive-fraud theories. The content of the July 6 docket entries — potentially adversary-proceeding filings, asset-sale motions, or plan amendment notices — is the most urgent near-term unknown.

### Madoff / BLMIS — Post-Seventeenth Distribution Posture; Fee Record

The seventeenth and most recent interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund closed in February 2026. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution commenced; the Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion; the seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts.
 
Approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid when combined with the prior 16 distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.
 The docket anchor is Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB), before Judge Loretta G. Preska (the case was reassigned from Judge Stuart M. Bernstein after his senior status designation).

On the fee side, BakerHostetler filed its 50th Interim Fee Application on February 13, 2026. 
The application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services, billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
The 10% public-interest discount from standard rates was applied.
 Fee applications at this cadence — 50 applications over 17-plus years — are the defining administrative cost signal for net-winner clawback targets assessing settlement value: all fees and expenses are borne by SIPC, not the Customer Fund, which means the recovery-rate calculation for allowed claimants is not diluted by professional fees. 
No funds recovered in the Madoff Recovery Initiative are used to pay costs associated with the recovery; all trustee, legal, and accounting fees are paid by SIPC.



As of March 2026, the trustee had recovered $15.3 billion toward claims through legal action against Madoff associates, feeder funds, and beneficiaries; the Department of Justice recovered an additional $4.3 billion.
 No eighteenth-distribution motion has appeared on the public record as of this morning. Given the pattern — motions filed in December, court approval in January, distributions commencing in February — an eighteenth distribution motion would not be expected before late Q4 2026 at the earliest, unless a settlement of unusual size forces an earlier filing.
