# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | May 29, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The most consequential generative-AI copyright filing of the week landed yesterday in the Southern District of New York, where Cable News Network ("CNN") brought its first-ever AI copyright action against Perplexity AI, accusing the answer-engine startup of "massive copyright infringement" arising from the scraping, reproduction, and downstream distribution of more than 17,000 CNN stories, photographs, videos, and other newsroom assets. The complaint pleads copyright infringement alongside trademark and unfair-competition counts and, of particular note for litigators tracking the post-*Bartz* landscape, alleges that Perplexity continued to market a paid "Comet Plus" tier promising premium CNN content even after the parties' 2025 negotiations collapsed and CNN's robots.txt expressly blocked the Perplexity crawler. CNN seeks statutory damages, a permanent injunction, and an order requiring destruction of infringing copies — the same remedial template adopted by the *NYT v. OpenAI* and Chicago Tribune plaintiffs. The filing is the first AI copyright action by any U.S. television broadcaster and squarely tees up the *output*-side fair-use question the *Bartz* and *Kadrey* summary-judgment opinions left open. ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/cnn-sues-perplexity-alleging-copyright-infringement-1236760987/)) ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/28/cnn-sues-perplexity-alleging-massive-copyright-infringement/)) ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/28/g-s1-124680/cnn-sues-ai-company-perplexity-alleging-it-violates-copyright-protections))

Perplexity's public response — chief communications officer Jesse Dwyer's statement that "you can't copyright facts" — telegraphs an *Associated Press v. Meltwater* / *Barclays v. TheFlyontheWall* hot-news framing that has now failed in three consecutive AI-search dockets (NYT, Wall Street Journal/Dow Jones, Forbes). The CNN complaint's pleading of Perplexity's "real-time" indexing of CNN URLs into its retrieval-augmented generation pipeline directly mirrors Judge Stein's reasoning in the *NYT v. OpenAI* discovery rulings, where the substitutive value of generated answers — not the underlying factual content — drove the relevance analysis. The pattern across the Perplexity dockets (NYT, Dow Jones, Forbes, and now CNN) increasingly resembles the *Concord Music* aggregation theory: rights-holder coalitions filing serially in the same district to consolidate fair-use rulings and amplify statutory-damages exposure. ([The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/public-policy-legal/cnn-sues-perplexity-ai-stealing-news-chatbot-copyright/)) ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/05/28/perplexity-sued-by-cnn-over-alleged-ai-powered-content-scraping/))

On the *Bartz v. Anthropic* docket, the silence following the May 14 fairness hearing is itself the development. Class counsel and Anthropic timely filed their supplemental briefs on the late-opt-out question by Judge Martínez-Olguín's May 21 deadline, but as of this morning no final approval order, no attorneys'-fees ruling, and no decision on the disputed late opt-outs has issued — pushing the settlement administrator's June 11 distribution-calculation date into jeopardy and leaving roughly $1.29 billion in net distribution funds idle in escrow. The reduced fee petition of $187.5 million (12.5 percent of the fund, down from class counsel's original 20 percent ask) remains the court's most visible open question after the judge spent the bulk of her May 14 bench colloquy on fee allocation and the lead-plaintiff service-award structure. Practitioners should treat the June 11 distribution date as aspirational pending Judge Martínez-Olguín's signature. ([Authors Alliance — Fairness Hearing Observations](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/05/14/bartz-v-anthropic-fairness-hearing-observations-and-takeaways/)) ([Publishing Perspectives](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/05/anthropic-settlement-appears-to-cruise-through-its-final-fairness-hearing/)) ([Bloomberg Law — Fee Reduction](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/authors-lawyers-lower-fees-ask-in-anthropic-pact-approval-bid))

The parallel music-publisher track in *Concord Music Group v. Anthropic* (5:24-cv-03811, N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi Lee) has its dispositive-motion briefing fully due June 8, 2026, with the parties' cross-motions on fair use of lyrics-as-training-data now the most directly post-*Bartz* application of the §107 framework outside the literary context. The collateral *Concord II* matter (5:26-cv-00880) — Anthropic's motion to stay heard May 20 — remains under submission, with publishers pressing the court to allow simultaneous prosecution of the $3 billion follow-on complaint covering 20,000-plus additional musical works. Each Concord ruling will inform the appellate posture in any *Bartz* objection — eight of the original 53 objectors retained appellate counsel as of last week, signaling a likely Ninth Circuit track even after final approval. ([CourtListener — Concord I Docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68889092/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)) ([CourtListener — Concord II Docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72199828/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/)) ([BakerHostetler — Concord Update](https://www.bakerlaw.com/concord-music-group-inc-v-anthropic-pbc/))

Across the broader docket, the Authors Alliance's analysis of *Elsevier v. Meta* (filed May 5 by Hachette, Macmillan, McGraw Hill, Elsevier, Cengage, and Scott Turow in SDNY) continues to attract amicus interest, with the complaint's allegations that Mark Zuckerberg "personally authorized and actively encouraged" the LibGen and Anna's Archive ingestion creating individual-officer exposure of a kind not previously pleaded in any AI-training matter — a doctrinal innovation that may be cited in motions for personal jurisdiction over Anthropic and OpenAI executives in subsequent matters. Meanwhile, the *In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation* (MDL No. 3143) continues to expand its production order: the March 9 ruling compelling production of the further 78-million and 10-million log reservoirs (atop the original 20-million sample affirmed by Judge Stein on January 5) has cemented chat-log discoverability as the most distinctive procedural feature of the AI MDL — a feature that CNN's complaint references in its prayer for "complete training-data discovery." ## Recommended Actions

For publishers and broadcasters evaluating whether to follow CNN into the SDNY against retrieval-augmented-generation platforms, the *CNN v. Perplexity* complaint is the cleanest pleading template now in the field — practitioners should study the Comet Plus-tier misrepresentation allegations as the model for trademark-and-unfair-competition counts that meaningfully amplify a copyright case's settlement leverage and survive a Section 230 challenge. Rights-holders whose works appear in the *Bartz* class-claim file should verify their claim status one final time at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com pending final approval; with no order yet on file, the June 11 distribution-calculation date is no longer reliable, and any late-opt-out applicant should be prepared for a denial absent extraordinary circumstances given the supplemental briefing record. AI developers should treat the OpenAI MDL's continually expanding chat-log production order (now totaling more than 100 million logs across the January 5 and March 9 orders) as the new baseline for any preservation hold the moment a copyright demand letter arrives — and benchmark their licensing posture against the rapidly hardening industry standard documented in the UK Publishers Association report and the *Concord II* dispositive briefing schedule. Counsel for objectors and opt-outs in *Bartz* should preserve their appellate rights now: the Ninth Circuit's *Bartz, et al. v. Anthropic, PBC*, 25-4843 appellate docket remains open even pending final approval, and the eight objector groups already represented signal the appellate channel is the most likely venue for the remaining publisher-favoritism, group-registration, and statutory-damages-adequacy challenges.
