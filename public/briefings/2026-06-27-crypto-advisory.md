# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Saturday, June 27, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

### BlockFills (★ Key Focus): Sale Approved — Confirmation Now the Fulcrum

The dominant development of the past week in this beat is the resolution of the BlockFills § 363 sale process. 
On June 16, 2026, a Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group, as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 That judge is the Honorable Thomas M. Horan. The case remains *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, 
Case No. 26-10371, before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.


The buyer is Keyrock SA, a Brussels-based market maker. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock confirmed it was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors in an official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
BlockFills had reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 The $3.25 million purchase price against that backdrop illustrates the severity of value destruction — 
Reliz reported $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities at the petition date.


The sale approval clears one track of the dual-path restructuring, but the more consequential proceeding remains ahead. 
The case is now operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with a sale hearing set for June 16, 2026 and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 The Veritaglobal claims-administration site confirms a September 11, 2026 claims bar date remains on calendar. 
(September 11, 2026, 4:00 p.m. ET.)
 The eleven docket entries filed in the 72 hours through June 26 (Dkt. Entry 467, June 26; Entry 465, June 25, and nine additional recent entries per the CourtListener feed at Docket ID 72494188) indicate active pre-confirmation briefing traffic, though the underlying descriptions were not publicly available in time for this advisory.

**The estate-vs.-trust question has not been resolved by the sale.** 
Four debtor entities operating as BlockFills pursue a dual-track restructuring through a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process, while the central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 That question — the § 541 property-of-the-estate issue that Celsius litigated for months in the Southern District of New York — will land squarely before Judge Horan at the July 13 confirmation hearing. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 If those customer obligations are property of the estate under § 541, general unsecured creditors share pro rata across the pool. If the court finds a trust or bailment relationship based on the terms of service or applicable state law, customer assets stand outside the estate entirely — a materially different recovery posture.


The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 Whether the liquidating trust structure adequately captures and distributes the customer-property claim is what confirmation objections will test. Practitioners monitoring this docket should note the parallel adversary proceeding: 
*Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. v. Artha Investment Partners LLC*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH)
, which raises discrete counterparty asset-tracing claims relevant to the overall estate value calculation. A status conference in that matter was calendared for June 22, 2026. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge granted conditional approval to the Chapter 11 plan disclosure statement, noting that issues over the opt-out release mechanism in the proposal can be argued at a confirmation hearing.


The opt-out release fight is worth flagging separately. In the post-*Purdue Pharma* environment — after the Supreme Court's June 2024 ruling limiting non-debtor third-party releases — any broad release of officer and director liability that requires an affirmative opt-out rather than an opt-in faces heightened scrutiny under § 1129. Judge Horan will need to distinguish consensual releases from the coercive opt-out structure the Court of Appeals has now rejected in the Second and Third Circuits.


In the weeks preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits were filed against certain debtor entities and current or former directors and officers, alleging misappropriation of customer funds. The first was filed February 27, 2026 in the Southern District of New York; the second March 5, 2026 in the Northern District of Illinois. Both courts granted temporary restraining orders restricting the debtors' operations and asset control.
 Those pre-petition TRO proceedings — now stayed — will resume in some form if the confirmation plan's third-party releases do not cover the underlying conduct, or if the court narrows their scope.

### FTX Recovery Trust: Fifth Distribution Mechanics and the Claim-Valuation Overhang

The FTX beat has shifted from legal posture to operational mechanics, but the mechanics matter for claim-trading strategy. 
FTX Trading Ltd. (d/b/a FTX.com) and the FTX Recovery Trust announced on May 26, 2026 that the record date for the next distribution will be June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 The record date has now passed — meaning claim positions as of June 16 are locked for the July 31 tranche.


This fifth distribution carries a record date of June 16, 2026, and payments commence July 31, 2026. It follows the fourth distribution of $2.2 billion on March 31, 2026, and brings cumulative distributions above $10 billion.
 Separately, 
holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims, as defined in FTX's confirmed Chapter 11 Plan of Restructuring, became eligible to begin the NFT distribution process on June 30, 2026.


The reserve compression announced alongside the fifth distribution is the more significant number for residual claimants. 
As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That $600 million liberation, if approved, flows into distributable assets for subsequent tranches.


For institutional creditors holding crypto that appreciated post-petition, the structural grievance persists: if a creditor held one BTC on FTX when Bitcoin was trading at approximately $16,871, the claim is valued at that petition-date price — producing roughly 120% recovery ($20,245) against a Bitcoin now worth multiples of that.
 The dollarization cap is settled law in the FTX estate; 
the FTX estate will never pay based on current market prices — it is legally settled at petition-date values.
 The secondary market remains the only exit for creditors seeking current-price exposure. For claim transferees, 
distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.


The FTX docket (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068, originally before Judge Dorsey, now operating through the FTX Recovery Trust) logged an omnibus hearing on June 18, 2026, and the next scheduled omnibus is July 23, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. ET. 
FTX is represented by Sullivan & Cromwell LLP as legal counsel, assisted by Alvarez & Marsal North America LLC as financial advisor, Perella Weinberg Partners LP as investment banker, Quinn Emanuel Urquhart & Sullivan LLP as special counsel, and Landis Rath & Cobb LLP as Delaware counsel.


### Genesis/DCG Wind-Down: Active Adversary Litigation Front

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063) reached plan effectiveness on August 2, 2024 and has made four distributions to creditors since then. The current posture is a wind-down litigation phase, not a distribution mechanics phase, but several live adversary proceedings directly affect residual creditor recoveries.


The Genesis docket (Case No. 23-10063) shows active adversary proceedings including *Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230
 — a 2026 filing, suggesting new litigation against DCG commenced this year on top of the earlier confirmed plan. Additional active proceedings include 
*Digital Currency Grp. v. Genesis Global Cap.*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129; *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Redacted Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028; and *Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.
 The LOC (Litigation Oversight Committee), advised by Selendy Gay PLLC, is actively prosecuting these actions; the WDOC, advised by Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP, oversees the distribution mechanics. 
From the effective date through the present, the Wind-Down Debtors have made four distributions to creditors pursuant to the Plan.


The substantive distinction Genesis established at confirmation remains the landmark creditor-rights precedent of this cycle: 
unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition-date value; instead, Genesis sought to provide restitution through the chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the NYAG, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 
Per the initial distribution, Bitcoin creditors received 51.28% recoveries, Ether creditors 65.87%, and Solana creditors 29.58% of recoveries on an in-kind basis.
 
As part of the Genesis bankruptcy plan, creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue legal action against various third parties, including parent company DCG — comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.
 The litigation fund is the mechanism through which additional creditor value is still being unlocked in the 26-03230 and related proceedings.

### Cross-Docket Pattern: Claim-Valuation Date as the Recurring Fault Line

The BlockFills, FTX, and Genesis dockets collectively surface the same structural tension that has defined this insolvency cycle: whether customer claims in crypto bankruptcies are valued at the petition date in USD (FTX model, which is now settled law in that estate) or on an in-kind basis at distribution (Genesis model, which required novel plan architecture and NYAG cooperation to execute). For BlockFills — where the claims are approximately $145 million and the sale produced only $3.25 million — this distinction will determine whether customers holding crypto positions receive meaningfully different treatment depending on how the liquidating trust is structured and what valuation methodology Judge Horan approves at confirmation. The Adversary Proceeding 26-50230 against Artha Investment Partners adds a third variable: to the extent estate assets are augmented through avoidance recoveries, the denominator problem shrinks.

The 11 U.S.C. § 541 estate-vs.-trust issue, as litigated in *In re Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964) and referenced in Gatecoin (*HKCFI* [2023] HKCFI 914), will be directly implicated at the July 13 BlockFills confirmation hearing — the most important single hearing date in this beat over the next 30 days.

## Recommended Actions

Claim-holders and recovery counsel with positions in the BlockFills estate should treat the July 13, 2026 confirmation hearing before Judge Horan (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371) as the critical window: file or review objections now on the § 541 customer-property issue, the opt-out release mechanism flagged in the conditional disclosure statement approval, and the adequacy of the liquidating trust's claim-valuation methodology — the $3.25 million Keyrock sale proceeds will be insufficient to satisfy the $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations, making the plan's priority waterfall and any in-kind vs. fiat valuation election the decisive recovery determinant. FTX claim-holders whose transfers were not fully registered on the Kroll claims register by the June 16, 2026 record date will miss the July 31 distribution and should immediately verify their status at claims.ftx.com, confirm KYC clearance, and select a Distribution Service Provider (BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer) before the next record date is announced; the disputed-claims reserve reduction from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion suggests a further distribution event is likely within the next 90 days. Creditors with positions in the Genesis wind-down should monitor the active 2026 adversary docket — particularly *Genesis Global Holdco v. DCG*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-03230 — as litigation recoveries flowing through the LOC's $70 million fund will determine the magnitude of any additional distributions beyond the four tranches already paid.
