### Sleep Number — $415M Stalking-Horse Sale, $260M DIP, Compressed Timeline


Sleep Number Corporation and four affiliated debtors (Select Comfort Retail Corporation, Select Comfort Canada Holding Inc., Select Comfort SC LLC, and Sleep Number Health Corporation) initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, with the cases jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.



Substantially all of the company's debt — with balances of approximately $672.5 million in the aggregate principal amount as of the Petition Date — is in default and accelerated, subject to the automatic stay under the Bankruptcy Code.
 
The debtors are requesting approval of a $260 million DIP financing facility structured as $65 million in new money tied to a $195 million rollup of prepetition secured obligations on a 3-to-1 basis. Sleep Number's Chapter 11 filing was precipitated by a historic industry recession driven by a consumer shift toward e-commerce, diminishing profit margins, supply chain vulnerabilities, and the unpredictable impact of U.S. tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA).



On June 12, 2026, the company entered into a "stalking horse" Asset Purchase Agreement with SNBR, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc., pursuant to which the Purchaser agreed to purchase substantially all of the assets of the company for a purchase price of $415 million in cash and the assumption of certain liabilities, subject to certain potential purchase price adjustments.
 
The debtors obtained a $260 million superpriority priming DIP facility documented as the Fourteenth Amendment to the existing Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement, with U.S. Bank National Association as administrative agent.
 Prepetition counsel to the debtors is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick, Angela M. Libby, Stephen D. Piraino, Richard J. Steinberg, Sihui Ma, Mordechai Rivkin — 450 Lexington Avenue, New York, NY 10017). (__[Kroll Restructuring Administration](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber/)__)

The sale timeline is the defining pressure point for every unsecured creditor in this case. 
The company is seeking a July 2 hearing on bid procedures, a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, and a July 15 hearing to approve the sale, with the sale needing to close by July 31.
 
Parties in interest may investigate — but not prosecute — the prepetition liens and obligations within a $100,000 budget; the Challenge Deadline runs to the earlier of 60 days after committee formation (or 75 days after the Interim Order if no committee is appointed) and the commencement of the Sale Hearing.
 That mechanic is structurally punishing for any UCC seeking to run a meaningful lien investigation: if the committee was formed in the days following petition, the 60-day clock likely expires before or contemporaneous with the July 15 sale hearing, meaning any challenge to the 3-to-1 rollup must be filed under extreme time pressure or is waived.


The § 341 meeting of creditors is scheduled by Zoom conference on July 8, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET
 — the same day as the qualified bid deadline, which creates a meaningful compression problem for any creditor seeking to use the § 341 examination to surface information relevant to bid adequacy or collateral valuation.

The 572-store U.S. retail footprint creates a discrete landlord-creditor dynamic. 
Sleep Number has already been undertaking a review of its store footprint and, in connection with this process, will continue the work with the intention of maintaining the vast majority of locations based on profitability; A&G Real Estate Partners is assisting the company with this effort.
 
The assets to be acquired pursuant to the Asset Sale do not include, among other things, any executory leases or contracts that the Purchaser chooses to reject or are otherwise rejected in the Chapter 11 Cases.
 Landlords of stores not assumed by Sleep Country take rejection damages capped at the § 502(b)(6) formula (greater of one year's rent or 15% of remaining term rent, not to exceed three years) and will need to file proofs of claim before the bar date Kroll sets. 
The company cautions that trading in its securities is highly speculative; trading prices for company securities may bear little or no relationship to actual recovery by holders; the company expects that holders of common shares will experience a complete or significant loss on their investment.



Section 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code authorizes the United States Trustee to appoint an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, and the Committee represents the interests of, and acts on behalf of, all unsecured creditors.
 
Sleep Number reports that the number of creditors is in the range of 50–99
 — an unusually thin creditor matrix for a $672 million liability stack, which suggests the petition count may exclude product-warranty claimants, gift card holders, and individual consumers whose claims are not yet liquidated. Distressed investors tracking this case should watch whether a supplemental matrix amendment swells that number and whether the UCC moves to broaden the universe of represented constituencies.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology) — Post-Sale Posture, September 11 Bar Date, Plan Path


On March 15, 2026, each of the four debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 of the United States Bankruptcy Code; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371, before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.



The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. The filing was supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers that provides the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.



The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius; compounding these balance sheet pressures were stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.


The asset sale track has closed. 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors
, per a bankruptcy filing dated May 26, 2026. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock confirmed in a statement that its bid was recorded in an official Bankruptcy Court filing on May 26, 2026, and that a hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 The Veritaglobal key-dates register now shows September 11, 2026 as the next major deadline — consistent with a claims bar date — indicating the June 16 sale hearing produced an approval and the case has advanced to the plan-and-distribution phase. (__[Veritaglobal — BlockFills Key Dates](https://veritaglobal.net/BlockFills)__)

The economics for unsecured creditors are stark. 
BlockFills filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy reporting between $100 million and $500 million in liabilities but only between $50 million and $100 million in assets.
 The Keyrock purchase price of $3.25 million for the operating assets represents a fraction of the $145 million in general unsecured claims, and the recovery path for creditors not in the Ad Hoc Group depends entirely on what the liquidating trust captures from the estate's residual assets, the adversary proceeding against Artha Investment Partners LLC (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH), filed April 9, 2026), and any further avoidance actions the trustee pursues. (__[Veritaglobal — Artha adversary filing](https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260409000000000003)__)


The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt; the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Celsius's secured position at $4.8 million sits ahead of the general unsecured pool, but its lien is confined to specific collateral. Creditors with claims in the $145 million unsecured pool are effectively subordinate to Keyrock's assumption of "certain liabilities," and the precise scope of assumed liabilities in the final sale order will determine what remains in the estate for liquidating trust distributions. Claim traders should request that sale order before pricing any position.


In the weeks preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits were filed — the first on February 27, 2026 in the Southern District of New York, and the second on March 5, 2026 in the Northern District of Illinois — with courts in both matters granting temporary restraining orders that restricted the debtors' ability to conduct operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 The fate of those TRO-frozen assets and whether they were incorporated into the Keyrock sale or remain estate property is a material open question for creditor recovery analysis.

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC counsel: Morris James LLP, retention effective March 30, 2026, Dkt. 262) (__[Veritaglobal — Morris James retention order](https://veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260429000000000004)__) will be the principal fiduciary driving the plan confirmation and liquidating trust mechanics. Claims trading activity, if any, will appear in Rule 3001(e) transfer notices on the CourtListener docket (Docket ID: 72494188).

### Structural Notes for Distressed Investors — DIP Rollup Mechanics and § 506(c) Surcharge Risk

The Sleep Number DIP structure deserves analytical attention independent of that case. 
The Sleep Number debtors are requesting approval of a $260 million DIP financing facility with $65 million in new money tied to a $195 million rollup of prepetition secured obligations on a 3-to-1 basis.
 This ratio — three dollars of prepetition debt rolled up for every dollar of new money — is at the aggressive end of market practice. Post-*Hartford Underwriters Ins. Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A.*, 530 U.S. 1 (2000), § 506(c) surcharge of secured collateral is restricted to the trustee (and by extension, the UCC acting with trustee authority), but a waiver of § 506(c) rights is routinely extracted in DIP orders. Creditors' counsel should scrutinize whether the interim DIP order already contains that waiver, since the 60-day challenge deadline noted above may coincide with or precede the July 9 final DIP hearing. (__[Kroll Restructuring Administration — Sleep Number](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber/)__)

The 3-to-1 rollup also creates structural risk for unsecured creditors that the prepetition lender emerges from the sale with a secured claim that has been cleansed of any lien defects by the DIP order — a classic priming-lien problem where junior constituencies bear the cost of legitimizing a prepetition collateral package that might otherwise have been challengeable. 
The sale remains subject to higher or better bids, antitrust clearance, court approval, and a no-material adverse effect condition
 — meaning the auction on July 13 is a genuine opportunity for competing bidders to displace Sleep Country's $415 million stalking-horse bid and potentially improve the distribution pool.

**For creditors and distressed investors:** In *Sleep Number* (No. 26-11399), the July 8 qualified-bid deadline and July 8 § 341 meeting fall on the same day — file any statement of claim or competitive bid inquiry now and attend the creditors' meeting prepared to examine the debtor on DIP rollup terms, prepetition lender identity, and the lien investigation budget constraint; any UCC member should confirm that committee counsel has a plan to file or preserve § 506(c) and lien-challenge rights before the July 15 sale hearing forecloses them. In *BlockFills* (No. 26-10371), claim purchasers should obtain the June 16 sale order from Veritaglobal to verify the scope of liabilities assumed by Keyrock SA before pricing positions against the $145 million unsecured pool; the September 11 bar date is the next hard deadline, so claimants without filed proofs of claim must act before that date or face disallowance. In *Power Block Coin* (No. 24-23041), monitor Dkt. 519 for filing description and watch for any adversary proceedings that create avoidance-action recovery vectors. Across all three cases, Rule 3001(e) transfer notices on CourtListener are the most efficient real-time signal of claims-market activity.
