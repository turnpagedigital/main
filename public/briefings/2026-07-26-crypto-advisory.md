## Analysis & Developments

**The dominant new filing this week is Poolin Technology's Chapter 11 in New Jersey — a liquidating case carrying $163.7 million in frozen wallet IOUs against a $52 million stalking-horse bid, making recoveries for 11,700 retail claimants contingent entirely on auction competition. ### Poolin Technology Pte. Ltd. — Bankr. D.N.J. No. 26-18325 — Liquidating Chapter 11, IOU Creditor Math Is Brutal


The filing was made on July 22, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey under Case No. 26-18325. Poolin Technology Pte. Ltd. and its U.S. affiliates, Lonestar Dream Inc. and Lonestar Taproot LLC, are jointly listed in the case.
 
The filing is a liquidating Chapter 11, not a reorganization; the company has no intention of emerging as a going concern.



The filing details a $52 million stalking-horse bid from Thor CALAP LLC for the company's Pyote and Tarbush mining sites in West Texas. Poolin's prepetition liabilities stand at $173.1 million, including $163.7 million in unsecured IOUs owed to roughly 11,700 Poolin Wallet users after withdrawals were frozen in 2022.
 The arithmetic is unsparing: 
the signed offers equal just 31.8% of wallet IOUs in gross arithmetic, before estate boundaries, liens, and bankruptcy costs.


The structural story behind the IOU liability tracks a familiar 2022 pattern. 
In mid-2022, sharp declines in bitcoin prices triggered margin calls from lenders, forcing Poolin Wallet to pledge digital assets as collateral. In September 2022, the company declared a liquidity crisis and halted withdrawals, replacing customer deposits with IOUs. A further crypto price drop in late 2022 led to collateral liquidations by lenders.
 The operational wind-down was complete before the petition: 
Poolin permanently halted all mining and hosting operations on July 10, 2026, retaining only a minimal workforce to secure equipment and facilitate the asset sale process.


The stalking-horse structure allocates value between two sites: 
$37 million for the Tarbush assets (including assumed liabilities) and $15 million for the Pyote site (power rights, equipment).
 
The bids followed a three-month marketing process that contacted more than 335 potential buyers, including AI and high-performance compute operators.
 
As public miners pivot power assets toward AI infrastructure, Poolin's Texas sites could attract data-center buyers seeking cheap power in an undersupplied market
 — which is the operative variable for whether auction competition moves the floor materially. A prior attempted sale underscores the execution risk: 
a proposed $49 million sale of the Texas business to China Green Agriculture, announced in December 2023, never closed.



Chief Restructuring Officer Michael DuFrayne estimates prepetition obligations at approximately $173.1 million.
 
Zhibiao Pan owns 100% of the equity interests in Poolin Technology Pte. Ltd. and Lonestar Dream Inc.; Lonestar Taproot LLC is wholly owned by Lonestar Dream.
 The equity concentration in a single individual, combined with the 2022 lender collateral liquidations, makes insider fraudulent-transfer analysis a material open question — though no adversary proceedings have been reported to date (unverified as of this run).

The customer-property characterization question that defined Celsius and Voyager is live here too. Poolin Wallet IOUs are denominated as unsecured promissory notes, meaning customers are general unsecured creditors — there is no trust or bailment theory in the current record. 
Recovery for the 11,700 IOU holders will depend almost entirely on what the Texas power and mining assets fetch at auction.
 With only 
$173.1 million in liabilities against $1.2 million cash
, the estate has no liquidity buffer. This is the relevant distinction from Celsius: no § 541 property-of-the-estate dispute is in play because Poolin never took the position that wallet assets were segregated. The fight here is purely arithmetic — auction price vs. administrative claims priority.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Post-Confirmation Administration


On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Disclosure Statement Order scheduling confirmation and establishing the voting deadline, claim objection procedures, and record date pursuant to Docket No. 388.
 
The Confirmation Hearing was set for July 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET
 — that date has now passed, and the docket reflects six new entries in the 72 hours through July 24, consistent with post-hearing order entry and administrative follow-through. The docket does not yet show a confirmed plan order in the tracked-case data as of this run; confirmation status should be verified against Docket Entry 538 (filed July 24) when accessible.

The asset sale that forms the plan's economic backbone closed earlier: 
a Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group
 — identified as 
Keyrock SA, which was declared the Successful Bidder for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.


The capital structure context matters for unsecured creditor recovery expectations. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt. The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against a $3.25 million sale price, unsecured recoveries are structurally thin unless the plan captures additional litigation value — particularly from the two pre-petition customer actions.

The pre-petition litigation backdrop is relevant to plan releases. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 27, 2026); and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al., Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. March 5, 2026). The plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief, and the courts granted temporary restraining orders that significantly restricted the Debtors' ability to conduct business operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge had granted conditional approval to the Chapter 11 plan disclosure statement, noting that issues over the opt-out release mechanism in the proposal can be argued at confirmation.
 Whether those releases survived confirmation under the post-*Purdue Pharma* third-party release standard is the remaining legal question here — and the answer directly affects whether D&O liability is extinguished or preserved for dissenting claimants.

The case's foundational § 541 question — 
whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested
 — likely required resolution at or before confirmation. 
The company's liquidity crisis was attributable to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults, specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy, alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution, $900 Million, July 31, 2026


FTX will release roughly $900 million to eligible creditors on July 31, 2026 under a court-approved restructuring plan, marking the fifth distribution and a significant milestone in the exchange's bankruptcy recovery.
 
Recipients who completed verification by the June 16 record date will receive funds via BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer within three business days, and the payout lifts cumulative recoveries to 105% for Dotcom and U.S. customer entitlement claims and 103% for general unsecured and digital asset loan claims.



This round also includes a first: preferred equity holders will receive payments, expanding the circle of people finally getting something back from the wreckage Sam Bankman-Fried left behind.
 
The bankruptcy estate distributed $2.2 billion in March and has now returned nearly $10 billion to creditors and other claimants.


The claims reserve position has also shifted. 
FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release cash to be distributed to holders of allowed claims.
 
For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection
 — a mechanics point that has stranded some claim purchasers who completed transfers after the record date.

The claim-valuation-date dispute embedded in FTX — petition-date fiat value vs. in-kind crypto restitution — remains the legacy complaint. 
Under FTX's court-approved reorganization plan, billions of dollars have been returned to creditors based on the November 2022 value of customer assets
, which crystallized claims at the trough of the bear market. Claimants who held Bitcoin or Solana in 2022 and received petition-date fiat values have absorbed the full opportunity cost of the subsequent bull cycle in silence. The plan is confirmed and distributions are in progress; that fight is over in Delaware, though its resonance for future crypto insolvency plan design is lasting.

### Cross-Docket Pattern Recognition: The IOU/Custodial-Deposit Taxonomy

Poolin's IOU structure, BlockFills' customer deposit exposure, and the legacy Celsius TOS litigation all sit on the same fault line under 11 U.S.C. § 541: are custodied crypto assets property of the estate or held in trust? The outcomes to date have sorted by contract language and operational commingling. Celsius's general pool accounts were held to be estate property; segregated custody accounts were treated differently. BlockFills' first-day record does not disclose whether Vision Trader platform deposits were ring-fenced or commingled — that question was central to the § 541 fight and would have been resolved at confirmation. Poolin's IOU structure forecloses any trust theory from the outset: the notes are express obligations, not bailment instruments. The practical takeaway for future claimant due diligence: the moment a crypto platform converts a deposit to a promissory note, it collapses the segregation argument and relegates the customer to general unsecured status under § 541.

Sector bifurcation is also sharpening. 
The sector is bifurcating: large-cap operators (MARA, Hut 8, IREN, Bitfarms) are pivoting aggressively toward AI and high-performance compute, while smaller and mid-tier operators carrying legacy custodial products and leveraged equipment purchases are getting cleared out.
 Poolin is 
the second Chapter 11 from a mining operator in 2026; NFN8 Group filed for Chapter 11 in the Western District of Texas on February 2, citing a catastrophic fire at its Crystal City, Texas, facility as the primary catalyst.

## Recommended Actions

Counsel and claims-holders tracking the Poolin matter (Bankr. D.N.J. No. 26-18325) should monitor bid procedures and auction scheduling immediately — the stalking-horse floor of $52 million is the recovery ceiling absent competitive bidding, and the 11,700 IOU holders have no contractual or trust-based argument to improve their position relative to secured and administrative claims; the only lever is auction premium. In BlockFills (Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371, Judge Horan), creditors should pull Docket Entry 538 to confirm whether a confirmation order has been entered and whether the opt-out third-party release language survived intact; D&O claimants who did not affirmatively opt out may have forfeited claims against former directors and officers depending on the final confirmation order language. On FTX, claim purchasers should verify transfer reflection on the official claims register and confirm KYC/tax documentation status with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer before July 31 — the June 16 record date has passed and late-processing claims will miss this distribution round. Across all three matters, the § 541 customer-property question continues to drive recovery stratification; counsel structuring new crypto lending or custody products should treat commingled pool accounts as estate-property risk until further appellate guidance closes the gap left by the Celsius district court rulings.
