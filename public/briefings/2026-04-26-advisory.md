# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | April 26, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

With Anthropic's second $300 million settlement installment due in four days on April 30, the Bartz v. Anthropic settlement enters its most consequential week since preliminary approval, and the stakes for the May 14 fairness hearing before Judge Martínez-Olguín continue to sharpen as legal commentary proliferates and congressional momentum accelerates around the very AI-training copyright questions the settlement leaves doctrinally unresolved. The Kluwer Copyright Blog's newly published comprehensive analysis of the settlement structure — one of the first serious international legal treatments of Bartz — underscores the $1.5 billion fund's dual significance: it simultaneously validates the theory that unauthorized reproduction of copyrighted works from shadow libraries like LibGen and PiLiMi for AI training constitutes actionable infringement, while leaving entirely untouched Judge Alsup's August 2025 summary judgment finding that training on lawfully acquired copies is transformative fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107. ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/)) The Kluwer analysis pointedly identifies the publisher-versus-author allocation formula as the single most consequential unresolved issue at the May 14 hearing, noting that the six-plus unsealed objections — including Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's structural challenge — collectively argue that publishers may claim roughly 50% or more of the total fund despite the lawsuit's author-centric origins, a distribution pattern that tests the Rule 23(e)(2) adequacy-of-representation requirements and could prompt Judge Martínez-Olguín to condition final approval on allocation adjustments or supplemental notice. Writer Beware's April update confirms the post-claim timeline: the settlement administrator will calculate distributions by June 11, 2026, with payment disbursement expected to begin in June 2026, subject to change depending on whether appeals are filed from the fairness hearing. ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2026/04/10/anthropic-copyright-settlement-april-update/))

The legislative landscape has shifted materially this week following the conclusion of the Recording Academy's 25th GRAMMYS on the Hill (April 21–23) and the Blackburn-Welch roundtable that convened more than 20 artists — including Grace Potter, Taylor Hanson, and Molly Tuttle — in Washington to advocate for the bipartisan NO FAKES Act and TRAIN Act. ([Blackburn Senate Office](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/4/ai/blackburn-welch-hold-roundtable-with-over-20-artists-who-support-no-fakes-act-and-train-act-to-protect-creators-from-ai-harms)) Senator Blackburn is now announcing growing momentum for the comprehensive TRUMP AMERICA AI Act, the 291-page discussion draft released on March 18 that, among its sweeping provisions, would legislatively declare that an AI model's unauthorized reproduction, copying, or processing of copyrighted works for the purpose of training does not constitute fair use under the Copyright Act — a provision that would effectively override the pending judicial determinations in both the Concord v. Anthropic summary judgment motion (hearing July 15 before Judge Lee) and the OpenAI MDL's anticipated fair-use briefing expected this summer. ([Blackburn Senate Office](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/4/ai/what-they-are-saying-blackburn-announces-growing-momentum-for-trump-america-ai-act)) ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/03/ai-legislation-copyright-hollywood-trump-1236759660/)) The legislative tension is now three-directional: the White House's March 20 National AI Policy Framework recommends a hands-off approach that defers to courts on fair use; the CLEAR Act (S. 3813) from Senators Schiff and Curtis focuses on transparency and disclosure with a $5,000 per-work penalty for noncompliance; and the TRUMP AMERICA AI Act would resolve the fair-use question legislatively in favor of rights-holders — creating a scenario in which the Concord PSJ ruling on July 15 could arrive into a rapidly shifting statutory landscape that either renders it moot or elevates it as the definitive judicial statement before Congress acts.

Norton Rose Fulbright's newly published 2026 AI copyright litigation update provides the most authoritative law-firm synthesis of the current docket posture, confirming that the OpenAI MDL's 108 million log production (20M ordered January 5 plus 78M and 10M additional reservoirs ordered March 9) represents the largest AI-related discovery production in any copyright case, and that the Supreme Court's March 2 denial of certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter has definitively settled the human-authorship requirement as a prerequisite for copyright registration, closing the door on AI-as-author theories while leaving open whether AI-assisted works with sufficient human creative control may qualify for protection. ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026)) Meanwhile, the output-side liability framework continues to develop along the lines identified in last week's advisory: the doctrinal pincer created by Judge McMahon's Advance Local v. Cohere "substitutive summaries" ruling (expanding direct infringement beyond verbatim copying) and the Supreme Court's unanimous Cox Communications reversal (narrowing contributory liability to inducement or services without substantial noninfringing uses) is pushing the entire AI copyright battlefield toward output-side direct infringement theories — precisely the theory that Anthropic's Concord MSJ is attempting to foreclose and that the 108 million ChatGPT logs in the OpenAI MDL are designed to prove. ([New York Law Journal](https://www.law.com/newyorklawjournal/2026/04/20/recent-decisions-reshape-copyright-liability-for-ai-platforms-and-their-users/)) ([Copyright Lately](https://copyrightlately.com/court-rules-ai-news-summaries-may-infringe-copyright/))

The Mozilla Foundation's two-day Copyforward symposium at USC Gould School of Law concluded yesterday, bringing together legal scholars, filmmakers, guild representatives, and entertainment lawyers in what the Foundation described as Hollywood's most significant public engagement with the AI-copyright nexus since the SAG-AFTRA negotiations. ([USC Gould](https://events.law.usc.edu/event/copyforward)) The symposium's timing — bracketed between the GRAMMYS on the Hill advocacy push and the approaching Bartz fairness hearing — underscores the extent to which the creative industries are coordinating across entertainment, publishing, and music to build both judicial and legislative pressure on AI developers, with the RIAA, NMPA, AAP, Authors Guild, and News/Media Alliance all maintaining active amicus positions across the Concord, OpenAI MDL, and Bartz dockets while simultaneously supporting the three-bill legislative package on Capitol Hill.

## Recommended Actions

Class members who submitted claims by the March 30 deadline should verify their confirmation status through anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com and calendar the April 30 installment (a bellwether for Anthropic's ongoing financial commitment under Rule 23(e)(2)(D)) and the May 14 fairness hearing, which will be accessible via Zoom and at which the court will address the unsealed objections, the revised fee petition of $187.5 million (12.5% of the fund), and the publisher-versus-author allocation formula that the Kluwer analysis identifies as the settlement's most vulnerable structural element. Rights-holders with works across multiple AI training datasets should track the TRUMP AMERICA AI Act's progress through the Senate Commerce Committee, as the bill's provision declaring AI training not fair use — if enacted — would fundamentally alter the litigation calculus for every pending case, particularly the Concord PSJ hearing on July 15 and the OpenAI MDL's anticipated fair-use motions; practitioners advising AI developers should prepare compliance assessments under both the current judicial framework and the hypothetical statutory framework, since the legislative timeline could produce enacted law before several pending motions are resolved. AI developers subject to the CLEAR Act's proposed $5,000 per-work penalty and the TRAIN Act's transparency requirements should accelerate training-data provenance audits, as the Blackburn-Welch roundtable's bipartisan momentum suggests that at minimum the disclosure-oriented provisions will advance even if the fair-use override faces resistance from the White House's hands-off posture. Practitioners monitoring output-side liability should integrate the Advance Local v. Cohere "substitutive summaries" doctrine into their compliance assessments immediately, as Judge McMahon's rejection of a bright-line copying threshold means that summarization, paraphrasing, and RAG workflows across the industry face direct infringement exposure without regard to whether the outputs reproduce verbatim text.
