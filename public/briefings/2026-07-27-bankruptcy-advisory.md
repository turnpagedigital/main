### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Sale Approved; Closing Imminent

The week's pivotal development on this desk is the Sleep Number § 363 sale, which moved from auction to court approval in less than a week. 
After a competitive auction, the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York approved the sale of substantially all of Sleep Number Corporation's assets and ongoing business operations to Sleep Country Canada Inc. on July 20, 2026.
 The approved consideration substantially exceeded the stalking-horse floor: 
Sleep Country's original stalking-horse proposal contemplated approximately $415 million in cash plus assumed liabilities; following the competitive auction process, Sleep Country increased its proposal to a court-approved bid valued at approximately $701 million, with $529.5 million payable in cash.
 
The purchase was made through SNBR Inc., a wholly owned Sleep Country subsidiary, which had served as the stalking-horse bidder throughout.


The judge presiding over the case is the Honorable Kyu Young Paek — 
the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 by Judge Paek, with the transaction undertaken pursuant to § 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code.
 
Sleep Number announced court approval on July 20, and the sale remains subject to customary closing conditions and is expected to close by July 31, 2026.
 The timing matters: closure this week would terminate the DIP facility, which 
provided up to $260 million in financing, comprising $65 million in new-money superpriority term loan commitments and up to $195 million in roll-up loans converting prepetition credit facility obligations.


For creditors and claimants, the critical post-sale question is what the residual estate looks like after DIP payoff. 
The Sleep Number petition disclosed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion against liabilities exceeding $1 billion.
 
The New York bankruptcy court's approval was conditioned on an agreement to postpone certain potential future severance payments until the court can determine their treatment
 — a signal that administrative and priority claims sequencing remains open. The gap between the $701 million sale price and the $1B+ liability base means unsecured creditors face structural subordination to the DIP lenders' superpriority claims and any assumed cure and severance obligations. Creditors monitoring distribution mechanics should watch the wind-down plan filing, which has not yet been docketed as of this writing. The § 341 meeting had been continued and was held by Zoom on July 24, 2026 — 
the Meeting of Creditors was continued and held by Zoom conference on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET.

### BlockFills — Customer Property Characterization: The Recurring Analytical Frame

The BlockFills case continues to present the question that has defined the crypto-insolvency wave since Celsius: do customer-deposited digital assets constitute property of the estate under § 541 of the Bankruptcy Code, or do they remain property of the customer? 
Customers, including the seventh-largest depositor Dominion Capital, moved quickly to sue prepetition, with Dominion arguing that when it deposited assets on the BlockFills platform the onboarding agreement specifically stated that all customer deposits would be completely segregated from the company's general balance sheet.
 
Both prepetition courts granted temporary restraining orders restricting the debtors' ability to exercise control over certain assets; the automatic stay was then invoked to halt further litigation and prevent individual customers from pursuing assets outside the bankruptcy process.


The resolution of this issue under the plan — whether through a negotiated allocation to the NewCo track or through the liquidating trust — is the single most important variable for retail creditors. No court ruling on the § 541 characterization appears in the public docket as of the most recent sync. Distressed investors acquiring claims in this case should price that uncertainty accordingly. 
Keyrock's acquisition of substantially all of BlockFills' assets is subject to court approval and appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid
 — the regulatory overlay (particularly any FCA or MiCA implications given Keyrock's Brussels base) adds a closing-condition risk that is not purely domestic.

### Broader Sector Signals

The Sleep Number outcome illustrates how a well-constructed § 363 process with a committed stalking-horse can compress a retail-insolvency timeline to under 40 days from petition to approved sale while still generating a competitive auction. 
Sleep Country's original $415 million cash proposal was improved substantially following the competitive process
 — a roughly 27% increase from opening floor to approved consideration, suggesting real price discovery occurred. For distressed investors, the lesson is that stalking-horse credit bid discounts in retail cases remain contestable where the brand has genuine going-concern value and a strategic acquirer exists with cross-border synergies.

On the BlockFills side, the Keyrock $3.25 million purchase price for substantially all operating assets against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a recovery rate on the § 363 sale consideration alone well below 3 cents on the dollar before distribution. The plan's liquidating trust mechanic and the NewCo equity allocation to the ad hoc customer group represent the primary recovery levers above that floor. Claims trading is the natural pressure valve: any Rule 3001 transfer notices filed on the docket after the July 13 confirmation hearing would signal whether institutional buyers are pricing in a plan-based recovery premium. The September 11 bar date creates a secondary market window for late-filed claimants.
