### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova — DOJ Remission Program Closes Tonight (S.D.N.Y.)

This is the most operationally urgent item on the desk. 
The FBI urged eligible investors to submit petitions through the official OneCoin Remission Program before the June 30, 2026, deadline.
 The underlying facts: 
victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency; the Department of Justice filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York; several key figures involved in the scheme have been sentenced and the Department pursued criminal forfeiture of property derived from the proceeds; more than $40 million in forfeited assets are currently available for victim compensation.


Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is administering the remission process. 
According to the FBI, victims who purchased OneCoin packages between the fourth quarter of 2014 and the fourth quarter of 2019 and suffered a net financial loss may qualify for compensation.
 The petition portal is at www.onecoinremission.com. The structural tension for claims investors is acute: the $40 million recovery pool sits against a $4 billion loss figure — a recovery rate below 1% of total victim losses. That arithmetic makes secondary claims pricing here largely academic for claims-fund purposes, but the tonight-close date is a hard administrative event that forecloses any petition not already in Kroll's possession.

The cross-border enforcement picture remains active independent of the remission program. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP £8,590,200.92, which had been found, in German legal proceedings, to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The proceedings in Guernsey commenced following mutual legal assistance requests from the Public Prosecutors Office in Bielefeld, Germany; those requests sought the assistance of the Guernsey authorities to restrain the funds and then to forfeit them in accordance with Guernsey law.
 
In early 2026, a total of £8,590,200.92 was forfeited from the Guernsey-registered property structures; these funds will be returned to Germany to compensate global victims of the OneCoin fraud.
 That recovery — proceeds from two Kensington flats owned through Guernsey-registered structures — illustrates the continuing productivity of the MLAT/mutual-legal-assistance architecture for cross-border tracing, even when the primary defendant (Ignatova) remains a fugitive. 
The FBI and IRS-CI continue their investigative pursuit of Ignatova, an FBI Top Ten Fugitive, alongside partners at the Southern District of New York.

### Stanford Financial Receivership (N.D. Tex., SEC v. Stanford Int'l Bank Ltd., Judge David C. Godbey) — Final Distribution in Execution

The Stanford receivership has entered its terminal distribution phase after more than 15 years of recovery work. 
On March 9, 2026, Receiver Ralph Janvey filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend (together, the "Final Distribution Orders").
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups; payments are being issued on a rolling basis and the process is expected to take several weeks.


Execution of that schedule is ongoing. 
On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver had issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups
 pursuant to the court-approved Final Distribution Plan.

Swiss ancillary proceedings have closed. 
On December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded; as a result, the JLs transferred to the Receiver $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol, and advised that no further distributions of funds are expected from that channel.
 A separate claimant group (COViSAL) has raised unresolved queries about whether those $8.1 million in Swiss funds have been incorporated into the Final Distribution Schedule amounts, and the Receiver had not publicly responded to that inquiry as of early April. For claims investors holding Stanford CD instruments, the key remaining variable is the pace at which the rolling payment schedule completes and whether any reverted/uncashed checks from prior interim distributions generate supplemental redistribution tranches.

One practical friction point for international claimants: 
beginning December 11, 2025, the Receivership was unable to arrange delivery of distribution payments via physical checks to Venezuelan addresses; however, as of March 11, 2026, the Receivership is once again able to arrange courier delivery of physical checks to Venezuela.

### Madoff / BLMIS (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, Judge Stuart M. Bernstein) — Post-Seventeenth Distribution Interim Period

The BLMIS liquidation remains the largest and most operationally mature Ponzi recovery in U.S. history. The seventeenth pro-rata interim distribution — the most recent — commenced on February 27, 2026. 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC); the SIPA Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.



Eligible BLMIS customers have now received approximately 72.848 percent of their allowed claims from the seventeen interim distributions, plus advances from the Securities Investor Protection Corporation of up to $500,000 per allowed claim.
 
As of January 31, 2026, and since his appointment in December 2008, the SIPA Trustee has amassed approximately $15.366 billion as a result of recoveries and settlement agreements.



As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation
 in the form of cash advances. The residual recovery gap — roughly 27.15% of allowed claims outstanding — will close only through continued litigation and settlement activity. Picard has signaled additional recoveries in the pipeline, but timing of an eighteenth distribution has not been publicly announced. For secondary-market buyers of BLMIS allowed claims, the pricing calculus remains a function of: (a) the residual litigation pool, (b) the time-value discount on an indeterminate additional distribution cycle, and (c) the fee-sharing structure under which all trustee, legal, and accounting fees are borne by SIPC rather than the Customer Fund — a structural advantage that improves net recovery relative to typical receivership estates where professional fees erode distributable assets.


All trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC
 — not drawn from the Customer Fund. This remains a material structural distinction relative to the Stanford receivership (where Baker Botts professional fees are funded from recovered assets) and should be priced into claim valuations accordingly.

### SEC Enforcement Posture — "First Principles" Frame and Cross-Border Task Force

The broader enforcement backdrop against which new receivership appointments will be evaluated has clarified. 
Chairman Atkins has promised that the SEC's enforcement program will return to "first principles" by focusing on fraud and investor harm, such as cases involving offering frauds, insider trading, accounting and financial frauds, and investment advisers' breaches of fiduciary duties.
 
In September 2025, the Commission formed the Cross-Border Task Force to help address the serious threat that fraudsters located abroad pose to U.S. investors and markets, and several enforcement actions from fiscal year 2025 demonstrate the Commission's commitment to pursuing transnational fraud that harms American investors.



During fiscal year 2025, the Commission filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions and 69 "follow-on" administrative proceedings, obtaining orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.
 The enforcement posture shift — toward fraud/market-integrity "bread and butter" cases and away from crypto-regulatory perimeter enforcement — is constructive for recovery-desk pipeline monitoring: offering frauds generating federal receiver appointments remain a priority lane, while the Cross-Border Task Force increases the probability of 28 U.S.C. § 1782 discovery tools being deployed in multi-jurisdictional matters. Claims investors should treat the Task Force's formation as a forward indicator of enforcement actions with cross-border asset-tracing components, which historically generate better pro-rata recovery rates (as the OneCoin Guernsey forfeiture illustrates) than purely domestic receiverships.

On Power Block Coin (No. 24-23041), monitor CourtListener docket ID 68873409 for adversary proceeding filings, which would be the primary trigger for § 548 clawback exposure analysis for net winners in that estate. Finally, the SEC Cross-Border Task Force — active since September 2025 — warrants ongoing monitoring as an early-warning signal for new enforcement-driven receivership appointments with international asset-tracing components; those cases are the desk's next-generation pipeline.
