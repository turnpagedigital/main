**The controlling development this week remains the same as last week's close: Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín has not yet issued a final approval order in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), and the settlement now sits in a brief but consequential interregnum.**

As of the June 2026 reporting cycle, the court had declined to approve the settlement, and final approval remains pending.
 For rights-holder counsel and claims investors, the operative question is no longer whether the deal survives objection — it almost certainly does — but when the final approval order issues, which starts the clock on Anthropic's $300 million first installment obligation and the Administrator's distribution calculations.

### Bartz v. Anthropic: Settlement Mechanics, Distribution Timeline, and the Fee-Reduction Posture


The settlement's core monetary structure is a $1.5 billion total fund; plaintiffs' counsel originally sought 25% — $375 million — in attorneys' fees.
 
In papers urging final approval, class counsel revised the fee request down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 That reduction to 12.5% of the fund is notable: it reflects both judicial pressure and the strategic calculus of a settlement administration that cannot afford appellate delay. 
Separately, Anthropic argued that the $75 million in fees that nonclass counsel requested is "far too high" given "scant justification" for the request.


On the claims side, participation rates vastly exceeded projections. 
Class counsel filed papers reporting that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3% rate — far surpassing the 54% rate reported in the March 19, 2026 motion for final approval, indicating that a large number of claims came in at the deadline.
 
The high claim rate means that earlier forecasts of a larger per-work payout will not materialize; the actual payout will likely be closer to the original $3,000 per work figure.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson confirmed at the May 14 fairness hearing that the per-work payout will be approximately $3,100.


The distribution waterfall, per the settlement agreement and the IPG-published timeline, is structured across four installments from Anthropic: 
October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 
The third distribution wave and Anthropic's $450 million Schedule Payment 1 are pegged to September 25, 2026, twelve months after the September 25, 2025 preliminary approval order.
 Rights-holder counsel should note that 
payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.
 An objector appeal to the Ninth Circuit — even a non-meritorious one — would hold disbursement. 
Anthropic's post-hearing supplemental brief urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs, arguing the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect."
 How Judge Martínez-Olguín resolves the late opt-out question will appear in the final approval order and could influence the scope of the release.

**The structural limitation of the Bartz settlement warrants repetition for new readers:** 
Bartz is not a settlement about AI training. In June 2025, Judge William Alsup ruled that Anthropic's use of books to train its LLMs was fair use, and that Anthropic was entitled to scan lawfully acquired books. Nothing about the settlement disturbs those rulings.
 
What remained unresolved was Anthropic's potential liability for downloading and storing files from LibGen and Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi). Upon final approval, Anthropic must destroy the pirated copies and pay the $1.5 billion into the settlement fund.
 The settlement releases Anthropic from liability for past piracy; it creates no forward-looking licensing obligation and binds no other AI developer.

### Concord Music Group v. Anthropic: Two-Case Docket, Cross-MSJ Approaching

The music-publisher docket has fragmented into parallel tracks, both before Judge Eumi K. Lee in the Northern District of California. 
*Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal.), is assigned to Judge Eumi K. Lee with Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen on discovery matters.
 The first-filed action (*Concord I*) has a significantly more advanced posture than the tracked-case ground truth previously reflected.


Concord I has completed fact and expert discovery and is approaching summary judgment. Anthropic filed a motion to stay the second-filed action (*Concord II*, No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK) set for hearing on May 20, 2026 in Courtroom 7 of the San Jose Division.
 
Concord I's threshold legal issues — specifically whether Anthropic's use of song lyrics as training data constitutes fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 — will determine the viability of most if not all of the publishers' claims in Concord II and could have preclusive effect.
 Anthropic's briefing schedule in Concord I had dispositive motions fully briefed by June 8, 2026 per Dkt. 587. 
The parties' summary judgment motions in Concord I were set to be fully briefed by June 8, 2026.


The publishers' theory in Concord I is that Anthropic's use of copyrighted lyrics to train Claude constitutes infringement because, unlike legally acquired books in *Bartz*, this was 
use of lyrics to build a competing commercial product — the § 107(4) market-substitution argument — which precludes a fair-use defense.
 The defense's posture tracks *Bartz*: training is transformative and therefore fair use. The court's resolution will be the first merits ruling on lyrics-as-training-data in the Ninth Circuit and will have direct bearing on licensing valuations across the music-AI sector.


In a separate Concord II filing, the major music publishers have brought a second $3 billion suit against Anthropic for newly discovered mass infringement of sheet music and songbooks.
 Anthropic's motion to stay Concord II pending Concord I is the operative procedural question; if granted, the stay consolidates the fair-use question in a single track and defers the sheet-music claims until Concord I is resolved — a favorable outcome for Anthropic's litigation management posture.

An episode worth flagging for credibility-on-counsel purposes: 
a Latham & Watkins associate representing Anthropic confirmed that she used Anthropic's own Claude.ai tool to help draft an expert's declaration that included an erroneous citation.
 
A California federal magistrate judge partially struck the expert report, calling the issue "serious," but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 The practical consequence was limited, but the episode signals the evidentiary risk when AI-assisted drafting meets adversarial expert practice.

### In re OpenAI Copyright MDL, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.): Discovery Advancing, Output-Log Preservation Order Affirmed


The OpenAI MDL (No. 1:25-md-03143) was last updated in CourtListener as of July 2, 2026, and is assigned to Judge Sidney H. Stein with Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang (S.D.N.Y.).
 
In April 2025, the JPML consolidated twelve cases against OpenAI originating in the Southern District of New York and the Northern District of California; the MDL encompasses class actions by authors, news organizations, DMCA-focused suits, and a case by an online video creator.


The most consequential procedural development in this docket is the output-log preservation order. 
When Magistrate Judge Wang issued a May 13, 2025 preservation order requiring OpenAI to retain all ChatGPT conversation logs — affecting over 400 million users worldwide — the presiding judge subsequently affirmed it, representing the first time a court has mandated mass preservation of AI-generated content at that scale.
 For rights-holder counsel, the order is significant because preserved output logs are the evidentiary predicate for claims that ChatGPT reproduced or closely paraphrased copyrighted works at scale — directly relevant to § 17 U.S.C. § 504 statutory damages exposure.


In a December 2025 order, the court granted OpenAI's motion to dismiss claims 4 and 5 of the Ziff Davis complaint in part, and granted OpenAI's motion to stay as to models not already within the MDL's scope — specifically the o1, o1 mini, o1-pro, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.5, o3, o3-mini, o4-mini, and GPT-5 models.
 The model-scope limitation is a meaningful win for OpenAI: it keeps the core discovery dispute focused on legacy training datasets rather than expanding into newer architectures, limiting immediate exposure while buying time before the fair-use question is briefed.

### Cross-Docket Framework: What Bartz's $3,113-Per-Work Benchmark Means for Parallel Litigation

The *Bartz* settlement number has already begun functioning as an industry pricing floor. 
Anthropic settled for $1.5 billion covering approximately 482,000 works, at an implied rate of roughly $3,113 per work.
 
With a public settlement benchmark now on the record, legal teams have a number to model worst-case exposure against when negotiating indemnification caps with AI vendors.
 For the music-publisher plaintiffs in *Concord I*, who are pursuing claims under § 504 statutory damages, the leverage calculus is different: statutory damages for willful infringement under § 504(c)(2) run from $750 to $150,000 per work, and the publishers' Exhibit A to the original complaint listed thousands of works. The *Bartz* per-work figure, grounded in piracy liability rather than training-data use, will be argued as a floor — but the statutory damages ceiling for a willfulness finding dwarfs it.


There are dozens of other AI copyright suits at varying stages — including consolidated actions against OpenAI and Microsoft, and active putative class actions against Meta, Apple, Salesforce, and Adobe — and several appear to be adopting the strategy that proved effective in *Bartz*: rather than challenging AI training as such, focusing on underlying piracy and infringing copies.


On human authorship, the Supreme Court resolved one front of this docket in March. 
On March 2, 2026, the Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, confirming that material must have human authorship to be copyrightable under 17 U.S.C. § 411 and related provisions.
 That ruling forecloses AI-output copyright claims by developers but has no bearing on the training-data infringement theories at issue in *Bartz*, *Concord*, and the OpenAI MDL.

Internationally, the divergence is widening. 
Germany's GEMA ruling reached the opposite conclusion from *Bartz* on the training question for song lyrics used to train ChatGPT.
 
In India, the Delhi High Court reserved judgment on ANI v. OpenAI on March 27, 2026; the ruling is expected to set India's first legal doctrine on AI training and copyright.
 Rights-holder counsel with cross-border portfolios should treat the international landscape as materially unsettled.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claims investors should take three immediate steps: first, confirm that any work in the *Bartz* class list was claimed before the March 30, 2026 deadline — the 91.3% participation rate means the distribution pool is nearly fully subscribed, and late-filed claims are almost certainly barred; second, monitor the *Bartz* docket (CourtListener No. 69058235) for the final approval order, which triggers Anthropic's $300 million second installment due April 30, 2026 per ¶ 2.1(a)(ii) of the settlement agreement, and the September 25, 2026 $450 million scheduled payment — both disbursements are contingent on no pending Ninth Circuit appeal; third, map client catalog exposure to the *Concord I* cross-MSJ schedule (dispositive motions fully briefed as of June 8, 2026, before Judge Lee in San Jose Courtroom 7), since the fair-use ruling in that case will be the first § 107 merits decision on lyrics-as-training-data in the Ninth Circuit and will reset licensing negotiation leverage across the music-AI sector — portfolios with heavy song lyric holdings should be preparing for either outcome. For any client involved in the OpenAI MDL (No. 1:25-md-03143, S.D.N.Y., Judge Stein), ensure document preservation protocols are consistent with Magistrate Judge Wang's affirmed output-log preservation order of May 13, 2025, which covers ChatGPT conversation logs across the entire user base.
