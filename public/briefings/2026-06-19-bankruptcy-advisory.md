### I. BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group — Post-Sale-Hearing Posture; Plan Confirmation Targeted July 13


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities, operating under the trade name "BlockFills," filed for Chapter 11 protection on March 15, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), before Judge Thomas M. Horan.
 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


The sale track has now largely run. 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors, as set out in an official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The June 16 sale-approval hearing before Judge Horan — the critical gating event — has passed as of today's advisory. Confirmation of a plan is separately targeted. 
Reliz is operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around a sale process and a plan path, with confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.


For creditors, the economics are stark. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc. and three affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 With Keyrock's $3.25 million asset-purchase price as the principal liquidity event — representing recovery on a balance sheet gap in excess of $50 million at the low end — general unsecured creditors holding the $145 million in claims face recovery rates almost certainly in the low single digits absent a meaningful distribution from non-operating assets or litigation. The Veritaglobal docket portal confirms a September 11, 2026 deadline (4:00 p.m. ET) as the next major milestone listed in the case, consistent with a post-confirmation wind-down posture. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
On February 6, 2026, the company publicly announced a broader suspension of deposits and withdrawals, communicating with customers through written updates and video conferences.


The case also carries a live adversary proceeding: the debtors filed Adv. Pro. No. 26-50224 (TMH) early in the case, seeking to enjoin litigation filed against them in the S.D.N.Y. (Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026) and the N.D. Ill. (filed March 5, 2026), both alleging misappropriation of customer funds. 
The courts in both matters granted temporary restraining orders that restricted the debtors' ability to conduct operations and exercise control over certain assets.
 The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors was appointed effective March 30, 2026, with Morris James as local counsel (Dkt. No. 262, filed April 29, 2026). Distressed investors tracking Rule 3001 claim-transfer notices should monitor the docket carefully; with a $145 million claim pool against a $3.25 million sale price, any secondary-market activity will reprice rapidly around plan confirmation outcomes and whether litigation against former D&Os surfaces incremental value for the liquidating trust.

### II. Sleep Number Corporation — § 363 Sale Racing to July 13 Auction; UCC Formation Under Way


On June 12, 2026, Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 of title 11 of the United States Code in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 
The cases are being administered by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.



Sleep Number Corporation reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 
The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors.
 That representation is notable: with $672 million in debt and a $415 million stalking-horse floor, the arithmetic is tight, and much depends on auction dynamics and the treatment of the Travis Kelce sponsorship arrangement, which gave the NFL star a reported 5% equity stake — and whose bankruptcy treatment remains unresolved.


The transaction is being undertaken pursuant to Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, and under the terms of the agreement, Sleep Country will serve as the "stalking horse" bidder.
 
Pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the stalking-horse bidder has committed — subject to court approval — to acquire substantially all of the company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.
 
Building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties (19 NDAs, five preliminary proposals), the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction.


The procedural calendar is compressed and consequential for unsecured creditors. 
A hearing on the Debtors' First Day Motions was held on June 12, 2026 at 3:00 p.m. ET before the Honorable Kyu Y. Paek.
 
A final hearing on certain of the Debtors' First Day Motions will be held on July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 A bidding procedures hearing is scheduled for July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET, and the § 341 meeting of creditors is set for July 8, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET. The U.S. Trustee (Region 2, William K. Harrington; contact: Andrea B. Schwartz, Brian Resnick, Esq., Tel. (212) 510-0500) has solicited UCC nominees with a June 22, 2026 deadline at 12:00 p.m. ET — that deadline falls in three days. Prospective UCC members should act immediately.


Sleep Number blamed its bankruptcy, in part, on "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (Brian M. Resnick). Claims administration is through Kroll at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber. 
Sleep Number, which operates 572 stores and is known for its customizable beds, will continue operations while seeking a quicker-than-usual court-supervised sale process.
 The case caption is *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, Case No. 26-11399 (KYP).

For distressed investors, the critical question is whether the $415 million stalking-horse clears enough to fund meaningful general unsecured recoveries — particularly given the $1.3 billion in liabilities — and whether the July 13 auction surfaces a topping bid. The 14-week prepetition process with only five preliminary proposals from 53 outreach contacts is not an encouraging data point for auction competition.

### III. Georgia-Pacific / Bestwall — A Second Texas Two-Step Filing Takes Shape; SCOTUS Declines Cert

The most consequential macro development for the mass-tort/asbestos creditor class this week is the WSJ exclusive confirming that Georgia-Pacific, the Koch Industries subsidiary, is exploring a new Chapter 11 filing to resolve asbestos claims — nearly nine years after its original Texas Two-Step maneuver placed Bestwall LLC into bankruptcy in the Bankr. W.D.N.C. (Charlotte Division), Case No. 17-31795, before Judge Laura Turner Beyer.


Since that filing, Bestwall has paid zero dollars to its approximately 56,000 current asbestos claimants. According to a bipartisan Senate amicus brief filed with the Supreme Court in March 2026, that number rises with every passing day as more victims are diagnosed with cancer.
 
Court filings disclosed that Koch Industries received more than $5 billion in dividends from Georgia-Pacific since Bestwall filed for bankruptcy in 2017.



According to court filings and the Wall Street Journal's reporting, Georgia-Pacific plans to use an existing affiliate or a newly created entity to file a fresh Chapter 11 case, seeking enough claimant support to fast-track a resolution.
 
Law firms representing claimants have characterized this as the company having given up on the Bestwall approach after years of procedural battles and failed plan negotiations.
 
Negotiations for the new approach apparently stalled when victims' advocates asked for details of the financial terms of the settlement, as well as preservation of their rights to object to any future bankruptcy plan.
 
The group is now requesting appointment of a Chapter 11 trustee in the Bestwall case, arguing that an independent trustee is "essential" as Georgia-Pacific is "hatching a next assault."



A committee of asbestos claimants urged the Supreme Court in February to overturn an August decision from the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit that rejected the group's efforts to dismiss the nearly nine-year-old bankruptcy.
 Bloomberg Law has confirmed that the Supreme Court has passed on the petition — declining cert — which leaves the Fourth Circuit's August ruling intact and, critically, removes the strongest judicial backstop claimants had against the existing Bestwall structure. That ruling — sustaining the automatic stay extension protecting Georgia-Pacific — is now the law of the circuit.


The financial terms of any bankruptcy trust are what determine whether victims receive meaningful compensation or a fraction of what a jury might have awarded. Payout schedules, disease category values, documentation requirements, and claimants' rights to challenge the plan all matter enormously. None of those terms are public yet.
 The strategic posture is: claimant constituencies should treat any prepackaged or prenegotiated new filing as a solicitation environment, not a neutral process, and should preserve all objection rights from the outset — including the right to challenge § 524(g) injunction scope and the adequacy of trust funding relative to aggregate claim values.

The disclosure statement hearing in the Bestwall case — which has now been continued nineteen times before Judge Beyer (most recently February 19, 2026, per the NCWB court calendar) — underscores how completely that track has stalled.

### IV. Macro Creditor-Rights Backdrop: Texas Two-Step, § 363 Compression, and the Institutional Crypto Claim Market

Three structural themes animate all of this week's tracked developments and warrant carrying forward:

**Texas Two-Step viability post-SCOTUS declination.** With the Supreme Court declining to hear the Bestwall claimants' cert petition, the Fourth Circuit's validation of the extended automatic stay stands. But the reported pivot to a second Georgia-Pacific filing signals that even the architects of this tactic recognize that a nine-year fight with zero claimant payments and $5 billion in parent dividends is politically and legally untenable. The question for asbestos claimants and their counsel is whether Georgia-Pacific's "sufficient support" threshold for a prepack is achievable, and on what financial terms. 
The Georgia-Pacific situation gained legislative attention in March 2026, when Senators Dick Durbin, Sheldon Whitehouse, and Josh Hawley filed a bipartisan amicus brief urging the U.S. Supreme Court to reject the Texas Two-Step strategy in the Bestwall case. The senators argued that Congress intended bankruptcy relief only for debtors in genuine financial distress, not for profitable corporations using the bankruptcy system to manage tort liability on terms favorable to shareholders.


**Compressed § 363 timelines (Sleep Number).** The 26-day postpetition marketing window in Sleep Number — compressing a 14-week prepetition process into a sub-30-day auction sprint — reflects the post-pandemic orthodoxy that pre-marketed § 363 sales move fast. For unsecured creditors without committee representation on day one, the information asymmetry is acute. The July 2 bidding procedures hearing is the first meaningful intervention point.

**Institutional crypto claim pricing (BlockFills).** The $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price against $145 million in general unsecured claims implies a roughly 2.2 cents-on-the-dollar ceiling on asset-side recoveries, with any incremental return dependent on the liquidating trust's ability to monetize litigation claims against former D&Os and recover on the Celsius, Babel Finance, and AEXA exposures. Claim buyers active in distressed crypto — including those who were monitoring BlockFills since the February 6, 2026 withdrawal suspension — should be evaluating secondary pricing in light of the post-sale, pre-confirmation window.
