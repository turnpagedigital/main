### Sleep Number Corporation — Case No. 26-11399 (KYP) | Bankr. S.D.N.Y. | Judge Kyu Young Paek


Sleep Number filed for Chapter 11 on Friday, June 12, 2026, in the Southern District of New York under docket No. 26-11399.
 
Judge Kyu Young Paek is overseeing the case and held a first-day hearing at 3:00 p.m. ET on the petition date.
 The filing is the dominant new Chapter 11 matter on the beat this week, and the case's structural features warrant close attention from unsecured creditors and distressed investors.

**Capital structure and balance sheet.** 
The petition listed total assets of $642.3 million against total debt of roughly $1.28 billion, comprising approximately $475 million outstanding under a revolving credit facility, $177.5 million under a term loan entered in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered in 2026.
 
The core tension is a straightforward capital structure problem: Sleep Number is selling substantially all of its assets for $415 million while carrying $672.5 million in prepetition secured debt.
 That arithmetic leaves general unsecured creditors facing a significant impairment absent a competitive auction that breaks materially above the stalking horse price.

**The § 363 process.** 
The transaction is being undertaken pursuant to § 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, with Sleep Country Canada serving as the stalking horse bidder in a court-supervised sale process; the proposed transaction remains subject to higher and better offers, court approval, and other closing conditions.
 
The Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026 — executed by SNBR, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada — commits the stalking horse to acquire substantially all of the Debtors' assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.
 
Because the company had been pursuing a sale prepetition, the debtors are seeking a compressed 26-day sale process, with competing bids due July 8 and a target close by July 31.
 
An auction is scheduled for July 13.


**DIP financing.** 
The company expects to secure up to $260 million of DIP financing to support operations during the court-supervised process, comprising up to $65 million in new money and a roll-up of $195 million of prepetition obligations converted on a 3:1 basis.
 
Interest under the DIP is SOFR+800 bps; U.S. Bank is the agent.
 
The company received three competing DIP proposals for priming facilities — including one unsolicited offer — but existing lenders would not consent to being primed.
 The roll-up structure is precisely the kind of provision that draws U.S. Trustee scrutiny in large retail cases, and the $195 million prepetition debt conversion is an immediate point of focus for any UCC.

**Procedural posture and U.S. Trustee.** 
A first-day hearing was held on June 12, 2026, at 3:00 p.m. ET before Judge Paek; a final hearing on certain first-day motions is scheduled for July 9, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
The U.S. Trustee filed an objection challenging two of Sleep Number's most consequential first-day motions
 — the specifics of those objections remain worth tracking into the July 9 final hearing. 
The bidding procedures hearing is set for July 2, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. ET, and the § 341 creditors' meeting is set for July 8, 2026, at 1:00 p.m. ET.


**UCC formation.** 
§ 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code authorizes the U.S. Trustee to appoint an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors to represent the interests of, and act on behalf of, all unsecured creditors.
 
Creditors interested in serving were given a deadline of June 22, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. ET to submit interest forms.
 Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP is debtors' counsel (partner Brian M. Resnick). 
The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — a representation that may be tested if the § 363 sale closes at or near stalking horse pricing.

**Sponsor and executory contract risk.** 
In January 2026, Sleep Number announced a strategic partnership with Travis Kelce under which Kelce committed to acquire Sleep Number common stock on the open market and to receive compensatory restricted stock units vesting over an initial three-year term.
 
Sleep Number could either assume that contract and assign it to the sale buyer, or reject it — in which case Kelce would hold a general unsecured claim for any resulting damages.
 
It was not clear how the sponsorship agreement would be treated in the bankruptcy.
 That indeterminacy makes the Kelce contract a notable executory contract issue that will likely surface in bidding procedures or assumption/rejection schedules.

**Macroeconomic causation.** 
Sleep Number attributed its bankruptcy, in part, to "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 That framing matters for any distressed investor assessing competitive bidder motivation: tariff exposure on imported components is a forward-looking risk that a purchaser assumes unless affirmatively addressed in the APA.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) | Bankr. D. Del. | Judge Thomas M. Horan


On March 15, 2026, each of the four debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions; the cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 The sale hearing that anchored last week's advisory — scheduled for June 16 — has now passed. The docket reflects eight new filings in the 72 hours through June 18 (entries 442–445 and related matters), though the specific content of those filings was not accessible through our scanning window. The framework heading into this weekend is as follows.

**Sale posture.** 
Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" in an official bankruptcy court document filed on May 26, 2026.
 The June 16 sale hearing was the gating event for court approval. No public confirmation of the outcome has surfaced in verified sources as of this morning's run; creditors should check the Veritaglobal administrator page and CourtListener docket 72494188 for the sale order.

**Dual-track and plan timeline.** 
Reliz was operating under final cash-collateral authority while pursuing a dual-track restructuring built around the sale process and a plan path, with the sale hearing set for June 16, 2026, and confirmation targeted for July 13, 2026.
 If the sale order was entered on or around June 16, the estate now pivots to plan confirmation — with a July 13 target that is aggressive given the adversary proceedings still pending.

**Adversary proceedings.** 
An adversary proceeding is pending before Judge Horan captioned *Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. v. Artha Investment Partners LLC*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH).
 
A hearing in that adversary is scheduled for June 22, 2026, at 10:00 a.m.
 — the most proximate actionable date in this case. The outcome may affect distributable value if Artha holds counterclaims against the estate or if the proceeding is resolved for cash consideration.

**Capital structure and creditor claims.** 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt: the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — including an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.


**Pre-petition litigation context.** 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain debtor entities were named in two customer lawsuits asserting misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims: *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026), and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill., March 5, 2026); both courts granted temporary restraining orders.
 Those TROs complicated the debtors' operations pre-petition and may carry implications for allowed claim amounts post-confirmation.

**Claims trading signal.** The scan guidance for this case specifically flags Rule 3001 transfer notices as a claims-trading signal. With the Veritaglobal administrator site now current through September 11, 2026 (the bar date), the claims register is the primary tool for identifying whether institutional investors have begun accumulating the roughly $145 million GUC exposure at a discount. No 3001 transfer notices have been surfaced in verified docket data as of this run — that absence is itself useful signal that active trading has not yet developed, or that trades are occurring in OTC markets without public notice.

### Macro Context: Tariff-Driven Distress and Retail Insolvency

Sleep Number is not an isolated data point. 
The company's first-day declaration notes that cost-cutting measures, prepetition term loan financing, and an IEEPA tariff refund sale provided runway to pursue a prepetition marketing process, but were not sufficient to fully address continuing financial obligations.
 That sequence — tariff exposure → cost reduction → asset monetization → Chapter 11 — is a pattern creditors and distressed investors should expect to see in additional consumer goods, retail, and manufacturing names with significant imported-input exposure. The compressed § 363 sale timeline Sleep Number is seeking (petition to close in under 50 days) reflects a playbook designed to preserve going-concern value before operational deterioration, but also sharply limits the time available for a UCC to organize, conduct diligence, and negotiate DIP covenant modifications.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors in *Sleep Number* (26-11399, KYP) should move immediately: the UCC formation deadline is June 22, 2026 (Monday), and unsecured creditors who wish to serve on the committee must submit interest forms to the U.S. Trustee before 12:00 p.m. ET that day — debtors' counsel at Davis Polk (Brian Resnick) is the operative contact; the DIP roll-up ($195 million, 3:1 conversion, SOFR+800) is the first committee work product and warrants scrutiny as to whether prepetition lenders received an unfair advantage in exchange for the new $65 million tranche. The bidding procedures hearing is July 2 — any party wishing to object to bid protections, break-up fees, or stalking horse topping covenants must be prepared by that date. In *BlockFills* (26-10371, TMH), creditors should pull the sale order from the Veritaglobal administrator site to confirm whether Judge Horan approved the Keyrock transaction on June 16 and on what terms; the Artha adversary (Adv. Pro. 26-50230, TMH) has a hearing on June 22 and may affect distributable value; creditors holding claims in the ~$145 million GUC pool should assess whether secondary market pricing reflects an opportunity given the compressed sale-to-confirmation runway. In *Power Block Coin* (24-23041, Parker, Bankr. D. Utah), monitor docket entry 519 and any subsequent filings for asset sale or plan activity.
