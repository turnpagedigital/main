### Madoff / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789): Seventeenth Distribution Running; Recovery at 75¢ on the Dollar


The SIPA Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims; commencing February 27, 2026, this is the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund.
 
The seventeenth interim distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; when combined with the prior 16 distributions, approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid.



As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 SIPC advances remain partially unreimbursed: 
as of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation; advances will continue to increase as claims currently in litigation are allowed; SIPC is reimbursed once each respective claim is fully satisfied, and has so far received $272.4 million in reimbursement, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


On the fee-application front, 
on February 13, 2026, Picard and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest-discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 For claims-holders and secondary-market participants, the operative pricing signal is unchanged: cumulative distributions have reached 72.848 cents per dollar of allowed claim; the unrecovered residual (roughly 27 cents, before DOJ restitution proceeds) remains the addressable upside, but each successive distribution has been smaller than the last, and the 1.302% increment of the seventeenth round confirms that the recovery curve is flattening. Litigation against remaining solvent defendants is what drives the next pool.

The judge designation for the current docket should be noted: the case was transferred from Judge Bernstein to Judge Lashann DeArcy Hall (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) upon his retirement; the SIPC case reference remains No. 08-01789.

### Stanford Financial Receivership (N.D. Tex., SEC v. Stanford International Bank, No. 3:09-cv-00298): Final Distribution Plan in Active Execution

The Stanford receivership — now in its seventeenth year under Receiver Ralph Janvey, represented by Baker Botts (Kevin Sadler, Scott Powers, Ariel House) — has reached the terminal distribution phase. 
On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving the Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups, to be issued on a rolling basis beginning 14 days after the filing; the process of issuing payments was expected to take several weeks.


The rollout has proceeded, albeit slowly and with logistical friction. 
On April 3, 2026, the Receiver issued payments to over 2,000 claimant groups under the Final Distribution Plan totaling approximately $47.5 million; the Receiver has stated it will post further updates as additional payments are issued.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver had issued payments to over 5,600 claimant groups.


A separate tranche of Swiss assets has also been resolved: 
on December 17, 2025, the Antiguan Joint Liquidators informed the Receiver that the Swiss Ancillary Bankruptcy proceeding concerning Stanford International Bank had concluded, and transferred $8.1 million in funds from the Swiss bankruptcy proceeding to the Receiver pursuant to the Cross-Border Protocol; the JLs further informed the Receiver that no further distributions of funds are expected.


The practical takeaway for claims holders: the rolling payment process has been proceeding since April 3. For claimants still awaiting payment — particularly those at Venezuelan addresses, who faced a disruption in physical check delivery beginning December 11, 2025 — 
the receivership was not able to arrange for delivery of distribution payments via physical checks to Venezuelan addresses beginning December 11, 2025; however, as of March 11, 2026, delivery by courier had been restored.
 The $8.1 million Swiss tranche disposition (whether folded into the Final Distribution Plan or handled separately) remains an open question; as of the available record, the Receiver had not publicly responded to claimant inquiries on that point.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova (S.D.N.Y.): $40 Million Remission Window Has Closed; Kroll Processing Begins

The single most important near-term development on this docket is procedural: the victim petition deadline has passed. 
The Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-founded OneCoin, which was based in Sofia, Bulgaria, and marketed and sold a fraudulent cryptocurrency through a global MLM network, causing victims worldwide to invest over $4 billion.
 
The deadline for filing a petition was June 30, 2026; the Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission compensation process.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator.


The recovery gap is severe: a $40 million fund against $4 billion in victim losses implies a gross recovery rate on the order of 1%, before administrative costs. The DOJ has indicated its intent to continue seizing assets, but the pipeline is thin relative to the claim pool. Parallel European asset recovery has added modest increments: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order of GBP 8,590,200.92 — funds held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies through which Ignatova had commissioned the purchase of shares to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 
The seized funds will be paid into Guernsey's seized assets fund and then returned to the German authorities, where they will be used to compensate victims.



German proceedings found that OneCoin was released with fraudulent intent by Ignatova and others; criminal proceedings against her could not conclude because her whereabouts are unknown; the German authorities accordingly initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings," which led to the overseas forfeiture order.
 This is a significant structural note for recovery practitioners: non-conviction-based asset forfeiture under the German independent confiscation framework — operative here in lieu of standard criminal forfeiture — can reach offshore assets held in shell structures even when the primary defendant remains a fugitive. 
The U.S. Department of State's Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program currently offers a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to Ignatova's arrest or conviction.


For claims buyers on this docket: the fund is de minimis relative to losses. The real secondary-market question is whether DOJ's stated commitment to expand the pool produces material additional seizures before Kroll closes the eligibility determination. Given the current pool size, even a recovery order of magnitude larger would still represent a fraction-of-a-cent distribution. Position accordingly.

### Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker): Approaching Plan Consummation; Fee Applications in Final Stage

The ground-truth docket for this case (CourtListener Docket ID 68873409) reflects the most recent filing at Entry 531, dated July 6, 2026, with no new activity in the 72-hour scan window ending July 11. The case posture, based on verified filed documents, is as follows.


Power Block Coin, which operated under the name SmartFi, filed for bankruptcy protection on June 20, 2024; the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors later filed a comprehensive liquidation plan proposing to wind down the company under Chapter 11.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million, and involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage assets, pursue potential legal claims, and oversee distributions to creditors.



The committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning insider transactions, including a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah, with concern about that loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances on the underlying property.


The fee-application track is now in its terminal phase. 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker set a hearing on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for Allowance of Compensation (ECF 506) for June 9, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. (Prevailing Utah Time); by that application, Amplēo, financial advisor and accountant to the Debtor, requested final allowance of fees in the amount of $10,436.50 for services rendered between April 1, 2025, and April 22, 2026, plus previously allowed interim fees.
 The confirmed judge on the active docket phase is Judge Cathleen D. Parker (per ECF 507, filed May 14, 2026, Case No. 24-bk-23041-CDP), consistent with the tracked-case ground truth.


The Celsius-related objecting creditors had asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending Celsius Network bankruptcy (Case No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The disposition of those preference claims — and whether the Liquidating Trust will pursue avoidance actions under 11 U.S.C. § 548 or §§ 547/550 against pre-petition transferees — remains the primary recovery variable for unsecured creditors in the non-Celsius claim pool. The $17.4 million Solara loan and the unexplained crypto-asset losses identified by the committee are the natural targets for § 548 Ponzi-presumption and fraudulent-transfer theories, though the committee's standing and funding posture for pursuing those claims has not been confirmed in the available record.
