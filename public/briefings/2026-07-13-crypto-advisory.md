### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al. — Bankr. D. Del. 26-10371 (TMH)): Confirmation Hearing Today

The BlockFills case has moved from petition to confirmation in roughly 120 days, a compressed timeline explained in part by the pre-negotiated ad hoc group term sheet and the parallel § 363 sale process that resolved the asset-disposition question before the plan vote. 
On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities filed Chapter 11 petitions in Bankr. D. Del. before Judge Horan, following cascading financial losses, two prepetition customer lawsuits with temporary restraining orders, and the collapse of multiple recapitalization attempts.


The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than funded debt. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Assets were reported at between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million — the spread itself signals the degree of uncertainty that dominated early case proceedings. 
Cash-collateral authority remained contested through the spring, including objections over waivers and whether the sale process primarily benefited Celsius.


The § 363 sale leg resolved in late May. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Brussels-based Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock confirmed its designation as successful bidder in a court document filed May 26, 2026.
 
BlockFills reported approximately $75 million in losses prior to filing.
 The sale hearing was held June 16, 2026; confirmed search results show no indication the sale was blocked, and the plan confirmation timeline proceeded on schedule.

The plan itself is structured on a dual-track toggle: 
the debtors proposed either a NewCo transaction, in which participating customers would contribute distributions for interests in a new private entity receiving operating assets free and clear, or an alternative transaction through an asset sale or liquidation, with residual claims and causes of action moving through a GUC trust.
 With Keyrock as the successful § 363 bidder, the operating-assets question is largely answered; what remains for Judge Horan at today's 10:00 a.m. hearing is whether the Amended Plan (Dkt. No. 391) satisfies the § 1129(a) confirmation standards — best interests of creditors, feasibility, and good faith — given the capital structure's customer-heavy composition.


The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, and the confirmation objection deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Fourteen new docket entries were filed in the 72 hours ending July 10 (docket entries including Dkt. No. 515 and 510, filed July 10), consistent with the final pre-confirmation wave of submissions — vote certifications, plan supplement updates, and any last-mile objections. The docket as of July 12 shows 515+ entries; the most recent filing date is July 10, 2026. (__[CourtListener Docket 72494188](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72494188/)__)

The central creditor-rights question embedded in this case — whether customer crypto deposits constitute property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or are held in trust/bailment — has not been resolved at the doctrinal level in this case. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute property of the estate or are held in trust or bailment runs through the dual-track restructuring built around a customer-led NewCo transaction and a competitive Section 363 sale process.
 That question is likely papered over rather than adjudicated in the plan confirmation order — the GUC trust / NewCo toggle structure essentially defers the economic fight between customer claimants and Celsius (the secured creditor asserting lien priority) to distribution mechanics rather than resolving it through a contested § 541 ruling. Creditors should expect the plan confirmation order, if entered today, to include broad release and exculpation provisions; review those carefully against the Ward Trust recoupment precedent already established in this case. 
The Richard E. Ward Revocable Trust, which held 140.42664583 BTC as collateral against a loan to Reliz, was granted recoupment rights under 11 U.S.C. § 553(a) at Dkt. No. 357 (filed May 22, 2026), reducing its claim by the loan value and leaving a net general unsecured claim of $2,815,649.
 That order establishes the methodological framework — petition-date BTC price of $71,708.25 per coin — which has direct downstream relevance for how the broader GUC pool will be valued at distribution.


A claim objection deadline is set for September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET
 — the next major creditor-rights inflection point following today's hearing, and the date by which the debtors or GUC trustee must file any omnibus objections to proofs of claim before distributions can be calculated.

### FTX Recovery Trust (In re FTX Trading Ltd. — Bankr. D. Del. 22-11068 (JTD)): Fifth Distribution Targeting July 31

The FTX matter has crossed from restructuring into trust administration, but the distribution mechanics remain consequential for claim-trading counterparties and transferred-claim holders. 
The anticipated record date for the next distribution to holders of allowed claims and interests was set for June 16, 2026, with distributions expected to commence on July 31, 2026; payments to eligible Preferred Equity Holders are also scheduled for July 31, 2026.



As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the FTX estate filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 That reserve reduction is the mechanical predicate for the fifth distribution: freed-up capital moves from disputed reserve to distributable pool. Four distributions have preceded this one: 
distributions began in February 2025 with approximately $1.2 billion paid to Convenience Class claimants, followed by larger institutional payouts in May 2025, a third round totaling roughly $1.6 billion in September 2025, and a fourth round launched on March 31, 2026.
 
The March 31 fourth distribution delivered approximately $2.2 billion, lifting recovery rates so that many customer claim classes — including U.S. customers in Class 5B and Classes 6A and 6B — reached 100%, while Class 7 received a cumulative 120%.



For holders of transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 This is a hard operational gate for anyone who acquired FTX claims in the secondary market after the March distribution: if your transfer notice was not processed and settled on the claims register by June 16, you are excluded from the July 31 payout and must wait for the sixth round. Counsel should confirm register status now. (__[FTX Restructuring Administration — Kroll](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/ftx/)__)

The petition-date valuation tension — all FTX distributions are denominated in USD at November 2022 prices, not current crypto market values — remains the unremedied structural grievance for creditors who held BTC or SOL. 
Every percentage is measured against the dollar value at November 2022, so for anyone who held crypto on FTX, even 100%+ is worth less in today's terms.
 The next FTX omnibus hearing is calendared for July 23, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. ET. (__[FTX Kroll Administration Calendar](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/ftx/)__)

### Genesis/DCG Post-Confirmation Litigation: Fraud Claims Revived (July 6, 2026)

The Genesis insolvency is in its wind-down phase — the Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) administers Case No. 23-10063 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Sean Lane) — but the affiliate litigation layer remains live and has just generated a significant ruling. 
As of July 6, 2026, a federal judge revived part of an investor lawsuit against Barry Silbert, DCG, and other defendants over the failed Genesis Yield crypto lending program; the ruling restored a New York common law fraud claim after plaintiffs successfully argued that the court had jurisdiction under the Class Action Fairness Act, and the judge also kept the lawsuit's federal securities claims alive, finding the allegations sufficient to proceed.
 
Investors claim the defendants misrepresented Genesis' financial health before it froze customer withdrawals and entered bankruptcy in 2023, while most remaining state consumer protection claims were either dismissed or stayed.


This ruling compounds DCG's existing exposure from the Genesis WDOC's own adversary proceedings. The Kroll docket for Case No. 23-10063 shows multiple active adversary proceedings, including: 
*Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053 and a related matter at 26-03230;
 and 
*Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129.
 The WDOC separately filed suit against DCG at Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312 over the $1.1 billion promissory note that DCG issued to backstop Genesis's Three Arrows Capital exposure. 
Genesis has argued before the New York bankruptcy court that DCG should not be able to argue it has no further obligations under that $1.1 billion promissory note meant to "backstop" its bankrupt subsidiary.


The separate class action — *McGreevy v. Digital Currency Group* — 
applied the U.S. Supreme Court's 1946 *Howey* test and its 1990 *Reves* test to find that the Genesis Yield program constituted a security, rejecting DCG's argument that the program was merely a "run-of-the-mill loan."
 That securities-law characterization, now reinforced by the July 6 revival of the common law fraud claim, creates a parallel recovery track for Genesis Yield participants that operates outside the WDOC's distribution waterfall. The next Genesis omnibus hearing is July 15, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET. (__[Genesis Kroll Administration Calendar](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/)__)

### Carry-Forward: Plan Valuation Date and the Petition-Date Trap

The valuation-date dispute remains the unresolved structural problem across the 2022–26 insolvency wave. Genesis resolved it creditor-favorably: 
breaking new ground, the Bankruptcy Court's confirmation decision allowed creditors to receive in-kind distributions of cryptocurrency assets based on the present value of the assets they loaned to Genesis, rather than being limited to the value as of the petition date; as a result, Genesis was authorized to distribute all of its assets to creditors, even if those assets appreciated in value after the petition date.
 FTX went the other way — petition-date USD valuation locked in at November 2022 prices — and BlockFills is now navigating its own version of this question, with the Ward Trust recoupment order establishing the $71,708.25/BTC petition-date price as the operative benchmark for at least one claim calculation.

The pattern is now clear: in cases where debtors hold actual crypto assets (Genesis), courts have been willing to use current-value distributions; in cases where the estate monetized assets into fiat (FTX), the petition-date lock-in prevails. BlockFills sits between these poles — the § 363 sale to Keyrock for $3.25 million converted the operating assets to a cash equivalent, but the GUC trust will inherit whatever residual claims and causes of action the debtors retain. Where those residual claims include in-kind crypto or crypto-denominated receivables, the valuation-date argument remains available to the GUC trustee.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors holding claims in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.* (BlockFills, No. 26-10371) should monitor today's 10:00 a.m. ET confirmation hearing before Judge Horan for any last-minute objections that survive the July 1 deadline, and obtain a copy of the confirmation order once entered — pay particular attention to the release and exculpation provisions, the GUC trust distribution mechanics, and any treatment of the Celsius secured claim, which is the primary impediment to meaningful GUC recovery given the $145 million unsecured pool against assets recovered at $3.25 million in the § 363 sale. FTX claim-trading counterparties should immediately verify that any transferred claim was processed and reflected on the official Kroll claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date; absent that, the July 31 distribution will not include the transferred position and the next window is months away. Genesis Yield participants who have not already done so should consult with counsel regarding both the WDOC distribution track (Case No. 23-10063) and the parallel class action (*McGreevy v. DCG*) revival, particularly in light of the July 6 ruling restoring common law fraud and federal securities claims; these two tracks may yield different (and potentially additive) recoveries depending on the class definition and the WDOC's remaining asset pool. Across all three matters, the petition-date valuation trap deserves renewed attention: the Genesis in-kind distribution precedent established by Judge Lane is available as persuasive authority in any case where the estate retains undistributed crypto assets.
