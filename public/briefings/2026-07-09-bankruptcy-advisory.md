### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del. | Confirmation Hearing: July 13, 2026

This case is four days from its confirmation hearing, and the arithmetic for general unsecured creditors is stark. 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 Against that, 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and the company's proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 That purchase price reflects a recovery rate in the low single-digit percentage of face claims — assuming no meaningful litigation upside from the GUC trust.

The case architecture matters for how creditors read that number. 
The plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a § 363 competitive sale process.
 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" per a bankruptcy court document filed May 26, 2026.
 The auction was cancelled, confirming Keyrock's bid was unopposed — which, in a case with 
an estimated $50–$100 million in assets and $100–$500 million in liabilities across 1,000–5,000 creditors,
 tells you something about third-party appetite for the operating business.

The foundational creditor-rights question — whether customer digital assets are estate property or customer property — has not been finally resolved. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
On January 16, 2026, Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes. On February 11, 2026, the debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 That fact pattern, if confirmed by the court, forecloses any tracing argument and converts the full customer shortfall into a pro rata GUC claim.

A May 22, 2026 order (Dkt. 357) resolved one smaller dispute in favor of a creditor seeking recoupment: 
Judge Horan granted the Richard E. Ward Revocable Trust's motion, pursuant to §§ 362(d) and 553(a) of the Bankruptcy Code, permitting the Trust to exercise its right of recoupment against Reliz.
 
The claim was reduced, leaving the Trust with a general unsecured claim of $2,815,649, calculated as (140.42664583 BTC × $71,708.25) – ($7,224,000 loan value) – ($30,100 unpaid loan fees).
 The Ward Trust outcome is instructive: it shows Judge Horan will enforce recoupment rights arising from the same transactional relationship, but the order does not affect the estate-property/customer-property question for the broader pool of unsegregated digital assets.


The voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the confirmation objection deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the confirmation hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The docket shows 12 new filings in the past 72 hours (entries 500–501 filed July 8), the content of which is not yet fully described in public-facing sources reviewed this run. Any late-breaking objections to plan confirmation filed on or before July 1 will be argued Monday. Distressed investors holding claims should confirm whether their proof of claim was filed, properly scheduled, and whether any temporary allowance motion for voting purposes was granted — the May 28, 2026 disclosure statement order (Dkt. 388) established those procedures.

The capital structure background: 
the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
Cash-collateral authority remained contested through the spring, including objections over waivers and whether the sale process primarily benefited Celsius, but the docket later reflects a final cash-collateral order as the operative liquidity document and no new-money DIP facility.
 McDermott Will & Emery serves as lead bankruptcy counsel; Verita Global (f/k/a KCC) is voting agent. The claims administrator site is at veritaglobal.net/blockfills.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Case No. 26-11399, Bankr. S.D.N.Y. | Second-Day Hearing: Today, July 9, 2026, 10:00 a.m. ET

Sleep Number's second-day hearing before Judge Kyu Y. Paek is proceeding this morning. 
On July 2, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the bidding procedures in connection with the sale of all, substantially all, or any portion of any of the Debtors' assets.
 
The bid deadline was July 8, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET.
 Bids were due yesterday. The court will hear today what was received and determine whether an auction is warranted or whether the stalking horse bid proceeds to a sale hearing.

The stalking horse terms set the floor and frame the creditor recovery math. 
The case is structured as an expedited sale of substantially all assets under § 363, built around a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 That bid runs against 
$672.5 million in secured debt,
 producing a structural shortfall of roughly $257.5 million before the secured lenders even reach general unsecured creditors. 
The company discloses significant risks tied to its Chapter 11 process, including the likely cancellation of its common shares and uncertainty around completing a § 363 sale or reorganization — equity is explicitly warned of a likely wipeout.


The DIP facility is the liquidity spine. 
On June 16, 2026, Sleep Number entered a fourteenth amendment to its credit and security agreement, establishing a DIP credit facility of up to $260 million while in Chapter 11. The package combines up to $65 million in new superpriority term loans and up to $195 million of prepetition obligations rolled into DIP loans, following interim approval by the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on June 15, 2026.
 
The financing, guaranteed by all subsidiaries, carries high interest rates, matures on September 16, 2026, and is secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on substantially all collateral.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans bear an interest rate per annum equal to either SOFR plus 8.00% or the base rate plus 7.00%.
 Today's second-day hearing is expected to yield the final DIP order.

The roll-up structure deserves scrutiny. The prepetition lenders are converting $195 million of existing exposure into priming DIP claims, ahead of all other creditors. In a $415 million stalking horse scenario against $672.5 million in secured debt, the roll-up means the lenders are securing repayment of legacy loans through the DIP priority position — a mechanism that U.S. Trustee offices have increasingly challenged in large-case § 363 processes. 
A same-day objection from the U.S. Trustee was already on the docket at filing.



Sleep Number retained Guggenheim Securities as its investment banker and Davis Polk & Wardwell as restructuring counsel.
 
The first-day motions hearing was held June 12, 2026 at 3:00 p.m. ET before Judge Kyu Y. Paek, United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 The sale objection deadline is July 10, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. Claims are being administered by Kroll at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/SleepNumber.

### Tehum Care Services, Inc. (YesCare / Corizon Successor) — Case No. 23-90086, Bankr. S.D. Tex. | Post-Confirmation Trust Litigation, Adversary No. 26-03138

The adversary proceeding filed April 27, 2026 (Adv. No. 26-03138, Bankr. S.D. Tex.) generated a material litigation update this week. 
Tehum Care Services Inc. director Isaac Lefkowitz disputed allegations by creditor trusts that he orchestrated a fraudulent bankruptcy scheme related to a divisional merger — a legal maneuver known as the Texas Two-Step.
 
Lefkowitz also argued in a Monday filing that the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas lacks jurisdiction over claims against him as a non-bankrupt individual.
 The jurisdiction challenge is the sharper legal issue: if the court accepts it, the GUC Trustee and PI/WD Trustee must pursue Lefkowitz in a non-bankruptcy forum, extending timeline and expense substantially.

The complaint's theory is well-documented. 
The complaint pleads sixteen counts: actual and constructive fraudulent transfer attacking both the divisional merger and approximately $31 million in prepetition cash transfers; aiding and abetting; multiple breach-of-fiduciary-duty counts; alter ego against Lefkowitz; veil piercing against Perigrove 1018; successor liability against both YesCare and CHS TX; and breach of contract on the Plan and Cure Agreement.
 
The successor-liability count against YesCare is potentially case-determinative: a finding that YesCare is a mere continuation of Corizon would strand every debtor under the full pre-merger liability exposure.


The settlement funding default is what triggered the adversary. 
A defined slate of Settlement Parties — YesCare and its wholly-owned subsidiaries (including CHS TX), Geneva, Perigrove 1018, Perigrove LLC, M2 HoldCo, M2 LoanCo, and PharmaCorr — agreed to pay $50 million ($2 million on the Effective Date, $48 million over 30 monthly installments at 6.00% per annum).
 
The PI/WD trustee's first annual report showed the trust had received $6.95 million of contributions from inception through December 31, 2025, had paid only $77,000 on 15 settled claims, had 323 submitted PI/WD claims in the trust process, and reported delinquent installment payments — with a later cure agreement also defaulting by March 2026.



Critically, the plan releases were structured to vest only on the Final Payment Date; the Channeling Injunction terminated automatically on any payment default not cured within five business days. Plan Article XII.A reserved exclusive jurisdiction in the Texas Bankruptcy Court over all matters arising out of or related to the Plan and the Estate Party Settlement; the Settlement Parties separately and expressly consented to Texas jurisdiction in § 11 of the February 1, 2026 Cure Agreement.
 That consent-to-jurisdiction provision is Lefkowitz's most direct obstacle to his jurisdictional challenge. Lefkowitz agreed to Texas Bankruptcy Court jurisdiction in writing less than six months ago.

The aggregate creditor exposure in this ecosystem remains large: 
prison medical provider Tehum Care Services faces $775 million in personal injury claims.
 With the trust having paid $77,000 on 15 claims against 323 submitted, the individual recoveries for claimants — most of them incarcerated individuals with no counsel and limited ability to participate — remain negligible. 
Those creditors include incarcerated people, in particular pro se plaintiffs, who brought lawsuits for unlawful healthcare practices — a point on which civil rights organizations including the ACLU, Rights Behind Bars, and the Center for Constitutional Rights have filed amicus briefs.

### Contextual Beat Note: Texas Two-Step Jurisdiction — Structural Issue to Watch

Lefkowitz's jurisdictional argument in Adv. No. 26-03138 is worth tracking beyond this individual case. The question of whether a bankruptcy court has "related to" jurisdiction (28 U.S.C. § 1334) over claims against a non-debtor individual who was the directing mind of a divisional merger — but never himself filed — has not been cleanly resolved at the circuit level. The S.D. Tex. bankruptcy court has consistently exercised broad related-to jurisdiction in the Tehum matter, including retaining jurisdiction post-confirmation per Plan Article XII.A and the Cure Agreement. A ruling on Lefkowitz's motion will either confirm that consent-based jurisdictional arrangements in confirmed plans bind non-debtor insiders, or introduce a circuit-level issue that could reshape how creditor trusts pursue post-confirmation recoveries against Texas Two-Step architects. SCOTUS has not granted certiorari on Texas Two-Step validity generally; the Fifth Circuit's pending jurisprudence on divisional mergers is the next meaningful appellate checkpoint.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and claimants across these three matters face compressing timelines. In *BlockFills*, any claim holder who filed a timely proof of claim should verify that their claim appears on the voting register as properly scheduled and was either allowed for voting purposes under the May 28 solicitation order (Dkt. 388) or that a temporary allowance motion was submitted — with the July 13 confirmation hearing now four days out, there is essentially no curative runway. Examine whether any pending plan objections filed by July 1 touch on the estate-property vs. customer-property issue, which remains the only mechanism for improving the GUC recovery rate above what the $3.25 million Keyrock purchase price implies. In *Sleep Number*, the bid deadline passed yesterday (July 8, 4:00 p.m. ET); any qualified bidder who did not submit by that deadline is foreclosed from the auction process, and the sale objection deadline is July 10. Secured lenders in the $672.5 million stack should confirm their lien positions are correctly reflected in the DIP order being finalized today at the second-day hearing before Judge Paek — the roll-up mechanic and priming lien structure is the most contestable element of the DIP. In *Tehum*, beneficiaries of the GUC Trust and PI/WD Trust should monitor the jurisdictional briefing in Adv. No. 26-03138 closely; if Lefkowitz succeeds in stripping the Texas Bankruptcy Court of jurisdiction over claims against him individually, trust counsel will need to refile in state or federal district court, with attendant delay and potential statute-of-limitations exposure for some claimants. The February 1, 2026 Cure Agreement's consent-to-jurisdiction clause is the trusts' strongest counter-argument and should be the anchor of any reply brief.
