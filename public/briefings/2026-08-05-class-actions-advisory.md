### 2. Securities Class Action Macro Trend — Bloomberg Law (Aug. 3, 2026)

Bloomberg Law's piece published two days ago is the most substantive market-structure read of the week for this desk. 
Large alleged investor losses and increased AI adoption are the key factors driving a robust level of securities case filings and more than $2 billion in aggregate settlements at the midyear mark of 2026.
 
Investors filed roughly 120 new proposed securities class actions in the first half of the year, on pace to exceed 2025 filings if sustained through year-end, according to recent reports by Cornerstone Research and NERA.
 
Claims involving artificial intelligence arrived at nearly twice the 2025 rate.


The settlement-value denominator observation is material: 
Roger Cooper, co-head of Cleary's group, noted that the median settlement amount as a percentage of plaintiff-style damages in 2026 "is actually lower" than it was in eight of the past nine years.
 That compression matters for recovery modeling — higher filing velocity plus lower percentage recovery is a structural headwind for per-claim values in the securities vertical, even as aggregate dollars look robust. 
The aggregate settlement amount for H1 2026 already represents 75% of the inflation-adjusted total for all of 2025, per NERA; Stanford's Joseph Grundfest noted that "AI-related cases represented a modest share of total filings but an outsized share of alleged investor losses in the first half of 2026."



Cornerstone's data on resolutions of older cases shows that healthcare, along with communications and technology, were prominent industry sectors in which settlements took place in H1 2026, with 14 and 6 deals, respectively.

### 3. Discover Interchange Settlement — Post-Approval Distribution Watch

No new docket activity today, but this is the relevant status for any merchant-side claim holder: 
On May 20, 2026, the court held a final approval hearing and granted final approval of the settlement.
 The case is *CAPP, Inc. v. Discover Financial Services*, 
Case No. 1:23-cv-04676, consolidated with *Lemmo's Pizzeria, LLC v. Discover Financial Services*, Case No. 1:23-cv-14250, and *Support Animal Holdings, LLC v. Discover Financial Services*, Case No. 1:23-cv-15297, pending in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois.
 
The final approval hearing was held before the Honorable Steven C. Seeger in Courtroom 2319.
 
Under the settlement, Discover will pay at least $540 million and up to $1.225 billion plus interest to affected end merchants, merchant acquirers, and payment intermediaries, with attorneys' fees, litigation expenses, class representative service awards, and all settlement administration costs paid separately.
 
Payment allocation determination notices are expected to be sent in late 2026.
 The claims window closed May 18, 2026; no new claims are being accepted.

Downstream note: 
Visa and Mastercard's proposed $38 billion deal — which would trim the combined average effective credit interchange rate by 0.10% for five years, cap standard consumer card rates at 1.25% for eight years, and expand surcharge rights — moved to Judge Brodie for preliminary approval in early 2026 and faces active objections from the National Restaurant Association, NACS, Walmart, and Hugo Boss.
 That is the larger live story in the interchange vertical; the Discover matter is now in disbursement posture only.

### 4. Mid-Year Mega-Settlement Landscape — Duane Morris (July 1, 2026)

For contextual positioning: 
corporate defendants face unprecedented settlement amounts across all areas of class action litigation, with mid-year 2026 data showing aggregate settlements of $53.795 billion — more than double the halfway mark of 2025.
 
At the midway point of 2026, there are three settlements over the billion-dollar mark.
 
The leading verticals by dollar volume through mid-2026: antitrust class actions at $34.875 billion, products liability/mass tort at $8.609 billion, government enforcement at $4.25 billion, and securities fraud at $1.979 billion.


These figures carry a caveat: antitrust dominance reflects one or two outsized transactions (the Visa/Mastercard $38B deal, if it clears approval, would reshape the figures materially). Recovery counsel should not read the aggregate as evenly distributed signal.

### 5. Mass Arbitration — Near-Term Watch Items (No New Movement Today)

No overnight rulings or settlement disclosures in the tracked mass-arbitration matters. The near-term docket items to monitor:

- **Sega v. Consovoy McCarthy** — 
discovery closed February 2026, pretrial underway; no settlement announced.

- **Microsoft 365 / Copilot pricing** — 
Milberg LLC is bringing mass arbitration claims against Microsoft on behalf of Microsoft 365 subscribers who renewed after November 1, 2024 at the higher Copilot-included price without being told a lower-cost, AI-free plan was still available.
 No settlement or AAA case number has been publicly disclosed at this stage; unverified as to filing volume.
- **Pandolfi v. AviaGames / Jones v. Starz** — 
the Ninth Circuit struck provisions that bind non-participating claimants (*Pandolfi v. AviaGames*) while upholding JAMS consolidation authority (*Jones v. Starz*).
 Both rulings are prior-cycle; no new appellate activity confirmed today.

The structural pressure point remains fee economics: 
mass arbitrations — where a plaintiffs' firm brings dozens, hundreds, or thousands of identical claims against a business — enable a plaintiffs' firm to create settlement pressure by leveraging unavoidable arbitration fees borne by a business regardless of the merits of the claims filed.
