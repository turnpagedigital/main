# An Appeal Has Been Filed in Bartz v. Anthropic: What It Means for Your Payment

**The short answer:** on **August 18, 2026** — one day before the window closed — a notice of appeal to the **Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals** was filed in Bartz v. Anthropic (Dkt. 682, by the law firms Edelson PC and Oppenheim + Zebrak). The settlement is not undone: appellate courts affirm the vast majority of approved class settlements. But an appeal typically **pauses distribution** while it is pending, and Ninth Circuit appeals commonly take **12–18 months** to resolve. The near-term payment milestones — August 21 and October 9 — should now be treated as uncertain until the administrator says otherwise.

## What was filed

A notice of appeal is a short filing that starts the appellate clock; it does not state the grounds, so what exactly is being challenged — the settlement's approval, the fee award, or a specific ruling like the handling of objections or late opt-outs — will only become clear when the opening brief is filed at the Ninth Circuit. The notice was filed by two law firms that participated in the approval fight on the objectors' side of the docket. The appeal fee was paid the same day, which signals intent to prosecute the appeal rather than a placeholder filing.

## What it means for your check

- **Distribution likely holds.** Administrators generally do not pay out a fund whose approval might be vacated. The Plan of Allocation's August 21 mailing to non-filing claimants and the October 9 second wave are now subject to the appeal; JND Legal Administration has not announced its handling as of this writing. Watch the official settlement site for the administrator's notice.
- **The money doesn't disappear.** The $1.5 billion commitment stands unless the appeal actually succeeds — historically the rare outcome. The realistic cost of an appeal is **time**, not the fund.
- **Timing math changes.** "Checks this fall" can become "checks after the Ninth Circuit's mandate issues." Some settlements distribute to the uncontested portion of the class during an appeal; no one should assume that here until the court or administrator says so.

## What happens procedurally from here

The Ninth Circuit will docket the appeal and set a briefing schedule; appellate mediation is common in settlement appeals and sometimes resolves them early. Class counsel may also move to dismiss the appeal or seek an appeal bond, both of which can shorten the timeline.
