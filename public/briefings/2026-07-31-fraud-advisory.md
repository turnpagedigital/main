### Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Check-Void Deadlines Now Passed (N.D. Tex. 3:09-CV-0298-N)

The Stanford receivership's final-distribution program has crossed its two July check-cashing cutoffs. 
Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 were required to cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 faced a July 26, 2026 deadline.
 
Claimants whose checks remained uncashed were contacted by email as those checks approached void status; checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and the funds represented will revert to the receivership.


The practical consequence: any claimant who did not cash by the applicable date — or who failed to request reissuance before that date — has forfeited their final distribution payment. 
Any claimant who desired reissuance was required to submit their request and all necessary processing information before the applicable deadline; claimants who did not timely request reissuance will not be eligible to receive a reissued payment.
 
Since the filing of the Final Distribution Schedule on March 9, 2026, the receivership has received and processed approximately 9,000 emails from claimants concerning issues related to distribution payments.


For claims-buyers who acquired Stanford interests from claimants who may have been unresponsive: confirm directly with the receiver's office whether your assigned claim's check was cashed, voided, or is pending reissuance. The receivership's July 2026 status report, filed July 9 with the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, is the operative document. (__[Stanford Financial Receivership](https://www.stanfordfinancialreceivership.com/)__)

### OneCoin Remission — Claims Window Has Closed; Post-Deadline Processing Begins (S.D.N.Y.)


The DOJ formally opened a remission compensation process for victims of the OneCoin scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood co-orchestrated a large international cryptocurrency investment scheme through a global multi-level-marketing network, with victims investing over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 
The OneCoin Remission Program is a DOJ initiative using over $40 million in forfeited assets seized from multiple key figures in the scheme.


The June 30, 2026 petition deadline has now passed. The program is in post-filing review. 
No payment date has been announced.
 
The remission process was open to victims worldwide who purchased OneCoin during the covered period and still showed a net loss after accounting for withdrawals or other recoveries.


The $40 million fund against a $4 billion loss pool is the critical recovery-pricing variable: even if every submitted petition is approved, pro-rata recoveries will be deeply fractional. Claims-market pricing on secondary OneCoin interests should reflect this arithmetic. 
Ruja Ignatova, the figure most closely associated with OneCoin, remains on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list,
 meaning further criminal forfeitures are theoretically possible but speculative and undated. The DOJ has stated it will continue working to seize criminal proceeds, but no supplemental fund has been announced. (__[DOJ Press Release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-compensation-process-onecoin-fraud-victims-funds-recovered)__)
