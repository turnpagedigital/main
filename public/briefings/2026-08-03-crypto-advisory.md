### BlockFills (*Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.*) — Post-Confirmation Posture, Claims Bar Approaching


On July 13, 2026, Judge Thomas M. Horan confirmed the debtors' Second Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan, just 122 days after the March 15, 2026 petition date.
 Counsel (McDermott Will & Emery) has publicly noted the pace benchmarks favorably against BlockFi (309 days), Celsius (484 days), Genesis Global (498 days), and FTX (697 days). 
The confirmation order is at Dkt. 527; McDermott's team also ran the competitive, court-supervised sale that resulted in the purchase of substantially all BlockFills assets, with the sale funding the estates and supporting the expedited plan process.


The immediately actionable date: 
September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) is a posted key date on the Verita claims administrator site.
 The nature of that deadline — likely the claims objection or distribution-record cut-off under the confirmed plan — has not been separately characterized in available public filings as of this run, but creditors should treat it as a hard deadline and confirm its scope against the confirmation order.

Capital structure context for new readers: 
the first-day record identified roughly $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million for substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The gap between the $145 million GUC pool and $3.25 million in sale proceeds signals a recovery that will depend heavily on whatever distributable value survives in the liquidating trust — a figure not yet publicly confirmed.

The central customer-property question that defined the first half of this case — 
whether customer digital assets constitute estate property
 — was resolved at confirmation. Creditors who did not file proofs of claim or timely object to treatment should confirm their status through Verita's portal before September 11.

### MVMT Labs, Inc. (Movement Blockchain) — Subchapter V Filing, Creditor Bar September 14

Not a tracked case, but pattern-relevant. 
MVMT Labs, Inc., the corporate entity behind the Movement blockchain, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, electing Subchapter V — the streamlined restructuring path for eligible small businesses.
 
The petition lists estimated assets between $100,001 and $500,000, against liabilities reaching as high as $10 million.


The precipitating event: 
an intermediary named Rentech, connected to market maker Web3Port, held an arrangement that let 66 million MOVE tokens — roughly 5% of total supply — reach the market one day after the token debuted, generating approximately $38 million and driving a sharp price decline.
 
In May 2025, Movement suspended co-founder Rushi Manche after announcing an independent investigation; Binance later said the market maker rapidly sold the 66 million tokens, contributing to the price decline, and Coinbase suspended MOVE trading after saying the token no longer met its listing standards.


Creditor posture: 
creditors have until September 14, 2026 to file claims, and the court expects a restructuring plan by October 13, 2026.
 
Largest creditors include co-founder Rushi Manche, the Delaware Division of Revenue, Anchorage Digital, and other entities.
 The Subchapter V election caps the reorganization timeline at approximately 90 days from filing for plan submission — consistent with the October 13 target. Token holders are general unsecured creditors at best; the estate's de minimis asset base makes meaningful recovery structurally improbable absent fraudulent-transfer or insider-disgorgement claims.

The MVMT Labs filing is distinct from Move Industries, the successor operating entity. 
Move Industries is a separate legal entity and is not part of that filing; it continues to operate normally.
