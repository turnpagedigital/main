### I. Madoff / BLMIS — Post-17th Distribution Posture and Claim Pricing Implications

The BLMIS liquidation proceeding (*Sec. Investor Protection Corp. v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB)) entered a relative quiet period in the weeks following the 17th interim distribution. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the 17th pro rata interim distribution, paying more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The 17th distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; combined with the prior 16 distributions, approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.



As of May 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.411 billion.
 
As of May 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
As of the 17th distribution, SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied claims, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


From a claim-trading standpoint, the current 72.848% cumulative recovery rate — against admitted claims of approximately $20.3 billion — sets a floor for secondary-market pricing of remaining BLMIS claims. 
The admitted claims amount totals approximately $20.3 billion, against a fictitious statement balance of approximately $64 billion.
 Claims priced materially below the 72.8% mark reflect time-value discount and residual litigation risk on still-disputed accounts; claims priced above it incorporate an expectation of additional Picard litigation recoveries. The 50th fee application — filed February 13, 2026 per the trustee's press release page — remains a signal event: BakerHostetler's cumulative fees in this engagement are a recurring subject of creditor scrutiny, and fee application rulings affect net-of-cost distribution projections.

No 18th distribution motion has been announced as of this writing. Picard's language in connection with the 17th distribution — 
"eligible BLMIS customers have now received approximately 72.848 percent of their allowed claims" and "we anticipate achieving additional recoveries through litigation and settlements"
 — suggests further distributions depend on continued adversary-proceeding settlements rather than identified pool proceeds already in hand.

### II. Stanford Receivership — 12th (Final) Distribution Under the Final Distribution Plan; $157M SG Suisse Settlement Funding Sequence

The Stanford receivership (*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd. et al.*, N.D. Tex., Dallas Div., before Judge David C. Godbey) has entered what the Receiver describes as the Final Distribution Plan phase. 
According to the Receiver's update posted April 6, 2026, payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 
A May 4, 2026 update concerning payments under the Final Distribution Plan followed an April 6, 2026 update; the Receiver also filed a response in late May addressing a submission by Jaime Escalona regarding the distribution process.



On June 1, 2026, the Receiver responded to Mr. Escalona's submission, which response includes an update concerning the status of the Receiver's ongoing distribution efforts.
 The response is available on the receivership's public website. 
On May 12, 2026, the Court granted the Receiver's motion for authorization to dispose of certain Stanford records and equipment, with the order containing applicable deadlines.
 The records-disposal authorization is a meaningful late-stage signal: receivers typically seek this relief only when they expect no further material litigation requiring documentary support.

The SG Suisse settlement — $157 million, memorialized in a February 25, 2025 order from Judge Godbey — remains the principal unfunded asset. 
The Court's February 2025 order stated that all proceedings with respect to the entry and finality of the Bar Order are closed and further objections are barred; once that order becomes final and no longer subject to any objections, the parties can proceed with filing motions to dismiss the cases pending against SG Suisse, and SG Suisse will thereafter be obligated to fund the $157 million settlement to the Receivership.
 The funding trigger is therefore the finality of the SG Suisse Bar Order — that sequence, not any further court hearing, determines when $157 million enters the distribution pool.


A separate inquiry flagged $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025, with claimants questioning whether those funds will be included in the current Final Distribution Plan or addressed in a future distribution.
 The Receiver has not publicly responded to that specific inquiry as of this writing. For claim-pricing purposes, the $8.1 million figure is small relative to the overall distribution pool (total recoveries now exceed $2.7 billion across all settlement tranches) but material to individual claimants expecting a known final distribution percentage.

The 90-day check validity window flagged in the Final Distribution Plan documentation is a practical trap for foreign-domiciled claimants. 
All checks in the 12th distribution are valid for only 90 days from their issuance date.
 For Venezuelan claimants in particular, courier logistics create real forfeiture risk within that window. Claims-buyers acquiring distressed Stanford positions at this stage should factor that forfeiture risk into pricing.

### III. Power Block Coin / SmartFi — Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041; Liquidating Trust, Insider-Transaction Claims, and the Celsius Overhang

The Power Block Coin, LLC case (*In re Power Block Coin, LLC*, Bankr. D. Utah, No. 24-23041) continues in active posture before Judge Cathleen D. Parker, with the most recent docket activity at entry 519, filed June 9, 2026. The case was originally assigned to Judge Joel T. Marker before the current judge designation reflected in the tracked-case ground truth.

The case's structural complexity derives from the intersection of three claim pools: (1) retail crypto-lending claimants who held SmartFi-branded products, (2) Celsius Network as a supersized creditor asserting claims exceeding $133 million, and (3) potential fraudulent-transfer and insider-transaction adversary proceedings not yet filed of record. 
Power Block Coin, which operated under the name SmartFi, filed for bankruptcy protection on June 20, 2024; the plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.



Greenberg Traurig, LLP, on January 30, 2025, filed a comprehensive liquidation plan on behalf of the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, outlining a strategy to maximize value for creditors through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
A key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors; the committee's filing raises concerns about the company's pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates.



A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah; the committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 Under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B), the Liquidating Trustee would bear the burden of demonstrating that the debtor received less than reasonably equivalent value in exchange for the transfer — a relatively straightforward showing if the loan was structurally subordinated at origination and the collateral was subsequently encumbered. State UFTA/UVTA claims under Utah Code Ann. § 25-6-202 through § 25-6-308 provide an additional avenue with a four-year lookback that extends beyond § 548's two-year window.

The Celsius overhang is the fulcrum variable for non-institutional SmartFi claimants. 
The Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against the Debtor in connection with the pending bankruptcy case of Celsius Network LLC et al. (case No. 22-10964) pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 If Celsius's $133 million claim is allowed in full, general unsecured creditors below that tier face a recovery percentage that is highly sensitive to the outcome of the insider-transaction and preference litigation the Liquidating Trustee is expected to pursue. Docket entry 519 (June 9, 2026) carries no public description in the available data; claimants and prospective claim-buyers should pull the filing directly from CourtListener docket 68873409 or PACER before drawing conclusions about case posture.

### IV. SEC Enforcement Backdrop — Fraud-Driven Receiver Pipeline and Whistleblower Channel

The institutional backdrop for new receiver appointments has shifted under the current SEC leadership. 
The Commission under President Trump's administration has reoriented its enforcement program away from what it characterizes as headline-driven, high-volume enforcement activity and toward a "back to basics" approach to fraud, investor protection, and individual accountability.
 
During fiscal year 2025, the Commission filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions, obtaining orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion.
 
Notable 2025 actions included charges in connection with several large-scale Ponzi schemes, including one involving approximately 2,700 investors and an alleged $400 million in losses and another involving approximately 300 investors and more than $140 million in alleged losses.


The whistleblower pipeline remains the leading indicator for the next wave of receiver appointments. 
During fiscal year 2025, the Commission returned approximately $262 million to harmed investors and awarded approximately $60 million to 48 individual whistleblowers; the SEC received a record 53,753 tips, complaints, and referrals in fiscal year 2025, nearly 19 percent more than in the prior fiscal year.
 The record tip volume — 53,753 — is a forward-looking signal: tip surges typically precede enforcement-action surges by 12–24 months, meaning the receiver-appointment pipeline for FY 2026 and FY 2027 is likely larger than the raw FY 2025 action count suggests.


The SEC has appointed David Woodcock, an SEC veteran currently in private practice, as the new director of the Enforcement Division.
 Woodcock's appointment follows the abrupt departure of Judge Margaret Ryan on March 16, 2026. The new director's posture — focused on fraud and individual accountability — is constructive for recovery-side claimants and adverse to net winners who might otherwise expect regulatory forbearance to shadow private clawback exposure.
