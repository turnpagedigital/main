### Linqto Texas, LLC — S.D. Tex. | Customer Turnover Motions Denied

The operative ruling was issued August 4 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas. 
A Texas bankruptcy judge rejected motions by Linqto customers that sought an order forcing the investment platform to hand over shares in crypto company Circle Internet Group Inc., ruling that the customers' requests were legally deficient.
 The article, by Alex Wittenberg, was published at 3:20 PM EDT August 4. 
The case is pending in the U.S. District & Bankruptcy Courts of the Southern District of Texas.


The procedural backdrop: 
Linqto Texas, LLC and affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 7, 2025, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, following the shutdown of a private investment platform that facilitated over $460 million in transactions. The filing followed the discovery of alleged securities law violations and structural defects that raised questions about what customers own and whether title was properly transferred.
 
The company's portfolio included significant holdings such as a $399 million stake in Ripple, $35 million in SpaceX equity, and $106.6 million in shares of the publicly traded fintech Circle Internet Group Inc.



On February 6, 2026, the plan was approved in court with support from 95% of customers, despite objections from the former CEO and certain shareholders.
 
Customers were given a choice to select how to allocate their claim between a Liquidating Trust, a Closed-End Fund, or a combination of both options. On January 13, 2026, VanEck was selected to manage the Closed-End Fund, and Forge Global, Inc. was chosen as the Liquidating Trustee of the Liquidating Trust.


The August 4 ruling is significant for two reasons. First, it confirms that the plan's channeling structure is holding: customers who disagree with the confirmed plan's treatment cannot bypass it through standalone turnover motions — the court found those motions legally deficient on their face. Second, the Circle shares specifically are a live asset inside the estate: 
the portfolio included $106.6 million in Circle Internet Group Inc. shares
, and customers seeking direct delivery of those shares in-kind — rather than accepting the plan's liquidating trust / closed-end fund allocation — lost that argument as of yesterday. This lines up with the broader SPV-title problem at the heart of the case: 
investors may not actually own any of the private shares they thought they were purchasing. Instead, they may face reclassification as unsecured creditors, with no claim to actual shares, just a cash claim worth far less.


The Forge Global trustee dispute — reported approximately two weeks ago — remains unresolved on the public record and continues to shadow plan implementation. 
Linqto and its creditors sued Forge Global Holdings after the private-market platform allegedly attempted to withdraw as trustee of the customer recovery trust just five days before Linqto planned to launch the trust on July 20. Forge was expected to hold customer assets, manage transfers and help administer the recovery plan.
 The August 4 ruling does not resolve that dispute, but it does narrow the range of unilateral remedies available to individual customers outside the plan structure.

The presiding judge's name on the Circle turnover ruling is not verified from the Law360 abstract — the full article is behind a paywall. The February confirmation judge was Hon. Alfredo R. Perez (S.D. Tex.), per the February 2, 2026 hearing agenda at Epiq. (__[Law360 — Linqto Customers Can't Force Turnover Of Crypto Co. Shares](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy/articles/2508964)__)
