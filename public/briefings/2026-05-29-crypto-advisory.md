# CRYPTO INSOLVENCY ADVISORY | May 29, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The FTX Recovery Trust today commences its fifth distribution to creditors and, for the first time, makes payments to holders of allowed preferred equity interests — a milestone signaling that the estate's distribution waterfall has descended past the senior and customer classes to its most junior tranche. The fifth distribution carries an April 30, 2026 record date, and preferred-equity holders are paid through a dedicated vehicle, the Preferred Shareholder Remission Fund Trust ("PSRT"), contingent on each holder having completed ownership, KYC, and tax documentation. That equity is being reached at all is a function of recovery rates extraordinary by chapter 11 standards: prior rounds have returned roughly $10 billion, with U.S. customer Class 5B and Convenience Classes 6A and 6B at 100 percent and Class 7 set to receive a cumulative 120 percent — recoveries driven not by petition-date asset preservation but by the post-petition appreciation of the estate's crypto and venture holdings, an inversion of the usual liquidation arithmetic in which estate value erodes from the filing date forward. ([FTX Payout Schedule](https://qredax.com/en/ftx-payout-schedule-2026)) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/18/sam-bankman-fried-s-bankrupt-exchange-ftx-set-to-repay-creditors-usd2-2-billion-this-month))

The mechanics of the next tranche, set out in the Recovery Trust's May 26 notice, illustrate how a maturing estate converts contingency into cash. The sixth distribution will carry a June 16, 2026 record date and a July 31, 2026 payment date, again administered through BitGo, Kraken, and Payoneer, with the next preferred-equity payment riding the same calendar and the NFT distribution process for holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims opening June 30. More consequential for claim valuation is the Trust's amended notice seeking to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $600 million — from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion — which, if approved by the Bankruptcy Court, releases held-back cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims. The reserve reduction is the quiet engine of every late-stage liquidation: as disputed claims are resolved, adjudicated down, or expunged under 11 U.S.C. § 502, the over-provisioned reserve is freed and the marginal recovery to allowed claimants rises without any new asset entering the estate. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ftx-sets-next-distribution-date-and-amends-proposed-disputed-claims-reserve-reduction-302782254.html)) ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260318ny13262/ftx-recovery-trust-to-distribute-approximately-22-billion-to-creditors-in-fourth-distribution-on-march-31-2026))

FTX now sits at the mature end of a distress spectrum whose newest entrants are still litigating the threshold questions FTX resolved by plan. BlockFills, the Chicago trading and lending firm that filed chapter 11 in Delaware in March following the February 2026 market dislocation, is pursuing a customer-led reorganization against estimated liabilities of $100 million to $500 million — and, like every exchange estate before it, will turn on whether customer crypto is property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or property held in trust for customers, the same § 541 fault line that defined Celsius and that FTX's plan ultimately routed around through a negotiated global settlement. The recurring lesson across the wave is that terms-of-service drafting, not the petition-date balance sheet, frequently dictates whether a depositor stands as a trust beneficiary or an unsecured creditor — a question that BlockFills's creditors' committee will now contest from first principles. ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/11/crypto-trading-firm-blockfills-has-filed-for-bankruptcy)) ([Disruption Banking](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2026/03/16/from-61b-volume-to-chapter-11-blockfills-falls-amid-ongoing-crypto-downturn/))

Where FTX is harvesting appreciation, the remainder of the docket is harvesting litigation. In the Genesis estate, Judge Stefan Underhill's February denial of Digital Currency Group's motion to dismiss — applying the Howey and Reves tests to treat the lending program as a security and lifting the PSLRA discovery stay — keeps alive both the investor class action and Genesis's own affiliate-recovery claims seeking some $3.3 billion from DCG and Barry Silbert over the post-Three Arrows promissory note. Celsius's Litigation Oversight Committee has allocated more than $125 million from its litigation-recovery account to a further creditor distribution, layered atop the roughly $300 million it extracted from Tether — a fraction of its $4.3 billion claim but a meaningful augmentation of estate value sourced entirely from retained causes of action. Prime Trust's PCT Litigation Trust, for its part, is pressing near-$1 billion clawback theories against Swan Bitcoin and a separate avoidance action against Strike over pre-petition Bitcoin transfers, the digital-asset analogue to the constructive-fraudulent-transfer recoveries that anchor any conventional liquidation. ([Silver Golub & Teitell](https://www.sgtlaw.com/media/press-releases/2026-02-25-federal-judge-allows-crypto-class-action-to-proceed-against-digital-currency-group)) ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/374625/celsius-wins-300-million-tether-bankruptcy-case-fraction-4-3-billion-claim)) ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/368244/swan-bitcoin-nearly-1-billion-lawsuit-prime-trust-collapse))

Read together, today's preferred-equity payment is less an FTX-specific curiosity than a marker of where the 2022–2023 insolvency cohort now stands: the estates that monetized appreciating tokens and venture stakes (FTX, Celsius) are paying down their waterfalls and reaching junior tranches, the estates whose value lives in litigation (Genesis, Prime Trust) are converting adversary proceedings into distributable cash, and the newest filers (BlockFills) are re-litigating the customer-property and TOS questions from the ground up. The through-line for cross-border practitioners is coordination: Three Arrows Capital's liquidators continue to press a contested $1.53 billion claim against the FTX estate even as they pursue founder asset freezes offshore, a reminder that a single counterparty's claim can remain live in one forum while its assets are chased through Cayman, BVI, and Singapore in another, and that claim valuation in any one estate is hostage to the resolution calendar of every other. ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/06/23/bankrupt-crypto-exchange-ftx-slams-three-arrows-capital-s-usd1-53b-claim-3ac-is-owed-nothing)) ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/three-arrows-capital-founders-hit-with-1-billion-asset-freeze/))

## Recommended Actions

Allowed-claim holders should confirm their eligibility against the April 30 record date and verify that KYC and tax documentation is current before the fifth distribution settles, while preferred-equity holders should confirm PSRT onboarding is complete, as undocumented holders will be passed over rather than reserved for; both classes should calendar the June 16 record date and July 31 payment date for the next round and the June 30 NFT distribution window. Claim purchasers and secondary-market participants should reprice FTX paper in light of the proposed $600 million reduction of the disputed claims reserve — a reduction that, once approved under 11 U.S.C. § 502, mechanically lifts recoveries to allowed claimants — and should monitor the reserve-reduction docket for objections that could delay release of the freed cash. Creditors of the newer estates, BlockFills foremost, should preserve their account agreements and terms-of-service versions now, because the § 541 trust-versus-estate determination will be decided on that contractual record rather than on the balance sheet, and the Celsius and FTX precedents cut in opposite directions depending on the drafting. Finally, cross-border claimants holding positions against multiple estates should coordinate their proofs of claim and asset-tracing strategy across U.S. chapter 11 and offshore (Cayman, BVI, Singapore, Hong Kong) proceedings, recognizing that the Three Arrows posture demonstrates how an unresolved intercompany claim can hold an otherwise-distributable estate in suspense.
