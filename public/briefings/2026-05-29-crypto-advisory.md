# CRYPTO INSOLVENCY ADVISORY | May 29, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The FTX Recovery Trust today commences its fifth distribution to creditors and, for the first time, makes payments to holders of allowed preferred equity interests — a milestone signaling that the estate's distribution waterfall has descended past the senior and customer classes to its most junior tranche. The fifth distribution carries an April 30, 2026 record date, and preferred-equity holders are paid through a dedicated vehicle, the Preferred Shareholder Remission Fund Trust ("PSRT"), contingent on each holder having completed ownership, KYC, and tax documentation. 

Prior rounds have returned roughly $10 billion, with U.S. customer Class 5B and Convenience Classes 6A and 6B at 100% recoveries and Class 7 set to receive a cumulative 120%. ([FTX Payout Schedule](https://qredax.com/en/ftx-payout-schedule-2026)) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/18/sam-bankman-fried-s-bankrupt-exchange-ftx-set-to-repay-creditors-usd2-2-billion-this-month)). The sixth distribution, as broken down in the Recovery Trust's May 26 notice, will be subject to a June 16, 2026 record date and a July 31, 2026 payment date. Meanwhile, the NFT distribution process for holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims opens June 30. 

More consequential is the Trust's amended notice seeking to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $600 million, which, if approved by the Bankruptcy Court, releases held-back cash for distribution to holders of allowed claims. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ftx-sets-next-distribution-date-and-amends-proposed-disputed-claims-reserve-reduction-302782254.html)) ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260318ny13262/ftx-recovery-trust-to-distribute-approximately-22-billion-to-creditors-in-fourth-distribution-on-march-31-2026))

While the FTX case bypassed many of the burning questions, such as customer ownership of crypto assets, these questions are getting teed up in BlockFills. The recurring lesson is that terms-of-service drafting, not the petition-date balance sheet, frequently dictates whether a depositor stands as a trust beneficiary or an unsecured creditor — a question that BlockFills's creditors' committee will now contest from first principles. ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/11/crypto-trading-firm-blockfills-has-filed-for-bankruptcy)) ([Disruption Banking](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2026/03/16/from-61b-volume-to-chapter-11-blockfills-falls-amid-ongoing-crypto-downturn/))

In the Genesis estate, Judge Stefan Underhill's February denial of Digital Currency Group's motion to dismiss — applying the Howey and Reves tests to treat the lending program as a security and lifting the PSLRA discovery stay — keeps alive both the investor class action and Genesis's own affiliate-recovery claims seeking some $3.3 billion from DCG and Barry Silbert over the post-Three Arrows promissory note. 

Celsius's Litigation Oversight Committee has allocated more than $125 million from its litigation-recovery account to a further creditor distribution, layered atop the roughly $300 million it extracted from Tether — a fraction of its $4.3 billion claim but a meaningful augmentation of estate value sourced entirely from retained causes of action. Prime Trust's PCT Litigation Trust, for its part, is pressing near-$1 billion clawback theories against Swan Bitcoin and a separate avoidance action against Strike over pre-petition Bitcoin transfers, the digital-asset analogue to the constructive-fraudulent-transfer recoveries that anchor any conventional liquidation.
