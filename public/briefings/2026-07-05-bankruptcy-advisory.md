### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Bankr. D. Del. 26-10371 (TMH)

**Where things stand.** 
On March 15, 2026, Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated debtor entities — collectively operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed Chapter 11 petitions in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware before Judge Thomas M. Horan.
 
The filing reported $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.


**The dual-track resolved — Keyrock is the buyer.** 
As of a bankruptcy court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million, and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The $3.25 million figure is striking against a $60-billion-per-year trading volume business — the spread reflects the practical reality that what is being transferred is infrastructure and client relationships rather than performing book value. A sale approval hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026; whether the order entered on that date is unconfirmed from available sources and has not been verified this run.

**Plan track: confirmation hearing July 13.** 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered an order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing objection and claim deadlines, setting the record date, approving solicitation materials and ballot forms, and establishing voting procedures.
 
The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 The plan vehicle is the Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Dkt. 391), with the operative disclosure statement at Dkt. 388. Creditors who did not file objections by July 1 have forfeited the ability to be heard at confirmation on grounds not reserved by the court. Any holdout customer who filed a late objection — or who attempted to press the commingling dispute outside the plan process — is likely to face a ripeness or stay-violation argument at the July 13 hearing.

**The commingling issue: still the defining recovery question.** 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
The Debtors' position rests on a procedural deficiency: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 
If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, to be distributed pro rata under the Plan, and to serve as the basis for the cash collateral arrangement.
 This framing is outcome-determinative. Customer claimants who believe their digital assets are traceable and segregable must either (a) have initiated adversary proceedings before the bar date or (b) press the issue at confirmation through objection — which the July 1 deadline has now foreclosed for any party that did not timely act. The plan's treatment of customer claims relative to general unsecured claims is the central recovery-driver for this estate.

**Pre-petition context, for distressed-buyer reference.** 
On February 6, 2026, BlockFills publicly announced a broader temporary suspension; clients could still open and close trading positions but could not move funds off the platform.
 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 
On January 16, 2026, Alberta transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; these assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes and the wallet was empty within 1.5 hours.
 That fact pattern will be Exhibit A in any tracing analysis.

### Sleep Number Corporation — Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 26-11399 (KYP)

**A compressed 363 sale with a $257.5 million gap.** 
On June 12, 2026, Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 by the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 
On June 12, 2026, the Company entered into a stalking horse Asset Purchase Agreement with SNBR, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc., to purchase substantially all of the assets of the Company for $415 million in cash and the assumption of certain liabilities.


**The secured debt overhang is the defining creditor dynamic.** 
Substantially all of the Company's debt, with balances of approximately $672.5 million in the aggregate principal amount as of the Petition Date, is in default and accelerated, but subject to the automatic stay under the Bankruptcy Code.
 
The bid that emerged covers roughly 62 cents on the dollar of the funded debt, well short of the full payment the milestones contemplated.
 
As of April 30, 2026, the Debtors reported consolidated assets of roughly $642.3 million against consolidated debts of roughly $1.281 billion, a negative net worth near $639 million.
 The stalking horse price does not cover secured debt in full, which structurally forecloses any residual value for unsecured creditors unless competing bids emerge materially above $672.5 million — a scenario the compressed timeline and the pre-marketing results make unlikely but not impossible.

**Pre-marketing data and bidding calendar.** 
The proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8, 2026 bid deadline, a July 13, 2026 auction, and a July 15 sale hearing
 — all before Judge Paek. 
Building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, with 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals, the sale process is well-advanced.
 The section 341 meeting of creditors was calendared for July 8 at 1:00 p.m. ET; the second day hearing on remaining first day motions is July 9 at 10:00 a.m. ET. Debtors' counsel is Brian M. Resnick of Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP.

**Prepetition credit history and the forbearance bridge.** 
The Prepetition Credit Agreement, dated February 18, 2018 and amended through a Forbearance Agreement and Thirteenth Amendment dated April 27, 2026, governs the Company's obligations, with U.S. Bank National Association as Administrative Agent.
 
The Thirteenth Amendment and Forbearance Agreement provided $25 million in new term loans maturing June 30, 2026, and suspended the minimum liquidity covenant into early July.
 
When the secured lenders advanced an additional $25 million in late April, they conditioned it on milestones requiring a strategic transaction that would pay their prepetition obligations in full.
 That condition was not met — the $415 million bid falls approximately $257.5 million short of the $672.5 million in funded debt. The lender group supported the filing anyway, suggesting the secured creditors concluded that a court-supervised process would maximize their recovery over a workout or foreclosure alternative.

**Tariff dimension.** 
The filing narrative cites the April 2025 wave of U.S. tariffs — including IEEPA-based levies on certain direct-import products — as a compounding stressor, followed by regulatory uncertainty after a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling.
 Sleep Number monetized at least one IEEPA tariff-refund claim through a prepetition sale. Unsecured claimants and landlords with 572 store locations across the U.S. are the primary constituencies watching the July 13 auction for upside relative to the stalking horse floor.

### Subchapter V Legislative Gap — S. 3977 and the July 2 Law360 Commentary

**Why this matters now.** A July 2, 2026 Law360 Expert Analysis piece by Ted Gavin of Gavin Solmonese argues that 
Congress must pass a bill to permanently restore the Subchapter V debt limit and clarify several other key points of the law to prevent a practical restructuring tool from becoming a costly procedural morass.
 The piece lands as the legislative picture remains unresolved. 
Despite bipartisan support to maintain the increased debt limits, Congress failed to pass the bill, and on June 21, 2024, the increased debt limit expired.
 
As of January 1, 2026, the Subchapter V debt limit was $3,424,000.


**S. 3977 — the pending fix.** 
On March 3, 2026, six Senators — Grassley (R-IA), Durbin (D-IL), Cornyn (R-TX), Whitehouse (D-RI), Graham (R-SC), and Coons (D-DE) — introduced S. 3977, titled the "Bankruptcy Threshold Adjustment Act of 2026."
 
The essential features of S. 3977 are: it sets the debt limit for Subchapter V eligibility at $7,500,000; it sets the debt limit for Chapter 13 eligibility at $2,750,000; and it provides that the new debt limits shall apply to cases commenced on or after the date of enactment.
 
A similar bill has been introduced in the House.
 The current $3.424 million ceiling is deterring filings that would otherwise be appropriate Subchapter V cases, forcing debtors into more expensive full Chapter 11 proceedings with creditor committees and more complex disclosure requirements. The practical impact falls disproportionately on small manufacturers and service businesses whose debt profiles place them between the old and new thresholds.

**Prior history counsels skepticism about timing.** 
In June of 2024, a bankruptcy amendment to extend the $7,500,000 debt limit was before Congress on a unanimous-vote procedure in both houses. Everyone in both houses of Congress was ready to vote "Aye" on that extension — except for one Senator. That means 435 members of the House and 99 members of the Senate were prepared to vote "Aye," but one Senator defected.
 Given that history, distressed investors and practitioners should not assume S. 3977 will clear before the next disruption cycle peaks.

## Recommended Actions

**BlockFills creditors** should treat the July 13 confirmation hearing as the effective deadline for any remaining procedural leverage — parties who filed timely objections by July 1 should coordinate with counsel on whether to appear, negotiate, or withdraw; those who did not are bound by whatever the court confirms. The critical unresolved question — whether commingled customer digital assets are estate property or customer property under section 541 — will effectively be decided at or before confirmation unless an adversary proceeding was timely filed under FRBP 7001(b); any creditor who believes it has a tracing argument should audit that procedural question immediately with restructuring counsel. **Sleep Number creditors, landlords, and trade claimants** face a narrow window before the July 10 sale objection deadline: the sale process is moving fast, the $415 million stalking horse covers approximately 62 cents on the prepetition secured debt, and there is no structural path to unsecured recovery unless competing bids clear the full $672.5 million funded-debt stack — landlords with non-assumed leases should file timely objections or negotiate lease rejection damages now rather than waiting for the sale order. **On Subchapter V**, any small-business debtor currently evaluating eligibility under the $3,424,000 cap should preserve optionality: if S. 3977 passes and applies prospectively from the date of enactment, a filing deferred by weeks could access the $7.5 million threshold; file too early and that eligibility is locked to the current lower limit. Monitor Congress.gov for floor scheduling — the bill has bipartisan Senate sponsors but no confirmed floor date as of today.
