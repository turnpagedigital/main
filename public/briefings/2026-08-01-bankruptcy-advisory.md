### RNDC — Post-Filing Posture (Running Coverage; Next Milestone August 24)

*Republic National Distributing Company, LLC, et al. — Bankr. S.D. Tex. (Judge Lopez) — filed July 26, 2026*

Covered here July 27–31. No new docket activity verified in the last 24 hours. The case remains in its opening phase.


RNDC — formerly the second-largest U.S. wine and spirits distributor — filed voluntarily on July 26 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division, along with 17 affiliated entities, with major creditors including Proximo Spirits, Edrington, and Pernod Ricard.
 
The petition listed liabilities between $1 billion and $10 billion against assets of $500 million to $1 billion.
 
More than 100,000 creditors are involved, and the filing noted that no alcohol distributor of its size had previously sought Chapter 11 protection.


The size of the creditor body is the primary distress-investment consideration. 
The company estimated over $400 million in general unsecured claims.
 
Among the 30 largest unsecured creditors, Proximo Spirits (José Cuervo maker) holds a $93.9 million claim, while Edrington carries $5.64 million.


The case is a wind-down, not a reorganization. 
RNDC initiated Chapter 11 to pursue potential sales of remaining markets and wind down operations that cannot be sold.
 
From late 2022 through 2025, the debtors lost key suppliers that collectively accounted for more than $3 billion in annual revenue; the renewal of one key contract alone reduced gross profit by approximately $50 million.
 
As of the petition date, approximately 700 suppliers had transitioned to new distributors.



The second-day hearing is scheduled for August 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. CT.
 
Debtors are represented by Kirkland & Ellis (Joshua Sussberg, Christopher Marcus, Maddison Levine, Joshua Raphael, Rachel Golden, Jennifer Davis) and Porter Hedges LLP (John Higgins, M. Shane Johnson, Megan Young-John, Joanna Caytas), with AlixPartners' John Castellano serving as CRO and Lazard (Christian Tempke) as investment banker.


**Distressed-investor focus:** With $400M+ in general unsecured claims spread across 100,000+ creditors and a liquidating posture, claim aggregation and secondary-market positioning ahead of any 363 sale proceeds distribution will be the primary recovery lever. The August 24 second-day hearing is the first major substantive checkpoint.

### Legislative Watch — Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026

This is a running legislative matter, not a 24-hour delta, but press coverage spiked this week and the bill's posture has evolved since last covered here.


Senators Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI), Josh Hawley (R-MO), Dick Durbin (D-IL), and Representatives Emilia Sykes (D-OH) and Lance Gooden (R-TX) reintroduced the Consumer Protection and Corporate Accountability in Bankruptcy Act of 2026, targeting the Texas Two-Step divisive-merger maneuver.
 
The bill is endorsed by the National Association of Consumer Bankruptcy Attorneys and the National Association of Consumer Advocates.


The bill's operative mechanism: 
courts would presume a bankruptcy has been filed in bad faith if it shows clear indicia of a Texas Two-Step, including if the debtor manufactured venue, if a purpose or effect of the filing is to gain a tactical litigation advantage, impose delay upon creditors, or cap liability even where the debtor or an affiliate has sufficient funds to pay.
 A second prong — not yet enacted — would block automatic-stay extensions to non-filing affiliates where the debtor executed a divisive merger within the prior four years. (__[Sen. Whitehouse Press Release](https://www.whitehouse.senate.gov/news/release/whitehouse-hawley-durbin-sykes-gooden-reintroduce-bipartisan-legislation-to-end-texas-two-step/)__, April 21, 2026)

The bill has not cleared committee. Its relevance for active cases: any debtor that executed a Texas-style divisive merger post-enactment faces a rebuttable bad-faith presumption at the outset, shifting the burden and substantially raising dismissal risk. For distressed investors monitoring J&J talc successor liabilities and Georgia-Pacific asbestos exposure, the legislative trajectory warrants close attention.
