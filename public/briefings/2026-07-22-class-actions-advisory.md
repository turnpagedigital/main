### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Final Approval Entered; Fee Haircut Sharpens the Lodestar Benchmark


On July 20, 2026, Hon. Araceli Martínez-Olguín entered Docket Entry 680 in *Bartz et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 4:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), granting final approval of the class action settlement and granting in part the motion for attorneys' fees, expenses, and service awards.



The 23-page order resolves several outstanding administrative details and rules definitively on attorneys' fees.
 
Judge Martínez-Olguín found that the settlement provides "meaningful relief" to affected authors and publishers.


**The settlement economics.** 
Anthropic is required to pay $1.5 billion to authors and publishers of nearly half a million books it downloaded from notorious pirate sites, and to destroy all original files torrented or downloaded from those sites, as well as any copies originating from those torrented copies.
 
Under the approved agreement, rightsholders receive roughly $3,000 per work, covering over 500,000 books.


**Claims rate.** 
The settlement recorded a claims rate of 92.77 percent — an extraordinarily high participation figure that, in the structural math of a claims-made fund, dramatically compresses per-claim recovery relative to a low-filing-rate scenario.
 
Approximately 91% of the more than 482,000 books covered by the ruling have been claimed by authors or publishers now due payment.
 At that participation level, the fund is effectively fully subscribed; there is no windfall available to late entrants.

**Fee award — the lodestar discipline.** This is where the order carries the most instructive value for fee-award watchers. 
Class counsel initially sought $300 million, later trimmed to $187.5 million; the court approved approximately $101.5 million.
 
The court rejected the requested multiplier of 6.92 on class counsel's estimated lodestar of $27,082,963, instead approving a multiplier of 3.75 for a total fee award of $101,561,111.
 The Ninth Circuit's recognized range of 1×–4× (per *Vizcaino*) effectively capped the analysis; the court anchored at 3.75× rather than the 6.92× premium plaintiffs' firms sought for what they characterized as exceptional risk and result. 
By rough math, the approved award works out to approximately $2,953 per hour across the 34,381.6 hours of attorney time the court credited.


**Named plaintiff service awards.** 
The court also reduced the service award for each named author plaintiff from the $50,000 requested to $15,000.


**Class definition and non-class member objections.** 
The court declined to entertain objections from non-class members but took care to remind those individuals that, because the settlement does not cover them, they remain free to bring their own claims.
 
As defined by the court, the class includes legal or beneficial copyright owners of any book downloaded by Anthropic through Library Genesis (LibGen) in June 2021 or from Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi) in July 2022, that carries an ISBN or ASIN, was registered with the Copyright Office within five years of first publication, and was registered before being downloaded or within three months of publication.
 The specificity of those two acquisition windows — LibGen June 2021 and PiLiMi July 2022 — is the operative boundary. Works outside those two events are not covered and carry independent claim potential.

**Settlement administrator.** The Anthropic Copyright Settlement Administrator (JND Legal Administration) is positioned to begin distribution promptly. No appeal is pending as of this writing; the 30-day objector window to appeal ran against the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing, and the court received only 54 written submissions. 
The motions were heard before the court on May 14, 2026; having considered the parties' papers and the submissions from 54 class members and putative class members, the court granted final approval.


**Precedential framing.** 
This is the first major settlement in dozens of AI copyright lawsuits that are still working their way through courts.
 Counsel on both sides of the broader AI-training copyright docket will study the 3.75× multiplier, the claims-rate math, and the class-definition architecture. For pending matters — including the various suits against OpenAI, Meta, and Stability AI — *Bartz* now functions as the practical settlement benchmark.

### II. BCBS Subscriber Antitrust Settlement (*In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406) — Distribution in Active Rollout; Tier-1 Wave Expected to Complete by End of July

This matter entered active distribution in May and remains the largest antitrust settlement currently paying out to U.S. consumers. 
The case is *In re: Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation*, MDL 2406, Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Alabama, Southern Division.



Judge Manasco signed the distribution order on April 24, 2026, granting Class Counsel's motion (filed April 23, 2026) for distribution of the Net Settlement Fund to approved Damages Class members, and directing settlement administrator JND Legal Administration to begin issuing payments in the week of May 11.



The $2.67 billion gross settlement (approximately $1.9 billion Net Settlement Fund after attorneys' fees and administrative costs) is one of the largest antitrust settlements in U.S. healthcare history.
 
After deducting attorneys' fees and administrative costs, the net fund will be distributed across roughly 6 million claimants, with an average payout of $333.


**Distribution mechanics.** 
Tier 1 (individual subscribers): Wave 2 of prepaid debit cards mailed the week of May 19; paper-check wave began rolling out May 25; direct deposit confirmations run 5–10 business days behind notification email.
 
Tier 2 (employee): Notification emails started June 1 for the first batch (approximately 15% of validated Tier-2 claims), with payments arriving 4–8 weeks after notification. Tier 3 (employer): First payments not yet started.
 
Estimated total Tier-1 wave completion is targeted by end of July 2026
 — meaning this week represents the closing phase of the first distribution wave.

**Eligibility hard stop.** 
Claimants must have filed claims by November 5, 2021, to be eligible.
 No remediation path exists for late filers. The second parallel track — the **Provider Settlement** ($2.8 billion, preliminary approval December 2024) — is a separate matter at an earlier procedural stage and is not yet distributing.

### III. Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Claims Live; $218B Exposure Estimate; Mandatory Arbitration Clause Now Modified

The Google advertiser mass-arbitration campaign has been running since April–May 2026 and warrants continued monitoring as the most structurally significant mass-arbitration exposure in the current cycle.


Keller Postman, the Chicago litigation firm, announced on May 11, 2026 that it is representing thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google, following two landmark federal antitrust rulings that found the company engaged in monopolistic behavior across key segments of the digital advertising ecosystem.


**Liability foundation.** 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta, search monopoly) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema, ad tech) ruled in favor of the DOJ, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in its advertising business.
 Those rulings establish the liability predicate under Sherman Act §§ 1–2 and Clayton Act § 4 (treble damages) that mass-arbitration claimants are now seeking to convert into individual recovery.

**Exposure math.** 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases estimate that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs, creating the potential for substantial recovery.
 
Keller estimates potential claims for online search and display ads could reach $218 billion or more, based on calculations from an economist his firm retained.


**Arbitration mechanics and timeline.** 
Any company that purchased ads on Google on or after August 2016 may qualify for the arbitration process.
 
Similar mass arbitrations have lasted 12 to 24 months between the filing of claims and resolution.
 
This Google campaign may be the first mass arbitration to seek to represent corporate plaintiffs, as most prior group proceedings have sought to arbitrate consumer or labor-related claims.


**Google's countermove.** As reported by a third-party commentary site, Google updated its Ads Terms of Service in July 2026, making changes to its mandatory arbitration clause — a pattern seen in prior mass-arbitration campaigns (Amazon Alexa, TurboTax). 
Ashley Keller has previously architected the Amazon Alexa campaign, which prompted Amazon to remove its mandatory arbitration clause entirely after 75,000 demands, and the TurboTax campaign that ran parallel to FTC enforcement and resolved in a confidential settlement alongside a $141 million FTC fund.
 The playbook — file at scale, impose per-claim AAA administrative costs, force a global resolution — is now well-established. The question for advertiser-claimants is whether the ToS modification affects already-accrued claims filed before the amendment, which AAA and JAMS precedent generally holds it does not for pre-amendment conduct.

## Recommended Actions

Claimants who filed in the BCBS subscriber settlement (MDL 2406, N.D. Ala.) before the November 5, 2021 deadline and have not yet received payment should check claim status through the official BCBSsettlement.com portal using their Claim Number or Unique ID — Tier-1 wave completion is targeted by end of July 2026, Tier-2 notifications began rolling June 1, and Tier-3 employer payments have not yet started. For rights-holders within the *Bartz v. Anthropic* class — meaning owners of reproduction rights to ISBN/ASIN-bearing works downloaded through LibGen in June 2021 or PiLiMi in July 2022 — the JND settlement administrator is now authorized to distribute; monitor the Anthropic Copyright Settlement Administrator site for disbursement timing. Non-class-member authors and publishers whose works fall outside those two acquisition windows should consult counsel on independent 17 U.S.C. § 501 infringement claims; Judge Martínez-Olguín's order explicitly preserved those rights. For businesses that purchased Google search or display advertising on or after August 2016, the Keller Postman mass-arbitration campaign is live on a full-contingency basis; the 12–24-month resolution timeline means early enrollment has strategic value before the arbitration queue saturates. On the mass-arbitration defense side, any company with a mandatory arbitration clause and sector-specific established liability (whether from DOJ, FTC, or private adjudication) should audit its ToS amendment procedures and per-claimant fee exposure under current AAA Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules before a campaign launches — the Google ToS modification in July 2026 is instructive precisely because it came after the claims were filed.
