### BlockFills / Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (TMH): Confirmation T-Minus 8 Days

The central legal question in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), has never changed: are the commingled customer deposits property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541, or are they held in trust or bailment and therefore not available to satisfy general unsecured claims? 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission — made under oath at the March 15 first-day — now runs through the plan mechanics headed into the July 13 confirmation hearing. (__[Veritaglobal.net / BlockFills Claims Administrator](https://www.veritaglobal.net/BlockFills)__)

The case posture entering the confirmation hearing is as follows. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc. and three affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, reporting $50 million to $100 million in assets and $100 million to $500 million in liabilities.
 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt — roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 (__[Elevenflo / Reliz Case Summary](https://elevenflo.com/cases/5378)__) The Celsius lien — itself a relic of an adverse arbitration obligation predating the filing — creates a senior secured creditor in the estate's own primary creditor: an awkward structural feature that creditors will scrutinize at confirmation. 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings entered two secured promissory notes — a $12.6 million note and a $3.6 million note — with the Prepetition Secured Party (Celsius), secured on a first-priority basis by substantially all of the Debtors' assets; interest accrued at 12% per annum; and as of the petition date, the outstanding principal was approximately $4.8 million.
 (__[Veritaglobal.net — BlockFills Doc 16, filed 03/16/26](https://www.veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260316000000000011)__)

The § 363 sale component of the dual-track was resolved in June. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 (__[Law360 — June 16, 2026](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy-authority/mid-cap/articles/2490218)__) The buyer is Keyrock SA. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property; Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" in an official bankruptcy court document filed May 26, 2026.
 (__[CoinDesk — June 1, 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/01/crypto-investment-firm-keyrock-is-acquiring-bankrupt-lender-blockfills)__)

The sale closed the § 363 track, but the plan confirmation now carries the primary creditor-recovery question forward. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order approving the disclosure statement, scheduling the confirmation hearing, establishing deadlines and procedures for objections to confirmation, claim objections, and temporary allowance of claims for voting purposes (Docket No. 388).
 The Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Dkt. 391) was then circulated for creditor vote, with the voting deadline set at July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET and confirmation objection deadline co-incident. (__[Veritaglobal.net / BlockFills Claims Administrator](https://www.veritaglobal.net/BlockFills)__) The opt-out third-party release mechanism in the plan was flagged by Law360 as a contested issue that the Delaware court reserved for the confirmation hearing itself. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge granted conditional approval to the Chapter 11 plan disclosure statement of cryptocurrency trading platform BlockFills, saying issues over the opt-out release mechanism in the proposal can be argued at a confirmation hearing.
 (__[Law360 — May 2026](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy-authority/mid-cap/articles/2482985)__)

The official creditors' committee — appointed March 27, 2026 (Dkt. 104) — comprises SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc. 
On March 27, 2026, the U.S. Trustee for Region 3 filed the Notice of Appointment of Committee of Unsecured Creditors (Docket No. 104); committee members are SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.; and the committee selected FTI Consulting as its financial advisor on April 1, 2026.
 (__[Veritaglobal.net — BlockFills Doc 264, filed 04/29/26](https://www.veritaglobal.net/blockfills/document/2610371260429000000000004)__) The committee's presence of Dominion Capital — one of the two pre-petition TRO plaintiffs — creates a structural tension: Dominion Capital's claim against the estate includes both a general creditor position and a pre-petition fraud allegation that the Debtors' commingling of customer assets constituted misappropriation.

The pre-petition litigation backdrop remains material. 
Two customer lawsuits followed the withdrawal suspension: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd. (S.D.N.Y., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672, filed February 27, 2026), alleging ongoing misappropriation and fraudulent concealment of customer assets; and on March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 (__[Bondoro — BlockFills Case Summary](https://bondoro.com/blockfills/)__) The second action, 
1548199 Alberta Ltd. and Robert J. Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. et al. (N.D. Ill., Case No. 1:26-cv-02451, filed March 5, 2026), asserted similar claims against multiple Debtor entities and named individuals, including Interim CEO Perry, former CEO Hammer, and President Wallace, with a TRO also granted.
 (__[Bondoro — BlockFills Case Summary](https://bondoro.com/blockfills/)__) The automatic stay halted both actions on March 15; how the plan treats the underlying claims — including any de facto waiver by releasing insiders — goes directly to the opt-out release issue the court reserved for July 13.

What drives the recovery posture for creditors: 
the company's liquidity crisis traces to cascading counterparty defaults — an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, and stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment.
 (__[Bondoro — Filing Alert: BlockFills Chapter 11](https://bondoro.com/blockfills-filing-alert/)__) Against $145 million in estimated customer and trade liabilities, the Keyrock § 363 sale produced only $3.25 million. Plan distributions, to the extent available, will be shaped by how the court treats the commingling question and whether any fraudulent transfer or insider liability claims survive confirmation.

### Bitcoin Depot Inc. — Bankr. S.D. Tex., Case No. 26-90528: Sale Hearing July 8, Plan Confirmation August 7

The parallel wind-down at Bitcoin Depot is now at its most consequential procedural moment as of this advisory. 
The cases are pending before the Honorable Christopher M. Lopez, U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, jointly administered under Case No. 26-90528.
 
On June 29, 2026, the Debtors conducted the Auction via video and telephone conference and selected the Winning Bidders; the Sale Hearing has been adjourned to July 8, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. CT.
 (__[Kroll Restructuring / Bitcoin Depot](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/bitcoindepot/)__)


Bitcoin Depot Inc. and certain affiliates filed for Chapter 11 protection on May 18, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, reporting $11.3 million in assets and $26.9 million in liabilities as of March 31, 2026.
 (__[Bondoro — Bitcoin Depot Case Summary](https://bondoro.com/bitcoin-depot/)__) 
Bitcoin Depot filed for Chapter 11 protection in a Texas bankruptcy court with nearly $27 million in debt and plans to wind down and sell its assets, blaming increasingly stringent regulation and enforcement.
 (__[Law360 — May 18, 2026](https://www.law360.com/articles/2478846/bitcoin-depot-hits-ch-11-blaming-crypto-crackdown)__)

The operational picture at petition date: 
kiosks generated $613.6 million of revenue for FY 2025, roughly 99.8% of total company revenue, implying average revenue per kiosk of approximately $63,100/year.
 That was the top-line. The deterioration was rapid: 
Bitcoin Depot reported a 49.2% revenue decline year-over-year for the first quarter of 2026, posting a $9.5 million net loss compared with $12.2 million in net income a year earlier.
 (__[Yahoo Finance / Reuters — May 18, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/crypto-atm-operator-bitcoin-depot-093609055.html)__)


Bitcoin Depot is facing a high-profile lawsuit led by attorneys general in Massachusetts and Iowa over allegations that it facilitated crypto scams; crypto ATM fraud hit a record $389 million in reported losses last year, a 58% increase from 2024, drawing increased regulatory scrutiny.
 (__[CoinDesk — May 18, 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/18/bitcoin-depot-once-north-america-s-largest-bitcoin-atm-operator-files-for-bankruptcy)__)


The Chapter 11 filing triggered an event of default under the company's term loan credit agreement, although creditor enforcement is stayed by the Bankruptcy Code; Bitcoin Depot has issued WARN Act notices to all employees, including executives, with executive terminations currently anticipated to be effective July 17, 2026, after a 60-day notice period.
 (__[StockTitan / 8-K — May 18, 2026](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/BTM/8-k-bitcoin-depot-inc-reports-material-event-48ece577653e.html)__)

On the plan track, 
on June 18, 2026, the Debtors filed their Combined Disclosure Statement and Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation; on June 23, 2026, the Debtors filed their First Amended Combined Disclosure Statement and Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation; the Bankruptcy Court will hold a combined hearing on the Disclosure Statement and confirmation of the Chapter 11 Plan of Liquidation on August 7, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. CT.
 (__[Kroll Restructuring / Bitcoin Depot](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/bitcoindepot/)__) 
The Bankruptcy Court has set the following claims deadlines: General Bar Date: July 21, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. CT; Governmental Bar Date: November 23, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. CT.
 (__[Kroll Restructuring / Bitcoin Depot](https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/bitcoindepot/)__) Any creditor without a filed proof of claim by July 21 risks disallowance under the plan.

The five-buyer auction result — identities of winning bidders not yet confirmed in any source available to this desk as of press time — will be tested at the July 8 hearing before Judge Lopez. The sale approval is the critical near-term gating event for any recovery; plan confirmation follows on August 7.

### Pattern Recognition: BTM-Operator Insolvency as Systemic Signal

The Bitcoin Depot collapse warrants a structural observation for this beat. 
Restructuring advisor Roshan Dharia of Echo Base called Bitcoin Depot's bankruptcy "a preview of what the broader crypto ATM industry will face in the United States over the next several years," explaining that bitcoin ATM firms' traditional model historically depended on "high transaction spreads and limited regulatory scrutiny" to offset compliance, logistics, fraud remediation, and retail revenue sharing costs, and that operators' margins are coming under threat as states impose consumer protection standards that compress fees, expand operator liability, and raise expectations around transaction monitoring and reimbursement.
 (__[Yahoo Finance — May 18, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/crypto-atm-operator-bitcoin-depot-093609055.html)__) The state-by-state compliance fragmentation — Connecticut suspended Bitcoin Depot's money transmission license in early 2026; Tennessee and Indiana enacted outright BTM bans — mirrors the pattern that preceded the embedded-fintech failures in the Synapse/Mercury/Evolve cluster. Regulatory non-uniformity creates structural insolvency risk at scale.

## Recommended Actions

**Creditors and claimants** with exposure to the BlockFills estate should act immediately on two fronts: first, confirm whether a ballot was submitted before the July 1 voting deadline, and if not, assess whether a motion for late voting or claim-allowance for voting purposes is still viable prior to the July 13 confirmation hearing; second, review the Amended Joint Plan (Dkt. 391) for the opt-out third-party release mechanism and, if an objection was not filed by July 1, consult insolvency counsel immediately regarding any post-deadline objection rights or reservation of claims against named individual defendants (Perry, Hammer, Wallace) before confirmation resolves those rights. For Bitcoin Depot creditors: the general bar date is **July 21, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. CT** — file proofs of claim through Kroll's portal before that date without exception; unsecured creditors should also monitor the July 8 sale hearing for the identities and terms of the five winning bidders, as sale proceeds will define the liquidation waterfall feeding the August 7 plan confirmation. BTM-operator exposure across the Synapse/Evolve and Prime Trust receivership tracks suggests a class of embedded-fintech creditors who may hold overlapping claims across multiple proceedings; those creditors should map their exposure now before bar dates compound.
