## Analysis & Developments

### BlockFills (In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Post-Confirmation Posture, Commingling Question Unresolved


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating under the trade name "BlockFills" — filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on March 15, 2026 (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)).
 The primary issue on this beat has not changed: whether customer digital assets are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541 or held in trust or bailment — and debtors' counsel staked out an unambiguous position at the outset. 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
The court noted that no evidence before it established the funds as anything other than estate property, but preserved the issue for further hearing.
 That preservation matters: the commingling admission forecloses most constructive-trust theories under prevailing Third Circuit authority, but creditors with bilateral agreements that contemplate segregation retain colorable arguments.

The case moved on a compressed timeline. 
The Debtors filed the Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Docket 128), the Disclosure Statement (Docket 129), and the Solicitation Procedures Motion (Docket 130), establishing the complete framework for plan solicitation and confirmation.
 
The Plan features a dual-track "toggle" mechanism allowing the Debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a Section 363 competitive sale process.
 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations. The filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on the creation of a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.



The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the Confirmation Hearing was July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 As of this writing, the docket's last recorded filing date is July 17, 2026 — consistent with post-hearing activity — but no confirmed order has been verified through public sources as of run time. Creditors should confirm current docket status directly at CourtListener (Docket ID 72494188) or veritaglobal.net/BlockFills. 
The Veritaglobal case page separately reflects a September 11, 2026 deadline entry
, which may indicate post-confirmation wind-down milestones or a continued hearing date; that entry is unelaborated in available public records.

One adjudicated side dispute is worth noting for its structural signal. 
On the motion of the Richard E. Ward Revocable Trust, the court entered an order, pursuant to Sections 362(d) and 553(a) of the Bankruptcy Code, permitting the Trust to exercise its right of recoupment against Reliz — or in the alternative, granting relief from the automatic stay to permit setoff.
 
After the recoupment calculation, the Trust held a residual general unsecured claim of $2,815,649.
 The arithmetic — 
140.42664583 BTC × $71,708.25 per coin, less the $7,224,000 loan value and $30,100 in unpaid fees — yielded the $2,815,649 figure
 — is a clean illustration of the petition-date BTC valuation methodology operating at the individual creditor level. Creditors with loan-collateral arrangements should audit whether similar recoupment or setoff rights are available before the claims bar date closes.

The capital structure context remains material for recovery analysis. 
The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 
The company's balance sheet was stressed by an $8.5 million impairment from the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy, alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 The Celsius secured position ahead of general unsecured creditors is a material structural subordination point; GUC recovery percentages depend directly on the resolution of that lien — and on whether the NewCo path or the § 363 sale path prevails. 
Cash-collateral authority remained contested through the spring, including objections over waivers and whether the sale process primarily benefited Celsius, but the docket later reflects a final cash-collateral order as the operative liquidity document and no new-money DIP facility.



The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors was appointed, comprising seven members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Energy Conversion Group; Fuel Labs Inc.; and three individual creditors. The Committee is represented by Morris James LLP.
 The presence of Dominion Capital — which obtained the prepetition TRO freezing 70.56 BTC — on the UCC is a notable dynamic: Dominion entered with an adversarial posture and now participates in the collective creditor governance structure.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: $900 Million Commences July 31

The FTX matter is now a distribution-mechanics and claims-trading story rather than a plan-confirmation story. 
The FTX Recovery Trust will begin distributing roughly $900 million to creditors on July 31, marking the Trust's fifth distribution since creditor repayments began in 2025.
 
The bankruptcy estate distributed $2.2 billion in March 2026 and has now returned nearly $10 billion to creditors and other claimants.



The full distribution sequence: approximately $1.2 billion in February 2025, $5 billion in May 2025, $1.6 billion in September 2025, and $2.2 billion in March 2026.
 
Eligible customer claims will exceed 105% cumulative recovery, while Convenience Class claims reach 120%.
 That headline figure requires context: 
the "120% recovery" is calculated against the USD value of assets on November 11, 2022, when FTX filed for bankruptcy — the date Bitcoin was trading at approximately $16,871.
 For creditors who held BTC, the dollar-denominated recovery diverges sharply from in-kind economic loss.


The latest distribution covers holders of allowed claims in the Convenience and Non-Convenience Classes who completed the required steps by the June 16, 2026 record date.
 
Eligible creditors are expected to receive funds through BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer within one to three business days after the distribution begins.
 
As part of the ongoing claims reconciliation process, the Trust filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court seeking approval to reduce the disputed claims reserve by approximately $600 million, decreasing the reserve from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion.
 Reserve compression of this magnitude, if approved, will unlock additional capacity for downstream distributions. 
The estate has not announced a date for a sixth distribution, but disputed claims resolution and final wind-down work continue.


Third-party litigation is expanding the recovery pool at the margin. 
In May 2026, Fenwick & West — which had served as FTX US's principal outside counsel before the exchange's collapse — agreed to pay $54 million to settle claims that it helped enable Sam Bankman-Fried's fraud.
 Claim buyers and secondary-market participants should note: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made where the transfer is fully processed and reflected on the official claims register as of the June 16, 2026 record date and the applicable 21-day objection period has expired without objection.
 Any transfer not perfected by that record date is excluded from the July 31 payout. 
Some creditors have criticized the pace of the process, pointing to KYC hurdles and delays for international claimants.

### Genesis / DCG — Active Adversary Litigation; Pre-Trial Conference Today

Genesis Global Holdco (Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) reached its plan effective date in August 2024, but post-confirmation litigation against parent Digital Currency Group is active and consequential for residual creditor recovery. The Kroll docket confirms 
a pre-trial conference is scheduled for July 21, 2026 at 10:00 a.m.
 — today — in the adversary proceedings between the Genesis Wind-Down Oversight Committee and DCG.

The adversary landscape is dense. 
Active adversary proceedings include: Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053; and a related New York state action, NYAG v. DCG et al.
 
Post-confirmation litigation between the wind-down debtors and DCG has continued. Genesis Global Capital sued DCG seeking to recover billions in assets and preferential transfers, alleging that DCG insiders engaged in fraud and mismanagement while Genesis was insolvent. DCG filed its own adversary complaint, and the wind-down debtors have moved to dismiss that action.


The recovery model at Genesis was structurally distinct from every other major crypto insolvency. 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value. Instead, Genesis sought to provide restitution through the Chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the New York Attorney General, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 
Per the initial distribution, BTC creditors received 51.28% recoveries, ETH creditors 65.87%, and SOL creditors 29.58%.
 
Creditors established a $70 million litigation fund to pursue claims against DCG, comprised of $26 million in BTC, $13 million in ETH, and $31 million in USD.
 The outcome of the DCG adversary proceedings — today's pre-trial conference is a procedural step in that track — will determine whether the residual recovery gap closes.


The claims objection process also remains active, with the court granting the wind-down debtors' second extension of the deadline to object to claims in February 2026.
 Unresolved claim objections directly affect the allocation mathematics for remaining distributions.

### Valuation-Date Architecture: The Structural Fault Line Across All Open Dockets

The petition-date vs. distribution-date valuation dispute is now the defining creditor-rights question across the 2022–2026 crypto insolvency wave. FTX dollarized at petition-date value and is paying 105–120% of that fiat figure — a strong nominal recovery that nevertheless leaves BTC-denominated claimants economically short at current prices. Genesis took the opposite path: in-kind recovery without a petition-date cap, negotiated through the Distribution Principles framework. 
In the wave of crypto-winter bankruptcy cases, the valuation of crypto creditors' claims has become a key issue. Virtually all crypto debtors "dollarized" claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values. Genesis, however, obtained court approval of a Chapter 11 plan that provided "in-kind" recoveries without capping claims at their petition-date values. Because some crypto prices nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected recoveries for crypto creditors.


BlockFills is now the next case in which this fault line matters. The commingling admission forecloses easy trust-based arguments, but if the NewCo path prevails, the treatment of customer balances in the NewCo subscription mechanics will effectively set the exchange rate — and the structural precedent for how an institutional crypto brokerage resolves the § 541 property-of-estate question without a formal in-kind recovery framework.

Across all three, the valuation-date architecture — petition-date fiat cap versus in-kind recovery — remains the most consequential structural variable in claim pricing and should be the first analytic lens applied to any secondary purchase or new proof-of-claim filing.
