# 🤖 LLM / Copyright | Tuesday, July 07, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**The controlling delta today is settlement administration, not new litigation.** In *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal., Courtroom 12, 19th Floor), final approval of the $1.5 billion settlement remains pending before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín as of the last confirmed docket update on July 2, 2026. 
CourtListener reflects a last-updated timestamp of July 2, 2026 on the *Bartz* docket
, with no final approval order yet entered in the public record. 
Judge Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement at the May 14 fairness hearing
, and 
in a supplemental filing ordered after that hearing, Anthropic urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs, arguing that the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect." Final approval remains pending.


That posture matters for the distribution timeline. 
Per ¶ 2.1(a)(ii) of the settlement agreement, Anthropic's $300 million second installment was due April 30, 2026, and a subsequent $450 million installment (Schedule Payment 1) is due September 25, 2026 — twelve months after the preliminary approval order.
 Distribution to claimants is conditioned on final approval and exhaustion of any objector appeal; no checks have issued. The practical question for claim-position holders this week: whether the late opt-out dispute resolves quickly or triggers iterative briefing that pushes the final order into August.

The claims economics are now fully fixed. 
Class counsel filed papers reporting that 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a 91.3% rate — vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in the March 19, 2026 Motion for Final Approval, with a large number of claims arriving at the close of the March 30 deadline.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson told the court at the May 14 hearing that the per-work payout will be approximately $3,100.
 
Plaintiffs' counsel, led by Susman Godfrey's Rohit Nath and Lieff Cabraser's Rachel Geman, originally sought 25% of the fund, or $375 million in fees; that request has since been revised down.
 
The revised fee request is $187.5 million, with counsel arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.


The settlement's structural limitation warrants restating for claimants assessing residual exposure. 
*Bartz* is not a settlement about AI training. In June 2025, Judge William Alsup ruled that Anthropic's use of books to train its LLMs was fair use, and the settlement does not disturb that ruling.
 
The settlement releases Anthropic only from liability for past conduct — specifically, acquisition, retention, and use of identified pirated works before August 25, 2025. It does not create any ongoing licensing scheme for future AI training or cover claims based on AI outputs.
 Rights-holders who settled retain no forward-looking control over Anthropic's use of their works going forward — a gap that continues to define the litigation space outside this docket.

**The Concord docket is the next fair-use inflection point.** *Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL-SVK (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee, referred to Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen for discovery), is the case that will produce the first contested judicial ruling on whether AI training on song lyrics constitutes fair use — and that ruling is now overdue. 
CourtListener last updated the Concord I docket on June 22, 2026, with Judge Eumi K. Lee still assigned and Magistrate Judge Van Keulen handling discovery.


The cross-MSJ briefing schedule in Concord I closed June 8, 2026. 
Concord I has completed fact and expert discovery and is approaching summary judgment practice that will determine threshold legal issues — including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — which will govern the viability of most if not all of the publishers' claims.
 
Music publishers moved for partial summary judgment arguing that fair use does not excuse Anthropic's conduct because it used the lyrics to build a competing commercial product.
 Anthropic's cross-motion asserts fair use and training transformativity. No ruling has been confirmed in the public record as of today's run.

The industry coalition supporting the publishers is unusually broad. 
The RIAA, NMPA, A2IM, SoundExchange, SONA, BMAC, the Music Artists Coalition, and the Artist Rights Alliance filed an amicus brief urging the court to reject Anthropic's fair use defense; a second amicus was filed by the Association of American Publishers, News/Media Alliance, the International Association of Scientific, Technical & Medical Publishers, and the Authors Guild.
 
The coalition characterizes Anthropic as a "holdout" that has opted to scrape copyrighted content rather than license it, and cites academic research from early 2026 challenging AI companies' claims that training data is not retained, pointing to studies showing LLMs can memorize and reproduce copyrighted texts.


Meanwhile, the companion case Concord II, No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL-SVK (same judge), filed January 28, 2026, expands the exposure dramatically. 
Filed by Oppenheim + Zebrak, the Concord II complaint alleges Anthropic illegally downloaded 714 musical works through BitTorrent from pirate websites and infringed 20,517 works through AI model training and outputs, with Benjamin Mann personally participating in the downloads and Dario Amodei authorizing the activity despite knowing it was illegal.
 
The Concord II suit seeks over $3 billion in statutory damages.
 Anthropic's motion to stay Concord II pending resolution of Concord I's summary judgment motions was heard May 20, 2026; the stay ruling has not been confirmed in the public record.

The strategic stakes in Concord are distinct from *Bartz*. Judge Lee is not bound by Judge Alsup's "exceedingly transformative" training-use holding, and the music context — particularly lyric reproduction in Claude's outputs — more readily implicates the fourth § 107 factor (market harm to licensing markets) than the book-training facts did. 
A California federal magistrate judge has partially stricken an expert report filed by Anthropic in the Concord litigation that cited a nonexistent study — an error created by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious."
 That evidentiary misstep, however resolved on the merits, has already colored Anthropic's litigation posture in the case.

**The OpenAI MDL moves into its most consequential discovery phase.** *In re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, No. 1:25-md-03143-SHS-OTW (S.D.N.Y., Judge Sidney H. Stein, Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang), 
last updated July 2, 2026, with Judge Stein presiding and Magistrate Judge Wang handling referred matters.
 
The MDL is a consolidated action combining 16 copyright lawsuits.


The discovery record is exceptionally dense and unfavorable to OpenAI. 
On January 5, 2026, Judge Stein affirmed Magistrate Judge Wang's order compelling OpenAI to produce the entire 20 million-log sample of anonymized ChatGPT conversations — not just the cherry-picked conversations implicating plaintiffs' works — marking a significant discovery victory for news organizations and authors.
 The court went further: 
on March 9, 2026, the court granted plaintiffs' motion to compel and ordered OpenAI to produce "the reservoirs of 78 million and 10 million logs," on top of the 20 million-log sample already ordered. Discovery is ongoing.


The NYT anchor case, No. 1:23-cv-11195-SHS-OTW, has seen additional depositions compelled. 
On April 8, 2026, Magistrate Judge Wang gave news organizations and consolidated author plaintiffs additional deposition time after finding OpenAI's corporate designee was unprepared on noticed topics. OpenAI was ordered to re-designate and produce a properly prepared witness.
 Summary judgment briefing closed April 2, 2026 per the scheduling order; 
the Times also seeks a permanent injunction and destruction of GPT models trained on Times works under 17 U.S.C. § 503(b) — a remedy that, if granted, would have industry-wide implications for deployed LLMs.
 Judge Stein has not issued a summary judgment ruling as of today's run.

The intra-circuit split that has emerged between the SDNY and the N.D. Cal. bench on secondary-infringement theories is worth tracking. 
Judge Stein's rulings allow The Times to continue pursuing claims that OpenAI's outputs included copyrighted content and resulted in user infringement, which diverges from the stance of California judges who have dismissed similar allegations.
 Whether that divergence survives summary judgment will shape the MDL's posture through trial.

---

**The broader § 107 framework: piracy vs. training use bifurcation is hardening.** The doctrinal bifurcation established in *Bartz* — lawful-acquisition training is fair use; piracy is not — is now being tested in every active case. 
In *Kadrey v. Meta*, No. 3:23-cv-03417 (N.D. Cal.), Judge Vince Chhabria went further than Judge Alsup, holding squarely that LLM training constitutes fair use regardless of whether the underlying materials were obtained from legitimate sources or shadow libraries.
 That ruling creates a circuit-level question: if shadow-library training data is also fair use under *Kadrey*, why did *Bartz* settle at $1.5 billion? The answer lies in the class certification mechanics and § 504 statutory damages exposure — 
because statutory copyright damages can reach $150,000 per infringed work, the exposure theoretically extended into the hundreds of billions of dollars, raising existential risk for Anthropic regardless of the fair-use merits.


The cross-border dimension adds a second pressure vector. 
In the U.S., courts have ruled that training on legally acquired copyrighted books is fair use, as in *Bartz* and *Kadrey*, but downloading pirated copies is not. Germany's GEMA ruling reached the opposite conclusion for song lyrics used to train ChatGPT.
 
On March 2, 2026, the Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, confirming the principle that material must have human authorship to be copyrightable.
 That ruling closes one avenue of AI-industry maneuvering but does not resolve the training-data question for human-authored works.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holder counsel and claim-position investors in the *Bartz* settlement should monitor the N.D. Cal. docket daily for entry of the final approval order in No. 3:24-cv-05417 — the late opt-out dispute is the only live obstacle, and its resolution will set the operative distribution timeline, including the trigger date for the September 25, 2026 $450 million Schedule Payment 1 installment; those holding secondary-market claims on expected distributions should note that no checks have issued and the order has not been signed as of today. On the Concord I docket, No. 5:24-cv-03811, the cross-MSJ ruling from Judge Lee is the most consequential near-term event in the AI-copyright system — it will be the first contested fair-use adjudication on lyric-training specifically, it carries preclusive potential for Concord II's 20,517-work exposure, and an Anthropic loss on the § 107(4) market-harm factor would materially revise licensing-market comparables across the entire AI-copyright litigation ecosystem; counsel advising rights-holders with active licensing negotiations should be prepared to update their § 107(4) analysis the moment the Concord I order drops. In the OpenAI MDL, No. 1:25-md-03143, the 108 million ChatGPT conversation logs now in plaintiffs' hands represent the most significant fact-development event of the cycle; any output-reproduction evidence surfaced in that production could reopen secondary-infringement theories that California courts have dismissed and substantially complicate OpenAI's summary judgment posture before Judge Stein.
