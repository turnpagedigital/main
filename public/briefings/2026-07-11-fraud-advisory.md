### I. BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Fee Scrutiny Ratchets at 50th Application

The core posture is unchanged from prior advisories but bears re-anchoring with the current figures. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims
 in *Securities Investor Protection Corp. v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB). 
The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and will be paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have been paid in the aggregate across all seventeen distributions, unless the claim has been fully satisfied.



As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation; those committed advances will continue to increase as claims currently in litigation are allowed through settlements or concluded litigation.
 
SIPC is reimbursed for its advances once each respective customer claim is fully satisfied; it has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The fee picture is the forward pressure point for claim-pricing. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application in Bankr. S.D.N.Y. for work on the global BLMIS liquidation covering August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025; the application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The 50th application is a round-number milestone that will draw judicial scrutiny. Critically, 
all trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC — not from the Customer Fund
 — so fee volume does not reduce pro-rata distributions directly. What it does signal is the pace of ongoing avoidance litigation: at roughly 64,000 hours per four-month compensation period, active adversary proceedings continue at scale. The practical read for claimants is that additional settlement-driven allocations — which have historically preceded each distribution motion — remain in the pipeline, though the timing of an eighteenth distribution has not been publicly noticed.

The clawback posture remains live. The Ponzi presumption under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(A) and the "net investment" method — upheld by the Second Circuit and rejected for review by the Supreme Court — continues to govern net-winner exposure. Claims trading at a discount to net-equity face the arithmetic risk that a clawback defendant holds a countervailing liability that offsets distribution value; buyers of secondary-market claims must conduct counterparty UFTA diligence before closing.

### II. Stanford Financial Receivership — Final Distribution Active; Escalona Response Filed June 1

The Stanford case has moved from preliminary to final-distribution execution, which is the most significant state change in this recovery ecosystem in the last quarter. 
During the first two months of 2026, the Stanford Receivership issued payments to eligible claimants totaling more than $100 million pursuant to Distribution Plans 1–11, in preparation for commencing the Final Distribution.
 Receiver Ralph Janvey, appointed by the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas (Dallas Division), filed the Final Distribution Plan; 
payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026, according to the Receiver's April 6 update.
 
As of May 4, 2026, the Receiver has issued payments to over 5,600 claimants
 pursuant to the Court-approved Final Distribution Plan (as amended).


In May 2026, Jaime Escalona submitted materials to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, regarding the Receiver's distribution process; on June 1, 2026, the Receiver responded to the Escalona submission, which response includes an update concerning the status of the Receiver's ongoing distribution.
 The Escalona filing and the Receiver's June 1 response represent the most recent substantive docket activity confirmed this run; the content of the Receiver's response is available on the receivership site but was not reproduced here.

One structural complication remains. 
Distribution of funds from the $157 million Societe Generale settlement must await the final resolution of the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' appeal.
 That appeal — which has been working through the Fifth Circuit — continues to be the principal delay vector for claimants in the highest-recovery tier. Until the SocGen tranche clears, final payout totals will remain uncertain. Claim buyers pricing into the Final Distribution must haircut the SocGen proceeds accordingly and model separately from the already-distributed Plans 1–11 tranches.

The cross-border coordination dimension is worth holding in view. The Stanford scheme involved Stanford International Bank, Ltd. in Antigua; the Antiguan Joint Liquidators' position on the SocGen funds is adversarial to U.S. claimants. Any § 1782 discovery or MLAT route to Antiguan-held assets remains effectively subordinated to the appellate outcome. Timing on an eighteenth-plus distribution — beyond the Final Distribution Plan — depends entirely on that resolution.

### III. OneCoin / Ignatova — DOJ Remission Window Closed June 30; Guernsey Forfeiture Confirmed

Two concrete developments have moved the OneCoin recovery story since the prior advisory.

On the remission side: 
victims of the $4 billion OneCoin fraud can now apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the U.S. Department of Justice; the scheme was co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, and defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 
The filing deadline was June 30, 2026.
 
The Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process; Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as the designated administrator.
 The window has now closed. Victims who did not file at www.onecoinremission.com by June 30 are presumed to have forfeited their remission claim in this tranche. A $40 million pool against a $4 billion fraud implies a recovery rate under 1% from this round; any secondary market in these remission claims is illiquid by design.

On the cross-border asset tracing side: 
on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, following international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The order followed an application by His Majesty's Comptroller; the German proceedings found that OneCoin was released with fraudulent intent by Ignatova and others; criminal proceedings against her could not conclude as her whereabouts are unknown; the German authorities therefore initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings," which produced the overseas forfeiture order.
 This is a live illustration of non-conviction-based asset forfeiture under the Guernsey legal framework — a cross-jurisdictional enforcement tool that does not require the defendant's presence or extradition.


In August 2024, a UK court ordered a global asset freeze for Ignatova and her OneCoin associates.
 
The FBI has offered up to five million dollars for any information leading to her arrest.
 The identity question — whether she is alive and hidden, or deceased as some Bulgarian investigative reporting suggests — has meaningful valuation consequences for recovery: a living fugitive's frozen assets remain accessible through enforcement proceedings; a deceased fugitive's estate requires separate probate and succession proceedings in every jurisdiction where assets are held.


In June 2026, following an investigation
 by Bulgarian investigative outlet BIRD, reporting linked Ignatova's disappearance to drug-trafficking networks, though those findings remain under review by law enforcement and should not be treated as established fact for recovery-modeling purposes.

### IV. Power Block Coin (Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041) — Fee Wind-Down; Liquidating Trust Posture

The tracked-case ground truth confirms the most recent filing at docket entry 531, dated July 6, 2026, with no new entries in the past 72 hours. The case is before Judge Cathleen D. Parker in the Bankr. D. Utah (docket ID 68873409 on CourtListener). The judge assignment noted in the current tracked-case block — Judge Parker — supersedes earlier filings in the record that reference Judge Joel T. Marker; the reassignment is authoritative per the ground truth.

The operative posture stems from the Creditors' Committee's January 2025 liquidating trust plan. 
The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors of Power Block Coin, L.L.C. filed a comprehensive liquidation plan in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Utah, Case No. 24-bk-23041, proposing to wind down the cryptocurrency financial services company; the committee's filing raises concerns about pre-bankruptcy management, citing unexplained losses of cryptocurrency assets and questioning several insider transactions, including loans made to affiliated entities at allegedly below-market rates.
 
A significant asset under scrutiny is a $17.4 million loan to Solara Communities LLC, an affiliate developing real property in Toquerville, Utah; the committee expressed concerns about this loan's unsecured status and recent encumbrances placed on the underlying property.
 
Filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, the plan outlines a strategy to maximize value for creditors through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.



The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million; a key component involves the appointment of a disinterested Liquidating Trustee to manage the company's assets, including pursuing potential legal claims and overseeing distributions to creditors; secured and priority claims would be paid in full, while general unsecured creditors would receive pro rata distributions from liquidated assets; the plan provides no recovery for equity interests, which would be cancelled.


The professional fee application filed May 14, 2026 (ECF 507, Doc 507) — setting a June 9, 2026 hearing on the third and final Ampleo/CFO Solutions fee application covering April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026 — signals that professional retention is winding down. 
Judge Cathleen D. Parker set a hearing on the Third and Final Application of CFO Solutions, LLC d/b/a Amplēo for allowance of compensation (ECF 506) for June 9, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court; Amplēo, financial advisor and accountant to the Debtor, requested final compensation for services rendered in the amount of $10,436.50 for the period April 1, 2025 through April 22, 2026.
 Final professional fee applications — particularly the financial advisor — are a structural precursor to plan confirmation. The July 6 filing (ECF 531) post-dates that hearing; its content is not reproduced in the docket scan, but its proximity to the Amplēo hearing suggests either an order on that fee application or a related confirmation proceeding. Counsel watching plan-confirmation timing should pull ECF 531 directly from CourtListener docket ID 68873409.
