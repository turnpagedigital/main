# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Sunday, July 19, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)) — § 541 customer-property question moves toward resolution.** The operative delta this week is the post-sale posture of the case. 
Judge Thomas M. Horan of the Bankr. D. Del. approved the $3.25 million sale of BlockFills to Belgian digital asset firm Keyrock on June 16, 2026.
 The auction was cancelled — 
a Notice of Cancellation of Auction was filed, followed by a Notice of Sale Hearing
 — meaning Keyrock emerged as the sole qualified bidder. 
The acquisition price of $3.25 million is set against a liability stack of $100 million to $500 million, and will give Keyrock access to BlockFills' institutional client network, which includes hedge funds, asset managers, market makers, and mining companies.
 The sale clears the § 363 track of the dual-toggle plan. What remains is the plan confirmation and, critically, the § 541 property-of-the-estate determination that will govern whether customer digital assets are distributable to the general creditor body or sequestered for direct restitution.

The property question has been live since the first day hearing. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That posture is legally aggressive. 
The commingling record is concrete: on January 16, 2026, one counterparty transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills, assets that were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes; the Debtors also disclosed to clients in February 2026 that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet."
 
The Debtors noted a procedural deficiency — objecting customers have not filed an adversary proceeding under Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate — and argued that if customer digital assets are estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization and to be distributed pro rata under the Plan.


The solicitation order entered May 28, 2026, authorizes plan balloting. 
On May 28, 2026, the Court entered the order approving the Disclosure Statement, scheduling a Confirmation Hearing, and establishing voting procedures [Docket No. 388].
 The claims bar date is 
September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET, with the voting deadline set for June 16, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET.
 As of July 17 — the date of the most recent docket entry per the tracked-case ground truth — the case is in its post-sale, pre-confirmation phase. Docket entry 530, filed July 15, and the undescribed entry from July 17 require direct review on the CourtListener docket (ID 72494188) for specific content; descriptions were not available in the sources retrieved this run.

**Liability profile and creditor committee posture.** 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations, and the filing is supported by a pre-negotiated term sheet with an Ad Hoc Group of the company's largest customers providing the framework for a reorganization plan centered on a new operating entity and a liquidating trust.
 
The U.S. Trustee appointed the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors on March 27, 2026 [Docket No. 104], with seven members: SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs, Inc.
 The Committee engaged Morris James LLP as Delaware co-counsel 
effective March 30, 2026
 and 
FTI Consulting, Inc. as financial advisor effective April 1, 2026.
 FTI's engagement positions the Committee to conduct independent forensic analysis of prepetition transfers — a material variable given that 
the Company paid approximately $12 million in employee bonuses in 2024 against only $900,000 in adjusted profits, a disparity that contributed to the approximately $77–$80 million balance sheet deficit that predated the February 2026 market crash.
 Fraudulent-transfer exposure on the bonus payments and on prepetition counterparty settlements is an open creditor-side leverage point.

**Causal chain: Babel Finance → AEXA → Celsius → 2026 collapse.** The petition-day narrative is instructive for recovery-posture analysis. 
BlockFills attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.
 
On February 2, 2026, Bitcoin dropped below $80,000 for the first time since April 2025, and BlockFills temporarily suspended certain deposit and withdrawal activity; on February 6, it publicly announced a broader suspension of deposits and withdrawals.
 
CoinDesk reported on February 19 that BlockFills had incurred approximately $75 million in losses and was seeking a buyer, and on February 25 confirmed the former CEO's departure, reporting that Hammer had allegedly urged some clients to withdraw assets before the platform froze.
 That allegation — selectively tipping certain clients — is a classic fraudulent-transfer and breach-of-fiduciary-duty exposure and sits squarely within the Special Committee's investigative mandate. 
On March 12, 2026, BlockFills formed a Special Committee comprised of a disinterested director, vested with authority to manage potential conflict matters, including investigating, releasing, or settling potential claims.

**FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution, $900 Million, commences July 31.** The largest mechanical event in the crypto-insolvency space this week is the FTX fifth distribution. 
FTX Trading Ltd. (d/b/a FTX.com) and the FTX Recovery Trust announced on July 17, 2026 that distributions will commence on July 31, 2026, to holders of allowed claims in the Plan's Convenience and Non-Convenience Classes that completed pre-distribution requirements by the June 16, 2026 record date.
 
In the Fifth Distribution, Allowed Class 5A Dotcom Customer Entitlement Claims receive an incremental 9% distribution (105% cumulative); Allowed Class 5B U.S. Customer Entitlement Claims receive 5% (105% cumulative); Allowed Classes 6A General Unsecured Claims and 6B Digital Asset Loan Claims each receive 3% (103% cumulative); and Allowed Class 7 Convenience Claims reach 120% cumulative.



The Chapter 11 Plan took effect January 3, 2025; the first distribution arrived in February 2025 at roughly $1.2 billion, followed by $5 billion in May 2025, $1.6 billion in September 2025, and $2.2 billion in March 2026.
 
Cumulative distributions since early 2025 now approach $10 billion, one of the largest creditor recoveries in cryptocurrency history.
 The fifth distribution is the smallest of the five rounds, reflecting the progressive resolution of disputed claims. 
FTX filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to court approval, would release additional cash for distribution.


For claim-transfer holders, timing is critical. 
Distributions to transferred claims are made only to the transferee of record on the official claims register as of future record dates, where the 21-day notice period has lapsed without objection.
 
These percentages calculate against claim values from the November 2022 petition date, when Bitcoin traded near $16,000 — far below current prices — which has helped drive recoveries above 100% for several classes.
 The gap between nominal over-recovery and economic reality is the FTX valuation-date argument in miniature: creditors hold fiat-denominated claims that appear to pay 105 cents on the dollar but represent a significant in-kind shortfall versus what they originally held. 
The latest repayment comes just days after the U.S. Senate unanimously passed Senate Resolution 772 on July 16, expressing bipartisan opposition to any presidential pardon for convicted FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried — a nonbinding measure that underscores lawmakers' continued attention to the accountability dimension of this proceeding.


Third-party recovery against professional advisors remains active. 
In May 2026, Silicon Valley law firm Fenwick & West — which had served as FTX US's principal outside counsel before the exchange's collapse — agreed to pay $54 million to settle claims that it helped enable Sam Bankman-Fried's fraud.
 That settlement extends the FTX estate's recovery posture beyond the core Alameda/Bankman-Fried defendants and signals continuing exposure for professional enablers.

**Genesis/DCG — Post-Effective-Date Adversary Litigation Enters Pretrial Phase.** The Genesis wind-down (Case No. 23-10063, Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) has moved well past distribution mechanics and is now dominated by multi-front adversary litigation against Digital Currency Group. 
Active adversary proceedings include Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al. v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al., Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053, and a related proceeding at 26-03230.
 
A pretrial conference in the Genesis docket is scheduled for July 21, 2026 at 10:00 a.m.
 — two days from today — making this the nearest-term Genesis calendar event to track.


Post-confirmation litigation between the wind-down debtors and DCG has continued, with Genesis Global Capital having sued DCG seeking to recover billions in assets and preferential transfers, alleging that DCG insiders engaged in fraud and mismanagement while Genesis was insolvent; DCG filed its own adversary complaint, and the wind-down debtors have moved to dismiss that action.
 The Genesis model matters analytically for the BlockFills and FTX beats: 
in the wave of crypto-winter bankruptcy cases, virtually all crypto debtors dollarized claims and capped crypto claims at petition-date values; Genesis took a different approach by obtaining confirmation of a plan that provided in-kind recoveries without capping at petition-date values — and because some crypto prices like Bitcoin nearly tripled after the petition date, this issue significantly affected recoveries.
 The Genesis distribution-principles framework is the template that creditor-side counsel in future cases will cite to resist dollarization.

**Celsius Clawback Litigation — Damages Framework Set July 13.** A July 13, 2026 Law360 headline — 
"Judge Sets Damages Framework In Celsius Ch. 11 Clawbacks"
 — signals a material development in the Celsius adversary docket. The specific ruling details are behind a Law360 paywall and were not fully accessible this run; the headline confirms, however, that a damages framework order has issued, which will govern the calculation methodology for preference recoveries in the Celsius adversary proceedings. This is directly relevant to BlockFills: BlockFills paid Celsius under a secured promissory note 
with Celsius asserting first-priority liens, and the first-day record identifies about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes.
 The Celsius clawback exposure — i.e., whether prepetition payments from BlockFills to Celsius are themselves subject to avoidance in the BlockFills estate — is an open question that the new damages framework order may clarify.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors holding claims in *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings* (26-10371-TMH, Bankr. D. Del.) should immediately verify their proof-of-claim status through the Veritaglobal portal (veritaglobal.net/blockfills) and confirm whether they filed a separate adversary proceeding to contest the Debtors' property-of-the-estate characterization — the failure to do so under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 7001(b) has been raised by the Debtors as a procedural bar that, if left uncured, could waive any trust or bailment argument and leave customer digital assets subject to pro rata distribution alongside general unsecured claims against a $145 million obligation stack supported by only $50–$100 million in assets; the September 11, 2026 bar date is the next hard deadline. FTX claim holders who completed KYC and onboarded with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer by June 16, 2026 should expect fifth-distribution funds on or shortly after July 31; those who missed that record date must complete requirements before a subsequent record date, and any claim transferee must confirm that the 21-day § 1126 objection period elapsed without objection to ensure they — not the transferor — appear on the claims register. Genesis creditors with exposure to the DCG adversary proceedings (Adv. Pro. Nos. 26-01053 and 26-03230) should monitor Tuesday's July 21 pretrial conference for any scheduling order or discovery ruling that may set the trajectory of the litigation fund established under the confirmed plan. Across all dockets, verify that no outstanding KYC or tax documentation deficiency remains open — distributions to unverified claimants will be withheld and rolled to future record dates without notice.
