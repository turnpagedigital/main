## Analysis & Developments

### Sector Stress Cluster: Four Failures in Seven Days — Storj, Movement, BitMEX, BitMart

The crypto insolvency landscape accelerated sharply this week. 
The filing of Storj Labs capped a week in which BitMEX and BitMart announced solvent wind-downs, and Movement Labs sought Chapter 11 protection — four crypto companies in seven days announcing failures or wind-downs as investor attention continues shifting toward AI.
 This is not a random cluster. 
Analysts attribute the pattern to collapsing retail trading volumes and rising regulatory costs, with only large, well-capitalized exchanges with strong compliance, transparent reserves, and diversified services likely to survive as MiCA and analogous frameworks take hold.


**Storj Labs, Inc.** — the most immediately actionable new filing — is today's analytical focus for creditors and claim-holders. 
Storj Labs, Inc. voluntarily filed Chapter 11 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of West Virginia, Case No. 5:26-bk-00512 (Bk N.D. W.Va.) on July 26, 2026.
 The filing is styled as a restructuring, not a shutdown: 
Storj filed the Chapter 11 restructuring to address certain legacy obligations while preserving ongoing business operations, and the company states it will continue operating in the ordinary course throughout the Chapter 11 process.
 
The filing follows approximately $35 million in fundraising
 — a figure that underscores the structural nature of the liability problem rather than a simple failure to raise capital.

The capital-structure story is one creditors should read carefully. 
Parent company Inveniam, which acquired Storj in a transaction announced in late 2025, has voiced support for the reorganization and views the court process as a pathway toward long-term sustainability.
 
Management has announced intentions to develop a framework enabling STORJ token holders to acquire equity stakes in the post-reorganization entity, with proposed ownership including participation from existing management, current investors, community stakeholders, and potentially new capital partners — but details regarding eligibility criteria, token snapshot requirements, lockup provisions, or equity percentage allocation remain undisclosed.
 
Token holders are being offered a path to equity in the reorganized business, but creditors come first, and the standard bankruptcy priority line stands in front of that equity offer.
 That sequencing — § 726 / § 1129 priority waterfall — is the central issue for unsecured creditors monitoring the Storj docket. No judge has been publicly confirmed for the case in available filings as of this writing; the docket is at its earliest stages with plan documents, first-day motions, and a creditor matrix yet to surface publicly.

### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings, Inc.) — Post-Confirmation Posture, Dkt. No. 26-10371 (Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan)

BlockFills remains the priority matter for claim-holders with institutional exposure, and the case has moved materially since the initial filing. 
On March 15, 2026, each of the four debtors filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11. The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before the Honorable Judge Thomas M. Horan in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.


The capital structure presents a severe recovery problem for unsecured creditors. 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt. The first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 The liquidity trigger was a series of compounding shocks: 
the company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment and delayed 2024 financials.


On the asset-sale track, the outcome is confirmed: 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors, pursuant to a Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026. Under the agreement, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.


On the plan track, the disclosure-statement order issued and solicitation proceeded. 
On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order Approving the Disclosure Statement, Scheduling a Hearing on Confirmation of Plan, and Establishing Deadlines and Procedures for Filing Objections to Confirmation, Claim Objections, and Temporary Allowance of Claims for Voting Purposes [Docket No. 388].
 
The Voting Deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline was also July 1, 2026; and the Confirmation Hearing was scheduled for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The most pressing current question for BlockFills creditors — unresolved in publicly available filings as of July 28 — is whether Judge Horan confirmed the plan at or following the July 13 hearing, or whether the confirmation hearing was continued. 
The Veritaglobal key-dates page reflects a September 11, 2026 date at 4:00 p.m. ET as a remaining calendar entry
 — which, read alongside the three new docket entries filed on July 27 (Dkt. entries 539 and 541, descriptions not yet populated in the CourtListener feed), suggests post-July 13 activity that may reflect a plan confirmation order, a continued confirmation hearing, or post-confirmation administrative filings. Counsel and creditors should pull those entries directly from PACER.

The pre-petition litigation backdrop remains relevant to recovery analysis. 
In the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two customer-action lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims: Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026)
 and a parallel action in the Northern District of Illinois. The automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 halted both actions at filing; any fraudulent-transfer or conversion claims against directors and officers would need to be advanced by the plan administrator or creditors' committee through adversary proceedings under § 548 or § 544(b).

### The Sector Pattern: AI Capital Rotation, MiCA Pressure, and Retail Volume Collapse

The four-firm failure cluster is analytically significant beyond the individual cases. 
Over 60 crypto projects have shut down or filed bankruptcy from January to July 2026.
 The structural driver is identifiable: 
since Bitcoin retreated from its October 2025 all-time high, balance sheets have tightened, hiring has slowed, layoffs are abundant, and funding rounds that once closed in weeks have stretched for months or disappeared altogether, with crypto-focused venture capital activity cooled to levels reflecting far more caution than conviction.


Movement Labs illustrates the governance-to-insolvency pipeline. 
The voluntary petition was filed on July 15, 2026, under Subchapter V — the restructuring process designed for qualifying small businesses. The filing reported assets ranging from $100,001 to $500,000 and liabilities of up to $10 million, with hundreds of potential creditors listed.
 
The project came under scrutiny after a market-making deal enabled the rapid sale of 66 million MOVE tokens, triggering a steep price drop and prompting investigations and a token buyback.
 The token-to-insolvency trajectory — market-making controversy → co-founder suspension → Coinbase delisting → Subchapter V — is a pattern creditors in post-TGE token projects should track as a leading indicator.

BitMEX and BitMart are not insolvency cases in the § 541 / § 1129 sense, but they present customer-asset risk of a different order. 
BitMEX and BitMart both explicitly say they are solvent and are announcing orderly wind-downs where customer assets exceed liabilities and users can still withdraw.
 
BitMEX announced on July 23 that it will permanently close on September 23 after 11 years of operation, with daily volume reportedly down to roughly $400,000.
 
For BitMart, trading will stop on August 26, and the platform will permanently close on January 31, 2027.
 However, 
following BitMart's announcement of closure, on-chain analysts reported potential issues with the withdrawal of customer funds
 — a fact pattern that should be monitored closely by any customer with assets on that platform, given the Celsius and BlockFills precedents on deposit-suspension-to-filing timelines.

### Background: The § 541 Property-of-the-Estate Question in Token-Adjacent Bankruptcies

The Storj filing raises, in a new configuration, the recurring question that has run through every major crypto insolvency since Celsius: are customer deposits and token balances property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541, or are they held in trust or bailment such that they pass outside the estate? 
Storj's decentralized storage model — in which the company proposes that token holders take equity in the restructured business — is an unusual arrangement in bankruptcy.
 The specific mechanism by which STORJ tokens are converted to reorganized-entity equity, and whether token holders are treated as equity interests (subordinated under § 510(b)) or as creditors with a contractual claim, will be a defining confirmation issue.

The Storj structure also implicates the valuation-date problem familiar from Celsius and Genesis. If token holders assert a claim denominated in STORJ, the conversion rate between STORJ and the reorganized-entity equity must be fixed as of some reference date. The petition date (July 26, 2026) is the default under § 502(b), but courts have discretion to adopt distribution-date values where post-petition appreciation would otherwise produce a windfall. The plan's current silence on "eligibility criteria, potential token snapshot requirements, lockup provisions, or the percentage of equity allocated" — as noted above — means that critical valuation mechanics are unresolved at this stage.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors and claim-holders across the BlockFills, Storj, Movement Labs, and BitMart matters should take the following steps without delay. In **BlockFills** (Case No. 26-10371, Bankr. D. Del., Judge Horan): pull docket entries 539 and 541 (filed July 27) from PACER immediately to determine whether a confirmation order was entered following the July 13 hearing; if confirmed, the § 1141 vesting date and plan-effective-date mechanics will govern when and how distributions to the ~$145 million unsecured creditor pool are made, and any appeal window under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 8002 will have begun running. In **Storj** (Case No. 5:26-bk-00512, Bk N.D. W.Va.): file a proof of claim as soon as the claims bar date is published; do not rely on management's equity-conversion proposal as a substitute for a timely filed unsecured claim, since token-to-equity conversion mechanics are entirely undisclosed and the § 726 priority waterfall places unsecured creditors ahead of any equity interests. In **Movement Labs** (Subchapter V, Bankr. D. Del., filed July 15): contact the Subchapter V trustee to confirm creditor-meeting scheduling under 11 U.S.C. § 341 and assess whether the plan will be consensual or non-consensual under § 1191; given the minimal asset base ($100,001–$500,000 declared), recovery prospects are limited and claim preservation matters. For **BitMart** customers: do not wait for the August 26 trading halt — initiate withdrawals now, document all positions and on-chain transaction hashes, and monitor on-chain analytics for fund-movement anomalies; the Celsius and BlockFills precedents both show that the interval between deposit suspension and formal filing can be compressed to days.
