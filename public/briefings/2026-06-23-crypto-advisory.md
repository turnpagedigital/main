# 🪙 Crypto Insolvency | Tuesday, June 23, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

**BlockFills / Reliz — the sale hearing has passed; plan solicitation is live; confirmation is now the critical path.** The most consequential near-term development in this portfolio is the evolution of *In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.* (Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Judge Thomas M. Horan) from a dual-track §363/reorganization process into what appears to be a confirmed sale track moving toward plan confirmation. 
As set out in the official Bankruptcy Court document filed on May 26, 2026, Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" for certain assets of Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and its affiliated debtors.
 
Keyrock is acquiring bankrupt crypto trading and lending firm BlockFills for $3.25 million, subject to court approval.
 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property.
 The sale hearing was scheduled for June 16, 2026 before Judge Horan at 824 Market Street, Wilmington — a date that has now passed as of today's advisory. No verified order has been published to the Verita docket as of the June 22 fetch, so the sale approval status is unconfirmed and creditors should verify directly at veritaglobal.net/blockfills.

**The structural gap that matters most:** 
BlockFills filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in March, reporting between $100 million and $500 million in liabilities but only between $50 million and $100 million in assets.
 
The Debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 Against that liability stack, a $3.25 million purchase price for "substantially all" operating assets leaves the liquidating trust facing a severe shortfall. The central creditor-recovery question is therefore not whether Keyrock closes — it is what non-operational assets (receivables, insider claims, litigation causes of action) the liquidating trust can monetize for the benefit of the $145 million general unsecured pool. That analysis turns directly on the customer-property issue discussed below.

### BlockFills — Plan Solicitation, Confirmation Timeline, and Committee Posture


On May 28, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court entered the Order (A) Approving Disclosure Statement; (B) Scheduling Hearing on Confirmation of Plan; (C) Establishing Deadlines and Procedures for Filing Objections to Confirmation of Plan, Claim Objections, and Temporary Allowance of Claims for Voting Purposes [Docket No. 388].
 That order launched the formal solicitation period under 11 U.S.C. § 1125. 
The plan-related deadlines on the Verita case page show dates of September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET) and June 16, 2026 (11:00 a.m. ET)
 — the June 16 date likely encompassing both the sale approval and related voting matters. Confirmation itself appears targeted for the September 11, 2026 window, giving the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors (UCC) roughly three months to conduct discovery on plan distributions, valuation, and any retained causes of action.

The UCC is well-staffed and adversarially positioned. 
The Committee members are: SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd.; Dominion Capital LLC; Karol Przybytkowski; Jeffrey Brandt; Tod Skarecky; Energy Conversion Group; and Fuel Labs Inc.
 — seven members spanning institutional crypto counterparties to individual creditors. 
On April 1, 2026, the Committee selected FTI Consulting as its financial advisor.
 Morris James LLP was retained as Delaware counsel (Docket No. 262, Apr. 29, 2026, approved). That professional apparatus is capable of mounting a serious objection to any plan that does not adequately monetize litigation claims against insiders or former counterparties.

The bankruptcy's root-cause narrative is relevant to recovery: 
the company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 Those disclosed impairments are third-party in character, but two pre-petition customer lawsuits allege something more: 
in the weeks immediately preceding the filing, two customer lawsuits were filed alleging misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and fraud-based claims.
 Those actions — Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd., Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026) and 1548199 Alberta Ltd. v. Reliz (N.D. Ill., Mar. 5, 2026) — are stayed by the Chapter 11 automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362(a), but the underlying factual record feeds directly into the UCC's investigation of whether insider transfers, misapplication of customer assets, or preferential payments exist as recoverable causes of action. The Special Committee formed March 12, 2026, to investigate conflict matters is the vehicle for that work; its findings, or lack thereof, will shape what the liquidating trust can pursue post-confirmation.

### Customer-Property Issue — The §541 Question That Remains Open


The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested.
 This is the defining legal question for recovery allocation in BlockFills, and it has not been resolved. The Debtors' terms of service and their actual custodial practices — which are now under forensic review by BRG, the estate's financial advisor — will determine whether institutional customer deposits are property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(1) or held in express or constructive trust, placing them outside the estate.

The controlling Celsius analogy is instructive but incomplete. In *In re Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 22-10964), Judge Martin Glenn held in October 2022 that assets deposited into "Earn" accounts were property of the estate based on the plain terms of Celsius's TOS, which transferred title to Celsius upon deposit. The BlockFills fact pattern differs in one potentially meaningful respect: 
the company focused solely on institutional, high-net-worth, and sophisticated customers and did not offer services to retail traders.
 Institutional brokerage agreements often contain express bailee or custodian language, and the Schedules of Financial Affairs (Docket No. 146, filed April 11, 2026) indicate that customer-facing trade settlement flows were tracked separately from BlockFills' own liquidity positions. Whether that operational separation is legally sufficient to establish a trust is unresolved and is likely the central confirmation objection if the plan's distribution waterfall treats all creditors as general unsecured.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution Approaching; $600M Reserve Reduction Filed

The FTX wind-down (FTX Trading Ltd., et al., Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 22-11068, Judge John T. Dorsey) has advanced materially since our last advisory. 
The FTX Recovery Trust announced that the anticipated record date for the next distribution will be June 16, 2026, for holders of allowed FTX claims and interests, with the next distribution expected to commence on July 31, 2026.
 
FTX also filed an amended notice with the Bankruptcy Court to reduce the disputed claims reserve by $0.6 billion, from $2.4 billion to $1.8 billion, which, subject to approval by the Court, would release cash to be distributed to holders of allowed claims in the next distribution.


Cumulative context: 
distributions to date total approximately $10 billion — Distribution 1 of approximately $1.2 billion in February 2025, Distribution 2 of approximately $5 billion on May 30, 2025, Distribution 3 of approximately $1.6 billion on September 30, 2025, and Distribution 4 of approximately $2.2 billion on March 31, 2026.
 
Class 5A — the Dotcom group — has been paid approximately 96% of principal so far, with a projected total of approximately 118–120%, earning the same 9% annual interest the plan accrues from the November 11, 2022 bankruptcy date.


For claim-transfer practitioners: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register of claims maintained by the Notice and Claims Agent as of the June 16, 2026 record date, where the 21-day notice period has elapsed without objection.
 That record date has now passed; any claim transfer recorded after June 16 will miss the July 31 distribution. The NFT entitlement track runs on a separate earlier schedule: 
holders of Allowed NFT Customer Entitlement Claims will be eligible to begin the NFT Distribution process on June 30, 2026.


The adversarial recovery posture remains active. 
Genesis Digital Assets, a bitcoin mining company, is fighting a lawsuit brought by the FTX estate seeking roughly $1 billion; the case is part of the broader clawback strategy, and Genesis is pushing to dismiss the suit, rejecting the estate's claims and challenging the legal grounds for pursuing the alleged transfers.
 Sullivan & Cromwell LLP serves as legal counsel to the FTX Recovery Trust; Alvarez & Marsal as financial advisor.

### Genesis / DCG Wind-Down — Post-Effective-Date Litigation Active

The Genesis Global Holdco, LLC wind-down (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 23-10063) reached its effective date on August 2, 2024, but the adversarial posture against Digital Currency Group (DCG) remains live and material for creditors tracking residual recovery. The confirmed plan authorized in-kind coin-for-coin distributions rather than capping recoveries at petition-date fiat values — a structural departure from the FTX and BlockFills approaches. 
On the effective date, Genesis distributed approximately $4 billion in digital assets and U.S. dollars to creditors pursuant to the Chapter 11 plan confirmed by the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York on May 21, 2024.
 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition date value; instead, Genesis sought to provide restitution to creditors through the Chapter 11 plan and a creative settlement with the New York Attorney General, providing in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.


The residual DCG litigation cluster is tracked at Kroll (restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/). Active proceedings include: 
Genesis Global Holdco, LLC et al v. Digital Currency Group, Inc. et al. (Adv. Pro. 26-03230), with a pretrial conference scheduled for June 9, 2026; Digital Currency Group, Inc. v. Genesis Global Capital (Adv. Pro. 25-01129); and Genesis Global Capital, LLC v. Insider Defendants (Adv. Pro. 25-01097).
 The June 9 pretrial conference indicates this litigation is moving toward a trial posture, which could generate material additional recovery for the Genesis wind-down estate — or, if DCG prevails, constrain it.

The plan's standing ruling retains significance as precedent: 
the Bankruptcy Court ruled that DCG lacked standing to object to the plan because it could not recover on account of its equity interests, which were junior to tens of billions of dollars in allowed claims, including at least $11 billion in governmental claims.
 That equitable mootness / standing framework is the one that UCC counsel in BlockFills and similar cases will invoke to foreclose parent-company interference with distribution mechanics.

## Recommended Actions

Counsel and claimants across this portfolio face three time-sensitive matters this week. **BlockFills (26-10371):** With the sale-approval hearing having occurred on June 16 and the disclosure statement order entered (Docket No. 388, May 28, 2026), creditors should immediately obtain and review the solicitation package from Verita Global at veritaglobal.net/blockfills, assess the plan's treatment of the §541 customer-property question, and determine whether to vote in favor or file an objection before the confirmation deadline targeting September 11, 2026; institutional counterparties who believe their deposits were held in trust should engage restructuring counsel now to preserve that argument at confirmation rather than waiting for the plan objection deadline. **FTX Recovery Trust:** The June 16 record date for the fifth distribution has passed — any claim-holder not onboarded with BitGo, Kraken, or Payoneer, or whose KYC or tax documentation remains incomplete, will miss the July 31, 2026 distribution; verify onboarding status at claims.ftx.com and confirm claim-transfer registrations are reflected on Kroll's official register before acting on any secondary-market transaction. **Genesis / DCG litigation (Adv. Pros. 26-03230, 25-01129):** Creditors with residual exposure to Genesis distributions should monitor the adversary proceeding docket at restructuring.ra.kroll.com/genesis/ for trial scheduling orders following the June 9 pretrial conference, as a DCG settlement or adverse judgment will directly affect the timing and quantum of any remaining plan distributions.
