### *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* — No. 3:24-cv-05417-AMO (N.D. Cal.) — Settlement Administration Phase

The defining event is two weeks old, but its administration mechanics are now the live story. 
On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California granted final approval of the landmark $1.5 billion class action settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic* and entered final judgment in the case.
 
Judge Martínez-Olguín granted final approval and dismissed the case with prejudice.


The court's 23-page order resolved every remaining contested point. 
The court called the settlement "fair, reasonable, and adequate," finding that it "was negotiated at arm's length; the relief provided for the Class is adequate; and the proposed Settlement treats Class Members equitably relative to each other." She overruled all 53 objections filed with the court opposing the settlement on various grounds.


**Claims rate and fund mechanics.** 
The certified class encompasses all beneficial or legal copyright owners of books found in the versions of LibGen or PiLiMi downloaded by Anthropic, as identified on a Works List containing 482,460 works, each possessing an ISBN or ASIN and a U.S. Copyright Office registration within five years of first publication.
 
As of April 16, 2026, 91.3% of works on the Works List had been claimed — a claims rate the court described as "magnitudes above the average for class action cases."
 The AAP separately reported 
the aggregate claims rate at 92.77 percent — the largest class action copyright settlement in history.



The court confirmed that the estimated per-work payment of approximately $3,000 is four times the statutory minimum for willful copyright infringement
 under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). 
$122 million of the settlement money will go toward attorneys and litigation costs.
 
The court also trimmed the requested service awards for the three class representatives from $50,000 to $15,000 each, while recognizing the substantial work Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson performed on behalf of fellow authors.


**Non-monetary and scope terms.** 
Under the settlement, Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion plus interest to a class of copyright owners whose works were allegedly torrented and commercially exploited from pirated datasets — specifically Library Genesis (LibGen) and Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi) — to train Anthropic's large language models.
 
Anthropic must destroy all original files of works torrented from LibGen or PiLiMi and any copies derived from them.
 Critically for future-conduct exposure: 
the settlement grants a past-only release for conduct up to August 25, 2025, and explicitly does not release claims related to AI outputs or future conduct.


**Distribution timeline.** 
The Authors Guild looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process so that authors can start getting paid; while a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not been announced, the Guild hopes and expects that this will happen quickly.
 
If money remains after all valid claims are paid, the parties anticipate redistributing it to class members; if a final balance remains that is economically infeasible to distribute, it will go — subject to court approval — to charitable purposes.
 The docket's last confirmed filing was July 21, 2026 (Entry 681); no administration-phase motion has surfaced in the 72-hour window. Claimants and rights-holder counsel should monitor the settlement administrator portal at AnthropicCopyrightSettlement.com for payout commencement notice — none has issued as of this morning.

**Pearson as a bellwether claimant.** 
Pearson PLC, in its FY2026 Form 6-K filed with the SEC, confirmed it is a claimant in the settlement and expects to be eligible for monetary distribution for qualifying titles, subject to further court proceedings and claims administration.
 
The Group has not recorded anything in its interim financial statements in relation to the matter, as the amount and timing of any settlement are not yet certain.
 Pearson's disclosure is the clearest public window into how large institutional publishers are accounting for recovery — conservatively, pending a payout schedule.

### *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC* — No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL (N.D. Cal.) — **Concord I**: Summary Judgment Pending

No new filings confirmed in the last 24 hours on this docket; last CourtListener sync was July 29, 2026. The matter is in the post-briefing interval before the October hearing.


The summary judgment hearing before Judge Eumi K. Lee is scheduled for October 21, 2026. The questions before the court are whether partial summary judgment on copyright infringement should be granted to plaintiffs, and whether summary judgment on fair use and non-infringement should be granted to Anthropic.


The fair-use frame in *Concord I* is materially narrower than what Judge Alsup resolved in *Bartz*. 
Plaintiffs' March 23, 2026 motion for partial summary judgment asks Judge Lee to rule that Anthropic's unlicensed copying of copyrighted song lyrics to train its Claude AI chatbot is not fair use.
 
The RIAA amicus brief, filed March 30, zeroes in on the fourth fair-use factor — market harm — arguing that AI-generated music already acts as a direct market substitute for human-created works, and that a functioning licensing market for AI training already exists — one that Anthropic has chosen not to participate in.
 The § 107(4) market-harm argument is the strongest vector for plaintiffs here; the Bartz precedent on pirated-source infringement is not directly transferable because *Concord I* centers on whether ingestion of lyrics — rather than wholesale downloading of pirated books — is itself transformative.

**Concord II parallel.** A separate, larger action — 
*Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:26-cv-00880, N.D. Cal., filed January 28, 2026
 — 
names Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann as individual defendants.
 
Anthropic moved to stay *Concord II* on the ground that *Concord I* has completed fact and expert discovery and is approaching summary judgment practice that will determine threshold legal issues — including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — that could have preclusive effect in *Concord II*.
 That stay motion was still active as of the last confirmed filing; the October 21 ruling in *Concord I* is therefore the pivot point for both cases simultaneously.

### Parallel Docket — Appellate and Structural Signals

**Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence — Third Circuit.** 
The Third Circuit's first appellate ruling on AI training and fair use is pending after oral argument on June 11, 2026. Three U.S. district judges have now ruled on AI training and fair use: Alsup (*Bartz*) and Chhabria (*Kadrey*) found for AI companies on training; Bibas (*Thomson Reuters v. Ross*) found against. The Third Circuit's ruling is the first appellate resolution of the question.
 The outcome matters directly to *Concord I*: if the Third Circuit affirms Bibas's rejection of fair use on the § 107(4) market-harm ground, it strengthens the RIAA's amicus framing and gives Judge Lee doctrinal cover to rule against Anthropic on the same factor.

**NYT v. OpenAI — Sanctions posture.** 
A group of newspapers including the New York Times and New York Daily News asked a federal court in Manhattan to sanction OpenAI in their high-stakes copyright dispute for allegedly lying to the court about its ability to search its systems for proof that it misused millions of their articles in AI training.
 
In June 2026, the NYT amended its complaint to allege Microsoft had built a dedicated supercomputer infrastructure specifically to help OpenAI use Times content without authorization — broadening the theory of liability.
 The sanctions motion and the amended complaint together suggest the NYT litigation is entering a more adversarial, discovery-conflict phase — the kind of procedural friction that historically precedes either an aggressive damages push or a settlement overture from defendants.

**Kadrey v. Meta — Continued active litigation.** 
The Kadrey case remains active and is proceeding through further pleading and discovery.
 No new docket activity confirmed in the 72-hour window.

**Human-authorship doctrine settled.** 
On March 2, 2026, the Supreme Court denied certiorari in Thaler's appeal, confirming the principle that material must still have human authorship to be copyrightable.
 This is background law for all training-output claims — the registration prerequisite under 17 U.S.C. § 411 and § 504 statutory damages analysis both assume a human-authored work in the chain. No delta from yesterday.
