### BlockFills — Sale Approved; Plan Confirmation Is the Live Exposure

The major procedural milestone since the case opened was reached on June 16, 2026. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge agreed on June 16, 2026 to approve the $3.25 million sale of cryptocurrency financial technology firm BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group as BlockFills prepares for a Chapter 11 plan confirmation hearing.
 That buyer is Keyrock SA, a Brussels-based market maker. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some of its equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property, with Keyrock SA confirmed as the "Successful Bidder" in a court document filed May 26, 2026.
 Regulatory clearance remains outstanding; 
final completion of the transaction remains subject to final court approval and the appropriate regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.


The sale price is striking in context. 
Court filings showed Reliz reporting assets between $50 million and $100 million against liabilities of $100 million to $500 million.
 A $3.25 million gross consideration against that liability range signals that unsecured creditors are fighting for recovery from plan distributions rather than sale proceeds — the estate's distributable value derives from claim reconciliation, litigation recoveries, and whatever cash remains post-sale. 
The plan features a dual-track toggle mechanism; the Debtors filed the Joint Chapter 11 Plan at Docket No. 128, the Disclosure Statement at Docket No. 129, and the Solicitation Procedures Motion at Docket No. 130.
 
The Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan is at Docket No. 391, and the Disclosure Statement Order (solicitation version) is Docket No. 388, entered May 28, 2026.


The threshold legal issue running through confirmation is whether commingled customer digital assets constitute property of the estate under § 541. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 
On January 16, 2026, a counterparty transferred 40.001 BTC and 650,050 USDC to BlockFills; those assets were swept from the deposit wallet within minutes. On February 11, 2026, the Debtors' representatives disclosed to clients that digital assets were "not segregated per client," "not segregated on separate wallets per customer," and were commingled into "one balance sheet." The Debtors also note a procedural deficiency: objectors have not filed an adversary proceeding as required by Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(b) for determination of property of the estate, and have not attempted tracing of specific funds.
 
If customer digital assets are determined to be estate property, they are available to fund the reorganization, to be distributed pro rata under the Plan, and to serve as the basis for the cash collateral arrangement.


Any creditor who intends to object to confirmation must do so by July 1 at 4:00 p.m. ET. Customer creditors who believe they can trace specific assets outside the estate — and thereby contest the commingling/§ 541 classification — face a compressing procedural window: no adversary proceeding has been filed, and the confirmation hearing is two weeks out. 
The filings followed years of cascading financial losses, two prepetition customer lawsuits with temporary restraining orders, and the collapse of multiple recapitalization attempts.
 The case background includes prepetition TROs in *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.* (S.D.N.Y., No. 1:26-cv-01672) and *1548199 Alberta Ltd. v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.* (N.D. Ill., No. 1:26-cv-02451), 
in which Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte temporary restraining order on March 3, 2026 freezing 70.56 BTC (approximately $4.8 million) belonging to Dominion and barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States.
 The fate of those TRO'd assets in the context of the confirmed plan will be a live issue at the July 13 confirmation hearing.

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, formed effective March 30, 2026, retained Morris James LLP as counsel. 
The UCC was formed effective March 30, 2026, with Morris James LLP serving as counsel pursuant to an order entered April 29, 2026 (Docket No. 262).
 Debtors are represented by McDermott Will & Emery (formerly McDermott Will & Schulte), with David R. Hurst as local Delaware counsel. 
The company attributed its liquidity crisis to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius.
 The docket has seen 14 new entries in the prior 72 hours (as of June 25), suggesting continued pre-confirmation activity.

### Sleep Number — § 363 Sale Sprint Under Judge Paek; DIP Terms and Bidding Procedures Are the Creditor Battleground

Sleep Number Corporation filed on June 12, 2026 in the Southern District of New York. 
The bankruptcy petition showed assets in the range of $500 million to $1 billion with liabilities in the range of more than $1 billion.
 More precisely: 
Sleep Number Corporation reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 
Substantially all of the company's debt, with balances of approximately $672.5 million in the aggregate principal amount as of the Petition Date, is in default and accelerated, but subject to the automatic stay.


The stalking horse is SNBR Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc. 
Pursuant to the Stalking Horse APA dated June 12, 2026, the Stalking Horse Bidder has committed — subject to court approval — to acquire substantially all of the Company's assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The sale is designed to address a balance sheet weighed down by approximately $672.5 million in prepetition funded debt, which includes a $475 million revolving facility and nearly $200 million in term loans.
 With $415 million of cash consideration against $672.5 million in funded debt, the recovery waterfall is straightforward in one direction: secured lenders are in the money; unsecured creditors' recovery depends entirely on whether the auction generates a materially higher bid or whether estate claims produce additional value.

DIP financing is up to $260 million. 
The Sleep Number debtors are requesting approval of a $260 million DIP financing facility with $65 million in new money tied to a $195 million rollup of prepetition secured obligations on a 3-to-1 basis.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans mature on September 16, 2026 and bear interest at SOFR + 8.00% or a base rate plus 7.00%. The loans are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral and are subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.
 The interim DIP order is in place; final DIP approval is set for July 9 before Judge Paek. 
A hearing on the Debtors' First Day Motions was held June 12 at 3:00 p.m. ET before Judge Kyu Y. Paek; a final hearing on certain First Day Motions will be held July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The bidding procedures hearing is set for **July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET**. 
The July 2, 2026 hearing is designated as the hearing to consider the Bidding Procedures Motion.
 Creditors and landlords with 572 stores on the line — Sleep Number operates one of the larger specialty retail footprints in the U.S. — should be at that hearing or filing position papers now. 
A 14-week prepetition marketing process solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, generating 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals; the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day post-petition diligence period.


The filing squarely blames trade policy for the terminal liquidity spiral. 
Sleep Number's Chapter 11 filing was precipitated by a historic industry recession driven by a consumer shift toward e-commerce, diminishing profit margins, supply chain vulnerabilities, and the unpredictable impact of U.S. tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA).
 
As macroeconomic headwinds strained the company's bottom line, Sleep Number faced acute liquidity and vendor distress prepetition, evidenced by BMO Harris delivering notice in April 2026 to terminate the debtors' corporate purchase card program.
 The prepetition credit agreement — a facility that traces back to February 18, 2018, as amended — was forbeared as recently as April 27, 2026 before the chapter 11 petition superseded it. 
The filing of the Bankruptcy Petitions constituted an event of default under the documents governing the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement, which accelerated the Company's obligations under the Prepetition Credit Agreement.


One unusual feature: the U.S. Trustee's solicitation for UCC membership indicated a June 22, 2026 response deadline. 
Case No. 26-11399 (KYP) has joint administration pending; § 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code authorizes the U.S. Trustee to appoint an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, and creditors were notified of eligibility for appointment.
 It is not yet publicly confirmed that a UCC has been formally constituted and seated. 
Sleep Number Corporation reports that the number of creditors is in the range of 50 to 99
 — a relatively small creditor body by consumer retail standards, though lease counterparties and trade vendors are not fully enumerated in those initial petition-level disclosures. Landlords with store leases at any of the 572 locations should be tracking the assumption/rejection schedule; lease decisions will follow the auction outcome and are not yet addressed in public-facing docket materials.


Management explicitly warns that common shares are "significantly out of the money," expects a complete or significant loss for shareholders, and anticipates a Nasdaq delisting.

### Broader Market Landscape — Consumer Retail Distress and Cross-Border Context

Sleep Number's case is a useful barometer. 
Over the January 1 through June 12, 2026 stretch, 2026 has recorded more Chapter 11s above the $10 million liability threshold than any year in the First Day Database; the $500 million to $1 billion liability range is significantly elevated over prior recent years.
 The tariff-driven distress cycle — IEEPA levies on direct-import components, followed by continued regulatory uncertainty after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariff authority — is accelerating filings across import-dependent consumer goods sectors. Sleep Number's first day declaration explicitly cited IEEPA-based levies on certain direct-import products as a contributing cause of insolvency. Distressed investors should be calibrating exposure in any consumer goods company with a meaningful direct-import component in its COGS structure, whether or not it has filed.

A separate cross-border note: SiFi Networks America LLC (Bankr. D. Del.) filed June 5 in the wake of its parent, SiFi Networks America Ltd., commencing UK insolvency proceedings and selling substantially all of its own assets to PATRIZIA, a German infrastructure investor. 
The company filed after its parent discontinued funding, took steps to sell substantially all of its own assets, and commenced an insolvency proceeding in the United Kingdom; in April 2026, the parent was acquired by PATRIZIA; ArcLink, a PATRIZIA affiliate, agreed to provide a $3.13 million new-money DIP loan plus a full rollup of the prepetition note, and is also serving as stalking horse.
 The case illustrates a recurring cross-border dynamic: a parent-level foreign insolvency drives a U.S. subsidiary filing on a near-freefall timeline, with the acquirer/lender serving as both DIP and stalking horse — a structure that warrants UCC scrutiny on conflicts and credit-bid rights.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors in BlockFills (26-10371) must act by **July 1 at 4:00 p.m. ET**: file any confirmation objections, file temporary allowance motions if claim amounts are disputed for voting purposes, and — critically — assess whether they have a viable asset-tracing argument against the debtors' § 541 commingling position; the absence of any pending Rule 7001 adversary proceeding on customer-property status means the plan confirmation hearing on July 13 is likely the last practical forum for that challenge before it becomes res judicata. In Sleep Number (26-11399), the immediate priority is the **July 2 bidding procedures hearing** before Judge Paek: any creditor or landlord seeking to influence break-up fee sizing, topping bid increments, or credit-bid eligibility should file a position paper or appear at that hearing, as bid procedure orders almost invariably preclude later objection to the auction mechanics they establish; lease counterparties with stores in markets where Sleep Country Canada has no footprint should assess whether they can negotiate assumption and assignment terms directly with a competing bidder before the July 13 auction freezes the field. DIP creditors on the $260 million facility (final approval July 9) are secured at SOFR + 8.00% and should confirm their collateral description and lien priority against any prepetition second-lien or trade creditor encumbrances before the final DIP order enters.
