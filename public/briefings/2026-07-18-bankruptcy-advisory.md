### Sleep Number — *In re Sleep Number Corporation, et al.*, No. 26-11399-KYP (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., J. Paek)

The trajectory heading into this weekend: 
Sleep Number and four affiliated debtors initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 sale process in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 The case is a pure § 363 vehicle with no reorganization path. 
It is built around a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 That bid lands against a distressed balance sheet: 
as of April 30, 2026, the debtors reported $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in liabilities.


The structural problem for unsecured creditors is arithmetic. 
The bid falls roughly $257.5 million short of the funded debt sitting ahead of unsecured creditors.
 
At roughly 62 cents on the dollar of funded debt, it is well short of the full payment the pre-petition milestones contemplated.
 The DIP facility is self-funding and structurally hostile to junior claimants. 
The prepetition lenders committed to provide up to $260 million of debtor-in-possession financing — $65 million in new-money superpriority term loans and up to $195 million in roll-up loans converting prepetition obligations — under a DIP Credit Agreement.
 
The DIP and roll-up loans mature September 16, 2026, and bear interest at SOFR plus 8.00% or base rate plus 7.00%.


**The critical delta as of this morning:** 
the July 15 sale hearing was adjourned, and the adjourned sale hearing is now set for July 20 at 10:00 a.m. ET — with the final DIP hearing, the hearing on the sale order, and related matters all consolidated into that date.
 The DIP milestones, as originally structured, required a sale order by July 15 and closing by July 31. 
Under the original DIP, an auction was to be conducted by July 13, a sale order entered by July 15, and the sale closed by July 31.
 Whether the adjournment to July 20 constitutes a milestone breach — or whether lenders have agreed to a waiver — is not publicly confirmed as of this writing. That answer matters: a milestone event of default would give the DIP lenders leverage to accelerate, which in this capital structure means whatever residual estate value exists would be absorbed before any distribution reaches unsecured creditors.

The unsecured constituency faces additional structural disadvantage. 
Any official committee ran immediately into a Challenge Deadline that effectively terminates when the sale hearing begins — only 33 days into the case — and with a $100,000 challenge budget set against more than $672.5 million in prepetition secured obligations, the practical ability to investigate lender conduct or DIP terms before the sale is approved is sharply limited.
 Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP; Brian M. Resnick is lead counsel of record. 
The filing indicates there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — but that representation, made at first-day, must be evaluated against the $257.5 million funding gap and a DIP that primes everything.

Lease creditors are in a distinct sub-class. 
The debtors filed a rejection schedule covering 44 locations under the Select Comfort Retail Corporation entity, with rejection effective as of the June 12, 2026 petition date; landlords of those locations are immediately left with capped rejection-damages claims under § 502(b)(6) — limited to the greater of one year's rent or 15% of the remaining term (not to exceed three years' rent), plus unpaid pre-rejection rent — with little to no prospect of meaningful recovery in the unsecured pool.
 
The buyer's real decisions on the surviving portfolio come post-closing, during a Designation Rights Period running up to 60 days after closing, in which it formally designates each unassumed lease for assumption and rejection — any lease not designated is automatically rejected without further court order. With closing targeted by July 31, that window could extend into late September 2026.
 
On assumed leases, prepetition cure costs are capped at $8 million in the aggregate, with any excess reducing the purchase price dollar-for-dollar.


The § 341 meeting of creditors has been continued. 
The Meeting of Creditors will be held by Zoom conference on July 24, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET.
 An August 20, 2026 hearing on a motion for relief from the automatic stay is also on calendar. The sale hearing adjournment to Monday is the controlling near-term event.

### New Fortress Energy — *In re NFE Global Holdings Ltd., et al.*, No. 26-11268 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., C.J. Glenn) — Chapter 15 Recognition Opinion

This is the week's most important development for cross-border restructuring practice. 
On July 14, 2026, Chief Judge Martin Glenn of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York issued a memorandum opinion in *In re NFE Global Holdings Limited, et al.*, Case No. 26-11268 (MG), recognizing two English Part 26A restructuring plan proceedings as foreign main proceedings under chapter 15 of the Bankruptcy Code and giving full force and effect to the English court's sanction order and restructuring plans, including the plan releases.


The factual predicate: 
New Fortress Energy Inc. is a Delaware corporation based in New York; the chapter 15 debtors, NFE Global Holdings Limited and NFE Brazil Newco Limited, are UK-incorporated affiliates.
 
NFE Brazil was incorporated in April 2026, shortly before the English proceedings, and was formed specifically to promote the restructuring of the group's Brazil-related business.
 That timing is what gave rise to the COMI tourism analysis. 
The restructuring plans were designed to reduce the New Fortress group's funded debt from approximately $5.7 billion to less than $1 billion, exchange existing debt for new debt and equity, and separate the group into two independent companies: BrazilCo and CoreCo.
 
Existing shareholders would retain 35% of CoreCo common stock before dilution from the management incentive plan and convertible preferred equity.


Creditor support was nearly unanimous. 
Plan Creditors showed overwhelming support at the June 15 meetings, with 99% voting in favor and unanimous consent obtained in nearly all classes.


The legal holding is what practitioners need to study. 
Where a Part 26A plan is used to restructure the debt of a non-UK company, the court stated that a chapter 15 court should scrutinize COMI closely to ensure the foreign proceeding has not been used unfairly to favor one creditor group over another or to achieve a result that could not be achieved in chapter 11; insider exploitation, untoward manipulation, or overt thwarting of creditor expectations may support denial of recognition or enforcement.
 
The court found no such abuse in New Fortress: there were no objections, creditors overwhelmingly supported the restructuring, affected creditors had notice and hearing rights in the English proceedings, and the Alvarez & Marsal analysis showed better projected recoveries under the restructuring plans than under the relevant alternative. In those circumstances, the court concluded the plans were not the product of exploitation or improper manipulation.


The practical implication for distressed investors: a U.S.-based corporate group with subsidiaries that can be domiciled or COMI-shifted to the UK can now use the Part 26A plan — with its lower voting thresholds and broader class composition flexibility relative to chapter 11 — and secure U.S. recognition, provided the process is not an abuse. Chief Judge Glenn's warning on COMI tourism is real, but the safe harbor is functionally defined: broad creditor support, no objecting creditors, and recovery analysis favoring the plan over the available alternative. 
The decision cautions that "COMI tourism" is not a per se bar to recognition, but abusive use of UK plan companies may justify denial of recognition or enforcement in future cases.
