### Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399 (KYP) — Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) — Bidding Procedures Hearing Today

The most time-sensitive matter for creditors tracking this advisory is unfolding this morning. 
Sleep Number Corporation is seeking a July 2 hearing on its bidding procedures, with a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, and a July 15 hearing to approve the sale — the entire process requiring a closing by July 31.
 The hearing before Judge Kyu Young Paek in Manhattan is where creditors and landlords will learn whether the court approves the proposed compressed timeline, modifies the bid protections, or requires additional marketing time.

The capital structure framing is important for recovery analysis. 
According to the first-day declaration, the company carries approximately $672.5 million of funded debt: roughly $475 million under a revolver, $177.5 million under a term loan entered into in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered into in 2026.
 Against that, 
the petition lists $642 million in assets and $1.28 billion in total debts.
 The gap between funded debt and total obligations signals meaningful unsecured exposure beyond the secured facilities. 
The filing indicates that funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors
 — a representation that warrants scrutiny given the arithmetic.

The proposed transaction structure: 
the filing aims to effectuate a going-concern section 363 sale of substantially all assets, backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., who has committed — subject to court approval — to acquire substantially all assets for $415 million in cash plus the assumption of certain liabilities.
 
The deal carries a 3% break-up fee along with an expense reimbursement cap of $4 million.


The prepetition process matters for how aggressively the court is likely to approve the compressed timeline. 
The company's expedited schedule is justified by a prepetition 14-week marketing process during which it contacted 53 potential strategic and financial purchasers; of those, 19 executed non-disclosure agreements and five submitted preliminary proposals.
 That history should ease the court's concerns about truncated market exposure — though landlords and unsecured trade creditors with objections have a narrow window.

DIP financing: 
the case is funded by up to $65 million in new money and up to $195 million via a roll-up of prepetition debt from the company's existing lenders.
 That roll-up structure warrants attention from the UCC (if one is appointed) — a priming roll-up of $195 million of prepetition debt at DIP priority is a significant subordination event for unsecured creditors.

Debtor's counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell (Brian Resnick). 
Guggenheim Securities serves as investment banker and AP Services (an AlixPartners affiliate) as interim management.
 Claims administration is through Kroll. 
A final hearing on certain first day motions is scheduled for July 9, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


Lease exposure deserves a separate note. 
Sleep Number has flagged a review of its store footprint with the intention of maintaining as many profitable retail locations as possible; it has filed a motion to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations.
 With 572 stores nationally, the section 365 rejection process will generate a significant administrative and unsecured claims queue for landlords. 
The company entered bankruptcy carrying $672 million in debt, citing tariffs and inflation.
 That tariff narrative has direct implications for lease-rejection damages calculations: landlords will argue that the company's operational decline was foreseeable and that the rejection damages cap under section 502(b)(6) should be interpreted strictly.

The judge assignment has now been confirmed. 
The cases are jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399 before the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.
 The docket had carried "judge not assigned" in the tracked-case ground truth as of the last run; that is now resolved. Judge Paek presides from the Southern District of New York in Manhattan.

### BlockFills / Reliz Technology (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH) — Bankr. D. Del.) — Confirmation Eleven Days Out

BlockFills enters the final stretch. The voting deadline and confirmation objection deadline both passed yesterday — 
the voting deadline was July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the confirmation objection deadline was also July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the confirmation hearing is set for July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET
 before Judge Thomas M. Horan at 824 Market Street, Wilmington, Delaware.

The docket logged nine new entries in the 72 hours ending July 1 (Dkt. Nos. 478–482 visible in the live data), consistent with end-of-solicitation housekeeping and the filing of any confirmation objections and voting tabulation.

The sale that anchors the plan has already been approved. 
A Delaware bankruptcy judge on June 16, 2026 agreed to approve the $3.25 million sale of BlockFills to a Belgian digital asset investment group.
 That buyer is Keyrock SA. 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume substantially all of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and IP; Keyrock was declared the "Successful Bidder" per the May 26, 2026 court filing.


The recovery mathematics are stark. 
The debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 A $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in unsecured claims implies a recovery in the low single-digit percentage range absent material avoidance actions or additional plan contributions. The plan structure — 
a dual-track toggle mechanism allowing the debtors to elect between a customer-led NewCo Transaction and an Alternative Transaction through a section 363 competitive sale process
 — resolved in favor of the 363 track. The residual question for the confirmation hearing is how the liquidating trust will treat the estate-versus-customer-property dispute and what, if anything, the plan distributes to general unsecured creditors beyond the sale proceeds.

The customer property issue was never fully resolved. 
At the first day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate; the court noted no evidence before it established the funds as anything other than estate property, but preserved the issue for further hearing.
 If confirmation proceeds July 13 without a final adjudication of that issue, the liquidating trustee will inherit a potentially contested assets pool — meaningful for any creditor calculating actual distribution timing.

The adversary proceeding against Artha Investment Partners LLC (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH)) remains in the docket. 
The debtors brought that adversary action against Artha Investment Partners LLC in the same court.
 Its status — whether resolved, settled, or being transferred to the liquidating trust — will affect the plan's projected recovery schedule.

The Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors, 
comprising seven members including SBI VC Trade Co., Ltd., Dominion Capital LLC, Energy Conversion Group, and Fuel Labs Inc., is represented by Morris James LLP.
 Any confirmation objections filed by July 1 would surface at the July 13 hearing; creditors tracking recoveries should monitor Dkt. Nos. 483 and above for objections, the voting tabulation report, and any plan modifications filed in response.

### Broader Creditor-Rights Landscape: Tariff-Driven Filings and the § 363 Compression Trend

Sleep Number's framing is representative of a pattern worth tracking systematically. 
Sleep Number attributed its bankruptcy in part to "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 That framing — tariffs as a proximate cause of a distressed filing — is a litigation posture as much as a business narrative. It will matter in lease-rejection disputes (landlords will push back on foreseeability), in preference-defense litigation (was the 90-day window during a period of market disruption?), and potentially in D&O exposure if creditors argue the board delayed the filing.

The Sleep Number sale timeline also illustrates the accelerating compression of section 363 processes when substantial prepetition marketing has occurred. A 26-day postpetition diligence window is aggressive by historical standards. 
The bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, a July 15 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.
 Courts have generally permitted this when the debtor can demonstrate a robust prepetition process — and with 53 parties contacted and five preliminary proposals, Sleep Number clears that bar. But objecting parties (landlords, trade creditors, any UCC) have effectively one week between today's bidding-procedures ruling and the July 8 bid deadline to identify and engage overbidders or lodge process objections. That window is functionally closed for most unsecured creditors without existing relationships in the bidding pool.

## Recommended Actions

Creditors in Sleep Number (Case No. 26-11399 (KYP)) should treat today's bidding-procedures hearing as the last meaningful process checkpoint before the auction: confirm whether the court approved the July 8 bid deadline and 3% break-up fee as proposed, assess whether any competing strategic or financial party has registered as a potential qualified bidder, and — for landlords specifically — determine whether lease-rejection motions at the 44 non-operational stores will be calendared before or after the July 15 sale-approval hearing, since the sequencing affects assumption/assignment leverage. In BlockFills (Case No. 26-10371 (TMH)), creditors holding general unsecured claims should review any confirmation objections filed on or before July 1 (now on the public docket) and the voting tabulation report; given the $3.25 million sale price against $145 million in unsecured claims, the confirmation hearing on July 13 is the forum to press for full disclosure of the liquidating trust's projected distribution waterfall, the status of the Artha adversary proceeding, and the plan's treatment of the commingled-customer-funds issue. Distressed investors considering claims transfers in either case should note that BlockFills has a September 11, 2026 objection deadline per the Veritaglobal calendar — the window for trading before plan-level resolution is measured in weeks, not months.
