### ConvergeOne — Equal Treatment Under § 1123(a)(4) on Appeal to the Fifth Circuit

The circuit-level posture of *In re ConvergeOne Holdings, Inc.*, Case No. 24-90194 (Bankr. S.D. Tex.) / Civil Case No. 4:24-cv-02001, is the single most consequential live issue in domestic creditor-rights practice and merits sustained attention from any counsel structuring exit financing in Chapter 11 cases.

On September 25, 2025, the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Texas — Judge Andrew Hanen presiding — reversed key portions of the bankruptcy court's approval of ConvergeOne's prepackaged Chapter 11 plan, holding that exclusive backstop and equity subscription rights granted to a subset of pre-bankruptcy first-lien creditors violated 11 U.S.C. § 1123(a)(4) by granting unequal treatment to similarly situated lenders. ConvergeOne had approximately $1.8 billion in secured debt when it filed for Chapter 11 in April 2024 with an RSA signed by approximately 81% of first-lien and 100% of second-lien holders; the plan exchanged approximately $1.6 billion of debt for equity and a $245 million capital raise in the form of a discounted equity rights offering backstopped by the majority lenders, with $159 million offered pro rata to all first-lien creditors while $86 million was reserved exclusively for the backstopping majority, who also received a 10% backstop premium plus rights to unsubscribed shares.

U.S. District Judge Andrew S. Hansen found that ConvergeOne's prepackaged plan violated 11 U.S.C. § 1123(a)(4)'s equal treatment requirement by allowing certain "Majority Lenders" to participate in an exclusive equity backstopping arrangement that generated approximately 30% higher recoveries compared to excluded "Minority Lenders" in the same creditor class. Unlike the Serta reversal, which was based on contract interpretation and could be drafted around, the ConvergeOne decision is rooted in the Bankruptcy Code itself and cannot be altered by a private contract.

On October 22, 2025, ConvergeOne filed a notice of appeal to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit. The Fifth Circuit will now have the opportunity to weigh in on this issue and potentially provide clarity regarding the scope and applicability of its Serta decision. For creditors and restructuring practitioners, the pending Fifth Circuit briefing schedule — not yet resolved — is the watch item of the cycle. Given the frequency of backstopped equity rights offerings in Chapter 11 cases, *ConvergeOne* and its application of *Serta* may significantly impact debtors' strategies for raising capital and marshaling creditor support for plan confirmation; it also remains to be seen whether courts will extend the prohibition on exclusive backstop opportunities to other contexts, such as debtor-in-possession financing.

Specifically, the plan violated § 1123(a)(4)'s equal treatment requirement in two ways: (i) it provided certain creditors within a class with an exclusive investment opportunity that resulted in higher recoveries than for other creditors in that class; and (ii) the debtors failed to market the backstop opportunity to demonstrate that the backstopping lenders paid adequate consideration rather than receiving the opportunity on account of their claims. The decision creates a binary compliance path: offer the backstop opportunity to all class members, or subject it to a genuine competitive marketing process before the RSA is finalized.

### Jones Day BRR Vol. 25 No. 3 — Chapter 15 Asset Requirement and Delaware Avoidance Recovery Against Subsequent Transferees

Jones Day's Vol. 25 No. 3 leads with the Texas court's decision in *In re Siu-Fung Ceramics Holdings Limited*, No. 24-33299, 2026 WL 382424 (Bankr. S.D. Tex. Feb. 10, 2026), adopting the *Barnet* rule and denying Chapter 15 recognition of Hong Kong proceedings on dual grounds: the corporate debtors' proceeding was ineligible because the attorney retainer funding it was not deposited until nine months after the Chapter 15 petition date, and the individual debtor's proceeding was denied because his center of main interests was not in Hong Kong on the petition date and he did not even maintain an "establishment" there. This decision is important for cross-border creditors seeking to block or contain Chapter 15-recognized stays on U.S. assets.

The same issue also covers the Delaware avoidance-recovery ruling. The Bankruptcy Code authorizes a trustee to avoid transfers made with fraudulent intent or that are constructively fraudulent, and also permits recovery from both initial recipients and subsequent transferees. Courts have disagreed over whether a transfer must actually be avoided before the trustee can seek recovery from a subsequent transferee and whether the initial transferee must be a defendant. The U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware weighed in in *Phillips v. SS Associates LLC (In re ONH AFC CS Investors, LLC)*, No. 23-10931 (CTG), 2026 WL 312892 (Bankr. D. Del. Feb. 4, 2026), adopting the majority view and ruling that the initial transferee need not be a named defendant in avoidance and recovery litigation against a subsequent transferee. For unsecured creditors, this ruling meaningfully expands the practical reach of fraudulent transfer avoidance actions by removing the procedural barrier of joining initial transferees who may be judgment-proof, dissolved, or otherwise unreachable.

### Subchapter V Debt-Limit Expiration and Post-Petition Interest Circuit Split — Ongoing Structural Issues

Two structural issues from the prior advisory cycle carry forward and remain unresolved. First, the Subchapter V debt-limit problem: a proposed amendment to the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026 (S.2296) that would have renewed the higher Subchapter V debt limits — originally extended by the Bankruptcy Threshold Adjustment and Technical Corrections Act of 2022 (Pub. L. No. 117-151) — and provided retroactivity for cases filed on or after the June 21, 2024 sunset, was not enacted. Power Block Coin is a direct illustration of why this matters: with less than three hours to spare before the Subchapter V debt limit dropped from $7,500,000 to $3,024,725, Power Block Coin filed its Chapter 11 petition on June 20, 2024, electing to proceed under Subchapter V as an entity that had aggregate noncontingent liquidated secured and unsecured debts of less than $7,500,000 on the petition date. The subsequent conversion of the case to a full Chapter 11 — with an Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors seeking a $192 million liquidating plan — is in significant part a product of that threshold election and the debt-limit policy gap.

Second, on post-petition interest, the circuit split remains live: the Fifth and Ninth Circuits have held that unsecured creditors whose claims are unimpaired have an equitable right to contractual post-petition interest, while the Second and Third Circuits have been more inclined to limit interest to the federal judgment rate. Until the Supreme Court takes up this split, creditors in large Chapter 11 cases — particularly solvent-debtor reorganizations — must assess which circuit's law governs their right to above-federal-rate interest on unimpaired claims.

### DIP Financing for Litigation Estates — Insider Priming Lien Approved in SPNY Court

A notable creditor-rights data point from the Jones Day BRR March-April 2026 edition remains analytically current. The ability of a debtor-in-possession to obtain financing is crucial where the estate's sole asset is litigation claims. In *In re SPAC Recovery Co.*, No. 25-12109 (JPM), 2026 WL 18778 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Jan. 2, 2026), the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York authorized a debtor whose sole asset was litigation claims to borrow money from insiders on a "priming lien" basis to fund the litigation, concluding that the loan was both necessary, beneficial to the estate, and fair. This is a meaningful precedent for the Power Block Coin Liquidating Trustee context, where estate litigation claims — including potential preference and fraudulent transfer actions against insider lenders, including Celsius-side transferees — may require financing to pursue, and where a priming lien on estate litigation recoveries may be the only available collateral.
