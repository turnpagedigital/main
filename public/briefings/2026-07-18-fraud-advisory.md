### I. Stanford Financial Group Receivership — Final Distribution; Uncashed-Check Deadline July 23–26

The Stanford Financial Group receivership (SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd., No. 3:09-CV-0298-N, N.D. Tex., Dallas Div.) is functionally in its final payment-cashing phase. 
Payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.
 
Payments on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.


The immediate operational pressure is the uncashed-check deadline. 
Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash those checks by July 26, 2026. Checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline.
 That is a seven-day gate from today's date, and any claimant holding an uncashed check is now in a forfeiture-risk zone. 
The Receiver recently contacted by email certain claimants whose Final Distribution checks remain uncashed, warning that such checks will become void and the funds will revert to the Receivership if not cashed timely.


On the fee-dispute track, the Fifth Circuit appeal of the October 8, 2025 fee order is proceeding on an abbreviated briefing schedule. 
The Court awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain of his professional firms appealed that Order to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals, filing their brief on appeal on March 23, 2026.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the Court's Order on May 23, 2026.
 The fee dispute matters for claims traders: the Receiver's fee appeal does not impair the Final Distribution Schedule, but any reversal that expands allowable professional fees would modestly reduce the residual estate available to pay void-check reversions and any subsequent supplemental distributions.

A separately tracked issue is the $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the Receiver on March 11, 2026 regarding the status of the $8.1 million in Swiss funds, and as of the date of that letter the Receiver had not responded.
 
On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, a Status Report regarding Payments Issued Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 The disposition of the Swiss tranche — whether it is folded into a supplemental distribution or remains sub judice — is unverified at this briefing date.

**Claims-trading read:** With the Final Distribution largely paid, the residual value in Stanford claims is now primarily a function of (a) the Swiss-asset tranche, (b) any void-check reversions after July 26, and (c) the fee-appeal outcome. Secondary market pricing on Stanford claims should already be reflecting these tails. Remaining liquidity window is narrow.

### II. BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Closed; Recovery at $15.456B; Fee Application Filed


On February 27, 2026, Irving H. Picard, SIPA Trustee for the liquidation of Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC (BLMIS), announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced, distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The seventeenth interim distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and is paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim will have now been paid when combined with the prior 16 distributions, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


The cumulative recovery benchmark has continued to advance. 
As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


On the fee track: 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his Counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York for work from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 The docket continues under Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789 (LGB) (now before Judge Loretta G. Beaumont following Judge Stuart M. Bernstein's retirement from the case).

The case caption currently reflects Judge LGB per the December 2025 distribution motion filings. Separately, 
Chief Counsel David J. Sheehan has stated that with the next anticipated distribution, 1,531 accounts will be made whole.
 No eighteenth distribution motion has been publicly filed as of this briefing. On the Ponzi-presumption clawback front, the litigation pipeline supporting future distributions continues in the SDNY adversary proceedings under § 548(a)(1)(A) and the good-faith defense (§ 548(c)). No new adversary judgments were reported in the 72-hour scan window.

**Claims-trading read:** At 72.848% recovered against a $20.3 billion allowed-claims pool, the BLMIS secondary market continues to price the residual ~27% at a steep time-value discount. The 50th fee application signals the case is generating substantial ongoing professional costs; however, those costs are borne entirely by SIPC, not by the Customer Fund, which is the correct reference for claim pricing. The next distribution cycle will likely hinge on settlement progress in the remaining adversary pipeline.

### III. OneCoin / Ignatova — DOJ Opens $40M Victim Compensation Process; Guernsey Forfeiture Order Registered January 2026

The most actionable development on the OneCoin track since the last advisory is the DOJ's formal opening of a victim compensation process. 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide; the DOJ filed a number of OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York; several key figures have been sentenced; and more than $40 million in forfeited assets are currently available for victim compensation.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator in this matter.
 Petition forms are available at www.onecoinremission.com. That $40 million pool against $4 billion in losses is a 1% recovery rate — underscoring the degree to which this case remains a future-recovery story, not a distribution story.

On the parallel cross-border asset-recovery track, the Guernsey forfeiture order registered in January 2026 is the most concrete recent recovery. 
German prosecutors in Bielefeld, working with the Royal Court of Guernsey, traced two of Ignatova's London properties through shell companies registered in the Channel Islands; after Ignatova ignored a 28-day ultimatum, the Royal Court on January 16, 2026 registered an overseas forfeiture order and released £8,590,200.92 plus interest into the Seized Asset Fund, to be claimed by Bielefeld for OneCoin victims.
 This forfeiture proceeded under non-conviction-based forfeiture principles — a model that, as practitioners note, 
allows the state to seize criminal proceeds even when the primary defendant has not yet been brought to trial.


On the enablers front: 
Frank Schneider, a former deputy chief of Luxembourg's foreign-intelligence service who advised Ignatova through his firm Sandstone, fled French house arrest in May 2023, was returned, and in April 2026 saw the Nancy Court of Appeal side with Washington's extradition request; he has indicated he will appeal.
 Enabler prosecution is progressing on the Bielefeld–SDNY coordinated track. 
The FBI's "Operation Masquerade," announced in mid-April 2026, represents a renewed effort using advanced digital forensics and international cooperation.
 
The U.S. Department of State currently offers a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to Ignatova's arrest or conviction.


**Claims-recovery read:** The $40M DOJ compensation pool is a floor signal, not a ceiling. The operative recovery levers are (1) continued non-conviction-based forfeiture in Guernsey, Germany, and other jurisdictions; (2) extradition and sentencing of Schneider and other enablers whose cooperation may unlock additional asset disclosures; and (3) any further tracing of Ignatova's residual offshore structures using 28 U.S.C. § 1782 orders and Mareva-equivalent freezes in UK/Channel Islands proceedings. Victims should file petitions with Kroll now — the compensation process may expand as further assets are seized.

### IV. SEC Enforcement Posture — Quality-Over-Quantity Pivot; WAMCO $100M Settlement; Structural Signal for Receiver Appointments

Two structural signals from the SEC's 2026 enforcement posture are material to the fraud-recovery desk.

First, the Commission's own FY2025 enforcement data: 
During FY2025, the SEC filed 456 enforcement actions, including 303 standalone actions and 69 follow-on administrative proceedings, obtaining orders for monetary relief totaling $17.9 billion; these actions prioritized cases directly harming investors, including offering frauds, market manipulation, insider trading, issuer disclosure violations, and breaches of fiduciary duty by investment advisers.
 
Total FY2025 enforcement actions represented a 22% decrease from FY2024
 — fewer cases, higher per-case value. That posture, articulated explicitly by Acting Enforcement Director Sam Waldon, 
signals the Division is moving "full steam ahead" by focusing on quality over quantity and bringing actions against those who "lie, cheat, and steal," rejecting traditional metrics like case counts and penalty totals.


The practical downstream consequence: new receiver appointments in 2026 will likely attach to larger, better-documented offering-fraud actions. Smaller schemes below the materiality floor the Division is now applying may receive less civil-enforcement attention, redirecting those victims toward state-level remedies and private clawback actions.

Second, the WAMCO settlement illustrates how the Commission is structuring relief in non-receivership adviser-fraud cases. 
On June 5, 2026, the SEC settled charges against Pasadena-based investment adviser Western Asset Management Company, LLC (WAMCO), ordering WAMCO to pay a $100 million civil money penalty, which will be distributed to harmed investors to the extent feasible.
 The "to the extent feasible" qualifier in the distribution commitment is the phrase claims practitioners should track — it introduces discretionary distance between the penalty and victim receipt.
