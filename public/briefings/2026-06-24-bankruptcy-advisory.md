### I. BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.) — Bankr. D. Del. No. 26-10371 (Judge Thomas M. Horan) — Plan Solicitation Underway; Confirmation July 13


On March 15, 2026, each of the four Debtors filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions, jointly administered under Case No. 26-10371 before Judge Thomas M. Horan in the Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.
 
The capital structure is concentrated in customer and trade exposure rather than traditional funded debt: the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million in general unsecured customer and trade obligations and approximately $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.


The sale process has now cleared its principal hurdle. 
According to a bankruptcy filing, Keyrock SA agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and its proprietary technology and intellectual property — and Keyrock confirmed in a May 26 court document that it had been declared the "Successful Bidder."
 
The acquisition remained subject to court approval as of the June 16 hearing date.
 The Veritaglobal case-administration page — which is the authoritative key-dates source per the tracked-case ground truth — lists two docket entries filed June 22 (Dkt. 448 and 449), indicating post-hearing activity consistent with entry of a sale order; the specific content of those entries is not yet confirmed through a public document URL as of the time of this run.

The Voting Deadline is July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; the Confirmation Objection Deadline is July 1, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET; and the Confirmation Hearing is July 13, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.


The adversary proceeding dimension warrants continued monitoring. 
Under the main case No. 26-10371 (TMH), the Debtors filed Adversary Proceeding No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners LLC.
 That adversary — origin-dated April 9, 2026 — is a potential avoidance or recovery action that could affect net distributable value available to general unsecured creditors. The Committee's engagement of FTI Consulting as financial advisors effective April 1, 2026 (Dkt. 264) signals active UCC scrutiny of the estate's inter-creditor dynamics.

**Creditor recovery arithmetic at a glance.** 
Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. reported $50 million to $100 million in assets against $100 million to $500 million in liabilities

The $3.25 million sale consideration from Keyrock represents the primary cash source; plan distributions will depend heavily on post-confirmation avoidance actions and any residual enterprise value preserved through the Keyrock transaction. ### II. Sleep Number Corporation — Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 26-11399 (KYP) (Judge Kyu Young Paek) — Bidding Procedures Hearing July 2; Auction July 13; UCC Formation Imminent


On June 12, 2026, Sleep Number Corporation and its subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, jointly administered under Case No. 26-11399.
 
The cases are assigned to the Honorable Kyu Young Paek.


The capital structure is under significant stress. 
Substantially all of the Company's debt — approximately $672.5 million in aggregate principal — is in default and accelerated as of the Petition Date, but subject to the automatic stay under the Bankruptcy Code.
 
Sleep Number reported $642.3 million in assets against $1.3 billion in liabilities.
 Debtors' counsel is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP (lead: Brian M. Resnick, Angela M. Libby, Stephen D. Piraino, Richard J. Steinberg) out of 450 Lexington Avenue, New York. 
Claims administration is being handled by Kroll.


**DIP Facility.** 
On June 16, 2026, the Company entered into the Fourteenth Amendment to the Amended and Restated Credit and Security Agreement (the "DIP Credit Agreement"), under which the prepetition lenders committed to provide up to $260 million of DIP financing in the form of new-money superpriority senior secured term loan commitments of up to $65 million, available in multiple draws.
 
The DIP Loans bear interest at SOFR plus 8.00% or the base rate plus 7.00%.
 
The Bankruptcy Court has entered an interim DIP order, with a final hearing scheduled for July 9, 2026.
 
The loans are secured by first-priority, priming, and junior liens on various collateral and are subject to strict covenants, case milestones, and budget variance tests.


**363 Sale Process.** 
The filing aims to effectuate a going-concern sale under § 363; the stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., commits to acquire substantially all assets for $415 million in cash plus assumption of certain liabilities, establishing the floor for competing bids.
 
A 14-week prepetition marketing process solicited 53 strategic and financial parties, generating 19 NDAs and five preliminary proposals; the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence window, with a July 8, 2026 bid deadline and a July 13, 2026 auction.


**UCC formation.** 
The U.S. Trustee issued questionnaires to eligible unsecured creditors under Case No. 26-11399 (KYP), with a response deadline of June 22, 2026 at 12:00 p.m. ET, pursuant to § 1102(b) of the Bankruptcy Code.
 A Committee should be formed imminently; its composition and professional retentions will determine the robustness of the §§ 506(c), 552, and 503(b) challenges that will define general unsecured recovery in a sale-driven case with a heavily primed collateral stack.

**Equity/creditor recovery outlook.** 
The Company cites significant risks related to its Chapter 11 process, including the likely cancellation of its common shares and uncertainty around completing the § 363 sale or reorganization.
 With $1.3 billion in liabilities against a $415 million stalking horse floor, unsecured creditors are looking at a significant deficiency claim pool — the DIP roll-up of prepetition secured debt is the critical structural feature to scrutinize.

### III. Delaware Bankruptcy Court on Initial Transferee Defense — Fraudulent Transfer Practice Point


The Jones Day Business Restructuring Review (Vol. 25 No. 3, May–June 2026) flags a Delaware Bankruptcy Court decision holding that an initial transferee need not be a defendant in fraudulent transfer avoidance and recovery litigation
 — a development with direct relevance to the BlockFills estate's adversary posture. If the Delaware court has narrowed the class of required defendants in avoidance actions, the estate may face litigation-efficiency constraints in its Artha Investment Partners adversary (Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH)) and any other avoidance targets. Practitioners structuring creditor-side avoidance strategy in Delaware should obtain and review this ruling before filing any amended adversary complaint.

The same review notes New York bankruptcy court analysis of adequate assurance of future performance under assigned leases and Chapter 11 plan exculpation provisions — both directly germane to the Sleep Number lease rejection / assignment dynamics given the company's 572-store footprint. Lease rejection or assumption elections, and the corresponding landlord rejection-damage claims capped under § 502(b)(6), will constitute one of the largest claimant cohorts in the Sleep Number general unsecured pool.

### IV. Structural Backdrop: Post-*Purdue* Third-Party Release Landscape

The BlockFills Amended Joint Chapter 11 Plan (Dkt. 391, filed on or about May 28, 2026) will be tested against the Supreme Court's 2024 holding in *Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. __ (2024). 
In that decision — one of the most significant appellate rulings on bankruptcy law since adoption of the Bankruptcy Code in 1978 — the Court held that a Chapter 11 debtor and its non-debtor joint tortfeasors may not effectuate a non-consensual third-party release of the non-debtor tortfeasors through a confirmed plan of reorganization.
 
The Court construed § 1123(b)(6)'s catchall provision narrowly, holding that it embraces only objects similar in nature to the specific examples surrounding it — all of which deal with the relationship between a debtor and its own creditors — and does not confer the "radically different" power to discharge a nondebtor's debts without the consent of affected creditors.


For BlockFills, the practical question is whether the Amended Plan's exculpation scope extends to Reliz insiders or non-debtor affiliates in a manner that would now require affirmative creditor consent. The July 1 confirmation objection deadline is the mechanism for pressing this issue. Creditors holding claims who have not reviewed Dkt. 391 should do so immediately.
