# ⚖️ Tariff Refunds | Monday, August 03, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

Today is a procedural inflection point on two fronts: the CAPE Phase 3 progress report due **tomorrow, August 4** is the first court-ordered accounting of CBP's finally-liquidated-entry refund pipeline since Judge Eaton's July 17 reliquidation order, and the Section 122 Federal Circuit appeal continues to grind forward against a tariff that has already expired by its own statutory terms. Both tracks are live and both carry meaningful refund exposure that is distinct from each other.

---

### 1. CAPE Phase 3: CBP Progress Report Due August 4 — Lead Case Shifts to *Freestyle World*

**The immediate horizon:** 
CBP is required to file a short progress report on CAPE with the court by 5:00 p.m. EDT on August 4, 2026.
 
The court also scheduled a closed settlement conference for August 5, 2026.
 Tomorrow's filing is the first CBP accounting since 
CIT Senior Judge Richard Eaton, on July 17, 2026, issued an order directing CBP to "reliquidate, without regard to IEEPA duties, any and all of Plaintiffs' entries that have been liquidated for more than 80 days and on which Plaintiffs made estimated deposits pursuant to IEEPA."


**Lead case change:** 
Judge Eaton announced that the new lead case regarding IEEPA tariff refunds is *Freestyle World, Inc. v. United States of America* (1:26-cv-01088). The plaintiff in the previous lead case, Euro-Notions Florida, Inc., had previously announced its intention to voluntarily dismiss its case and subsequently did so. See CIT Order dated July 15, 2026.


**Phase 3 mechanics:** 
Judge Eaton confirmed that the court intends to direct CBP to reliquidate certain finally liquidated entries in the approximately 3,700 pending CIT IEEPA refund cases once the next phase of CAPE becomes operational.
 
CBP special advisor Elena Ryan, during a July 15 webinar, stated that the next CAPE phase will allow entries filed on behalf of plaintiffs who filed court cases with the CIT and have reached final liquidation. She also shared that CBP is looking at other entry types not currently eligible for refunds using CAPE.


**Phase 3 is not open to the general public:** 
The CIT has issued its first Phase 3 procedure order for finally liquidated entries in pending IEEPA cases, but it requires counsel-submitted importer IDs and accepted CAPE declarations — it is not a public Phase 3 opening for every importer.
 Importers without a pending CIT case are effectively excluded from the Phase 3 pipeline under current court orders.

**Government challenge remains on appeal:** 
Technical readiness does not guarantee that refunds will follow. The DOJ continues to argue that CBP lacks authority to reliquidate finally liquidated entries without an individual lawsuit. That position is now before both the CIT and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit.


**Scoreboard through mid-July:** 
As of CBP's July 13 filing, CAPE has accepted 24.4 million entries for IEEPA-duty removal, with approximately $86.3 billion completed, certified, and sent to Treasury.
 Against the universe of 
approximately 330,000 importers who paid or deposited an estimated $166 billion in IEEPA duties across more than 53 million entries,
 that leaves a substantial residual — including the entire Phase 3 finally-liquidated population and 
an additional estimated $25 billion in refunds for entries not covered in Phases 1 through 3,
 comprising AD/CVD-adjacent entries and other excluded categories.

**ACH blocking:** 
CBP reported 9,837 consolidated refunds blocked for missing ACH information as of July 10. Paper checks have been discontinued. ACH/electronic is the core payment path.
 Importers and brokers with stale ACE account information should address that now — the refund window will not wait on administrative housekeeping.

### 2. Federal Circuit Appeal Dismissed — Section 122 Post-Expiry Posture

**Dismissal of Appeal No. 2026-1898:** 
The Federal Circuit dismissed Appeal No. 2026-1898 on July 28, and the next CAPE report is due August 4.
 The specific dismissed appeal is unverified beyond this reference; the Section 122 Federal Circuit docket (*State of Oregon v. Trump*, No. 26-1804) continues to run separately.

**Section 122 expired July 24 — Federal Circuit appeal survives on mootness and refund grounds:** 
The Section 122 tariff was a 10% surcharge on almost all U.S. imports that ran for exactly 150 days, from February 24 to July 24, 2026, and then expired by operation of law.
 Expiry does not moot the Federal Circuit appeal because 
the appeal is still running, and if it ultimately upholds the finding that the surcharge was unlawful, refund opportunities may open to importers beyond the original plaintiffs. If the government prevails, the duty stands.


**What the CIT held and who is covered:** 
On May 7, 2026, the CIT held that Proclamation 11012, which imposed a temporary 10% import surcharge under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. § 2132), was invalid because the proclamation did not identify the type of "balance-of-payments deficits" required by the statute.
 
The permanent injunction issued against the government's collection of the Section 122 tariffs extends only to the three importer plaintiffs who were found to have standing: the State of Washington, Burlap and Barrel, Inc., and Basic Fun, Inc. All other importers remain subject to the Section 122 duties unless they bring their own action, the administration withdraws the tariffs, the tariffs expire, or a court orders broader relief.


**Federal Circuit administrative stay:** 
On June 11, 2026, the Federal Circuit granted the federal government's motion to stay the lower court's injunction. This ruling allows the government to continue collecting the 10% Section 122 import surcharges from the plaintiffs who had previously won relief.
 That stay held through the July 24 statutory expiry, meaning the three named plaintiffs paid the surcharge right through expiration. Their refund order from the CIT — "refunded with interest as provided by law" — remains enforceable and is not extinguished by expiry.

**New complaint filed at expiry:** 
A new complaint was filed at the CIT on July 24 challenging the Section 122 regime, with the decision currently on appeal in the Federal Circuit.
 The Liberty Justice Center, which represents Burlap and Barrel, filed the complaint (Case No. 1:26-cv-03345) on the same day the statute expired, preserving refund claims for an expanded class of plaintiffs.

**Practical implication:** Section 122 is not CAPE-eligible. 
Section 122 is not refundable through CAPE — it is a completely separate authority from IEEPA.
 Importers who paid the 10% surcharge between February 24 and July 24, 2026 must pursue separate protest or litigation channels under 19 U.S.C. §§ 1514 or 1581(i). The window for administrative protests runs 180 days from liquidation; those clocks are ticking on early entries.

---

### 3. Remaining Refund Exposure — Unverified Successor Tariff Risk

The Section 122 expiry on July 24 ended the globally applicable 10% surcharge. The administration had previously signaled potential reliance on Sections 301 and 232 as successor authorities; 
the administration has indicated it continues to pursue alternative tariff measures, including investigations under Sections 301 and 232.
 No new proclamation has been verified as of this writing. Importers should treat the post-July 24 tariff environment as unsettled until a successor measure is announced or the absence of one is confirmed.
