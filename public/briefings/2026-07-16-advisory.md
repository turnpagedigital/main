### I. *Bartz v. Anthropic* — Final Approval Under Submission; Anthropic's Dkt. 679 Opposition Targets Untimely Opt-Outs


The $1.5 billion settlement — the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history — received preliminary approval on September 25, 2025. The final fairness hearing was held May 14, 2026, and Judge Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission.
 Final approval has not issued.

The live obstruction is a discrete procedural skirmish. 
In a supplemental filing ordered after the fairness hearing, Anthropic urged the court not to permit five untimely opt-outs from the proposed $1.5 billion settlement, arguing that the two objectors who sought relief failed to show "excusable neglect" and that the remaining three late opt-out requests should also be denied for lack of any record supporting relief and because other rightsholders have not consented.
 That filing is docket entry 679, dated July 13 — the most recent entry on the CourtListener docket as of this run. (__[CourtListener Docket 69058235](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69058235/bartz-v-anthropic-pbc/)__)

The legal posture favors Anthropic on this narrow issue. Excusable-neglect analysis under Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(b)(1)(B) requires the movant to demonstrate good cause for the delay, a factor courts in the Ninth Circuit weigh against prejudice to class members who relied on the finality of the opt-out deadline. 
The deadline to exclude oneself passed February 9, 2026; the claim-filing deadline was March 30, 2026.
 Both passed months before the fairness hearing. Any opt-out claimant who cannot point to affirmative misinformation or extraordinary circumstance is on weak ground. If the court denies the untimely requests and issues final approval, the next settlement-fund installment triggers: 
Anthropic will fund the settlement in four installments — October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 The September 25, 2026 tranche is the next payment obligation. Claims-investors and rights-holders tracking distribution timing should treat that date as the earliest realistic payment-processing milestone, assuming final approval issues this month or in August.

Fee context matters for net-recovery modeling. 
Plaintiffs urged the court to grant final approval along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 A fee award at $187.5 million represents 12.5% of the $1.5 billion gross fund — well within Ninth Circuit norms for common-fund cases of this size, and meaningfully below the 20–25% that plaintiffs' counsel initially sought.


In the June 2025 ruling that precipitated settlement, Judge William Alsup held that Anthropic's use of lawfully acquired books for AI training was "quintessentially transformative" and thus protected by fair use.
 
Because statutory copyright damages under 17 U.S.C. § 504 can reach $150,000 per infringed work, the exposure theoretically extended into the hundreds of billions of dollars. Anthropic sought an interlocutory appeal and a stay, both denied in early August 2025, leaving trial scheduled for December.
 Settlement followed within weeks. The Alsup ruling's transformative-use holding — covering lawfully acquired books — sits in direct tension with the shadow-library reproduction theory that drove the class certification, 
because Anthropic settled the pirated-library exposure specifically after Judge Alsup ruled its acquisition of pirated books from shadow libraries was not fair use.
 That doctrinal split — lawful acquisition = fair use, pirated acquisition = not fair use — is the single most important fair-use precedent in the AI-copyright docket for any rights-holder evaluating the scope of 17 U.S.C. § 107(1) (transformative use) and § 107(4) (market harm).


Approximately 120,000 authors and rightsholders filed claims, reflecting both the breadth of the alleged infringement and growing awareness among writers worldwide.
 At $3,000 per covered work, 
and with an additional $3,000 per work triggered if the number of affected works exceeds 500,000
, the per-work distribution could double for large-catalog claimants. Claims administrators JND Legal Administration are running distribution calculations; the precise per-work allocation will not be known until the court approves the plan and final claims are reconciled.

### II. *Richner Communications v. Microsoft & OpenAI* — 400 Regional Newspapers Folded Into MDL 3143; Case Placed on Hold Pending Motion Practice in Lead Track

This is the week's most structurally consequential development for the MDL. 
A case against OpenAI Inc. and Microsoft Corp. for allegedly scraping the copyrighted works of some 400 small newspapers to train AI programs will remain on hold pending resolution of motions filed in a broader case against tech companies. The news outlets, led by Richner Communications, sued the tech giants in June, accusing them of conducting AI scraping without authorization and compensation. The lawsuit has since been consolidated into multidistrict litigation involving numerous similar cases, according to a joint stipulation and order filed Monday in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York.
 (__[Bloomberg Law, July 14, 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/newspapers-openai-microsoft-case-on-hold-after-joining-mdl)__)

The underlying complaint, filed June 24 as No. 1:26-cv-05320 (S.D.N.Y.), 
is captioned *Richner Commc'ns, Inc. v. Microsoft Corp.*, No. 1:26-cv-05320.
 It lands in front of 
Hon. Sidney H. Stein, presiding over *In Re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.)
, with Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang on referral.

The Richner complaint advances a strategically distinct theory relative to the earlier publisher cases: 
it alleges that defendants' systems used both model memorization and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to reproduce or closely paraphrase protected news content in user-facing outputs. The complaint connects training, deployment, search substitution, RAG, Microsoft's Azure infrastructure, and Copilot monetization into one ecosystem rather than treating "training" as a sealed historical event.
 That framing is relevant to § 107(4) market-harm analysis — if RAG is extracting and serving current article content at query time, the substitution argument is considerably stronger than the training-corpus argument alone, because the product competes directly with the news outlets' digital subscription and advertising revenue.

The Microsoft vicarious-liability theory is the novel edge here. 
The vicarious-liability claims against Microsoft may survive initially, because the complaint pleads Microsoft as more than an investor: it allegedly built and operated the Azure supercomputing infrastructure, integrated OpenAI models into Copilot and Bing, profited from the products, and had the ability to influence the infringing system.
 If Stein sustains those claims through motion practice — as he largely did for the Times, Daily News, and CIR plaintiffs in his March 26, 2025 MTD ruling — it creates secondary-liability exposure for Microsoft on a scale that dwarfs any single-defendant AI case to date. 
The complaint notes that OpenAI generates $2 billion in monthly revenue and was most recently valued at $852 billion.


The stay-pending-MDL posture means Richner will not advance to its own motion practice until Stein resolves the lead-track motions — most immediately the fair-use and discovery questions that have been briefed in the core author and news-publisher cases. That is strategically double-edged: it protects Richner plaintiffs from an early adverse ruling on facts they haven't developed, but it also means years of delay before any damages exposure is quantified.

### III. *Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC* — Concord I Cross-MSJ Fully Submitted; Concord II Stay Motion Resolved; Fair-Use Ruling Imminent

The music-publisher track now has two active dockets before the same judge, both managed by 
Hon. Eumi K. Lee, with Magistrate Judge Susan G. Van Keulen on referral.


**Concord I**, No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal.): 
The first-filed action has completed fact and expert discovery and is approaching summary judgment practice that will determine several threshold legal issues governing these claims.
 Per the scheduling order at Concord I Dkt. 587, 
dispositive motions were to be fully briefed by June 8, 2026.
 Briefing is complete. The cross-motions present direct fair-use questions under 17 U.S.C. § 107: 
Anthropic has asked the court to find that its use of copyrighted materials to train Claude is "transformative" fair use, comparing Claude's learning to how humans learn from reading and internalizing the themes of various works.
 
The music publishers have asked the court to rule that Anthropic infringed their copyrighted song lyrics, arguing in a motion for partial summary judgment that fair use does not excuse Anthropic's conduct because it used those lyrics to build a competing commercial product.
 The market-harm argument under § 107(4) is particularly strong here: lyrics licensing markets are established and liquid — 
several music lyrics aggregators and websites have obtained proper licenses from publishers to use copyrighted works for display purposes.
 If Claude reproduces lyrics in output and displaces those licensing revenues, that directly implicates factor four in a way that the pure-training-data cases do not.

There is an additional procedural wrinkle: 
a California federal magistrate judge partially struck an expert report filed by Anthropic in this litigation that cited a nonexistent study — an error created by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious," but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 
A Latham & Watkins associate representing Anthropic said she used Anthropic's own Claude.ai tool to help draft an expert's declaration that included an erroneous citation, but argued the error was "an honest citation mistake and not a fabrication of authority."
 This episode carries reputational weight for the AI-hallucination-in-litigation debate, but its practical effect on the MSJ record depends on whether the stricken expert report covered matters the court will need to resolve the fair-use question.

**Concord II**, No. 5:26-cv-00880 (N.D. Cal., also before Judge Lee with Magistrate Judge Van Keulen): 
filed January 28, 2026
, bringing expanded claims regarding works-in-suit not covered by Concord I. 
Anthropic moved to stay Concord II pending final resolution of the substantially similar litigation in Concord I, or in the alternative, pending resolution of the parties' cross-motions for summary judgment and any subsequent appeal.
 
The threshold legal issues — including whether Anthropic's use of lyrics as training data constitutes fair use — will determine the viability of most if not all of the publishers' claims in Concord II and could have preclusive effect.
 The stay-motion hearing was set for 
May 20, 2026, at 10:00 AM in San Jose, Courtroom 7, 4th Floor, before Judge Lee.
 No stay-ruling has been confirmed in the open docket as of this run's search; this item remains unverified and will be updated when the order posts.

The practical implication: if Judge Lee issues a fair-use ruling in Concord I before Concord II is resolved, it sets the doctrinal floor (or ceiling) for all subsequent music-publisher AI-training cases. A ruling that input-side training on lyrics is not fair use would immediately revalue the licensing market for lyrics-data for AI training — a market that currently prices at near-zero because AI companies have refused to pay.

### IV. MDL No. 3143 — NYT Track; Spoliation Exposure; OpenAI IPO as Litigation Context


*In Re: OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation*, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.), is assigned to Judge Stein with Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang on referral.
 The MDL encompasses the *New York Times*, *Authors Guild*, *Ziff Davis*, and now *Richner* tracks, among others.


The New York Daily News and the Center for Investigative Reporting, each of which had filed separate actions, were consolidated into the Times' suit before Judge Stein. On March 26, 2025, Judge Stein denied the motions to dismiss in large part, allowing the central copyright infringement claims to proceed.
 That ruling is the precedential anchor for every subsequent news-publisher case in this MDL, including Richner.

The structural risk for OpenAI and Microsoft in this MDL is the spoliation motion track. 
The court held an in-person status conference specifically regarding potential spoliation motions for the deletion of output log data on May 27, 2025.
 If output logs were deleted after litigation was reasonably anticipated, sanctions exposure under Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e) could include adverse-inference instructions at trial — which would significantly impair OpenAI's ability to argue that its models do not reproduce training-corpus content at scale.

On the commercial side, the complaint's valuation allegations are significant litigation context: 
OpenAI generates $2 billion in monthly revenue and was most recently valued at $852 billion, and in June 2026, OpenAI filed confidentially with the SEC for an initial public offering.
 An IPO filing triggers new disclosure obligations; any material litigation contingency — including the MDL exposure — must be disclosed in the S-1. The magnitude of copyright-damages exposure across all pending cases is a quantifiable contingency that OpenAI's underwriters will need to address. That dynamic increases OpenAI's near-term incentive to settle or contain the MDL, because unresolved copyright claims at the time of an IPO roadshow create pricing uncertainty.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and claims-investors in the *Bartz* settlement should log docket entry 679 and monitor CourtListener docket 69058235 for the final-approval order — once it issues, the September 25, 2026 fund installment triggers and distribution processing begins; any party whose claim was filed before March 30, 2026 is in the queue, but per-work allocation will only be calculable once the court-approved distribution plan is published by JND Legal Administration. Music-publisher and lyrics-rights clients should treat the Concord I summary-judgment ruling at No. 5:24-cv-03811 as the single most consequential near-term binary in AI copyright: a ruling against Anthropic on the input-side training theory would immediately reopen licensing negotiations across the AI-training market and provide the first judicial authority distinguishing *Bartz*'s "lawful acquisition = fair use" holding from unauthorized training on commercially licensed works. New-media plaintiffs evaluating whether to join the OpenAI MDL should note that Richner's RAG-plus-deployment theory — No. 1:26-cv-05320 — is now on hold pending the lead-track motions in No. 1:25-md-03143; joining now means riding Judge Stein's schedule, which is the most efficient posture given that MTD precedent at the SDNY already strongly favors publisher plaintiffs on the core infringement claims. Any rights-holder considering independent action against OpenAI or Microsoft outside the MDL should weigh whether the Richner consolidation signals that Stein will centralize all news-publisher claims; a stand-alone action filed in SDNY will almost certainly be tagged for MDL transfer under 28 U.S.C. § 1407.
