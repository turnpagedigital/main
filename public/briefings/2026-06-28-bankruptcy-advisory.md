### Sleep Number: $415M Stalking Horse, $260M DIP, and a July 31 Hard Close


Sleep Number Corporation filed for Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026 in the Southern District of New York, Case No. 26-11399.
 
The cases are jointly administered before Judge Kyu Young Paek.
 The filing is a pre-arranged 363 sale, not a plan reorganization: 
the debtors are pursuing a going-concern sale backstopped by a stalking horse bid from SNBR Inc., an affiliate of Sleep Country Canada Inc., at $415 million in cash plus assumption of certain liabilities, pursuant to a Stalking Horse Asset Purchase Agreement dated June 12, 2026.
 Counsel for the debtors is Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP. (__[Bondoro](https://bondoro.com/sleep-number-filing-alert/)__)

The capital structure is substantial. 
The company carries approximately $672.5 million in funded debt, comprising roughly $475 million under a revolving credit facility, $177.5 million under a term loan entered in 2021, and an additional $20 million term loan entered in 2026.
 Against that debt load, 
Sleep Number reports $642.3 million in assets and $1.3 billion in total liabilities.
 
Management explicitly warns that common shares are "significantly out of the money" and expects a complete or significant loss for shareholders, anticipating a Nasdaq delisting.


DIP financing is the operational lifeline. 
Sleep Number has obtained support from its existing lenders for up to $260 million in DIP financing, comprising up to $65 million in new-money term loans and up to $195 million of rolled-up prepetition debt, priced at SOFR plus 8% or base rate plus 7%.
 
The Bankruptcy Court entered the interim DIP order on the petition date, with a final DIP hearing currently scheduled for July 9, 2026.
 The roll-up feature merits scrutiny: creditors' committees in recent 363 cases (cf. *In re Enviva Inc.*; *In re Red Lobster Mgmt. LLC*) have challenged aggressive prepetition roll-ups as unfairly elevating secured lenders at the expense of unsecured constituents. No creditors' committee has been publicly announced in this case as of this writing.

The sale timeline is notably compressed. 
Building on a 14-week prepetition marketing process that solicited 53 strategic and financial parties — 19 of whom executed NDAs and five of whom submitted preliminary proposals — the proposed bidding procedures contemplate a compressed 26-day postpetition diligence period, a July 8 bid deadline, a July 13 auction, a July 15 sale hearing, and a closing by July 31, 2026.
 
The stalking horse carries a 3% break-up fee along with an expense reimbursement cap of $4 million.
 The bidding-procedures hearing is set for 
July 2, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. ET.
 
The DIP facility imposes binding sale milestones that constitute events of default if missed: the Interim Order must be entered within three days of the petition date, the Final Order within 30 days, bidding procedures approved within 28 days, and if qualified bids are received, auction by July 13, sale order by July 15, and close by July 31. These milestones run concurrently with the committee challenge deadline, compressing the effective investigation window.


The bankruptcy attributable cause is tariff exposure. 
Sleep Number blamed its bankruptcy, in part, on "the unpredictable shifting of trade rules imposed by the current U.S. government, on top of an already vulnerable global supply chain."
 
The company entered bankruptcy with $672 million in debt, citing tariffs and inflation as contributing factors.
 
Sleep Number indicated it had already been reviewing its store footprint and filed a motion to reject leases in 44 non-operational locations, with A&G Real Estate Partners assisting.
 Landlords and counterparties with executory contracts face an aggressive rejection posture on a sub-30-day timeline.

For distressed investors: the $415 million stalking horse bid by Sleep Country Canada (
SNBR, Inc., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sleep Country Canada Inc.
) is the floor. 
The asset sale is subject to higher or otherwise better offers from competing bidders at auction.
 Whether any strategic or financial sponsor can conduct meaningful diligence on a 26-day clock — on a 572-store retail footprint — is the live question. The Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period is an explicit closing condition per the SEC 8-K, adding regulatory friction to the July 31 close target. (__[SEC Form 8-K, June 12, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000827187/000095010326008891/dp248362_8k.htm)__)

### BlockFills: Post-Sale Hearing Activity, Governmental Bar Date, and the Customer Property Question


Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc. and three affiliated entities — operating as BlockFills — filed Chapter 11 on March 15, 2026 in the Bankr. D. Del., Case No. 26-10371 (TMH).
 The case has moved from an open dual-track restructuring to a completed auction. 
Keyrock agreed to a purchase price of $3.25 million and will assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets, certain liabilities, some equity interests, customer lists, and proprietary technology and IP, with Keyrock SA declared the "Successful Bidder" per a bankruptcy court document filed May 26, 2026.



The hearing to consider approval of the sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026.
 The docket shows seven new entries filed in the 72 hours ending June 26 — the most recent activity as of the Veritaglobal claims administrator data — consistent with order entry, notice obligations, and post-hearing implementation. No confirmed public report of the sale order's entry has been retrieved this run; treat the sale approval as likely but unverified pending CourtListener docket confirmation.

The valuation gap is stark. 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% year-over-year increase, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 Against that operational scale, 
the debtors report approximately $145 million in general unsecured obligations.
 The $3.25 million sale price implies recovery to unsecured creditors is primarily a function of the liquidating trust — not the 363 sale proceeds. 
The filing indicates that there will be funds available for distribution to unsecured creditors,
 but the magnitude of that distribution depends heavily on resolution of the customer-property dispute.

The customer-property question remains the most consequential legal issue in the case. 
The central question of whether customer digital assets constitute estate property remains contested
 — a question that will govern whether customers recover assets as property owners or pro rata as unsecured creditors. This mirrors the framework litigated in *In re Celsius Network LLC* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10964) and *In re Voyager Digital Holdings* (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Case No. 22-10943), where court rulings on custody agreement language and applicable UCC Article 8 / Article 9 principles drove materially different recovery outcomes across customer classes. Judge Horan has not issued a published ruling on this question as of this writing; the ad hoc customer group's plan framework anticipated a NewCo solution that would sidestep a binary estate-property ruling, but it is unclear whether the Keyrock 363 track moots or preserves that dispute.

Pre-petition litigation provides context on how BlockFills arrived at Chapter 11. 
In *Bertram v. Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-cv-02451 (N.D. Ill. Mar. 5, 2026), plaintiffs sought emergency injunctive relief and obtained TROs that significantly restricted the debtors' ability to conduct business and exercise control over certain assets, creating substantial operational and liquidity challenges.
 A parallel SDNY action preceded it. 
The company attributes its liquidity crisis to cascading counterparty defaults — including an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, compounded by stranded capital from the failed 2022 mining hardware deployment.


The governmental bar date is September 11, 2026 at 4:00 p.m. ET. (__[Veritaglobal — BlockFills Key Dates](https://veritaglobal.net/BlockFills)__) Claims-trading activity (Rule 3001 transfer notices) will be the signal to watch as the case moves toward plan confirmation and liquidating trust distributions. As of the current docket snapshot, no Rule 3001 transfer activity has been publicly reported.

### Carry-Forward: Macro Themes in Active Restructurings

**Tariff-driven retail distress.** Sleep Number is the latest in a string of retail debtors — following *In re Joann Inc.*, *In re Big Lots Inc.*, and *In re Spirit Airlines* — to cite trade-policy disruption as a proximate cause of filing. The pattern suggests that IEEPA tariff uncertainty, particularly on Asia-Pacific manufactured goods, is compressing operating margins for inventory-heavy retailers faster than traditional financial distress cycles. For distressed investors, the relevant question is whether the stalking horse price in any given 363 process is pricing the tariff overhang as a terminal cost or a manageable risk under a successor operator.

**Compressed 363 timelines and committee rights.** Sleep Number's 26-day postpetition marketing window — on top of a 14-week prepetition process — continues a trend toward sale processes that effectively foreclose meaningful committee investigation. 
The milestones run concurrently with the challenge deadline, compressing the effective window for any committee investigation.
 Where no committee is appointed, unsecured creditors' only meaningful leverage point is the auction itself and the break-up fee structure. Sleep Number's 
3% break-up fee and $4 million expense reimbursement cap
 are at the lower end of market for a deal of this size, which marginally improves the economics for a competing bidder.

**Crypto estate-property disputes as a recurring circuit issue.** The BlockFills customer-property question joins a body of unresolved law across Delaware and the Southern District of New York. No circuit-level opinion has authoritatively resolved whether digital asset custody arrangements — particularly those structured as brokerage rather than custodial accounts — vest title in the customer or the estate. Until a circuit court speaks, each case turns on the specific custody agreement, jurisdiction, and applicable UCC treatment of digital assets, creating valuation uncertainty for claims buyers in every institutional crypto insolvency.

## Recommended Actions

Holders of Sleep Number general unsecured claims and landlord rejection claims should monitor the July 2 bidding-procedures hearing closely: if Judge Paek approves the procedures as filed, the July 8 bid deadline becomes binding and the window for any alternate buyer to surface closes fast; counsel should evaluate whether to file a bid-procedures objection on adequacy-of-notice and committee-formation grounds before July 1, while simultaneously engaging with A&G Real Estate Partners on lease treatment under § 365 — the 44 pending lease rejections carry cure-cost and administrative-claim implications that landlords must preserve. BlockFills claim-holders should confirm their proofs of claim are filed ahead of the September 11, 2026 governmental bar date, scrutinize any Rule 3001 transfer notices for indicative market pricing, and — if customer-class positioning is unclear — seek standing clarification before the liquidating trust instrument is finalized, since the resolution of the customer-property dispute will bifurcate recoveries between property-return and pro-rata-distribution tracks in ways that, historically, produce 30-to-70-point recovery spreads in comparable crypto insolvencies.
