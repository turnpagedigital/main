### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Eighteenth Motion Watch

The Madoff liquidation, *Securities Investor Protection Corp. v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789, remains the structural benchmark for all other Ponzi recoveries. 
On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard commenced the Seventeenth pro rata interim distribution, sending more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
The Seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim, paid on claims relating to 768 accounts; combined with the prior sixteen distributions, approximately 72.848% of each customer's allowed claim has now been paid, unless that claim has been fully satisfied.


The aggregate recovery posture has materially strengthened. 
As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation in advances as of that date.
 
SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund for advances paid on fully satisfied accounts, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.


The fee-application track is the next near-term event to watch. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the Bankruptcy Court for the S.D.N.Y., covering August 1 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval for approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour, with a 10% public interest discount applied.
 No ruling date has been publicly published on that application as of this writing; any order on the 50th fee application is a signal event for claims pricing, as it establishes the remaining administrative expense run-rate against the recoverable pool. The 51st application covering December 2025 through March 2026 has not yet been publicly noticed.

At 72.848 cents on the dollar distributed and $15.456 billion recovered, the Picard estate is approaching terminal recovery. The residual litigation pool — primarily offshore feeder fund disputes and 11 U.S.C. § 548 clawback actions against net winners — drives the remaining upside. Claims trading in the secondary market should price the time-discount on an eighteenth distribution carefully: absent a headline settlement, the next motion is unlikely before Q4 2026.

### Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Imminent Void Dates; Fee Appeal at Fifth Circuit

The Stanford receivership, *SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, No. 3:09-CV-0298-N (N.D. Tex., Dallas Div., Judge David C. Godbey), is the most operationally urgent matter this cycle.


Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash those checks by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash those checks by July 26, 2026; checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after the applicable deadline.
 Those void dates are four and seven days away, respectively. 
The receiver recently contacted by email certain claimants whose final distribution checks remain uncashed because those checks are approaching the deadline to be cashed; checks will become void and the funds represented by those checks will revert to the receivership if not cashed timely.



On March 9, 2026, the receiver filed with the N.D. Tex. the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan, in accordance with the October 8, 2025 Order Approving Receiver's Final Distribution Plan and the November 25, 2025 Order Approving Joint Motion to Amend (together, the "Final Distribution Orders").
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 
Payments under the Final Distribution Plan began on April 3, 2026.


The fee dispute is the most significant unresolved structural issue. 
The Court entered an order on October 8, 2025 approving the receiver's proposed final distribution and, among other matters, awarded the receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the receiver and certain of his professional firms appealed that order to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals.
 
The receiver and certain professional firms filed their brief on appeal on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting also filed a separate brief in support of the appeal.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the Court's order on May 23, 2026.
 The Fifth Circuit appeal — not publicly assigned a case number in the materials reviewed — pits the receiver's argument that $43 million in fees is proportionate against the district court's view that $14.9 million is adequate for a case of this tenure. The outcome does not affect individual claimant distributions (which proceed from the final plan), but it does determine how roughly $28 million of receivership estate value is allocated between fee claimants and the residual victim pool.


On July 9, 2026, the receiver submitted with the N.D. Tex. a Status Report regarding Payments Issued Pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 That July 9 filing is the most recent docket activity; the substance of the status report was not retrieved in full, but its existence confirms active distribution mechanics are still in process.

The Venezuelan-address claimant issue also remains open. 
The receivership site reflects an important update regarding distribution payments to claimants with Venezuela addresses, last updated March 11, 2026.
 Cross-border distribution to Venezuelan claimants implicates OFAC sanctions compliance and local banking access constraints — a chronic obstacle that has deferred material payments to that cohort throughout the proceedings.

A separate cross-border asset thread involves $8.1 million in Swiss funds released to the receiver on December 17, 2025. 
COViSAL sent a formal written request to the receiver and his attorneys on March 11, 2026, styled as a "Second Request: Status of $8.1 Million Swiss Funds (Stanford Receivership)," asking whether and when those funds will be distributed; as of the date of that filing, the receiver and his legal team had not responded.
 That $8.1 million sits outside the Final Distribution Schedule; its disposition — whether it will be added to a supplemental distribution or absorbed into administrative costs — is unresolved.

**Practical note for claims holders and claims investors:** the July 23/26 check-void deadlines are hard. Any claimant with an uncashed April 2026 check who has not yet presented it to their bank should treat this as an emergency-level action item. Reverted funds return to the receivership estate and would require a further court-approved re-distribution motion to recover.

### OneCoin / DOJ Remission — Petition Window Closed; Administrator Review Phase Begins

The OneCoin DOJ remission process has crossed its filing deadline. 
The Department of Justice announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the international investment scheme involving OneCoin Ltd., perpetrated between 2014 and 2019 by co-founders Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood.
 
Victims invested over $4 billion worldwide in the fraudulent cryptocurrency.
 
The deadline for filing a petition was June 30; the Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section manages the remission compensation process.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC is serving as the remission administrator.



The DOJ launched the remission process with over $40 million in forfeited assets available for distribution.
 That figure is deeply asymmetric relative to the $4 billion in total investor losses — a recovery ratio below 1%. The gap reflects the structural limitation of the DOJ remission process: it distributes only assets successfully forfeited in completed prosecutions, not the totality of scheme proceeds. Ignatova herself has never been apprehended, which means billions in assets attributed to her have not been seized.

On the international asset recovery front, a meaningful precedent was established in January 2026. 
Following international legal cooperation between Guernsey and Germany, on January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds to the sum of GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The German authorities had initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings" — a non-conviction-based forfeiture mechanism — which led to the overseas forfeiture order applying to the remaining proceeds of sale of two London flats.
 
The Royal Court's action followed a mutual legal assistance request from prosecutors in Bielefeld, Germany, demonstrating that even without apprehending the principal defendant, their financial footprint can be systematically recovered.


For sophisticated claims investors, the structural reality of the OneCoin remission is as follows: Kroll will review petitions filed before June 30, apply a pro rata methodology against the $40 million pool, and issue awards over a timeline that typically runs 12 to 24 months after petition close. The sub-1% recovery ratio and Ignatova's fugitive status mean secondary-market claims trading here is speculative at best. The incremental value is in the non-conviction forfeiture precedent — Bielefeld → Guernsey is a replicable template — but future asset seizures depend entirely on law enforcement's ability to identify and freeze additional Ignatova-linked accounts before she or intermediaries dissipate them.

### SEC Enforcement Posture — Structural Context for New Receivership Formation

The broader SEC enforcement environment shapes the pipeline of new receivership appointments, which are the earliest-stage entry points for claims investors. 
The Commission reported filing 456 enforcement actions in FY 2025, a significant decline from the 583 actions brought in FY 2024; according to the release, this reduction was driven by a deliberate refocus away from cases that do not meaningfully advance investor protection or market integrity.
 
David Woodcock was announced as Director of the Division of Enforcement on April 8, 2026, replacing Acting Director Sam Waldon effective May 4, 2026.
 Woodcock comes from Gibson Dunn and previously ran the Fort Worth Regional Office — a regional office with significant securities fraud enforcement exposure, given its jurisdiction over Texas-based promoters like Stanford.

The practical implication for the receivership pipeline: 
the SEC underscored its continued objective to return funds to harmed investors and combat fraud wherever it occurs, citing in particular its Cross-Border Task Force dedicated to addressing the threat that fraudsters located abroad pose to U.S. investors.
 The Cross-Border Task Force is the most relevant structural initiative for the Ponzi/fraud-recovery desk — it is the mechanism most likely to generate new receivership appointments in cross-border crypto and offshore-promoter cases.

## Recommended Actions

Claims holders and claims investors should treat the Stanford final-distribution check-void deadlines of July 23 and July 26 as the single most time-sensitive operational matter this week — any holder of an uncashed April 2026 check must present it before those dates or accept permanent forfeiture of that distribution tranche, with no clear pathway to recovery absent a new court-approved motion. Separately, Stanford-position holders should monitor the Fifth Circuit fee appeal (receiver vs. SEC, briefing completed May 23, 2026): if the court materially increases the fee award above the district court's $14.9 million, the residual estate available for any supplemental distribution — including the unresolved $8.1 million Swiss funds — will be reduced accordingly; conversely, affirmance leaves that buffer intact. On BLMIS, the 50th BakerHostetler fee application is the next pricing signal; claims buyers should not assume an eighteenth distribution motion materializes before Q4 2026, and should discount accordingly. OneCoin secondary-market positions should be priced against the sub-1% recovery ratio from the $40 million DOJ pool against $4 billion in losses — the Guernsey/Bielefeld non-conviction forfeiture is an additive thread but the quantum and timeline are entirely speculative at this stage. Power Block Coin (No. 24-23041, Bankr. D. Utah) warrants continued docket surveillance for adversary proceedings or § 548 clawback activity but does not require action this cycle.
