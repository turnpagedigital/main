### Bartz v. Anthropic, No. 3:24-cv-05417 — Final Approval Still Pending; Fee Cut, Installment Calendar, and Late Opt-Out Dispute Drive Post-Hearing Docket


At a May 14 hearing in a San Francisco courtroom, presiding Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín declined, for now, to approve the $1.5 billion settlement in *Bartz v. Anthropic*
 — the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history. 
The settlement fund must cover attorneys' fees (originally sought at 25% of the fund, or $375 million), administrative costs, service awards of $50,000 per named plaintiff, and Special Master fees.
 That fee request has since been revised downward: 
authors urged the court to grant final approval along with an attorney fee request revised down from $300 million to $187.5 million, arguing the deal is fair despite multiple objections.
 
Separately, Anthropic contends the $75 million in fees that non-class counsel requested is far too high, arguing there is "scant justification" for the request.


The May 14 hearing consumed 75 minutes and drew seven objectors, each allotted two minutes. 
Lead plaintiff's counsel Justin Nelson reported the opt-in rate had moved from 91.3% the prior month to 92.77%.
 
Class counsel had earlier filed papers showing that 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works were claimed — a 91.3% rate — vastly surpassing the 54% rate reported in the Motion for Final Approval filed March 19, 2026, eleven days before the March 30 claims deadline.
 
After deducting approximately $208.6 million in fees, costs, and service awards from the $1.5 billion fund, a net settlement fund of roughly $1.29 billion remains, yielding an estimated base payout of approximately $2,931 per work
 — with 
the final payout likely slightly higher once interest accruing on the escrowed funds is factored in.


Following the hearing, Judge Martínez-Olguín issued a post-hearing order. 
She directed that by May 21, 2026, Anthropic may file a supplemental brief of no more than two pages addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored, and that plaintiffs shall file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections.
 The most recent docket activity per the tracked-case ground truth is Docket Entry 679, filed July 13, 2026 — Anthropic's opposition to the objections brief. Final approval has not yet issued as of this advisory.

**Installment calendar.** 
Anthropic will fund the settlement in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 
The first $300 million has already been deposited in an interest-bearing escrow account.
 The September 25, 2026 installment of $450 million is triggered twelve months after the preliminary approval order and does not require a final approval order as a condition — but 
payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved, with payments likely commencing in the late fall at the earliest.


**Fee dispute and structural watch.** 
Martínez-Olguín's questions at the May 14 hearing focused on attorneys' fees and the structure of the cost reserve, rather than the merits of the deal itself.
 
The court has already signaled that class members cannot "seek a sweeter deal than other class members," rejecting an approach by a Canadian publisher's counsel that had contacted Anthropic about improved terms.
 The fee reduction from $375M to $187.5M — if approved at the lower figure — increases the net fund available for per-work distribution and could push per-work recoveries modestly above the $2,931 baseline. Recovery counsel tracking tradeable claims should model both fee outcomes.

### In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation, MDL 2406, No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP (N.D. Ala.) — First Distribution Wave Active; Rolling Payments Through 2026–2027


The initial distribution of payments to Damages Class Members with valid claims began starting May 11, 2026.
 
A court order directed payments to begin during the week of May 11, 2026; first waves are issuing to approved claimants by virtual prepaid card, paper check, and other methods selected during the claim process.



The total subscriber settlement fund is $2.67 billion; after court-approved attorney fees of 25% (approximately $667.5 million) and administration costs (roughly $100 million), approximately $1.9 billion remains for distribution to roughly six million claimants.
 
Payouts average $333; only those who filed by the November 2021 deadline are eligible.
 
Combined with a separate $2.8 billion provider settlement that received final approval on August 19, 2025, the total BCBS antitrust resolution exceeds $5.47 billion.



The initial round of payments from the $2.67 billion settlement fund began distribution in May 2026, approximately 13 years after the cases were consolidated as MDL 2406 in 2013.
 The claims window closed in November 2021 — no new subscriber claims are being accepted. The distribution is purely administrative at this stage: claimants who filed timely and have not yet received payment should verify contact information with JND Legal Administration (888-681-1142; info@BCBSsettlement.com) and check their spam folders.

### In re Payment Card Interchange Fee Antitrust Litigation, MDL 1720 (E.D.N.Y., Judge Brian Cogan) — Second Distribution Approved; September 2026 Payment Wave Scheduled; New Post-Release Class Action Filed


On May 26, 2026, Class Counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds in *In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation*, MDL No. 1720.
 
On June 15, 2026, the court approved the motion, finding that the proposed distribution is in the best interests of eligible claimants; the second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026.
 
Visa, Mastercard, and the bank defendants agreed to provide a settlement fund of $5.54 billion to merchants that did not exclude themselves from the class.


The injunctive relief track has a materially different posture. 
U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan of the Eastern District of New York granted preliminary approval of a revised $38 billion settlement covering approximately 12 million merchants that were party to the class action.
 
If finalized, the deal would reduce average U.S. credit interchange rates by 10 basis points for five years.
 
This newer settlement making headlines in June 2026 is entirely different from the $5.54 billion cash fund; the $38 billion figure represents cumulative merchant savings on interchange rates over a five-year period, not a fund for cash payouts.


A new damages action has already emerged. 
On April 21, 2026, *Potayto-Potahto, LLC* and two other merchants filed a putative class action in the S.D.N.Y. against Visa, Mastercard, and bank defendants, on behalf of merchants that have accepted Visa and/or Mastercard credit cards since January 25, 2019, and seeks damages from that date.
 
In April 2026, a putative class was also filed seeking damages related to interchange fees since January 2019, with named plaintiffs concurrently filing a motion for summary judgment seeking a declaration that the release from the Damages Class Settlement Agreement does not bar their claims.
 
Briefing on this motion has not yet been scheduled.
 This post-release litigation is the clearest signal that merchant-side counsel views the 2023-final damages settlement's prospective release as vulnerable.

### Keller Postman / Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — $218B Estimated Exposure; AAA Fee-Lever Mechanics in Play


Following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic and anticompetitive conduct, Keller Postman is representing U.S. businesses in a coordinated mass arbitration seeking recovery for alleged advertising overcharges
 — formally announced May 11, 2026. 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) have ruled in favor of the U.S. Department of Justice, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in its advertising business.



The $218 billion figure is an estimate, not a filed demand amount; in mass arbitration, the leverage comes from the volume of individual filings and the per-claim fee obligations they trigger — not from any single damages number.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade, and experts in the federal antitrust cases have estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs.


The fee-lever mechanics are the engine of this campaign. 
For each individual claim, Google is typically required to pay the arbitration forum's per-case initiation fees, which at AAA's current rates run into the thousands of dollars per case; at the scale Keller Postman is assembling — potentially thousands or tens of thousands of advertiser claimants — the fee exposure alone creates enormous settlement pressure before a single arbitration hearing occurs.
 
This is the same fee leverage model that drove Uber to settle 60,000 arbitration demands for between $146 million and $170 million, forced DoorDash to pay $9.5 million just to initiate proceedings for 5,000 workers, and caused Amazon to abandon its mandatory arbitration clause entirely rather than face the economics of 75,000 individual arbitrations.
 
The Google campaign has one structural advantage those earlier campaigns lacked: established liability.



Potential recovery is estimated at up to 30% of total Google ad spend, with treble damages available under federal antitrust law.
 
In addition to representing the private claimants in the arbitration, Keller Postman represents seven state AGs in a parallel case.
 
Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling in the ad-tech case is expected in 2026; Keller Postman is actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines.
 That remedies ruling — which may include AdX divestiture or behavioral remedies only — will directly shape the damages framing available to arbitration claimants. Businesses that purchased Google Ads on or after August 2016 are within the claimed class period; state statutes of limitations are the binding constraint on claim timing.


The broader mass arbitration landscape saw the 9th Circuit uphold JAMS consolidation of 7,300 Starz claims in *Jones v. Starz Entertainment*, and the 9th Circuit also found a bellwether provision unconscionable where non-participants were bound without notice in *Pandolfi v. AviaGames* (Aug. 2025).
 
AAA closed 26 mass arbitrations with 37,648+ claimants in H1 2025 alone
 — a pace consistent with the forum's trajectory toward becoming the primary venue for mega-campaign resolution.

### Visa/Mastercard Injunctive Relief Settlement — Preliminary Approval in Hand; Merchant Objector Risk Remains


Judge Cogan granted preliminary approval of the revised $38 billion injunctive relief settlement
, finding that the agreement "provides more extensive relief" than the prior settlement he rejected in June 2024. 
Cogan wrote that the court's responsibility "is not to determine whether the settlement is ideal by class members' varying standards, but whether it is fair, reasonable, and adequate."
 NACS (National Association of Convenience Stores) has indicated it will appeal to the Second Circuit if final approval is granted. Final approval proceedings require a notice-and-comment period for the approximately 12 million merchant class members. The injunctive relief track and the $5.54B damages track are legally distinct; merchants who opted out of the damages class are not covered by the injunctive release.

## Recommended Actions

Rights-holders and recovery counsel should take three concrete steps before August 1. First, *Bartz* claimants — particularly institutional publishers holding multiple claimed works — should monitor the N.D. Cal. docket (CourtListener ID 69058235) for the final approval order; the moment that order issues, the distribution timeline under the Settlement Agreement's installment schedule is triggered, and the September 25, 2026 $450M Anthropic payment becomes the relevant cash-flow anchor. Second, merchants in MDL 1720 who filed timely claims should confirm their payment instructions are current with the settlement administrator ahead of the September 2026 second distribution; the June 15 court order authorizing that distribution did not extend the claims window, and the denominator is fixed. Third, any business that spent materially on Google advertising since August 2016 should evaluate the Keller Postman AAA campaign against applicable state statutes of limitations before Brinkema's remedies ruling issues — because that ruling, whenever it arrives, will either strengthen or complicate the damages floor underlying each arbitration demand; filing ahead of the ruling preserves maximum optionality under the FAA framework and Clayton Act § 4's treble-damages multiplier.
