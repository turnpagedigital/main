### Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:24-cv-05417-AMO (N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín) — Distribution Clock Begins


On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the N.D. Cal. granted final approval, concluding the largest copyright class action settlement in history.
 That order is now the operative document for every distribution-timeline calculation.

**Fee award.** 
The court approved the settlement but reduced the attorneys' fee award from the requested $187.5 million to $101,561,111, applying a multiplier of 3.75 against class counsel's lodestar of approximately $27.08 million rather than the 6.92x multiplier sought.
 
The court will withhold 10 percent of that fee award until the parties file a post-distribution accounting in accordance with the district's Procedural Guidance for Class Action Settlements.


**Service awards and opt-outs.** 
The court trimmed service awards for the three class representatives — Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson — from the requested $50,000 to $15,000 each.
 
The court disallowed opt-outs submitted after the February 9, 2026 deadline, with narrow exceptions for two late requests (Esquivel and Castells), which were allowed on the facts presented.
 
Class counsel reported a 91.3% claims rate and 350 valid opt-outs representing 1,802 works, or 0.03% of the total number of works in the settlement corpus.


**Effective Date and payment mechanics.** 
When class members will actually be paid is now the most pressing practical question; under the Settlement Agreement, payments begin one business day after the "Effective Date," defined as the date on which the time to file or notice an appeal of the Final Judgment expires.
 That appeal window has been running since July 20. No notice of appeal has been publicly reported as of this writing; the docket shows zero new entries in the past 72 hours. 
The Authors Guild has stated it looks forward to the commencement of the distribution process but acknowledged that a specific date for the commencement of payouts has not been announced.


**Release scope reminder for opt-out and parallel-action counsel.** 
The settlement grants a past-only release for conduct up to August 25, 2025, and explicitly does not release claims related to AI outputs or future conduct.
 
The court declined to entertain objections from non-class members but took care to note that those individuals remain free to bring their own claims precisely because the settlement does not cover them.
 Rights-holders who opted out and counsel pursuing output-side claims should treat both points as operative scope boundaries.

### Concord Music Group, Inc. et al. v. Anthropic PBC, No. 5:24-cv-03811-EKL (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee) — Second Amended Complaint Filed; Summary Judgment Hearing Reset to October 21

**Second Amended Complaint — July 22, 2026.** 
Concord Music Group, Universal Music Publishing Group, and ABKCO Music filed a Second Amended Complaint on July 22 in the N.D. Cal. — an expanded version of the amended complaint first put forward in May — updating the original October 2023 suit, which covers approximately 500 songs.
 
The publishers have narrowed the case to direct infringement and alleged removal of copyright management information (CMI), dropping earlier contributory and vicarious infringement claims.
 The filing lands two days after the Bartz final approval order, a sequencing that is unlikely to be coincidental from a discovery-and-licensing-comparator standpoint: counsel will now be able to point to the $1.5 billion Bartz settlement as a § 107(4) market-harm data point during summary judgment briefing.

**Summary judgment schedule.** 
Following a reset of deadlines, the response brief is due August 17, 2026, the reply is due September 8, 2026, and the summary judgment motion hearing is set for October 21, 2026 at 10:00 AM in San Jose, Courtroom 7, 4th Floor, before Judge Lee.
 The questions presented are cross-motions: 
whether partial summary judgment on copyright infringement should be granted to plaintiffs, or whether summary judgment on fair use and non-infringement should be granted to Anthropic.


**Amicus activity.** 
The Chamber of Progress and Engine Advocacy have moved for leave to file an amicus brief in support of Anthropic's motion for partial summary judgment and in opposition to the publishers' motion; Copyright Law Professors have separately moved to file an amicus brief.
 The competing amicus posture signals that the October 21 hearing is shaping up as the most consequential fair-use ruling on AI training-data conduct not yet resolved by settlement — the first case where a federal court may actually rule on the § 107 merits for music-publisher claims.

**Parallel Concord II suit.** 
A second, separate suit filed in January 2026 covers more than 20,000 songs and seeks over $3 billion over Anthropic's alleged torrenting of lyrics from pirate "shadow" libraries.
 
On April 24, 2026, the court denied Anthropic's motion to stay that case pending Concord I; Anthropic's motion to dismiss or answer was due August 3, 2026.
 Whether that filing has landed is unconfirmed as of press time — watch the docket at CourtListener No. 5:26-cv-00880-EKL.

**Partially-stricken expert report.** For context on the evidentiary posture entering the August 17 response deadline: 
a California federal magistrate judge previously partially struck an expert report filed by Anthropic that cited a nonexistent study — an error generated by Anthropic's own Claude AI tool — calling the issue "serious," but "not quite so grave as it first appeared."
 That ruling adds a credibility overhang to Anthropic's expert submissions as the summary judgment record closes.
