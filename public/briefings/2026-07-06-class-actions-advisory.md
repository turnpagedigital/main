### I. Bartz v. Anthropic (No. 3:24-cv-05417, N.D. Cal.) — Awaiting Final Approval Order


On May 14, 2026, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín of the Northern District of California held the fairness hearing in *Bartz v. Anthropic* to determine the fairness of the proposed $1.5 billion settlement.
 
Judge Martínez-Olguín took the matter under submission rather than ruling from the bench, and indicated that short orders may follow.
 The court has not entered a final approval order as of the date of this briefing; the docket's last-filed entry is July 1, 2026 (CourtListener Docket No. 69058235), with zero new filings in the prior 72 hours.

The submission record before the court is unusually dense. 
Per the order following the May 14 hearing, Anthropic was directed to file a supplemental brief by May 21, 2026 addressing why late opt-outs should not be honored, and plaintiffs were directed to file a revised proposed order substantively addressing objections, with a redline to the proposed order filed April 30, 2026.
 
Seven objectors appeared and each spoke for approximately two minutes (with attorney James Bartolomei receiving five minutes on behalf of several objectors).


On the economics: 
class counsel filed papers showing that 440,490 of the 482,460 eligible works had been claimed — a 91.3 percent rate — vastly surpassing the 54 percent rate reported in counsel's Motion for Final Approval filed March 19, 2026, eleven days before the March 30, 2026 claims deadline.
 
Class counsel Justin Nelson stated at the hearing that the payout per work in the class will be approximately $3,100.
 
Plaintiffs' counsel are seeking 25 percent of the fund — $375 million — with a $50,000 service award per named plaintiff ($150,000 total for the three class representatives), and JND Legal Administration handling settlement administration.



Anthropic's funding schedule runs in four installments: October 2, 2025; April 30, 2026; September 25, 2026; and September 25, 2027.
 Two installments have already passed; the September 25, 2026 tranche is the next payment event and will only be triggered if final approval has been entered and any appellate stay motions resolved. 
Payouts to class members will only be issued after the judge grants final approval and any subsequent appeals are fully resolved.


The structural read on this case has not changed: the 91.3 percent claims rate is extraordinary relative to the ~10 percent industry baseline, which means the denominator math compressed per-claim recovery back toward the original $3,000/work floor rather than the elevated projections circulating at the 54 percent interim rate. Rights-holders who filed and are waiting for the order should account for the appellate exposure window — any objector who appeared at the May 14 hearing is preserved for appeal under FRCP 23(e)(5).

### II. In re Blue Cross Blue Shield Antitrust Litigation (MDL No. 2406, Master File 2:13-cv-20000-RDP, N.D. Ala.) — Distribution Underway; Provider Track Separately Active

The subscriber track has reached active distribution. 
The initial distribution of payments to Damages Class Members with valid claims began on May 11, 2026 under Master File No. 2:13-cv-20000-RDP.
 
Approximately $1.9 billion will be distributed to roughly six million claimants, with individual payouts varying based on coverage history and premiums paid.
 
Payment amounts depend on length of coverage, premiums paid, and plan type, with estimates averaging around $300 but ranging significantly.


The provider track is on a separate and somewhat more advanced trajectory. 
The U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Alabama, Southern Division granted final approval of the provider settlement on August 19, 2025, and the Settlement became effective on September 19, 2025.
 
The provider settlement is comprised of a $2.8 billion cash payment plus injunctive relief; economists have valued the long-term benefits of the injunctive relief at a minimum of $17.3 billion, with average administrative cost savings of $7.55 per BlueCard claim.
 
The Settlement Claims Administrator began issuing claim notices on February 16, 2026.


For claimants on both tracks, the practical issue is now verification and disbursement monitoring rather than claims filing — subscriber deadlines closed November 5, 2021. The ongoing distribution of the subscriber fund through waves (prepaid card, paper check, direct deposit) means individual claimants should confirm payment method registration against the official portal at bcbssettlement.com before the wave cycle completes.

### III. In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation (MDL No. 1720, E.D.N.Y.) — Second Initial Distribution Approved for September 2026

The Payment Card MDL is the most active large-dollar distribution matter this month. 
On May 26, 2026, Class Counsel filed a motion requesting court approval of a second initial distribution of settlement funds; on June 15, 2026, the court approved the motion, finding that the proposed distribution is in the best interests of eligible claimants while protecting those whose claims are not yet ready for distribution. The second initial distribution is anticipated to occur in September 2026.



Visa, Mastercard, and the Bank Defendants have agreed to provide a settlement fund of $5.54 billion to merchants that did not exclude themselves from the Settlement Class.
 
The settlement covers Visa and Mastercard payments accepted between January 1, 2004 and January 25, 2019.
 
U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan of the Eastern District of New York granted preliminary approval of the current agreement, noting it provides "more extensive relief" than a prior settlement the court rejected in June 2024; the pact covers approximately 12 million merchants.


Visa's own SEC filings confirm the broader litigation trajectory. 
Between October 1, 2023 and March 31, 2026, Visa made payments from its U.S. litigation escrow account of approximately $4.2 billion for settlements of U.S. covered litigation, reducing by more than 50 percent the estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims for damages.
 
The current U.S. litigation escrow account balance is approximately $650 million as of the most recent SEC filing.


The September 2026 distribution window is now the operative planning horizon for merchant claimants and their counsel. Claims included in this distribution are limited to the population detailed in Class Counsel's Motion for Second Initial Distribution; claimants with complex or disputed claim files may be deferred to a subsequent tranche.

### IV. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — ~$218B Claimed; TOS Modification Creates July 31 Opt-Out Deadline

This campaign remains the most structurally significant mass-arbitration development in the current cycle, and a new TOS wrinkle sharpens the timeline. 
Keller Postman's Ashley Keller is filing mass arbitration claims against Google/Alphabet on behalf of advertisers, with potential claims estimated at $218 billion — a figure based on calculations from an economist the firm retained, covering damages from both the search monopoly and the ad tech monopoly established by two separate federal court rulings.



Following two landmark federal antitrust rulings finding that Google engaged in monopolistic and anticompetitive conduct, Keller Postman is representing U.S. businesses in a coordinated mass arbitration seeking recovery for alleged advertising overcharges.
 
Federal judges in both the District of Columbia (Judge Amit P. Mehta) and the Eastern District of Virginia (Judge Leonie M. Brinkema) have ruled in favor of the U.S. Department of Justice, finding Google liable for anticompetitive conduct in its advertising business.
 
According to Alphabet's public filings, U.S. businesses spent roughly $728 billion on Google search and display ads over the past decade; experts in the federal antitrust cases have estimated that anticompetitive conduct may have significantly inflated those costs.



Due to contractual arbitration provisions implemented by Google, these claims are proceeding through individual arbitration rather than class action litigation; Keller Postman is coordinating thousands of individual claims on behalf of businesses nationwide, allowing each claimant to pursue recovery based on its own advertising spend.
 
Federal antitrust law permits treble damages, meaning a 10 percent overcharge claim could translate into a recovery of up to 30 percent of the advertiser's ad spend.


The July 1 TOS modification is a material development for this campaign. 
Google updated its Ads Terms of Service for the first time since 2018; the updated terms took effect July 1, 2026.
 
Certain actions, such as opting out of arbitration, must be taken within a specific window after July 1.
 
The 30-day period to opt out begins on July 1 when the terms are in force
 — meaning the opt-out window closes July 31, 2026. Advertisers who are weighing arbitration participation or who want to preserve court access need to act before that date. The interaction between the new TOS and existing Keller Postman retainer agreements is unverified at the time of this briefing and warrants direct inquiry with counsel.


Keller told Bloomberg in April 2026 that he had already signed up a "significant number" of advertisers and expected to file the first claims that week.
 
Judge Brinkema's remedies ruling in the ad tech case remains pending; Keller Postman is actively filing advertiser compensation claims ahead of state filing deadlines.
 The state-law statute of limitations question is non-trivial: antitrust claims under Clayton Act § 4 carry a four-year federal limitations period, but state-law overcharge theories vary, and Keller Postman has flagged that earlier filing increases the probability of a successful claim within applicable periods. Claimants that purchased Google Ads on or after August 2016 qualify for consideration.
