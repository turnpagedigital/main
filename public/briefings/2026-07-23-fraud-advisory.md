## Analysis & Developments

The three dominant distribution machines in the fraud-recovery universe each moved — or failed to move — in ways that carry direct pricing implications for secondary claims buyers this week. The Stanford Final Distribution Plan hit a hard check-cashing cliff today; the Madoff seventeenth distribution closed its active payout cycle as of late February with no new allocation motion yet filed; and the OneCoin remission fund crossed its claims-filing deadline on June 30, crystallizing the claimant universe for whatever is ultimately distributed from a $40 million pot against a scheme that is conservatively estimated to have stolen $4 billion. ### Stanford Receivership (N.D. Tex. No. 3:09-CV-0298-N) — Final Distribution: Cashing Deadline Expires Today

This is the most time-sensitive item in this advisory. 
Claimants who received Final Distribution checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash those checks by July 23, 2026
 — today. 
The Receiver recently contacted by email certain claimants whose Final Distribution checks remain uncashed, because those checks are approaching the deadline to be cashed; such checks will become void and the funds will revert to the Receivership if not cashed timely.
 
Claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 face a July 26, 2026 deadline; checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after that date.


That deadline mechanics matter beyond the individual claimant level: reverted funds return to the estate, potentially increasing the pool available to other allowed claimants or reducing the estate's unresolved tail. Claims buyers who acquired positions from claimants with uncashed checks should confirm negotiation status immediately — the right to payment lapses by operation of the court order, not by further hearing.

The deeper structural issue in Stanford remains the Fifth Circuit fee appeal. 
The Court entered an order on October 8, 2025 approving the Receiver's proposed final distribution but awarded the Receiver and his professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees — substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought — and the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed that Order to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals.
 
The Receiver and his professional firms filed their opening brief on appeal on March 23, 2026; FTI Consulting also filed a separate brief in support of the appeal.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 The fee appeal carries real consequences for the estate: if the Fifth Circuit reinstates fees closer to the $43 million sought, the incremental cost comes from the same pool already being distributed under the Final Distribution Plan, applying pressure to recovery rates for later-distributed claimants.


On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted a Status Report to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 The status report is not yet summarized in public docket releases accessible this run; claimants and secondary buyers should pull Doc. 3555 and related entries directly from the N.D. Tex. PACER docket.


Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.
 
A separate $8.1 million tranche of Swiss assets was released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025; COViSAL (a Latin American claimants' coalition) has outstanding written requests demanding clarity on when those funds will be distributed and whether they will be folded into the Final Distribution Plan.
 The Receiver has not publicly responded to those inquiries as of this writing. That $8.1 million Swiss tail is a secondary market consideration: if it flows into the existing plan structure, it modestly lifts per-claim recovery; if it is held for a supplemental plan, it creates an additional distribution event, with attendant timing risk.

### Madoff / Picard (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 08-01789 (LGB)) — Post-Seventeenth Distribution: Fee Application in View


On February 27, 2026, SIPA Trustee Irving H. Picard announced that the seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the Customer Fund to eligible BLMIS customers commenced; the Trustee is distributing more than $253 million on a pro rata basis to BLMIS account holders with allowed claims.
 
Including that distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, which includes approximately $850.9 million in advances made by SIPC.


No new allocation or distribution motion has been announced since February. The next data point is fee-related. 
On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and his counsel at BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, covering work from August 1, 2025 through November 30, 2025; the application seeks approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
No funds recovered in the Madoff Recovery Initiative are used to pay costs associated with the recovery; all Trustee, legal, and accounting fees, as well as administrative expenses, are paid by SIPC.
 That structural distinction — SIPC absorbs administrative costs, not the Customer Fund — remains the single most favorable structural feature of the BLMIS liquidation relative to any standard receivership. It means 100 percent of recovered dollars flow to claimants without fee leakage.

For secondary market participants, the current posture is a waiting period. 
As of June 26, 2026, SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation; SIPC-committed advances will continue to increase as claims currently in litigation are allowed as a result of settlements or the conclusion of litigation.
 The universe of remaining recovery is litigation-dependent — clawback actions against net winners, foreign feeder-fund recoveries, and ongoing settlement negotiations. The pace of those resolutions determines the cadence of future distributions. No eighteenth distribution motion has been filed as of July 23, 2026.

### OneCoin / Ruja Ignatova (S.D.N.Y. Nos. 17 Cr. 556 (ER), 17 Cr. 630 (ER), 18 Cr. 746 (KPF), 18 Cr. 793 (ER)) — Claims Filing Period Closed; $40M Remission Fund Next


The U.S. Department of Justice has opened a compensation process for victims of the OneCoin crypto Ponzi scheme; victims can apply for compensation from a $40 million fund of seized assets established by the DOJ.
 
The filing deadline was June 30, 2026.
 That deadline has now passed, which means the claimant universe is closed. The DOJ's Criminal Division, Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the remission process; 
the Criminal Division's Money Laundering, Narcotics, and Forfeiture Section is managing the process.


The mismatch between the $40 million fund and the scheme's scale warrants emphasis. 
OneCoin, co-founded by fugitive "Cryptoqueen" Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood, defrauded as many as 3.4 million investors worldwide through a fake cryptocurrency that never operated on a blockchain.
 Independent estimates of aggregate losses run to approximately $4 billion on the low end and $16 billion on higher estimates. Against that, a $40 million recovery fund represents a recovery rate well below one percent — a stark illustration of the gap between enforcement asset seizures and actual victim losses in large-scale cross-border crypto fraud.

The cross-border forfeiture picture has been incrementally active. 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been found in German legal proceedings to be the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
The money was held with a Guernsey financial institution for the benefit of two Guernsey-registered companies; Dr. Ruja Ignatova had commissioned the purchase of shares in both companies to acquire two freehold flats in Kensington, London.
 Whether those Guernsey proceeds will be repatriated and folded into the DOJ remission fund, or administered separately under UK or German legal frameworks, is unresolved.

Ignatova's whereabouts remain unknown. 
Ignatova boarded a Ryanair flight from Sofia to Athens on October 25, 2017, and has not been seen since.
 
Reporting in 2023 and 2024 suggested that Ignatova may have been murdered in 2018 on the orders of Bulgarian organised crime figure Hristoforos Nikos Amanatidis.
 DOJ continues to treat her as a live fugitive; the FBI maintains her on its Ten Most Wanted list with a $100,000 reward for information leading to her arrest. The open fugitive status has a concrete legal consequence: no final criminal forfeiture order has been entered against Ignatova personally in SDNY, which means substantial additional asset forfeiture — and therefore remission funds — remains contingent on either her capture, a death finding, or "independent confiscation proceedings" modeled on the German approach used in the Guernsey action.

### Power Block Coin, LLC (Bankr. D. Utah No. 24-23041, Judge Cathleen D. Parker) — Liquidating Plan Contested; $192M Claims Pool

The tracked-case ground truth for this docket reflects the most recent filing at entry 531, dated July 6, 2026, with no description available on the public feed. The case has been active on a near-weekly basis through mid-year 2026.

The structural posture: 
the Official Committee of Unsecured Creditors has proposed a liquidation plan in Utah bankruptcy court aiming to maximize value for creditors; the plan addresses over $192 million in claims and establishes an independent Liquidating Trust to manage the company's assets and potential legal claims.
 
Filed by Greenberg Traurig, LLP on January 30, 2025, the plan outlines a strategy to maximize value for creditors through the establishment of a Liquidating Trust overseen by an independent trustee.
 
The plan addresses claims totaling over $192 million, including significant claims from Celsius Network LLC exceeding $133 million.


The Celsius overhang is the single largest variable. Power Block Coin operated under the name SmartFi and had deep operational entanglement with Celsius before Celsius's own 2022 collapse (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. No. 22-10964). The interplay between the two estates — 
Celsius-related objecting creditors asserted over $133 million in preference claims against Power Block Coin in connection with the Celsius bankruptcy pending in the Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York
 — creates a situation where resolution in one docket directly conditions recovery in the other. For claims buyers, that dual-docket exposure compresses pricing: you are buying not a clean liquidating-trust payout, but a complex, intercompany-dispute-laden recovery that could litigate for years.

The judge assignment note: earlier docket entries reflect proceedings before Judge Joel T. Marker on eligibility and early motions; the tracked-case ground truth and the most recent calendar materials both confirm Judge Cathleen D. Parker is presiding as of the current posture. The November 2025 calendar entry confirms both a motion to appoint a Chapter 11 trustee and a motion to convert to Chapter 7 were pending before Judge Parker at that time — 
the November 18, 2025 calendar showed pending motions including a motion to appoint a Chapter 11 trustee and a motion to convert the case, both before Judge Cathleen D. Parker.
 The outcome of those motions is not reflected in the publicly available docket summary retrieved this run; the July 6, 2026 entry (docket 531) is the most recent confirmed activity, but its content is not yet publicly described.

### SEC Enforcement Infrastructure — Retail Fraud Working Group (July 7, 2026)

A structural development relevant to forward-looking recovery positioning: 
on July 7, 2026, the SEC announced the creation of a Retail Fraud Working Group designed to strengthen the Division of Enforcement's efforts to identify and combat fraud targeting everyday investors, including offering frauds, pump-and-dump schemes, market manipulation, and breaches of duties to customers by investment advisers and broker dealers.
 
The Working Group will be led by Kate Zoladz, Deputy Director, West, and Kim Frederick, Assistant Director, Asset Management Unit, of the Division of Enforcement.
 
The Working Group will serve as a dedicated resource for proactive case generation, play an important role in coordinating with regulatory partners and foreign counterparts, and participate in educational outreach to retail investors.


The practical significance for this desk: new enforcement actions generate new receiverships, and new receiverships generate new claims-buying opportunities — typically 12–36 months after appointment, once receiver counsel has stabilized the estate and filed a preliminary claims schedule. The Working Group's formation signals that Enforcement's retail-fraud pipeline, which has been relatively quiet in the early 2026 period, is being deliberately reinvigorated. Whistleblower award activity — historically the leading indicator for new enforcement cases by 6–18 months — is worth monitoring as a forward-looking input.
