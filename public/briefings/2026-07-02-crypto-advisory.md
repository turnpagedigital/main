### BlockFills (Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc.) — Post–Sale Approval Posture; Plan Confirmation Approaching

The dominant development in this beat today is the post-closing posture of the BlockFills case following the June 16 sale hearing, with plan confirmation now targeted for September 11, 2026. The Veritaglobal docket page for 
*In re Reliz Technology Group Holdings Inc., et al.*, Case No. 26-10371 (TMH), Bankr. D. Del., before Judge Thomas M. Horan
 reflects nine new docket entries over the last 72 hours (through July 1), though no entry descriptions have been publicly published by the claims agent as of this writing. The ground truth of the sale process is well-established: 
Keyrock SA was declared the "Successful Bidder" in a bankruptcy court document filed May 26, 2026, at a purchase price of $3.25 million, agreeing to assume "substantially all" of BlockFills' assets.
 
A hearing to consider approval of that sale was scheduled for June 16, 2026, with the parties continuing to collaborate on the administrative process in the meantime and the final closing remaining subject to court approval and regulatory approvals referenced in Keyrock's bid.


The Veritaglobal key-dates page now lists 
September 11, 2026 (4:00 p.m. ET)
 as the next major milestone — the likely plan confirmation hearing date — making this a critical interlude: the Keyrock sale should have closed (or be in post-approval closing mechanics) while the estates proceed to a plan that governs residual creditor recoveries and the liquidating trust. The exact content of those nine new docket entries filed in the 72 hours ending July 1 is not yet published in the Veritaglobal document portal. Counsel for the debtors, McDermott Will & Schulte LLP (lead: David R. Hurst, Wilmington; Darren Azman, New York), has not released a public statement on the outcome of the June 16 hearing. The advisories will update once the hearing record is available.

**The § 541 customer-property dispute remains live.** 
At the first-day hearing, debtors' counsel confirmed in open court that customer funds "have always been commingled with company funds and other customer funds," and that the Debtors' position is that all such funds constitute property of the estate.
 That admission forecloses a trust-or-bailment argument for institutional customers absent a successful adversary proceeding or plan modification. The practical consequence: 
the first-day record identifies roughly $145 million of general unsecured customer and trade obligations and about $4.8 million outstanding under Celsius secured promissory notes, with Celsius asserting first-priority liens.
 Against a $3.25 million § 363 sale price, the arithmetic for unsecured creditors is severe — recoveries under a liquidating plan will depend almost entirely on the litigation trust's pursuit of fraudulent-transfer and insider-liability claims.

**Adversary proceedings in motion.** 
The estate filed Adv. Pro. No. 26-50230 (TMH) against Artha Investment Partners LLC on April 9, 2026.
 The prepetition customer-action record adds further complexity: 
in the weeks immediately preceding the Chapter 11 filing, certain Debtor entities and current or former directors and officers were named in two lawsuits asserting allegations including misappropriation of customer funds, conversion, breach of contract, and other fraud-based claims, including *Dominion Capital LLC v. Reliz Ltd.*, Case No. 1:26-cv-01672 (S.D.N.Y., Feb. 27, 2026).
 
On March 3, 2026, Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil issued an ex parte TRO freezing 70.56 BTC (~$4.8 million) belonging to Dominion, barring BlockFills from moving assets outside the United States, and ordering an accounting and segregation of customer funds.
 Those pre-petition TROs were stayed by the automatic stay upon filing; their resurrection in the plan or via a creditor trust vehicle is a key structural question.

**Chapter 11 trustee motion — open record.** 
A BlockFills creditor sought appointment of a Chapter 11 trustee to take over the crypto group's bankruptcy
 — a motion surfaced in Law360 reporting from the spring docket. The outcome of that motion is not confirmed on the public record available today. If granted, it would alter the estate's litigation posture materially.

**Case background context.** 
The company's liquidity crisis traced to cascading exposure from several counterparty defaults — specifically an $8.5 million impairment in the Babel Finance bankruptcy and a $12 million settlement stemming from the AEXA bankruptcy — alongside an adverse arbitration obligation requiring payments to Celsius, with stranded capital from a failed 2022 mining hardware deployment compounding the balance sheet pressures.
 
BlockFills reported trading volume exceeding $60 billion in 2025, a 28% increase from the prior year, and served approximately 2,000 institutional clients.
 The disparity between that operational scale and a $3.25 million exit sale reflects how quickly institutional goodwill dissipated once customer withdrawals were frozen.

### FTX Recovery Trust — Fifth Distribution: Record Date June 16, Payment Date July 31

The FTX case has moved from plan confirmation into distribution mechanics. The operative data point entering July: 
the fifth distribution carries a record date of June 16, 2026, with payments commencing July 31.
 That record date has now passed, meaning any claim transfers filed after June 16 and before the July 31 payment date will not affect this tranche.


The fourth distribution (March 31, 2026) delivered an additional 18% to Class 5A Dotcom creditors, bringing cumulative recovery to 96%; U.S. customer claims in Class 5B reached full recovery at 100%; Classes 6A and 6B each received a 15% increment reaching 100%; and Class 7 received a cumulative 120% distribution.
 
The plan projects that 98% of creditors receive at least 118% of their claim value; Class 5A had received about 96% by May 2026, with a projected total of around 118–120% once the 9% annual interest is added — still measured at November 11, 2022 petition-date prices.


The petition-date valuation asymmetry remains the central grievance for creditors who held Bitcoin or other tokens with material price appreciation since November 2022. 
The "120% recovery" headline is calculated against the USD value of assets on November 11, 2022, when FTX filed; Bitcoin was then trading at approximately $18,000, compared to $82,000+ by early 2025. A creditor who held 1 BTC receives 120% × $18,000 = $21,600, while the same Bitcoin was worth multiples of that amount.


For secondary-market participants: 
for transferred claims, distributions will only be made to the transferee holder of an allowed claim that is processed and reflected on the official register maintained by the notice and claims agent as of future record dates, where the 21-day notice period has lapsed without objection.
 Any claim traded after the June 16 record date will not receive the fifth distribution; buyers in the current aftermarket are pricing in the July 31 payment plus the residual tail. 
Secondary market offer ranges as of June 2026: a clean Class 5A claim is trading in the 90–95% band; Class 7 Convenience claims in the 80–85% band; claims stuck in KYC review in the 70–80% band; and claims in restricted jurisdictions in the 50–60% band.



Through the fourth distribution, cumulative amounts returned to creditors have reached roughly $10 billion since distributions began in early 2025.
 
Distributions are projected through 2027; the estate still has disputed claim reserves and assets to liquidate, and larger non-convenience claims may see final payments well into 2027.

### Genesis/DCG Wind-Down — Active Adversary Proceedings; July 7 and July 15 Hearings Pending

Genesis Global Holdco, LLC, Case No. 23-10063 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y.) has completed its initial distributions but remains active in adversary litigation. The Kroll docket page for the Wind-Down Oversight Committee (WDOC) reflects two near-term calendar events: 
a LOC Town Hall at 12:00 p.m. on July 7, 2026, and an omnibus hearing on July 15, 2026 at 11:00 a.m. ET.


The active adversary docket is notable for its breadth. 
Pending proceedings include: *Genesis Global Holdco v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 26-01053; a second DCG action at 26-03230; *DCG v. Genesis Global Capital*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01129; *Genesis Global Capital v. Insider Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01097; *Genesis Global Capital v. Redacted Defendants*, Adv. Pro. No. 25-01028; and *Genesis Global Capital v. Digital Currency Group, Inc.*, Adv. Pro. No. 24-01312.
 The multiplicity of adversary proceedings against DCG reflects the WDOC's ongoing effort to squeeze additional recovery from the parent entity following its exclusion from any plan distribution.


Judge Sean Lane's confirmation ruling found: "The record here clearly establishes that there is not sufficient value in the Debtors' estates to provide DCG a recovery as equity holder after unsecured creditors are paid," writing that "DCG is out of the money as an equity holder by billions of dollars, even if the Court valued creditor claims using the method DCG proposes."


The in-kind, coin-for-coin recovery model Genesis employed — departing from petition-date USD valuation — remains the structural contrast with the FTX approach. 
Unlike other crypto bankruptcy cases, Genesis did not seek to cap creditor recoveries at their petition-date value; instead, it sought to provide restitution through the Chapter 11 plan via in-kind recoveries on a coin-for-coin basis until creditors are paid in full.
 The adversary proceedings against DCG are the principal mechanism for closing the remaining distribution gap. 
Cleary Gottlieb is working with the plan administrator to effectuate approximately $200 million in distributions to altcoin creditors, with the firm also commencing a $33 million adversary proceeding against DCG seeking repayment of amounts paid to Three Arrows Capital.

### Cross-Case Pattern: Customer-Property vs. Estate-Property — The Continued Fault Line

The commingling-at-first-day-hearing admission in BlockFills reinforces a pattern visible across the 2022–26 insolvency wave. The controlling statutory framework remains 11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(1) (property of the estate comprises "all legal or equitable interests of the debtor in property as of the commencement of the case") versus § 541(b) carve-outs and trust/bailment claims under applicable state law. Where a debtor's first-day record affirmatively establishes commingling, creditors face an uphill climb distinguishing their assets from estate property absent: (i) an express contractual segregation obligation; (ii) constructive trust theory under state law; or (iii) a statutory carve-out analogous to SIPA's customer-property definition (which does not apply in Chapter 11).

The Celsius model — in which the Terms of Service's express "title transfer" language was litigated through Judge Martin Glenn's ruling that interest-bearing Earn account assets became estate property — remains the leading negative precedent for institutional depositors. BlockFills' operational structure (a crypto brokerage with bilateral OTC and lending, not a retail earn product) arguably presents different TOS dynamics, but first-day counsel's open-court commingling admission largely forecloses that distinction here.
