### BLMIS / Picard — Seventeenth Distribution Complete; Fifty-First Fee Application Cycle Pending

The seventeenth pro rata interim distribution from the BLMIS Customer Fund commenced February 27, 2026 in *Securities Investor Protection Corp. v. Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC*, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., No. 08-01789. 
The SIPA Trustee distributed more than $253 million on a pro rata basis; including this distribution, the aggregate amount sent to eligible BLMIS customers totals nearly $15.38 billion, inclusive of approximately $850.9 million in SIPC advances.
 
The seventeenth distribution represents 1.302% of each allowed claim and was paid on claims relating to 768 accounts, bringing the cumulative percentage paid to approximately 72.848% of each allowed claim across seventeen distributions.



As of June 26, 2026, the SIPA Trustee has recovered or reached agreements to recover approximately $15.456 billion.
 
SIPC has committed approximately $850.9 million to the BLMIS liquidation, with advances expected to continue increasing as claims currently in litigation are allowed through settlements or judicial conclusions.
 
Under SIPA, SIPC is reimbursed for its advances once each customer claim is fully satisfied; SIPC has received $272.4 million in reimbursement from the Customer Fund, leaving $578.5 million in SIPC advances outstanding.



On February 13, 2026, the SIPA Trustee and BakerHostetler filed the 50th Interim Fee Application covering August 1 through November 30, 2025, seeking approval of fees representing approximately 63,774.40 hours of professional and paraprofessional services billed at an average public-interest discounted rate of approximately $588.88 per hour.
 
A 10% public-interest discount was applied from standard rates.
 The 51st fee application, covering December 2025 through March 2026, has not yet appeared on the docket per publicly available records — that filing is expected in Q3 2026 and constitutes the next fee-creep monitoring event for claims-pricing purposes.

**Claims-pricing note:** With cumulative recovery at 72.848% and aggregate recoveries at $15.456 billion, the remaining recovery pool against the $20.3 billion in admitted claims implies a theoretical residual of approximately $4.8 billion still in dispute or unliquidated litigation. Ongoing adversary proceedings against feeder funds and net winners continue to serve as the upside case for late-stage claim acquisitions. The Ponzi presumption under *In re Madoff*, established through prior Second Circuit jurisprudence applying 11 U.S.C. § 548(a), remains the operative clawback framework — net winners who received fictitious profits remain exposed to avoidance actions until those proceedings resolve.

### Stanford Receivership — Final Distribution Executing; Fee Appeal Live at Fifth Circuit; Uncashed-Check Deadlines Critical This Week

*SEC v. Stanford International Bank, Ltd.*, No. 3:09-CV-0298-N (N.D. Tex., Dallas Div.), Ralph Janvey receiver, has moved fully into final-distribution execution. 
Payments under the Final Distribution Plan began April 3, 2026.
 
On March 9, 2026, the Receiver filed with the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas the Schedule of Payments to Be Made to Eligible Investor CD Claimants pursuant to the October 8, 2025 and November 25, 2025 Final Distribution Orders.
 
Payments listed on the Final Distribution Schedule total more than $339 million and include payments to over 14,000 claim groups.



On July 9, 2026, the Receiver submitted a Status Report to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division, regarding payments issued pursuant to the Final Distribution Plan.
 This is the most current public filing in the case and the primary document for assessing distribution velocity.

**Uncashed-check deadlines — urgent.** 
Claimants who received checks dated April 24, 2026 must cash them by July 23, 2026; claimants who received checks dated April 27, 2026 must cash them by July 26, 2026. Checks not negotiated by the applicable deadlines will become void and will be rejected if presented for payment after those dates.
 
Certain claimants whose Final Distribution checks remain uncashed have been contacted by email because those checks are approaching the void deadline; uncashed funds revert to the receivership estate.
 Claims investors holding interests on behalf of claimants with foreign or inactive addresses should treat this as a loss-of-value event — any check that voids reverts to the estate, reducing distributable assets.

**Fee appeal — Fifth Circuit.** 
The October 8, 2025 distribution-approval order also awarded the Receiver and professionals approximately $14.9 million in supplemental fees, substantially less than the approximately $43 million sought; the Receiver and certain professional firms appealed to the Fifth Circuit.
 
The Receiver's opening brief on appeal was filed March 23, 2026.
 
The SEC filed its brief in support of the district court's order on May 23, 2026.
 
The Fifth Circuit denied the Examiner's motion for leave to intervene as a party in the appeal.
 The fee dispute does not stay distributions, but a successful appeal would reduce the net distributable estate by up to approximately $28 million — a pricing-relevant variable for any residual claim trades pending resolution.


In May 2026, Jaime Escalona submitted materials to the Northern District of Texas challenging the Receiver's distribution process; the Receiver responded on June 1, 2026, including an update on the status of ongoing distribution efforts.
 
COViSAL, a Latin American victim coalition, made a formal second request regarding the status of $8.1 million in Swiss assets released to the Receiver on December 17, 2025, asking whether and when those funds will be distributed; as of the April 2026 correspondence, no public response had been recorded.
 The Swiss tranche is small relative to the $339 million schedule but illustrates the continuing multi-jurisdictional tail inherent to these estates under 28 U.S.C. § 1782 and MLAT coordination frameworks.

### OneCoin / DOJ Remission — Petition Window Closed June 30; Distribution Sizing Now Active


The DOJ announced the beginning of the remission compensation process for victims of the international OneCoin investment scheme; between 2014 and 2019, Ruja Ignatova and Karl Sebastian Greenwood orchestrated the MLM-based fraud defrauding investors of over $4 billion worldwide from the scheme's Sofia, Bulgaria base of operations.
 
The Department filed OneCoin-related prosecutions in the Southern District of New York; several key figures were sentenced and the Department pursued criminal forfeiture of proceeds.



Over $40 million in forfeited assets are available for distribution through the DOJ's remission process.
 
Kroll Settlement Administration LLC serves as remission administrator; the FBI and IRS-CI conducted the underlying criminal fraud investigation.
 The June 30, 2026 petition deadline has now passed. 
Victims who purchased the fraudulent OneCoin cryptocurrency between 2014 and 2019 were eligible for compensation through the remission process.
 The post-deadline period will be consumed by petition review and fund sizing — distributions are unlikely before late 2026 or early 2027, consistent with typical DOJ remission timelines.

**Asset tracing — Guernsey forfeiture closed.** 
On January 16, 2026, the Royal Court of Guernsey ordered the registration and enforcement of an overseas forfeiture order made against funds totaling GBP 8,590,200.92, which had been identified in German proceedings as the proceeds of Dr. Ruja Ignatova's criminal conduct.
 
Ignatova used these criminal proceeds to purchase two luxury Kensington, London properties, relying on professional enablers across Europe to launder funds and register the properties in Guernsey trust structures deliberately chosen to obscure her involvement.
 
The total of £8,590,200.92 forfeited from the Guernsey-registered property structures will be returned to Germany to compensate global victims.



German proceedings found OneCoin was released with fraudulent intent by Ignatova and others; because criminal proceedings could not conclude given her unknown whereabouts, German authorities initiated "Independent Confiscation Proceedings" that led to the overseas forfeiture order.
 This non-conviction-based forfeiture mechanism — analogous to UK § 423 Insolvency Act 1986 and CPR 25 frameworks — is precisely the tool international recovery counsel have been using to reach Ignatova's layered asset structures without awaiting her surrender or conviction.


The scale of the fraud led the U.S. Department of State to offer a significant reward for information leading to her arrest; the reward currently stands at up to $5 million under the Transnational Organized Crime Rewards Program.
 Ignatova's asset base — estimated in the billions — remains largely unrecovered, meaning the $40 million DOJ remission pool represents well under 1% of victim losses. The recovery trajectory is entirely dependent on continued multi-jurisdictional enforcement seizures; current momentum via Germany/Guernsey MLA channels and FBI international outreach is encouraging but incremental.

### SEC Structural Shift — Retail Fraud Working Group and Disgorgement Authority Confirmed

Two structural developments this month carry direct implications for the next generation of SEC-driven receiverships and victim recovery.

**New Retail Fraud Working Group.** 
On July 7, 2026, the SEC announced the creation of the Retail Fraud Working Group, designed to identify and combat fraud targeting everyday investors, including offering frauds, pump-and-dump schemes, market manipulation, and breaches of duties by investment advisers and broker dealers.
 
The Working Group is led by Kate Zoladz, Deputy Director, West, and Kim Frederick, Assistant Director, Asset Management Unit, in the Division of Enforcement.
 
The group will serve as a dedicated resource for proactive case generation and will coordinate with regulatory partners and foreign counterparts.
 
In April 2026, David Woodcock assumed the role of SEC Director of Enforcement; in his first public remarks, he outlined a traditional enforcement agenda centered on high-impact fraud, market integrity, offering fraud, accounting and disclosure fraud, market manipulation, insider trading, private funds, and cross-border fraud.


The Retail Fraud Working Group is a leading indicator. New enforcement actions generating receivership appointments typically lag the working group's case-generation activity by six to eighteen months. Claims investors with capacity to move quickly on newly-appointed receiverships — where claim pricing is most favorable — should be monitoring SEC litigation releases on a daily basis for appointment orders going forward.

**Disgorgement authority confirmed by SCOTUS.** 
On June 4, 2026, the Supreme Court issued a unanimous decision in *Sripetch v. Securities and Exchange Commission*, No. 25-466, holding that the SEC does not need to prove that investors suffered financial harm as a condition of obtaining disgorgement.
 
The case involved an individual who operated pump-and-dump schemes across at least 20 penny-stock companies; Justice Gorsuch, writing for the Court, drew a distinction between legal damages measured by the plaintiff's loss and equitable remedies measured by the defendant's gain, rejecting the argument that disgorgement without pecuniary loss was inconsistent with *Liu v. SEC*, 591 U.S. 71 (2020).
 This ruling strengthens the SEC's hand in Ponzi and fraud enforcement: defendants can no longer argue that diffuse or difficult-to-quantify victim losses limit the government's disgorgement claim, which in turn expands the asset pool available for eventual Fair Fund distribution to victims.
