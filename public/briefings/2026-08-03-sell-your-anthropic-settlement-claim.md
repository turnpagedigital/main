# Can You Sell Your Bartz v. Anthropic Settlement Claim? Yes — Here's How It Works

**The short answer:** yes. A settlement payment right is property, and like most property it can generally be sold — assigned to a buyer who pays you a fixed cash price today and then stands in your shoes to collect from the fund as it pays out into 2027. Turnpage Digital Markets runs a desk that does exactly this for Bartz v. Anthropic claims. Here's the full picture, including the parts a buyer should be honest about.

## Why would anyone sell a ~$3,000-per-work claim?

Because the settlement pays on its schedule, not yours. The fund fills in installments — the final $450 million doesn't even arrive from Anthropic until **September 25, 2027** — and checks go out in waves ([full timeline here](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-payment-timeline)). Sellers are typically:

- **Authors with many works** — 20 eligible works is a meaningful five-figure position stuck on a two-year drip
- **Estates and trusts** that need to value and close out assets now, not in 2027
- **Small presses and rights companies** that would rather redeploy capital than wait
- **Anyone with a real near-term need** — the classic reason people sell any future payment stream

## How a claim sale actually works

1. **You tell us what you own.** Which works, whether you filed a claim, whether rights are shared with a publisher or co-author. ([Register here](/bartz-registration) — it takes minutes.)
2. **We verify and price it.** We check the works against the settlement's Works List and allocation rules and quote you a **fixed cash price** — a percentage of the expected recovery, reflecting the wait, the allocation math, and the risks we're taking on.
3. **You sign an assignment agreement.** This transfers the right to receive the settlement payments to us. The exact mechanics depend on the settlement's terms and the administrator's process — our desk handles that paperwork; it's part of what you're paying us to absorb.
4. **You get paid now.** Cash to your account. When the fund pays out — this fall, next year, 2027 — those checks are our problem and our reward.

## What you give up, plainly

A buyer pays less than face value; that discount is the price of certainty and speed. If distributions come out higher or sooner than expected, the buyer benefits. In a properly structured purchase, the flip side is also true: if the fund pays later or less than projected — an [appeal delay](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-appeal-risk), an allocation dispute — that downside is the buyer's, not yours. If a buyer offers you a deal where *you* still carry those risks after selling, read it again carefully.

## Sale vs. advance — know the difference

A **sale** transfers the claim outright. An **advance** is money now against your future payment, where you remain the claimant — [we compare the two structures here](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-cash-advance). Which is available and sensible depends on your claim's specifics; our desk quotes both where possible.
