# Can I Sell My Bartz v. Anthropic Settlement Claim?

**The short answer is yes.** 

The Bartz v. Anthropic settlement agreement has no "anti-assignment" clause that would bar claimants from assigning their right to payment as is common in some class actions. A right to payment from a class action settlement is property, and property that isn't expressly restricted can be sold like any other asset. In fact, Turnpage has already facilitated sales of multiple Anthropic settlement claims that have been acknowledged by the claims administrator and class counsel. Certain states do have restrictions about buying legal settlements before the settlement has been finalized or without certain licensing and we do not operate in those jurisdictions.

Some online searches about selling your class action claim will surface warnings that selling a class-action claim is barred, invalid, or fraudulent. These trace back to a company called ClaimsHero which was described as "a fraud of immense proportions." ClaimsHero allegedly pressured authors to opt out of the settlement entirely before the March 30, 2026 filing deadline on the promise of a bigger potential payout based on filing a separate, speculative lawsuit. ClaimsHero hoped to capture a share of recoveries from that larger potential payout and allegedly misled customers into believing they were guaranteed to get more than under the settlement. In part, due to these activities, the Bartz v. Anthropic court permitted claimants that had opted out to opt back in to the class for a short period of time.

Selling a filed claim to an independent buyer is exactly the opposite of the scheme that ClaimsHero allegedly ran.  Instead of giving up their claim in exchange for an even *more speculative* *asset*, the claimholder sells their rights to payment for a *less speculative asset* —namely cash, paid today.* In a sale, the buyer simply pays a fixed amount to the claim holder now in exchange for all of their rights under the settlement and collects from the settlement fund directly when it ultimately pays out. While the cash value may be less than the expected recovery, the discount in a claim sale is generally justified by the duration risk and other factors that could impact recoveries. If the fund pays late — for instance, if [an appeal pauses distribution](/briefings/2026-08-03-anthropic-settlement-appeal-risk) — or pays less than projected, that is the buyer's loss.The buyer generally absorbs these risks as part of the trade. 

**Why are Anthropic class action claim holders selling?** 

There are multiple reasons that claim holders may want to sell their recovery rights, but the primary reason is to accelerate payment. The settlement fund won't be fully funded until September 2027. Two appeals have been filed, which will delay the start of distributions indefinitely, potentially into 2028+. Some claimants may have current cash needs and would rather have cash now. Estates and trusts that are rights holders may need to convert assets to cash to close accounts.
