# Social Playbook — Turnpage Digital Markets

*Planning document, June 2026. Internal use only.*

One operator, one hour a day, two platforms. LinkedIn is the channel that matters — the buyers, sellers, and referrers of legal claims (bankruptcy attorneys, claims agents, fund analysts, authors' organizations) all live there. X is a secondary repeater. Everything below is sized for Andrew running it solo on top of the existing daily briefing pipeline.

---

## 1. The core advantage: the pipeline already writes the posts

The daily briefing system auto-drafts LinkedIn posts for three topics — **LLM/copyright, crypto insolvency, and bankruptcy creditor rights** — every run (each topic's `posts.html` has the draft, a character count, a copy button, and an "Adjust This Post" loop). Most solo operators fail at social because drafting daily is a grind. That problem is already solved here. The playbook is therefore a **review-and-publish discipline**, not a content-creation project.

### The morning routine (15–20 minutes, weekdays)

1. Run / open the daily briefing.
2. Read the three drafted LinkedIn posts. Pick **one** — the strongest story of the day, not all three. (Posting 3×/day from one profile suppresses reach and burns the audience; 2–5 posts *per week* is the research-backed cadence for personal profiles — https://www.salesandmarketingengineers.co.uk/the-ultimate-linkedin-posting-guide-for-2026.)
3. Edit for voice (Section 3 checklist), copy, post from Andrew's **personal profile** between 8:00–9:30 AM ET, Tuesday–Thursday preferred (https://www.salesandmarketingengineers.co.uk/the-ultimate-linkedin-posting-guide-for-2026).
4. Spend the next 10 minutes in the feed replying — the first 90 minutes after posting determine reach, because early engagement signals decide whether LinkedIn keeps distributing (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-engagement-strategy-guide).
5. Cross-post a compressed version to X (Section 6).
6. Check "I used this post" in the briefing UI so the pipeline learns preferences.

### Why personal profile, not company page

Personal profiles generate roughly 5.6× the reach and ~8× the engagement of company pages posting identical content (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-engagement-strategy-guide; corroborated by Sprout's algorithm guide, https://sproutsocial.com/insights/linkedin-algorithm/). The TPDM company page should exist, look sharp, and repost Andrew's best posts — but Andrew's profile is the channel. For a firm whose product is judgment and market access, that's a feature: people sell claims to a person they trust.

## 2. Content pillars (mapped to the six briefing topics)

| Pillar | Source topic(s) | Share of posts | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI copyright money-in-motion** | LLM/Copyright | ~30% | Bartz mechanics, payout timelines, what a claim is worth, OpenAI MDL and the next settlements. This is the headline brand — own the "what authors actually get paid, and when" beat. |
| **Crypto creditor recoveries** | Crypto Insolvency | ~25% | FTX/Celsius/Genesis distribution news, claim pricing, stub claims, Chapter 15 cross-border angles. |
| **Creditor-rights decisions that move claim prices** | Bankruptcy Creditor Rights | ~20% | Plan confirmation, third-party releases post-Purdue, equitable mootness — always translated into "what this does to recovery values." |
| **The mega-settlement cycle** | $1B+ Class Actions, Fraud Recovery | ~15% | BCBS, Roundup, interchange, mass arbitration, receivership clawbacks — claims-market commentary nobody else writes. |
| **How claim markets work** (evergreen) | Tariffs/Trade + own expertise | ~10% | Explainers: how a claim gets priced, what diligence looks like, discount math, why creditors sell. These are the carousel/document posts (below) and double as sales collateral. |

Rule that makes every pillar on-brand: **end on the market, not the law.** A ruling recap is commodity content; "here's what this does to the price of the claim" is content only a claims desk can write.

### Format mix (research-backed)

- **Document/PDF carousels** lead all LinkedIn formats at ~6.6% average engagement, 2–3× the dwell time of text posts (https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now). Do **one per week**: 6–10 slides, black background, neon #D4FF00 accents, Archivo — e.g., "How an FTX claim gets priced," "Bartz payouts: the timeline in 7 slides." High effort, longest shelf life, best brand artifact.
- **Text posts** (~2% engagement baseline, but near-zero effort given the pipeline) carry the daily cadence: 4×/week from the briefing drafts.
- **Link posts are penalized** — external links cut reach materially, with 2026 analyses citing penalties up to ~60% (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-engagement-strategy-guide). Put the substance in the post itself; drop the turnpagedigital.com/briefings link **in the first comment**, or post link-free and let the profile do the routing.
- Skip video for now — good performance (~5.6% engagement per the dataslayer analysis) but the production cost doesn't fit a solo operation yet.

### Weekly cadence at a glance

| Day | Post | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Text post — strongest weekend/Monday story | Briefing draft |
| Tue | Text post | Briefing draft |
| Wed | **Document carousel** (evergreen explainer or weekly market wrap) | Hand-built, ~45 min |

Total: 4–5 posts/week, squarely in the optimal 2–5 band.

## 3. Voice checklist for editing pipeline drafts (60 seconds per post)

Drawn from the brand styling guide (`briefing-generator/BRAND_STYLING.md`):

- [ ] Plain prose, direct sentences, 12th-grade reading level. No "delve," no "game-changer," no exclamation points.
- [ ] First line earns the click on "...see more" — a number or a tension, not a throat-clear. ("440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed. 91.3%. Here's what that does to the per-work payout." — figures: https://authorsguild.org/news/anthropic-settlement-update-91-percent-of-books-claimed/)
- [ ] Ends on the market angle and a soft prompt ("If you're holding one of these claims, you have options before late fall.") — never a hard "DM me to sell."
- [ ] No outcome guarantees, no legal advice, nothing implying TPDM is a law firm, advisor, or broker-dealer.
- [ ] Entity discipline: "Turnpage Digital Markets" (Rewind Tariffs is a DBA, never a separate firm); "Andrew" in prose, "Andrew Glantz" only in formal attribution.
- [ ] 0–3 hashtags max, at the end, niche over broad: #bankruptcyclaims #litigationfinance #AIcopyright — hashtags are a minor signal in 2026; substance does the ranking.
- [ ] No external link in the body (first comment only).

## 5. Engagement strategy (the half that actually grows the account)

For a niche desk, commenting is worth more than posting — comments put the headline ("OTC desk for legal claims") in front of someone else's audience, with no follower count required.

**Daily (10 min, can be afternoon):**
- Leave 3–5 substantive comments on posts by restructuring partners, claims-agent firms (Stretto, Kroll, Epiq people), litigation funders, legal journalists. A substantive comment = adds a fact or a market read, 2+ sentences. The algorithm weights comment threads heavily, and your comment is a free impression on their audience.
- Reply to every comment on your own posts within the 90-minute window when possible.

**Weekly (20 min):**
- 10–15 connection requests, personalized one-liner, no pitch. Target lists, rotating: bankruptcy/restructuring attorneys; claims agents and bankruptcy administrators; distressed-debt and claims-fund analysts; authors' organization staff and agented authors (Authors Guild orbit — they're highly active post-Bartz); crypto-creditor community voices (FTX/Celsius creditor advocates have large followings).
- Follow + turn on notifications for ~10 high-signal accounts (UCC committee counsel, settlement administrators, legal-news reporters) so commenting windows aren't missed.

**Monthly:**
- One "market letter" post summarizing the month in claim prices/news — the kind of artifact people forward. Candidates for the carousel slot.

## 6. X / Twitter — secondary, compressed, crypto-weighted

X earns a slot for exactly one reason: **crypto-creditor communities live there** (FTX/Celsius creditor groups, claims-price commentators), and finance/crypto are among X's amplified topic zones in 2026 (https://www.teract.ai/resources/twitter-algorithm-2026). Treat it as a 10-minute repeater, not a second program.

**What to cross-post:** the daily briefing pipeline already drafts an X version alongside each LinkedIn post (the posts.html X card, 280-char counter). Post the one matching the day's LinkedIn pick — crypto-insolvency content always; copyright and bankruptcy content when the hook survives compression.

**Mechanics that matter:**
- External links cost 30–50% of reach (open-sourced ranking code — https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-x-twitter-algorithm-works). Same rule as LinkedIn: substance in the post, link in a reply.
- Replies are weighted ~13.5× a like, and reply-chains where the author engages back outrank raw likes (same source). Replying to creditor-community threads with claim-market context is the highest-ROI activity on the platform.
- A defensible full-time cadence is 3–5 posts/day (https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-x-twitter-algorithm-works) — TPDM should not attempt that. **1 post/day + 2–3 replies in creditor threads** is the solo-operator version.
- X Premium materially boosts reach (documented in-network/out-of-network multipliers — https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-x-twitter-algorithm-works); at ~$8–16/month it's worth it if X produces a single lead.

**Hashtags/communities:** hashtags matter less on X than topic detection, but #FTX, #Celsius, #bankruptcy, #litigationfunding still aid search. The real targets are the recurring conversations: FTX creditor threads, Celsius distribution threads, claims-price commentary accounts, and fintwit's distressed-debt corner.

**What not to do on X:** no AI-copyright author outreach (authors congregate on LinkedIn, Bluesky, and newsletters — X authorship communities skew hostile to anything AI-adjacent; a claims desk wading in reads as ambulance-chasing), and no automated firehose posting.

## 7. Realistic growth expectations (single operator)

Set expectations so the channel isn't judged by influencer math. Niche legal/finance engagement benchmarks run ~2–6% depending on format (https://influenceflow.io/resources/linkedin-engagement-benchmarks-by-industry-2026-your-essential-guide-to-b2b-performance/, https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now).

| Horizon | LinkedIn (personal) | X | Leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1–3 | +150–400 relevant followers; posts averaging 500–2,000 impressions; one occasional 10k+ outlier when a Bartz/FTX post hits | +50–150 followers | 0–3 inbound conversations; mostly "what's my claim worth" |
| Month 4–6 | 1,000–2,500 followers; recognized commenter in the bankruptcy/claims niche; carousels saved/shared by attorneys | 300–800 followers if crypto threads engaged consistently | 2–5 qualified conversations/month; first referral from an attorney follower |
| Month 7–12 | 3,000–6,000 followers; inbound DMs around major distribution events (Bartz checks landing late fall 2026 is the moment — be the account authors already follow) | Plateau unless crypto news cycle spikes | Social becomes a steady single-digit monthly source of qualified leads + the top of the referral funnel |

Two honest caveats: (1) follower counts in this niche are a vanity metric — 800 followers containing 50 bankruptcy attorneys and 10 fund analysts outperforms 20,000 generic ones; track *DMs, profile-link clicks to /contact?source=linkedin, and named relationships* instead. (2) The single biggest growth lever on the calendar is event-driven: Bartz final approval (heard May 14, 2026) → payouts (late fall) and each FTX/Celsius distribution date will each produce more relevant followers in a week than a quiet month of posting. The daily pipeline ensures those days are never missed.

## 8. Compliance guardrails for social

- Every post is commentary, not advice: when in doubt append "Not legal advice." — the briefing system's own disclaimer standard.
- Never name a prospective counterparty or hint at a live negotiation. Confidentiality is the product.
- No claim-price predictions stated as fact; ranges with sources only.
- DMs asking "what's my claim worth": move to the contact form (`/contact?source=linkedin`) so intake, attribution, and records all run through one pipe.
