## I. Keller Postman Google Advertiser Mass Arbitration — Campaign Mechanics
Keller Postman announced on May 11, 2026 that it represents thousands of U.S. businesses in individual arbitration proceedings against Google for alleged digital-advertising overcharges, with the firm's economist projecting potential aggregate claims of $218 billion or more across the eligible August 2016-onward period. The campaign is structurally distinctive: it is not a class action but a coordinated mass-arbitration push, run on parallel individual-claim tracks under Google's own mandatory arbitration clauses, with damages calculated as up to 30% of advertiser spend and treble damages available under federal antitrust law.
The case theory rests on the two existing federal court findings of monopolization — the Section 2 liability finding in <em>U.S. v. Google</em> (search) and the parallel adverse finding against Google's sell-side ad-tech stack — that produce the structural overcharge predicate. The arbitration mechanics will run through AAA or JAMS depending on the controlling advertiser-agreement language; both administrators now have mass-arbitration rules with a 25-claim coordinated-filing trigger, generating significant administrative-fee exposure for Google. The [https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-13/google-faces-mass-arbitration-by-advertisers-seeking-billions](Bloomberg reporting) through the May 15–17 window confirms continued intake growth.
The defense-side posture remains arbitration-clause-enforceability + standing-and-causation contestation in the early-claim waves. Google has not yet challenged the underlying overcharge methodology at the merits level in any filed arbitration; that contest will materialize once the first wave of claims reaches the threshold-determination stage. The Cornerstone-style and Compass-Lexecon-style damages analyses circulating through the antitrust-economics community broadly support the structural-overcharge model but flag the per-advertiser damages allocation as the contested terrain.

## II. AAA / JAMS Mass-Arbitration Rule Mechanics
The AAA mass-arbitration rules — triggered at 25 or more coordinated filings — produce the administrative-fee architecture that has historically driven settlement leverage in mass-arbitration campaigns (the DoorDash and Postmates examples remain the controlling reference points). Each filed claim generates Google's filing-fee exposure independently; the cumulative fee load on a thousands-claim campaign is itself a settlement driver. JAMS' parallel mass-claim mechanism produces a similar but jurisdictionally distinct architecture. Practitioners modeling settlement leverage in the campaign should price both the underlying liability and the administrative-fee leverage.

## III. Apple / Meta / Amazon Parallel-Track Signaling
Advertiser-counsel firms continue to signal parallel-track interest in Apple App Store, Meta advertising, and Amazon advertising/Marketplace overcharge theories. No filed mass-arbitration waves have materialized through May 17 on any of those analogs. The Apple track is most advanced from a liability-predicate standpoint, given the existing <em>Epic v. Apple</em> appellate posture and the ongoing FTC challenges; the Meta and Amazon analogs await stronger underlying-liability findings before they can run a Keller-Postman-style overcharge model with comparable confidence.

## IV. DOJ Section 2 Remedies — U.S. v. Google
The Section 2 remedies phase in <em>U.S. v. Google</em> (search) continues to run through the structural-remedies questioning. DOJ has signaled a preference for divestiture remedies extending to default-search-engine contracts and ad-tech structural separation. The remedies-phase outcome will substantially shape the per-claim damages math in the Keller Postman campaign: a more aggressive structural remedy strengthens the going-forward overcharge theory; a behavioral-remedy-only outcome weakens it. No remedies-phase ruling expected before late summer 2026.

## V. UK CAT / EU DMA Private-Enforcement Comparators
The UK Competition Appeal Tribunal (CAT) and EU Digital Markets Act private-enforcement architecture continues to produce comparator data. The Google-related opt-out class actions filed in the UK CAT under the Consumer Rights Act framework remain on track. The EU DMA's Article 39 private-enforcement provisions have not yet produced a major filed case. Both jurisdictions will produce relevant precedent over the next 12–18 months.

## VI. Litigation-Funding Posture
The litigation-funding posture remains active. Burford, Longford, and Parabellum continue to be the principal underwriters of the larger mass-arbitration campaigns. No new funding-announcement filings of consequence over May 15–17. Funding terms in mass arbitration are tightening as the campaigns generate more empirical data on intake, fee-leverage, and settlement patterns.

## Recommended Actions
Advertiser-brand and agency GCs should: (i) evaluate the firm's Google ad-spend history against the August 2016-onward eligible period; (ii) assess whether to participate in the Keller Postman campaign or stand back; (iii) preserve advertising-spend records under the standard litigation-hold framework. Big Tech antitrust defense counsel should: (iv) finalize arbitration-clause-enforceability strategy as the first wave of claims comes to threshold determinations; (v) prepare damages-methodology contestation for the merits phase. Mass-arbitration claimant-aggregator firms should: (vi) monitor AAA / JAMS administrative-fee architecture for filing-pace optimization; (vii) coordinate parallel-track campaign signaling against Apple / Meta / Amazon underlying-liability developments.
