Tech Mass Arbitration — Knowledge Baseline

Generated: May 15, 2026 Purpose: Seed background knowledge for the daily-briefing topic project dir. Daily advisories rely on this baseline for context; daily runs use the 48-hour freshness window to find what's new.

Active Campaigns

Google advertiser mass arbitration (Keller Postman). Ashley Keller of Keller Postman publicly launched the campaign in April 2026, telling Bloomberg his firm had signed up a "significant number" of advertisers and was preparing to file the first claim waves that week. Keller estimates aggregate exposure at $218 billion or more based on his retained economist's modeling — built around a 5–10% overcharge on Google ad spend dating back to August 2016, trebled under Clayton Act §4 (Bloomberg, Apr 13, 2026; Claims Journal, Apr 14, 2026; Search Engine Land). As of May 11, 2026, Keller Postman's intake portal reports it "represents thousands of U.S. businesses" in the campaign (MassArbitrationClaims.com; adclaimfiling.com). Google Ads' TOS routes claims into AAA consumer/commercial arbitration; the campaign deliberately leverages the AAA's post-April 2024 mass-arb supplement.

Keller Postman track record / timeline benchmark. Prior Keller Postman campaigns took 12–24 months from first filings to settlement leverage: DoorDash (6,000+ filed May 2019, compelled Feb 2020); Postmates (10,000+ filed Feb 2020, Ninth Circuit affirmance Sep 2020, $10M fee order); Intuit (~200,000 claimants by March 2022) (Keller Postman — DoorDash; Postmates; Intuit).

Apple developer-tax track. No formal Keller-style mass-arb campaign has been announced yet. The dominant US vehicles remain (i) Cameron v. Apple developer class action (revived for reconsideration in Dec 2025 after Ninth Circuit set aside Judge Gonzalez Rogers' Oct 2025 dismissal), and (ii) the Apple consumer iOS antitrust class action with a February 2026 trial date (Top Class Actions; AppleInsider; 9to5Mac). The Epic v. Apple contempt order — left in place after SCOTUS denied Apple's stay request in May 2026 — strengthens any future developer mass-arb posture by establishing willful anti-steering conduct (SCOTUSblog, May 2026).

Meta advertiser track. DZ Reserve v. Facebook (long-running advertiser class action over inflated potential-reach metrics, est. damages $2.4–6.2B) is nearing trial. Meta's August 2025 motion to compel arbitration of post-2018 ad buyers was denied in December 2025 for waiver; Meta has filed for Ninth Circuit review, and counsel report over 100,000 individual arbitration claims have been filed in parallel since late 2024 (Courthouse News; MediaPost).

Amazon seller track. Amazon's Business Solutions Agreement contains a forced-arbitration clause, which has so far prevented seller class actions. The consumer-side antitrust class action (De Coster, ~288M class members) was pushed from October 2026 to June 2027 (Hagens Berman; Law360). Keller Postman's case page also flags Amazon as a target vertical (Keller Postman — Amazon).

Underlying Antitrust Posture

Search monopolization — U.S. v. Google (D.D.C., Judge Mehta). August 5, 2024 liability ruling: Google violated Sherman §2 in "general search services" and "general search text advertising" via exclusive-default deals with Apple, Samsung, Verizon, and others (Goodwin; White & Case). Remedies ruling Sep 2, 2025: Mehta declined Chrome divestiture, but ordered an end to exclusive-default contracts, mandated certain search-index/user-interaction data sharing with qualified competitors, and imposed a 6-year technical committee (CNBC; Hughes Hubbard; DOJ Press Release). Google filed Notice of Appeal Jan 16, 2026; DOJ cross-appealed seeking stronger divestitures by its Feb 3, 2026 deadline (tech-insider.org).

Ad tech monopolization — U.S. v. Google (E.D. Va., Judge Brinkema). April 17, 2025 liability ruling: Google unlawfully monopolized the open-web publisher ad-server market and open-web ad-exchange market, and unlawfully tied DFP to AdX. The advertiser ad-networks count was dismissed for lack of a defined relevant market (Simpson Thacher; AdExchanger; Adweek). Remedies trial September–November 2025; closing arguments Nov 21, 2025. Brinkema indicated she had begun drafting and signaled skepticism about full AdX divestiture on timing grounds. Most observers expect a final remedies order in mid-2026 (Norton Rose Fulbright; Adweek; The Current).

AAA / JAMS Mechanics

AAA Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings (effective April 1, 2024). Trigger: 25+ similar claims filed against the same or related parties with coordinated counsel, even if filed serially. April 2024 expansion brought B2B and construction disputes within the rules, but those non-consumer/employment categories have a 100-claim threshold (ADR.org; Goodwin; ABA Business Law Today). Note: the Google advertiser campaign involves business claimants, so the 100-claim threshold likely applies — material for defense strategy.

Fees. AAA now uses a single initiation fee plus a smaller per-case post-initiation fee. Consumer/employment: $3,125 per claimant side / $8,125 per business side, flat regardless of size (Fenwick).

Process Arbitrator / Global Mediator. Both providers appoint a preliminary decision-maker at intake to handle threshold issues before merits arbitrators are seated.

Counsel verification. AAA and JAMS both now require claimant counsel to affirm the accuracy of each claimant's information under Rule-11-style sanction risk (Privacy World).

JAMS Mass Arbitration Procedures (May 2024). JAMS defines a mass arbitration as 75+ coordinated demands, with a $7,500 flat initiation fee regardless of case count (JAMS; Mayer Brown).

Funding Landscape

Burford Capital — largest commercial legal-finance firm; $7B+ portfolio in commercial litigation and arbitration. Burford suffered a setback when the Second Circuit on March 27, 2026 overturned the Argentina-YPF judgment it had funded — relevant context for how funders model collection risk in mass-arb campaigns (Burford Capital).

Longford Capital — antitrust and IP focus; well-capitalized; recently secured a $37M arbitration award against Arigna Technology over allocation of a patent-enforcement campaign's settlement proceeds — under challenge in Texas state court. The ruling is being watched as a bellwether for funder-claimant fee allocation enforcement (Legal News Feed, Feb 19, 2026).

Parabellum Capital — recently raised a $754M fund, one of the largest dedicated US litigation finance vehicles.

Validity Finance, Omni Bridgeway, Pravati Capital, Statera — also active in the space; see Chambers Litigation Funding USA rankings.

Funder identity for the Google campaign is NOT public. Searches did not surface a confirmed funder — flag as open intelligence.

Damages Modeling

The overcharge theory. Plaintiff economist (unnamed in public coverage) models a counterfactual world where Google's tying of DFP/AdX and self-preferencing bidding (Project Bernanke, dynamic revenue share) did not exist. The methodology produces an estimated 5–10% overcharge on advertiser ad spend in the relevant period (August 2016 onward) (AdExchanger; PR Newswire).

Trebling math. Section 4 Clayton Act: proven overcharge × 3 + fees/costs. For an advertiser with $100M of historical Google Ads spend, max claim is roughly $30M (10% × 3) (eMarketing Association).

Issue preclusion exposure. Winston & Strawn flagged "Google's $2.3M Mistake" — Google's bench-trial strategy in liability phases created collateral estoppel risk in downstream private cases. The DOJ liability findings can be invoked as preclusive in subsequent advertiser/publisher proceedings (Winston & Strawn).

Defense Posture

The Heckman cloud. Heckman v. Live Nation (9th Cir. 2024) invalidated Live Nation/Ticketmaster's specifically anti-mass-arb clauses as procedurally/substantively unconscionable. Live Nation filed cert May 5, 2025; if SCOTUS reverses, defendants gain wider latitude to design mass-arb-specific procedures (batching, bellwethers, capped fees) (Jackson Lewis; O'Melveny; Gibson Dunn Jan 2026 webcast).

California SB 707 + Hohenshelt. California Supreme Court in Hohenshelt v. Superior Court (Aug 2025) held SB 707 is not FAA-preempted, but softened its 30-day fee-payment deadline — late payment can be excused absent willfulness/fraud/gross negligence (Privacy World — 2025 YIR).

Waiver attacks. Meta's December 2025 loss on its eleventh-hour motion to compel arbitration of long-pending advertiser claims is the dominant cautionary tale on waiver.

Corporate-plaintiff novelty. Most mass-arb doctrine developed in consumer/employee contexts. The Google campaign is the first major B2B / corporate-plaintiff mass arb at scale, raising open questions on unconscionability (less sympathetic claimants), TOS clickwrap enforceability against sophisticated advertisers, and whether AAA's 100-claim B2B threshold applies in lieu of the consumer 25-claim trigger.

Foreign Analogues

UK CAT. Aug 6, 2025 certification in Prof. Barry Rodger v. Alphabet ([2025] CAT 45) — collective proceedings on behalf of UK developers/advertisers (JMW). Jan 23, 2025 — CPO in Gutmann v. Apple. Apr 2026 — Which? v. Apple ([2026] CAT 29) certified with class-definition limits (Concurrences). Counterexample: Riefa v. Apple ([2025] CAT 5) — certification refused for class-rep authorization deficiency.

EU DMA enforcement. April 2025: Commission fined Apple €500M and Meta €200M for DMA noncompliance (Kluwer Competition Blog).

Germany — emerging private enforcement forum. Aug 2025: Mainz Regional Court enjoined Google's preferential default of Gmail on Android based directly on DMA tying prohibitions (1&1/GMX action). Nov 14, 2025: Berlin II Regional Court ordered Google to pay Idealo €465M in damages over preferential treatment of Google Shopping — built on the Commission's 2017 decision (Noerr Competition Outlook 2026). Germany is now the leading EU forum for digital antitrust damages.

Publisher Class Action (Parallel, Separate from Advertiser Mass Arb)

Distinct from the advertiser mass arb because publishers are not bound by Google's advertiser TOS arbitration clause. In In re Google Digital Advertising Antitrust Litigation (S.D.N.Y., Judge P. Kevin Castel) the court: - Oct 2025: partial summary judgment for Gannett, Daily Mail, and Inform on liability (Castel applied collateral estoppel from Brinkema's Apr 2025 ruling) (Courthouse News PDF; Law360). - Dec 15, 2025: certified a class of ~5,000 publishers (Dec 15, 2016 – Mar 31, 2024) seeking $1.7B+ (Law.com; Bloomberg Law). - Early 2026: notice plan approved; 45-day opt-out window from first notice (Digiday).
