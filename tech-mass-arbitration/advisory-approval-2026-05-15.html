Tech Mass Arbitration Advisory — May 15, 2026 ▼

Analysis & Developments

The dominant story across the U.S. tech mass-arbitration landscape continues to be Keller Postman's Google advertiser campaign, which Ashley Keller publicly launched in April 2026 with an aggregate damages estimate of approximately $218 billion built on a 5-to-10 percent overcharge model running from August 2016 forward, trebled under Section 4 of the Clayton Act. The campaign is structurally novel in two respects: first, it is the first mass arbitration at meaningful scale to target corporate (not consumer or employee) plaintiffs, which raises open questions about clickwrap-TOS enforceability against sophisticated advertisers and unconscionability defenses that have historically been calibrated to consumer-vulnerability frames; second, the procedural posture turns on the post-April 2024 AAA Supplementary Rules for Multiple Case Filings, in which the 25-claim trigger applies to consumer and employment categories but the 100-claim threshold applies to B2B and construction disputes — meaning the Google advertiser campaign almost certainly proceeds under the 100-claim variant rather than the more familiar consumer-mass-arb framework. (Bloomberg) (AAA)

The underlying antitrust posture continues to support the campaign's collateral-estoppel theory. Judge Brinkema's April 17, 2025 liability ruling in U.S. v. Google (E.D. Va.) — finding that Google unlawfully monopolized the open-web publisher ad-server market and the open-web ad-exchange market, and unlawfully tied DFP to AdX — is the dispositive Sherman Section 2 finding that the Keller campaign will press as preclusive. Remedies-phase closing arguments concluded November 21, 2025; most informed observers expect a final remedies order in mid-2026. The parallel D.C. track — Judge Mehta's August 5, 2024 search-monopolization ruling and his September 2, 2025 behavioral-remedies order — has now moved into the D.C. Circuit, with Google's January 16, 2026 notice of appeal and DOJ's February 3 cross-appeal seeking stronger divestitures. (Simpson Thacher) (Norton Rose Fulbright)

The parallel tracks signal asymmetric ripeness. Meta is the lowest-hanging fruit for a follow-on advertiser mass arbitration after its December 2025 loss on the eleventh-hour motion to compel arbitration of post-2018 ad buyers in DZ Reserve v. Facebook — Meta's Ninth Circuit appeal of that waiver finding is pending, but counsel report that over 100,000 individual arbitration claims have been filed in parallel since late 2024. The Apple developer-tax track remains pre-mass-arb: the Cameron v. Apple developer class action was revived for reconsideration in December 2025, and the SCOTUS denial of Apple's stay request in Epic v. Apple in May 2026 has left the contempt order's anti-steering findings intact — both developments materially strengthen any future Apple developer mass arbitration. (Courthouse News) (SCOTUSblog)

The defense-side posture continues to be shaped by the Heckman cloud. Heckman v. Live Nation (9th Cir. 2024) invalidated Live Nation/Ticketmaster's specifically anti-mass-arb clauses as procedurally and substantively unconscionable; Live Nation filed for certiorari on May 5, 2025, and SCOTUS' cert decision is the dominant pending procedural variable. If the Court reverses, defendants gain materially wider latitude to design mass-arb-specific procedures (batching, bellwethers, fee caps). California's Hohenshelt decision (Cal. S. Ct. Aug 2025) softened SB 707's 30-day fee-payment deadline by allowing late payment to be excused absent willfulness, fraud, or gross negligence. (Jackson Lewis) (Privacy World)

The publisher antitrust class action, distinct from the advertiser mass arb because publishers are not bound by Google's advertiser TOS arbitration clause, is moving on a parallel and arguably faster track. In In re Google Digital Advertising Antitrust Litigation before Judge Castel (S.D.N.Y.), the court granted partial summary judgment for Gannett, Daily Mail, and Inform on liability in October 2025 applying collateral estoppel from Brinkema's April 2025 ruling, and certified a class of approximately 5,000 publishers (December 15, 2016 – March 31, 2024) on December 15, 2025 seeking $1.7 billion-plus. The Castel class is the operational litmus test for how aggressively courts will apply Brinkema-derived collateral estoppel. (Law.com) (Bloomberg Law)

Recommended Actions

Plaintiff-side firms evaluating parallel campaigns against Apple, Meta, or Amazon should be pricing the entry sequence against the Heckman cert decision and the Brinkema remedies order; the Meta waiver loss makes Meta the lowest-hanging fruit. Defense-side counsel for Big Tech defendants should be auditing TOS arbitration provisions for Heckman-style anti-mass-arb-clause vulnerabilities and should be preparing alternative procedural designs that survive whatever post-Heckman framework emerges. In-house counsel at advertiser brands with material historical Google Ads spend should be evaluating Keller-style intake processes against alternative-claim-aggregator pricing, should be preserving advertising-spend documentation for the 2016-onward damages window, and should be weighing claim-aggregator participation against the reputational complications of media-buyer relationships with Google. Litigation funders should be tracking the Burford / Longford / Parabellum positioning across mass-arb campaigns. Publisher-side counsel involved in the Castel MDL should be calendaring against the opt-out window and notice plan deadlines.
