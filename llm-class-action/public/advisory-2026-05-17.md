## I. Bartz v. Anthropic — Fairness Hearing Under Submission
The Bartz v. Anthropic fairness hearing on the $1.5 billion class settlement proceeded on May 14, 2026 before Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín, N.D. Cal. As of May 17, the court has taken the matter under submission rather than ruling from the bench — a posture consistent with the size and complexity of the deal and the volume of post-objection record before the court. The settlement architecture remains as preliminarily approved: Anthropic to fund $1.5 billion into a common settlement fund, with approximately 500,000 titles meeting the class definition out of the universe of ~7 million books Anthropic downloaded from LibGen and PiLiMi.
The plaintiffs' counsel [https://copyrightalliance.org/participating-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement/](submissions and Copyright Alliance commentary) over the May 15–17 window emphasize three points: (i) per-title allocations across rightsholders will be governed by the registration-of-record matrix submitted in support of preliminary approval, with author/publisher splits handled by the court-approved allocation formula; (ii) administration and notice expenses are tracked against the $1.5B fund such that class members will see the net distribution figure after fee award; and (iii) the Pioneer v. Brunswick standard for evaluating fairness has been substantially satisfied based on the objector landscape that crystallized by the May 7 objection cut-off. The [https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/](Kluwer Copyright Blog analysis) released this week frames Bartz as the first published-author class action under the Copyright Act to clear the $1B threshold and argues that the settlement should be read as bookending the LibGen-as-source exposure for any AI developer that trained on the same corpus.

## II. Kadrey v. Meta — Seeding-Theory Discovery Posture
The Kadrey v. Meta docket remains live notwithstanding the November 2025 partial summary judgment in Meta's favor on the core fair-use defense as to the training-input side. Plaintiffs' theory of torrent-seeding liability — that Meta's use of BitTorrent to acquire 267 TB of LibGen content gave rise to independent distribution liability under § 106(3) — continues to drive depositions and document production. [https://thenextweb.com/news/publishers-meta-llama-copyright-class-action-2026](Five major publishers) joined the litigation universe over the past week against Meta over Llama-related infringement, layering a publisher-led docket onto the existing author class. The personal-liability claim against Mark Zuckerberg arising from internal communications approving LibGen use remains pending; the most recent public docket activity through May 17 centers on the scope of the seeding-period discovery and the privilege battles over the Meta legal-team memos.

## III. OpenAI MDL — 20M Log Production Continues
The In re OpenAI MDL (S.D.N.Y.) discovery posture remains active. The 20-million-ChatGPT-log production order continues to roll out under the existing privacy-screen protocol, with privilege and PII screening proceeding in batches. No published rulings emerged over the May 15–17 window. The next docketed milestone is the discovery-management conference set in early June, at which the court is expected to address (i) the scope of the production beyond the initial 20M-log tranche, (ii) the deposition-noticing pace into Q3 2026, and (iii) the bellwether-selection protocol that will drive the first-wave trial calendar.

## IV. Third Circuit AI Fair Use — Thomson Reuters v. ROSS
The Third Circuit's pending appeal in <em>Thomson Reuters v. ROSS</em> (No. 25-2153) — the first appellate AI fair-use argument — remains on the calendar. Briefing closed in April; oral argument has not yet been set. The case will produce the first published Circuit decision on the fair-use defense as applied to LLM training and is widely expected to become the controlling authority for downstream cases until either the Ninth Circuit or the Supreme Court speaks. Bench memos circulated by Norton Rose Fulbright and Banner Witcoff this week have not surfaced new substantive analysis; the field continues to model a late-Q3 or early-Q4 2026 argument.

## V. Music-Publisher AI Dockets
The Concord / UMG / ABKCO v. Anthropic cross-motion for summary judgment cycle continues toward the July 15 hearing. No new filings of substance over the May 15–17 window. The music-publisher track is structurally distinct from the book-author track: the fair-use defense plays out against (a) lyrical works as the underlying copyrighted material, (b) song-by-song registration-of-record questions that are sharper than the book-author universe, and (c) a different bundle of § 106 rights (display and derivative-work rights more central than reproduction). The case is being watched as the music-side analog to Bartz and is the most likely vehicle for the second major LLM-copyright settlement over the next six to twelve months.

## VI. Licensing Comparators
The licensing-deal pricing landscape continues to be set by the NYT-Amazon, Google-Reddit, and OpenAI-StackOverflow deals, with the Bartz $1.5B settlement now adding a settlement-economics anchor for damages modeling in the broader docket. The Authors Guild has begun publishing rightsholder-facing guidance on how to evaluate licensing offers against the Bartz benchmark; in-house counsel at AI developers should expect the post-Bartz licensing market to firm around a "Bartz-implied" per-title valuation.

## Recommended Actions
AI-developer GCs should: (i) treat Bartz approval as a working assumption for risk-modeling purposes and finalize their LibGen-corpus exposure analysis on that basis; (ii) review their internal seeding/torrenting protocols against the Kadrey distribution-theory framework; (iii) audit their training-data acquisition documentation for the deposition-readiness standard the OpenAI MDL is now setting. Publisher and rightsholder counsel should: (iv) finalize their Bartz claims-administration submissions if titles are within the class definition; (v) evaluate Concord/UMG cross-MSJ briefing for music-publisher-specific arguments. Plaintiffs' copyright firms should: (vi) calibrate damages models to the Bartz settlement-implied per-title number; (vii) monitor the Third Circuit ROSS calendar for oral-argument setting.
