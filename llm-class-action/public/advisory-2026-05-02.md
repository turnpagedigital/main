# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | May 2, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The Bartz v. Anthropic fairness hearing is now twelve days away, and as the May 14 proceeding before Judge Martínez-Olguín approaches, the music-publisher litigation front against Anthropic has expanded to three concurrent suits. BMG Rights Management filed a separate copyright infringement action on March 18, 2026, targeting 493 copyrighted compositions — including works by Bruno Mars, the Rolling Stones, Ariana Grande, and Justin Bieber — alleging that Anthropic used automated scraping tools and BitTorrent piracy to copy lyrics for Claude's training and that Claude reproduces infringing lyrics on demand when prompted. ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/03/19/bmg-joins-the-lyrical-lawsuits-against-ai-company-anthropic/)) ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/bmg-anthropic-copyright-lawsuit-justin-bieber-bruno-mars-1235533474/)) The BMG suit joins the original Concord Music Group v. Anthropic action — where competing summary judgment motions remain teed up, with the publishers' March 23 partial MSJ marshaling 218 undisputed facts and Anthropic's April 20 cross-motion framing training ingestion as transformative intermediate copying — and the January 28 follow-on filing by Concord, UMG, ABKCO, and Capitol CMG seeking $3.1 billion in statutory damages over 20,517 torrented compositions. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/bmg-sues-anthropic-alleging-ai-giants-380b-valuation-was-built-on-stolen-copyrighted-works/)) Anthropic now faces aggregate music-publisher exposure that could reach well into the billions, and the Concord v. Anthropic cross-motions remain the most direct path to the first judicial ruling on whether AI training on musical compositions constitutes fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107 — a ruling expected this summer that would radiate across the broader docket.

On the settlement front, the Bartz proceeding continues to move toward final approval with $600 million of the $1.5 billion fund now disbursed following Anthropic's second $300 million installment on April 30, and the six-plus unsealed objections — including Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's challenge to the distribution plan's alleged publisher favoritism and the group-registration collapse theory — will be addressed at the hearing alongside class counsel's revised fee petition of $187.5 million under Rule 23(h). ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/)) The Rule 23(e) fairness factors, the lodestar cross-check, and the pro rata versus weighted distribution methodology all remain live issues, and class members who wish to be heard at the hearing may attend in person at the San Francisco Federal Courthouse (Courtroom 12, 19th Floor) or join via Zoom at 2:00 p.m. PT. The settlement administrator's distribution computation is scheduled for June 11, and Wolters Kluwer's Kluwer Copyright Blog has published a comprehensive analysis characterizing the Bartz framework's bifurcated fair-use holding — training on lawfully obtained works constitutes fair use, but acquisition and storage of pirated copies does not — as "the most significant judicial statement on AI copyright in any jurisdiction." ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/))

A significant international development emerged in March with the UK Government's publication of its long-awaited Report on Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, which abandoned the previously proposed text-and-data-mining opt-out exception that would have permitted AI training on copyrighted works subject to rights-holder opt-out. ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/uk-government-maintains-status-quo-on-ai-and-copyr)) ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/uk-copyright-and-ai-report-the-opt-out-is-dead-but-what-comes-next/)) The report, published under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, maintains the status quo — existing UK copyright law applies to AI training without new exceptions — and commits to further evidence-gathering and monitoring of ongoing litigation, including the Getty Images v. Stability AI appeal to the Court of Appeal, before adopting any reform. Over 90 percent of consultation respondents, from both creative and AI sectors, agreed that AI developers should disclose training data sources, a consensus that aligns with the transparency requirements of the CLEAR Act in the United States. The UK report's abandonment of the TDM opt-out is particularly consequential for AI developers operating across jurisdictions: where the EU's DSM Directive Article 4 still provides a conditional TDM exception (subject to rights-holder reservation), the UK has declined to create any equivalent safe harbor, leaving AI companies exposed to infringement claims under existing CDPA provisions unless they secure licenses.

The parallel dockets continue to converge on a summer of consequential rulings. In the OpenAI MDL (No. 3143, S.D.N.Y.), summary judgment briefing in the NYT v. OpenAI track is expected by mid-2026, with the January 5 order compelling production of 20 million ChatGPT output logs — the most sweeping compelled disclosure in any AI copyright proceeding — establishing the discovery baseline. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright)) The Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence appeal in the Third Circuit, where briefing is complete and oral arguments are anticipated this year, will produce the first appellate ruling on whether AI training constitutes fair use — Thomson Reuters has urged the court to affirm the District of Delaware's rejection of ROSS's fair-use defense as to 2,243 Westlaw headnotes. ([LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/11/thomson-reuters-tells-appeals-court-rosss-copying-was-theft-not-innovation.html)) The Andersen v. Stability AI trial remains on track for September 8 before Judge Orrick, and the CJEU's Advocate General opinion in Like Company v. Google — the first binding EU-level guidance on whether generative AI training falls within the DSM Directive Article 4 TDM exception — remains pending following the March 10 hearing.

On the legislative front, the Senate Judiciary subcommittee is expected to hold a hearing on the NO FAKES Act in May, which would create a federal "digital replication right" extending up to 70 years post-mortem with $5,000 statutory damages per unauthorized replica, while the CLEAR Act's bipartisan coalition of 25-plus endorsing organizations continues to represent the most politically viable AI copyright transparency mandate in the 119th Congress. ([Remove Your Media](https://removeyourmedia.com/2026/04/26/the-no-fakes-act-explained-what-it-means-for-rights-holders-fighting-ai-voice-and-likeness-clones/)) Senator Blackburn's 291-page TRUMP AMERICA AI Act draft, which would legislatively declare AI training on copyrighted works not fair use, represents the most aggressive Congressional posture to date, though its political viability remains uncertain against the White House's March 20 National AI Policy Framework expressing the view that training AI models on copyrighted material does not violate copyright law.

## Recommended Actions

With the Bartz fairness hearing twelve days out, class members who filed claims should verify their claim status through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com and confirm that all contact, banking, and payment information is current, as the settlement administrator's distribution computation is scheduled for June 11 — authors whose works were registered under group Copyright Office registrations should be particularly vigilant given the live Bishop objection that group-registration collapse may reduce per-title compensation. Music publishers and songwriters tracking the expanded Anthropic litigation should note that three separate suits are now pending (Concord/UMG original, Concord/UMG II at $3.1 billion, and BMG at 493 compositions), with the Concord v. Anthropic cross-motions providing the most immediate path to a precedential fair-use ruling on music training; rights-holders with additional composition claims should evaluate whether to intervene or file independently before the Concord ruling narrows or forecloses certain theories. AI developers operating in the UK should evaluate the implications of the March 18 Government report's abandonment of the TDM opt-out: the status quo means that training on copyrighted works in the UK is subject to existing CDPA infringement provisions with no safe harbor beyond existing exceptions (which were not designed for AI training), and licensing is the only clear path to compliance pending the Getty appeal's resolution of whether model weights constitute infringing copies. Practitioners monitoring the Thomson Reuters v. Ross appeal should calendar the Third Circuit's expected oral argument date, as the first appellate fair-use ruling on AI training will establish the framework that district courts across the country will apply to the dozens of pending cases.
