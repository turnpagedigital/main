# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | May 1, 2026

## Analysis & Developments

The Bartz v. Anthropic fairness hearing is now thirteen days away, and the settlement's posture heading into the May 14 proceeding before Judge Martínez-Olguín is increasingly well-defined. Anthropic's second $300 million installment was due yesterday, bringing total funded amounts to $600 million of the $1.5 billion settlement fund — by any measure, the largest copyright settlement in American history by amount actually disbursed. Norton Rose Fulbright's comprehensive 2026 AI litigation update, published this week, characterizes Bartz as the "benchmark settlement" that has reshaped the AI copyright landscape, noting that the case's bifurcated fair-use framework — training on lawfully obtained works constitutes fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107, but acquisition and storage of pirated copies does not — has been cited in briefing across at least four other federal AI copyright proceedings. ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026)) The six-plus unsealed objections, including Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's challenge to the distribution plan's alleged publisher favoritism and the group-registration collapse theory, will be addressed at the hearing alongside class counsel's revised fee petition of $187.5 million — 12.5 percent of the fund, reduced from the original 25 percent following Judge Alsup's December 23 memorandum. ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/)) The Rule 23(e) fairness factors, the lodestar cross-check under Rule 23(h), and the pro rata versus weighted distribution methodology all remain live issues that Judge Martínez-Olguín must resolve before final approval. ([Anthropic Settlement Key Dates](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/dates))

The parallel dockets are converging on a summer of consequential rulings. In the Concord Music Group v. Anthropic litigation, competing summary judgment motions remain teed up for the May hearing calendar, with the publishers' March 23 filing marshaling 218 undisputed facts to argue that Anthropic's copying of lyrics was wholesale, commercial, and market-substitutive, while Anthropic's April 20 cross-motion frames training ingestion as "transformative" intermediate copying necessary for Claude to understand language. The RIAA, NMPA, and six industry groups' amicus brief characterizes Anthropic as a "holdout" that scrapes rather than licenses, while the CCIA, AI Progress, and NetChoice counter-amicus defends training as non-expressive copying protected by § 107. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/infringement-on-a-massive-scale-umg-concord-and-abkco-ask-court-to-rule-against-ai-company-anthropic-ahead-of-trial/)) ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/22/anthropic-music-publishers-lawsuit-summary-judgment-motion/)) The court's resolution will be the first judicial ruling on whether AI training on music constitutes fair use and could set precedent that radiates across the broader docket. Meanwhile, in the OpenAI MDL (No. 3143, S.D.N.Y.), Judge Stein's March 9 order granting plaintiffs' motion to compel additional discovery materials reinforces the expansive disclosure posture established by the January 5 order compelling production of 20 million ChatGPT output logs — Norton Rose Fulbright's survey identifies this as the most sweeping compelled disclosure in any AI copyright proceeding to date, with summary judgment briefing in the NYT v. OpenAI track expected by mid-2026. ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026))

A significant development in the visual-arts track is the confirmation that Andersen v. Stability AI is now firmly on a September 8, 2026 trial date before Judge Orrick in the Northern District of California. The plaintiffs filed a third amended complaint on February 27, with Stability AI and Midjourney filing answers on March 13, and the case is deep in discovery. This will be the first AI visual-arts copyright trial in any jurisdiction, testing whether training Stable Diffusion on the LAION dataset's 5 billion scraped images constitutes direct and induced copyright infringement — the outcome will have direct implications for the Disney v. Midjourney consolidated cases and the Getty Images US litigation, both of which track substantially similar theories of infringement. ([CourtListener — Andersen v. Stability AI](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/66732129/andersen-v-stability-ai-ltd/)) ([NYU JIPEL](https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/andersen-v-stability-ai-the-landmark-case-unpacking-the-copyright-risks-of-ai-image-generators/)) On the international front, the CJEU's Advocate General opinion in Like Company v. Google remains pending following the March 10 hearing — the first binding EU-level guidance on whether generative AI training falls within the DSM Directive Article 4 TDM exception — and Getty Images' appeal of the UK High Court ruling is proceeding, with the Court of Appeal's determination of whether model weights constitute "infringing copies" expected to be the first appellate ruling on the question in any common-law jurisdiction. ([Chambers and Partners](https://chambers.com/articles/copyright-and-ai-remain-in-focus-for-2026-with-getty-appeal-given-the-green-light))

On the legislative front, the Recording Academy's 25th GRAMMYS on the Hill event concluded its three-day Washington advocacy push on April 23, with coordinated lobbying for the TRAIN Act, the CLEAR Act (S. 3813), and the NO FAKES Act (H.R. 2794 / S. 1367) — the three bills the creative industries view as the legislative pillars of AI accountability. The Senate Judiciary subcommittee is expected to hold a hearing on the NO FAKES Act in May, which would create a federal "digital replication right" extending up to 70 years post-mortem and allowing individuals to block unauthorized AI-generated replicas of their voice and likeness. The CLEAR Act's bipartisan coalition of 25-plus endorsing organizations, including SAG-AFTRA, the Authors Guild, and the RIAA, continues to represent the most politically viable AI copyright transparency mandate in the 119th Congress, with its 30-day pre-release disclosure requirement and $5,000 civil penalties per failure creating a compliance framework that AI developers should be modeling against now. ([Recording Academy](https://www.recordingacademy.com/advocacy/news/grammys-on-the-hill-2025-no-fakes-act-advocacy-martina-mcbride)) ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/02/11/clear-act-establish-notice-requirements-copyrighted-works-ai-training-data/))

## Recommended Actions

With the Bartz fairness hearing thirteen days out, class members who filed claims should verify their claim status through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com and confirm that all contact, banking, and payment information is current, as the settlement administrator's distribution computation is scheduled for June 11 — authors whose works were registered under group Copyright Office registrations should be particularly vigilant given the live Bishop objection that group-registration collapse may reduce per-title compensation. Rights-holders tracking the Concord v. Anthropic cross-motions should be preparing for the possibility that the first judicial ruling on AI training of music could arrive within weeks, as a ruling either way will materially affect the valuation of independent training-side claims and the settlement benchmarking for future negotiations. AI developers should evaluate their training-data provenance documentation and licensing posture against three near-term compliance vectors: the CLEAR Act's 30-day pre-release disclosure requirement, the OpenAI MDL's demonstration that courts will compel production of millions of output logs, and the Andersen v. Stability AI discovery process that is stress-testing what internal documentation AI companies maintained during training-data acquisition. Practitioners monitoring international developments should calendar the expected Advocate General opinion in Like Company v. Google and the Getty v. Stability AI Court of Appeal hearing date, and should track the Senate Judiciary subcommittee's May hearing on the NO FAKES Act as a signal of whether federal digital-replication legislation will advance before the August recess.
