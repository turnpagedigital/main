# LLM CLASS ACTION ADVISORY | April 16, 2026

#### Analysis & Developments

The doctrinal tectonics underneath the LLM copyright docket have shifted materially in the twenty-two days since the U.S. Supreme Court's unanimous decision in *Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment*, 605 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026), and the first downstream ripple has now reached *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), and its parallel dockets. *Cox* reversed the Fourth Circuit's $1 billion verdict against the ISP and rejected the "mere knowledge" theory of contributory liability in favor of a two-track intent standard: a defendant contributes to infringement only through (i) active inducement or (ii) services "not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses." ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/04/supreme-court-narrows-secondary-copyright-liability-in-cox)) ([SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/03/court-rejects-billion-dollar-judgment-for-copyright-infringement-by-internet-service-provider/)) For general-purpose large-language-model developers whose products have undeniable substantial lawful uses, *Cox* functionally retires one of the plaintiffs' bar's principal secondary-liability theories at the entity level; but the same decision has sharpened a different blade, because the intent-based "active inducement" prong invites plaintiffs to pursue individual executives whose contemporaneous communications can satisfy *Cox*'s heightened mens rea.

That re-weaponization is already visible on the Concord Music docket. On April 7, 2026, the music-publisher plaintiffs in *Concord Music Group, Inc. v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 5:24-cv-03811 (N.D. Cal.), filed an amended complaint that moves the intentional-contributory-infringement claim off Anthropic PBC as an entity and onto CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann in their individual capacities, relying expressly on internal Anthropic communications and engineering records disclosed in the unsealed *Bartz* objector filings to plead inducement under the new *Cox* standard. ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/04/07/after-supreme-courts-decision-in-cox-communications-concord-music-changes-its-claim-of-intentional-contributory-infringement-from-anthropic-to-dario-amodei-benjamin-mann-based-on-evidence-revealed/)) The maneuver is consequential on three dimensions: it converts the *Bartz* unsealing from a fairness-hearing liability into live evidentiary fuel in the parallel publisher action; it tests whether post-*Cox* individual-officer liability will become the preferred route for plaintiffs holding discovery showing knowledge-plus-encouragement of pirate-library ingestion; and it creates a directors-and-officers insurance question that every AI-company general counsel now has to cost into the next quarterly reserves cycle. The *In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation* MDL, No. 1:25-md-03143 (S.D.N.Y.), should be read as the next laboratory for this framework, given that the January 2026 Stein affirmance of the 20 million-log production order now couples output-side substitution discovery with an inducement-friendly legal standard for individual defendants.

*Bartz* itself is in the final-approval pre-hearing window: Judge Martínez-Olguín's continued May 14, 2026, 2:00 p.m. PT fairness hearing in Courtroom 12 of the San Francisco federal courthouse is now 28 days out, the March 30 claims-submission deadline has closed, the second $300 million Anthropic installment is due April 30, 2026, and the Settlement Administrator's pro rata distribution computation is scheduled for June 11, 2026, on the published key-dates calendar. ([Settlement Site — Key Dates](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/dates)) ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/04/14/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-update-new-date-and-time-for-the-fairness-hearing-you-can-join-online-and-unsealed-objections/)) Professor Lea Victoria Bishop's unsealed objection — the structural template the Court will use to evaluate Rule 23(e)(2)(A)–(D) — remains unresolved on record-misrepresentation, publisher-versus-author allocation, foreign-works exclusion, and the per-title comparator risk, and Writer Beware's April 10 practitioner alert has surfaced additional class-member questions about arbitration mechanisms, escheat/cy pres, and unclaimed-share allocation to publishers that will frame oral argument. ([Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2026/04/10/anthropic-copyright-settlement-april-update/)) Parallel to the class action, the six *Bartz* opt-outs led by Pulitzer Prize winner John Carreyrou have filed their non-class suit in the Northern District against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI, and Perplexity seeking the full 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) willful-infringement ceiling of $150,000 per work per defendant — theoretical exposure of roughly $900,000 per book — on the explicit premise that the ~$3,000 per-title Bartz comparator is "pennies on the dollar." ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/99347-authors-file-new-lawsuit-against-ai-companies-seeking-more-money.html)) ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-anthropic-xai-hit-with-copyright-lawsuit-from-writers)) The filing is the first copyright training suit against xAI and the first authors' suit against Perplexity training, and it supplies a real-time market test of whether the Bartz number will set a floor or, as objectors have warned, a ceiling.

Parallel docket movement reinforces that 2026's second quarter is structured around pre-trial consolidation rather than fair-use rulings. In *Kadrey v. Meta Platforms, Inc.*, No. 3:23-cv-03417 (N.D. Cal.), Judge Chhabria's March 27, 2026 scheduling order now places the dispositive-motion hearing on the surviving BitTorrent seeding and § 106(3) distribution claims on February 25, 2027 at 10:00 a.m. in San Francisco, meaning the Kadrey distribution claim is functionally on ice until mid-2027 even as the June 2025 training-fair-use holding continues to govern the merits of the first phase. ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/03/27/kadrey-v-meta-gets-scheduling-order-summary-judgment-motion-hearing-for-distribution-claim-wont-be-heard-until-feb-25-2027/)) In *Disney Enterprises, Inc. v. Midjourney, Inc.*, No. 2:25-cv-05275 (C.D. Cal.), the April 13 docket entry signals that the consolidated Disney/Warner/Universal character-output theory is continuing in merits discovery on an accelerated schedule, with the post-mediation status conference holding at August 31, 2026. ([CourtListener — Disney v. Midjourney](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70513159/disney-enterprises-inc-v-midjourney-inc/)) Internationally, the English Court of Appeal's April 1, 2026 grant of permission to appeal in *Getty Images (US) Inc. v. Stability AI Ltd.* now sets the stage for the first UK appellate decision on whether intangible AI model weights can qualify as "infringing copies" within the meaning of Copyright, Designs and Patents Act § 27 — a doctrinal question with direct comparator value for U.S. § 106 reproduction-right theories, and one that Stability will litigate without a cross-appeal on the limited trade-mark findings. ([Wiggin LLP](https://www.wiggin.co.uk/insight/high-court-grants-permission-to-appeal-in-getty-images-v-stability-ai/)) ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102lydc/getty-images-v-stability-ai-getty-granted-permission-to-appeal/)) Taken together, the post-*Cox* re-pleading pattern, the *Bartz* pre-hearing posture, the Carreyrou opt-out suit, and the UK appeal collectively mark the first quarter of Bartz's afterlife: the settlement no longer stands alone as a doctrinal event but is being actively metabolized — cited, distinguished, and repurposed — across four jurisdictions and three copyright theories.

#### Recommended Actions

Class members who timely submitted claims through the anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com portal should treat the next four weeks as the operational validation window, confirming receipt acknowledgments, reconciling group-registration inputs against the Settlement Administrator's per-title match, and preparing to escalate any pre-distribution disputes through the approved administration protocol before the June 11 computation date. Rights-holders in the publisher action should recalibrate their case strategy to *Cox*'s new intent standard, ensuring that any contributory-infringement theory is pled with specific inducement allegations and supported by document productions of the kind revealed in the *Bartz* unsealing; defense-side AI-company general counsel should commission a cross-functional audit of internal communications — engineering commits, Slack channels, and executive email — that could be read as encouragement or active direction of pirate-library ingestion, because individual-officer liability under *Cox* now depends on exactly that record. AI developers facing parallel training-data theories — Meta, OpenAI, Stability, Midjourney, xAI, Perplexity, Google, and counterparties in the Concord/UMG and Carreyrou actions — should benchmark their licensing pipelines against the Bartz per-title comparator, audit DMCA § 1202 CMI preservation across ingestion pipelines given the disproportionate statutory-damages exposure on that theory, and ensure outside counsel have updated discovery protocols assuming the post-OpenAI MDL log-production standard applies to output-side substitution analysis. Publishers and authors' agents negotiating licensing arrangements should memorialize the Bartz number and the Copyright Office Part 3 framework in comparator rate sheets to resist the settlement's use as a downward benchmark, and rights-holders considering opt-out litigation on the Carreyrou model should conduct a § 412 registration audit — timely-registered U.S. works unlock the statutory-damages ceiling that is doing the economic work of that pleading. Finally, foreign-origin authors whose titles were ingested through the Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror corpora should engage counsel before the May 14 hearing to preserve direct claims outside the class release, because whichever way the Court rules on the foreign-works carve-out, the next 18 months of individual filings will be shaped by that ruling.
