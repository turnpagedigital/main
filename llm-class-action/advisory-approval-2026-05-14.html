LLM Class Action Advisory
May 14, 2026
Analysis & Developments

Today, May 14, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. PT, Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín will convene the Rule 23(e) fairness hearing for Bartz v. Anthropic PBC, 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), the $1.5 billion class action settlement that remains the largest copyright resolution in United States history. The hearing, held in Courtroom 12 of the San Francisco Federal Courthouse with Zoom participation available for objectors who preserved their Rule 23(e)(5) rights, will require the court to make individualized findings on at least five principal objection themes distilled from the unsealed filings ordered accessible in Judge Martínez-Olguín’s April 8 docket entry (Dkt. 632): the exclusion of foreign and non-U.S.-registered works from the class definition despite 17 U.S.C. § 411(a)’s registration exemption for foreign authors, a concern affecting over two million titles; the distribution plan’s systematic favoring of publishers over individual authors, with Professor Lea Victoria Bishop’s objection (Dkt. 630) estimating publishers could capture roughly fifty percent or more of the net settlement fund; the allegedly misleading class notice that obscured opt-out value by framing the choice as settlement-or-nothing rather than contextualizing the $3,000 per-title payout against 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)’s $150,000 statutory-damages ceiling; class counsel’s alleged concealment of Judge Alsup’s December 2025 concerns from the incoming judge; and the group-registration undercounting methodology that collapses dozens of distinct books registered under a single group number into one claimable work (Dkt. 641). The settlement’s claims data remains extraordinary: 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works have been claimed—a 91.3 percent claim rate that dwarfs the typical ten-percent benchmark—with approximately 120,000 individual authors filing. (Authors Alliance) (Authors Guild)

Anthropic’s second $300 million installment was funded on April 30, bringing the total in escrow to $600 million of the $1.5 billion total; the Settlement Administrator is scheduled to calculate distributions on June 11, with disbursement beginning later that month. Class counsel’s revised fee petition of $187.5 million—reduced from the original $300 million (20%) to 12.5 percent of the gross fund—awaits ruling alongside the fairness determination, with Judge Martínez-Olguín having signaled skepticism toward coordination-counsel fees outside the Susman Godfrey and Lieff Cabraser lead structure. Notable opt-outs include John Carreyrou and other authors who elected to pursue a separate lawsuit against Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta, xAI, and Perplexity, preserving their individual statutory-damages claims under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c). The court’s ruling today, whether issued from the bench or taken under submission, will establish the precedential framework against which every subsequent AI training-data settlement will be measured. (Copyright Alliance) (anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com)

In the most significant new filing since the last advisory, the May 5 class action Elsevier Inc. et al. v. Meta Platforms, Inc. (S.D.N.Y.) brought five major publishing houses—Hachette, Macmillan, McGraw Hill, Elsevier, and Cengage—together with bestselling author Scott Turow, against Meta and CEO Mark Zuckerberg personally. The complaint alleges Zuckerberg “personally authorized and actively encouraged” the torrenting of 267 terabytes of pirated books and journal articles from LibGen and Anna’s Archive to train Meta’s Llama large language models, after Meta abandoned a $200 million licensing budget at Zuckerberg’s instruction. Represented by Debevoise & Plimpton, Oppenheim + Zebrak, and Keller Rohrback, the plaintiffs seek statutory damages, a permanent injunction, and an order to destroy all infringing copies. The personal-liability theory against Zuckerberg raises novel D&O insurance and corporate-veil questions that, if sustained, could transform the risk calculus for AI executives authorizing bulk training-data acquisition without licensing. (AAP) (Washington Post) (NPR)

On the appellate front, supplemental briefs filed May 11 in Thomson Reuters Enterprise Centre GmbH v. Ross Intelligence Inc. (3d Cir. No. 25-2153) reveal sharply divergent readings of the Third Circuit’s recent ASTM v. UpCodes fair-use ruling. ROSS Intelligence argues that UpCodes is essentially controlling and demands summary reversal: training an AI model on copyrighted legal materials to build a new research platform is at least as transformative as UpCodes’ dissemination of incorporated building standards. Thomson Reuters counters that UpCodes changes nothing because ROSS’s purpose was commercially substitutive, and invokes Judge Alsup’s Bartz summary-judgment framework. Oral argument is set for June 11, 2026—the first appellate fair-use argument on AI training—and the panel’s treatment of the objective-versus-subjective purpose distinction will directly inform fair-use analysis across every pending AI copyright docket. (XIRA/LawNext) (ChatGPT Is Eating the World)

The parallel music-publishing front against Anthropic continues to sharpen. Anthropic’s fair-use response brief in Concord Music Group v. Anthropic argues that training on lyrics is transformative and publishers lack evidence of market harm, while the publishers’ cross-motion for partial summary judgment presents 218 undisputed facts. The cross-MSJ hearing is set for July 15, 2026, before Judge Lee. On the legislative front, the NO FAKES Act and TRAIN Act continue to gather bipartisan momentum, with Senators Blackburn and Welch convening their April 22 roundtable and the Copyright Office’s Part 1 report citing an “urgent” need for federal digital-replicas legislation. Senator Blackburn’s TRUMP AMERICA AI Act discussion draft would amend the Copyright Act to declare that AI training on copyrighted works does not qualify as fair use under 17 U.S.C. § 107, creating direct tension with the judicial determinations pending in Bartz, Thomson Reuters, and the OpenAI MDL. (Billboard) (Music Business Worldwide) (Deadline) (FedScoop)

Recommended Actions

Class members with claims already filed should verify their payment information through the settlement portal at anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com before the June 11 distribution computation, particularly authors who registered works under group copyright registrations—the undercounting methodology flagged in Dkt. 641 may require supplemental documentation to ensure each distinct title is recognized as a separate claimable work. Rights-holders who opted out to preserve individual statutory-damages claims under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c) should be monitoring today’s fairness hearing closely, as the court’s treatment of the per-work valuation framework and the publisher-author allocation methodology will directly inform litigation-value assessments across parallel dockets. Music publishers with compositions in Anthropic’s training corpus should coordinate with RIAA and NMPA counsel on the Concord summary-judgment briefing ahead of the July 15 hearing and benchmark internal licensing-deal comparators against the Disney–OpenAI $1 billion framework. AI developers facing similar input-side training theories should conduct outbound-training-data audits against the Bartz and Elsevier frameworks, evaluate DMCA § 1202 CMI-stripping exposure, and track the Thomson Reuters v. ROSS oral argument on June 11 for the Third Circuit’s treatment of the objective-versus-subjective transformative-purpose test.
