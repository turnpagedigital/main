## LinkedIn

Two clocks in the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history are about to converge in the same week.

Bartz v. Anthropic has now sat under submission for eighteen days since the May 14 fairness hearing — past the 7–21 day band where Judge Martínez-Olguín's final-approval order on the $1.5 billion fund should land. And the settlement administrator is scheduled to calculate per-work distributions by June 11.

Put those together and the picture sharpens: the final-approval order and the computed allocation arrive in the same window. Any appeal clock starts running not against an abstract fund, but against a number each claimant can see.

The record is built to survive that scrutiny. A 91.3% claims rate — 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed by roughly 120,000 authors — all but forecloses adequacy-of-notice objections. Class counsel's voluntary fee cut from 15% to 12.5% ($187.5 million) removes the most-litigated objection category before the order even issues.

Why it matters beyond this case: the ~$3,000-per-work structure becomes the operative comparator for every other AI training-data matter on the docket — the OpenAI MDL, Concord/UMG v. Anthropic, Disney v. Midjourney. Once Bartz is on the books, those negotiations stop starting from a blank slate.

If you have works in the claim pool, verify your payment information on the settlement portal this week — before the June 11 computation locks allocations.
