## LinkedIn

Fifteen days, and counting.

That's how long Bartz v. Anthropic has been under submission since the May 14 fairness hearing — the final step before Judge Martínez-Olguín signs off on the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history: a $1.5 billion fund for authors whose books were used to train Anthropic's models.

The customary window for a final-approval order after a fairness hearing is roughly 7 to 21 days. We're now past the midpoint. The order isn't late in any troubling sense — but it is squarely in the band where it should land any day now.

Why the wait is worth watching: the moment that order is entered, the settlement's economics stop being a one-case story and become the market's reference point. The implicit per-book value — roughly $3,000 at the high end of the claimed pool — becomes the comparator every other AI training-data case gets measured against. The OpenAI MDL. Concord/UMG v. Anthropic. Disney v. Midjourney. None of them will negotiate from a blank slate again.

And the claims data has already done the hard work of insulating the deal on appeal: 440,490 of 482,460 eligible works claimed — a 91.3% rate across roughly 120,000 rightsholders. That number effectively forecloses any serious adequacy-of-notice challenge.

For rightsholder counsel, the homework is the same whether the order lands today or next week: model your matters against the Bartz benchmark now, before the comparator is formally on the books.
