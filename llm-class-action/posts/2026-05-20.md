**Single theme:** The $3,000-per-work benchmark Bartz will lock in as the comparator for the rest of the AI training-data docket.

## LinkedIn

When Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín enters the final-approval order in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC*, 3:24-cv-05417 (N.D. Cal.), the docket will record the largest copyright class resolution in United States history. The settlement number — $1.5 billion — is the headline. The number that actually matters is the one underneath it: roughly $3,000 per work at the high end of the claimed pool.

That per-work figure is the operative consequence of the deal. Every AI training-data class action filed after Bartz will model damages against it. Counsel for the OpenAI MDL plaintiffs are already pricing publisher claims off the same arithmetic. The *Concord / UMG v. Anthropic* music-publisher track is doing the same. *Disney v. Midjourney*'s negotiating posture cannot ignore it. Whatever the next round of cases pleads in damages, the floor argument writes itself: Bartz already paid $3,000 a work, and our class is owed at least that.

Two procedural facts make the benchmark especially hard for defense counsel to argue around. First, the 92.77 percent claims rate is the highest ever recorded on a copyright class of this scale, which forecloses the standard adequacy-of-notice attack on the deal. Second, only 350 valid opt-outs were filed, covering 1,802 works — small enough that the residual non-class population is not a meaningful damages-comparison anchor.

The fee petition reinforces the per-work read. Class counsel revised down from 15 percent to 12.5 percent of the gross fund, yielding approximately $187.5 million plus $3 million in incurred expenses and an $18.22 million cost reserve. That fee discipline removes one of the standard objector attack vectors and makes the $3,000-per-work calculation the cleanest data point in the settlement architecture for downstream cases to cite.

The customary 7-to-21-day window after the May 14 fairness hearing puts Judge Martínez-Olguín's final-approval order anywhere from May 22 through the first week of June. From entry forward, the per-work number is the settled comparator.
