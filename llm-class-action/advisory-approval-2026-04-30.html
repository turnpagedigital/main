Anthropic Claims Fair Use in Push to End Music Publishers Lawsuit Legal
Digital Music News  ·  April 22, 2026
Anthropic's summary judgment brief argues training on lyrics is "transformative" fair use, setting up the first judicial ruling on AI training of music. With publishers' cross-motion filed March 23 and the $3 billion second complaint adding statutory-damages pressure under 17 U.S.C. § 504(c), the May hearing could produce the most significant fair-use precedent since the Bartz framework.
Anthropic Settlement Lawyers Cut Fees to Win Approval Settlement
JDJournal  ·  March 23, 2026
Class counsel reduced their fee request from $375 million (25%) to $187.5 million (12.5%) after Judge Alsup's December critique. The 12.5% rate is well below the typical 30% class-action benchmark and may strengthen the Rule 23(e) fairness showing, but the lodestar cross-check under Rule 23(h) remains pending at the May 14 hearing.
Copyright Infringement Suits Loom With Unchecked AI Vibe Coding Doctrine
Bloomberg Law  ·  April 29, 2026
Analysis warns that AI "vibe coding" — using natural language prompts to generate code with minimal human review — creates new output-side copyright infringement exposure. Directly implicates DMCA § 1202 CMI theories at issue in Doe v. GitHub and expands the frontier of AI copyright risk beyond training to deployment.
Copyright and AI Remain in Focus for 2026 With Getty Appeal Given the Green Light Policy
Chambers and Partners  ·  2026
Getty Images has been granted permission to appeal the UK High Court's dismissal of its copyright claims against Stability AI. The Court of Appeal's determination of whether model weights constitute "infringing copies" will be the first appellate ruling on this question in any common-law jurisdiction, with direct implications for EU Art. 4 TDM interpretation.
CLEAR Act Would Establish Notice Requirements for Copyrighted Works in AI Training Data Policy
IPWatchdog  ·  February 11, 2026
The bipartisan CLEAR Act (S. 3813) would require AI companies to file notices with the Copyright Office detailing copyrighted works in training datasets, with $5,000-per-instance civil penalties and a public database. With 25+ endorsing organizations including SAG-AFTRA and the RIAA, this is the most politically viable AI copyright transparency bill in the 119th Congress.
